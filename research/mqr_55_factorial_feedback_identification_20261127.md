## Problem and exact scope
0.48-D through 0.52-D opening examples and verdict/handoff directly reviewed. A shared observation can result from target drift, deployment response, standard change, or a deployment-standard interaction. Define a deterministic contrast on a common baseline and target: Y(p,m)=d+p*r+m*b+p*m*i, p,m binary, coefficients in Nat. This is an additive saturated two-factor design, established mathematics, not claimed novelty. No claims about realized clinical or institutional feedback, no historical causal inference. Common target/time and intervention comparability, invariant coefficients, no carryover or unmodeled interference are assumptions, not proved facts. Nonnegative restriction excludes negative effects; no statistical noise or heterogeneous units.

## Formal result
Lean source language/real/lean/MQR/FeedbackIdentification.lean has eight theorem statements/proofs without sorry: baseline and policy-only identify response; three cells 00,10,01 identify drift/response/bias; four identify all four coefficients. Concrete pairs with binary coefficients prove non-identification for before/after and each omitted cell. Examples: omit baseline, worlds (0,1,1,0) and (1,0,0,1) agree at 10,01,11; omit 10, (0,1,0,0) and (0,0,0,1); omit 01, (0,0,1,0) and (0,0,0,1); omit 11, (0,0,0,0) and (0,0,0,1). This also implies the baseline-policy pair is necessary for a universal response guarantee: if either is omitted the corresponding witness persists in any subset. Not a claim that every observed dataset needs four cells: particular extreme values can identify coefficients without all four.
Prolog language/real/prolog/feedback_identification_v55.pl enumerates all 16 binary worlds given a ground list of cell(P,M,Y), validates input, reports inconsistent vs identified(V) vs ambiguous(Vs). Empty observations leave all worlds; contradictory observations return none; invalid input fails. Closed family explicitly bounded; inconsistency does not establish which premise failed.
