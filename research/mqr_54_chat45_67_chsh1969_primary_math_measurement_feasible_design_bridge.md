## Source passage / assumptions (1969)

- Received **4 Aug 1969**, published **13 Oct 1969**. Original text p. 881 defines local deterministic A(a,λ), B(b,λ), source distribution ρ(λ) independent of settings, and emphasizes that Bell's original requirement of perfect correlation was experimentally unrealistic. Eq. (1a) is `|P(a,b)-P(a,c)| ≤ 2-P(b',b)-P(b',c)`; the rotationally invariant reduced Eq. (1b) uses α, β, γ.
- In a complete ideal ±1 measurement using photon detection/nondetection, those inequalities would apply to counts directly. Authors say detector efficiency **too low for a practical test** of that ideal version (p. 881).
- They reinterpret the latent ±1 variable as *emergence/nonemergence from a polarizer*, not necessarily detection/nondetection, and introduce the setting `∞` for polarizer removal. Crucial **additional empirical assumption**: conditional on the pair emerging, joint detection probability does not depend on polarizer settings; the input flux is constant across settings. This is needed to infer the emergence probabilities from measured coincidences. There is a separate caveat that single-arm rates R1(a), R2(b) are constants before using Eq. (2a).
- Eq. (2a) observable rate restriction: `|R(a,b)-R(a,c)| + R(b',b)+R(b',c)−R1−R2 ≤ 0`. Rotational reduction Eq. (2b): `|R(α)-R(α+β)| + R(γ)+R(β+γ)-R1-R2 ≤0`.
- The pre-existing **Kocher–Commins** calcium-cascade setup recorded orientations 0° and 90°, insufficient to test (2b), and its polarizers were inefficient (p. 882).
- Direct proposal changes **the design menu**: observe at two informative relative orientations 22.5° and 67.5° (for the 0–1–0 cascade), plus one-polarizer-removal calibration and both-polarizer-removal normalization, not just duplicate the original 0°/90° data (pp. 882–883).
- They explicitly quantify imperfect polarizer efficiencies ε_M^i, ε_m^i, collection cone half-angle θ and angular-acceptance factor F1(θ), rather than merely postulate an ideal four-correlation S.

## Three separate inferential steps

1. **Formal model-class discriminator:** local hidden variable hypotheses → inequality in counterfactual ±1 outcomes. This is a mathematical proof conditional on locality, setting independence etc.
2. **Measurement bridge:** operational coincidence rates substitute for hidden emergence probabilities only under photon transmission/detection assumptions and calibration conditions. Without these assumptions, measurement of R does not straightforwardly test the ideal bound.
3. **Experimental question/design:** apparatus geometry/angles and remove-polarizer controls are adapted to the bridged inequality. The paper is a **dated proposal**, therefore documentary evidence of theory-guided experiment *proposal*, not alone proof of actual laboratory execution.

This is a much stronger Q5/Q7 historical witness than the synthetic R61 PR-box postselection reconstruction; however, it is emphatically **not an original MQR discovery**. Its contribution is to separate ideal counterfactual theory, practical observable bridge and realizable settings, and to explain which transition required extra assumptions.

## R61 non-equivalence test

R61 used a *globally coordinated selection filter depending on both settings and both outcomes*. The 1969 CHSH paper instead assumes setting-independent conditional detection probability after polarizer emergence. The former violates admissibility; the latter attempts to **justify** a measurement bridge by an extra empirical assumption. These cannot be treated as the same sampling model or the same detector-efficiency threshold. In particular, R61's rho<1/3 threshold has **no physical claim** about photodetection efficiencies and does NOT describe the 1969 experiment.
