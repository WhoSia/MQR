## 1. Experimental discovery and variation: Ørsted, as reconstructed by Steinle

**Source:** Friedrich Steinle, *Exploratory Experiments: Ampère, Faraday, and the Origins of Electrodynamics* (2016 English translation from 2005 German), University of Pittsburgh Press. Drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-kviuErWDAEJMRlcbACBX7Fb55hV1G/view . **Direct reading:** discussion in the chapter 'Electromagnetism', around printed pp. 55–67, including reported Ørsted (1820) conductor–compass variations and accounts of replication.

**Episode and evidence roles:** Ørsted varied the conducting wire's location relative to a magnetized needle, replaced materials, interposed various substances, and found some failures/negative results. Needle deflection reversed for above/below wire, changed with geometry and polarity; replacing a magnetized needle with brass, glass or shellac did not reproduce the effect. Steinle reports broad replication including with smaller batteries, though the earliest conceptual interpretation of geometry and surrounding space remained unsettled.

**Important interpretive boundary:** Ørsted's 'electrical conflict' is a historical description of a not-yet-fully-defined process, not a contemporary electromagnetic field definition. A claim about what Ørsted directly wrote is mediated by Steinle, whose book cites original 1820 reports. The MQR author has **not** directly checked an original 1820 scan here.

**What changes:** A reproducible new **effect** plus variation conditions open novel experimental questions before everyone accepts one geometric or physical explanatory vocabulary. Not all negative results are evidence of 'no underlying phenomenon'; the probes have controlled specificity.

## 2. Explanatory analogies and graded ontological commitment: Maxwell, through de Regt

**Source:** Henk W. de Regt (2017), *Understanding Scientific Understanding*, Oxford University Press. Drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ykJzrDLwdxW0E_g5HkIAmUH_z6qnsK7e/view . **Direct reading:** chapter 6 §6.1.2 around printed pp. 194–198 and chapter 5 §5.4, chapter 4 §4.2.

**Episode:** Maxwell's 1856 hydrodynamical analogy between Faraday's lines of force and incompressible flow evolves into the vortex/idle-wheel mechanical model in the 1862 'On Physical Lines of Force'. De Regt emphasizes its creative power, understanding and analogy without unconditional literal realism about the ether's gears. It supported electromagnetic/light unification and new theoretical possibilities. This is a crucial disjunction: an analogical model can generate valuable inquiries and mathematical deductions without its pictured mechanical entities having earned literal ontological status. De Regt notes historian disputes about the precise historical derivation role for displacement current (his fn. 13); **do not turn the contested causal history into a certain singular mechanism.**

**What changes:** criteria and tools for *intelligibility*. Geometrical and mechanical analogies can make an otherwise abstract relation thinkable, but an analogy's inferential role does not prove the same kind of matter or mechanism exists in the world. Retrospective success of Maxwell's equations is not historical proof of the mechanical ether model.

## 3. Experiment / terminology / research-community uptake: tRNA through Rheinberger

**Source:** Hans-Jörg Rheinberger (1997), *Toward a History of Epistemic Things: Synthesizing Proteins in the Test Tube*, Stanford University Press. Drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1DDrn0i1jOZ79tx8VxftIgQmdTrNH4tr8/view . **Direct reading:** chapter 'Names Matter: Transfer RNA and Ribosomes, 1958–61', especially printed pp. 187–190; Ch. 2 printed pp. 29–30.

**Episode:** The experimental fraction labels 'pH 5 RNA' / 'soluble RNA' referred to biochemical preparation/solubility. Richard Schweet's 1959 'transfer RNA' terminology highlighted an amino-acid-carrier role and transfer function. The term gradually traveled across the field rather than being universally adopted instantly; Zamecnik initially held interpretive reservations. The changed labels could reorganize mechanistic narrative even around apparently unchanged drawings. More broadly, 'microsome' to 'ribosome' tracked changes in what preparations and functions researchers could differentiate. **Not just 'a rename creates new biological entities'**: experimentally constituted roles, theoretical tools and biological practices changed jointly.

**Key historical caution:** Rheinberger describes the molecular biology perspective supplementing the biochemical regime partially and gradually, not instant total paradigm replacement. Retrospective historical narration tends to create a straight 'path to tRNA' that the actors did not experience.

## 4. Distinct rival reading of the same biological episode: Fry 2022

**Source:** Michael Fry (2022), 'Crick's Adaptor Hypothesis and the Discovery of Transfer RNA: Experiment Surpassing Theoretical Prediction', *Philosophy, Theory, and Practice in Biology* 14:11, DOI **10.3998/ptpbio.2628**. Directly held paper in Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KVPJTOaL3eiu6_Z-ZCfZUw0seZ6CVOsV/view . **Direct reading:** abstract, §§1–2 discussion of prediction-versus-accommodation, account of the adaptor hypothesis and historical experimental findings, later citation-impact discussion and concluding comparative argument.

**Rival interpretive emphasis:** Crick correctly anticipated functionally appropriate adaptor molecules, but Fry argues Hoagland and Zamecnik's empirically discovered tRNA and experimental model had greater direct community impact than Crick's earlier prediction. Citation-count evidence is **a proxy for recorded uptake**, not a pure measure of truth, originality or actual impact; retrospective personal judgments and citation styles may bias the comparison. Fry and Rheinberger can be used as *complementary, contestable historical reconstructions*, not independent agreement on all details.

This forces three achievements apart:
- **P — predictive conjecture**: a proposed entity/function is thinkable and anticipates an empirical possibility.
- **D — material identification / assay**: researchers produce and stabilize experimental evidence for an object.
- **U — uptake/retooling**: new terms and assay methods reorganize subsequent work, potentially without following the chronology of a dramatic theoretical prediction.

No rule that D is always better than P is defended. Importantly P, D, U are not a chronological machine; they may interleave or be distributed over communities.

## 5. Comparative table and adversarial tests

| Case | First distinctive research move | Fragile bridge | What should not be inferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ørsted | Material variation, failure controls and repeated needle effects | From phenomenon to explanatory field geometry | Replication alone selects the unique theory |
| Maxwell | Analogy used as a productive reasoning resource | From heuristic mechanics to electromagnetic explanation | Literally real vortices or idle wheels |
| tRNA | Operational fraction, theoretical prediction, experimental assay, changing role vocabulary | From identifiable fraction to functional reference and community uptake | A name itself makes a molecule or a prediction alone makes the field adopt it |

**Adversarial question A (instrument stability):** Suppose a new device outputs a repeatable differential result but a new calibration protocol makes it disappear. New trace ≠ new world entity (Leonelli; Zong).

**Adversarial question B (theoretical prediction):** Suppose a model predicts a mechanism in advance but usable laboratory controls arrive years later. Predictive success and experimental validation need separate scoring; Fry's history limits a simple predictivist triumphalism.

**Adversarial question C (concept migration):** Suppose scientists reach stable consensus on a term that changes experimental practice, but the initial mechanism is revised later. Uptake can reshape the question space without licensing immutable referential claims.

**Adversarial question D (possible sciences):** Two scientific traditions may share a physical phenomenon/replicable signal yet have unlike 'intelligible explanations' and referential commitments. They could still be translated partially using shared interventions; neither immediate total incommensurability nor automatic formal identity follows.
