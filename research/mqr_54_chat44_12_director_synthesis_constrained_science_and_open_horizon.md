## 3. Proposed conceptual kernel: an inquiry-capability transition with typed claims
Rather than defining science as static propositional `(L,Q,I,G,S,E)`, describe a dated practice as `P_t=(L_t,Q_t,I_t,G_t,S_t,E_t; F_t)`, where F_t is a historically accessible, resource-bounded **question-production and method-learning policy**, not an omniscient oracle.
This schema is a **research comparison tool**; there is no claim that all scientific cognition literally follows such a representation.

A potential 'question-generating event' must meet at least some specified conditions:
- an actual newly executable observation/experiment or mathematical refutation, not only a renamed expression;
- a dated demonstration that a previously unavailable contrast now changes choices, explanations or proofs;
- a world-facing or proof-facing constraint, not just conceptual enthusiasm;
- a carefully bounded translation/relationship to predecessors, including losses and reformed questions;
- an independent rival reconstruction showing that the alleged innovation could be merely a skill transfer, better measurement or old knowledge renamed.

**Crucial corrective:** an experiment can create a new material operational ability without new conceptual primitives; a conceptual invention can occur without inventing a new sensor. Thus one does not imply the other. Likewise a new scientific question need not improve prediction accuracy or transfer across groups immediately.

## 4. Limits and adverse outcomes
The apparent ambitious meta-theory currently uses established pieces from Kuhn, Masterman, Steinle, Nersessian, Secord, Galison, Friedman, Lakatos, statistical decision theory, causal inference, Zollman's network epistemology and Abramsky–Brandenburger contextuality. Recombining them can be legitimate philosophical scholarship under the live doctrine, but it is **not** automatically a new law of science.

Failures to watch:
1. Ex post concept injection: declaring a concept historically unavailable while the evaluator's finite state space already includes it.
2. False independence: multiple AI or scientific opinions with common training/material origins counted as independent discoveries.
3. Simpson/Berkson conditioning: inferring broad world laws from selected admission or interview subpopulations.
4. Causal shortcut: forcing an analogy between scientific networks and individual selection procedures without corresponding interventions and outcome variables.
5. Mathematical-object abuse: equating incompatible metaphysical interpretations with a non-gluable probability presheaf.
6. 'Everything fits' theory: a schema with no discriminating forecast or exclusion is merely a classification system.

## 5. Research lane selection: next real discriminating tests
### DFS A: historically dated **new-question emergence**
Use held Steinle 2016 English edition (2005 German origin), Nersessian 2008, Steinle 1997, Secord 2004, and verified dated Ampère/Faraday original papers or notebooks. Freeze actual instruments, experimental actions and observed question types in 1820–1821 before reading later 1850s field discussions. ### BFS B: communities and institutions under limited information
Compare admission interview/selected cohort as an example of collider and institutional outcome selection, scientific community as evidence-sharing and method-production network, and an emergent laboratory method as a dynamic capability. Do not conflate predictive validity with fairness, scientific truth or applicant worth. Look for a historical record with **new experimental method dissemination** and trace whether independent questions arose at recipients.

### BFS C: formal partial cross-science bridge
Build a typed relation between observed outcomes, interventions, meanings, shared-event anchors and higher-order joint consistency. Enumerate non-bijective and context-dependent mappings, and distinguish 'no current evidence of translation' from 'translation impossible'. Use R10 symmetry as the cheap boundary case and R03 parity as local/global counterexample.

### BFS D: adversarial experiment design
Replicate selected *publicly specified* 2026 Dubova model conditions only if accessible methods/code and computational budget permit; separate reconstruction from independent replication. Build models where targeted choice wins and where broad sampling wins, with held-out losses frozen before result. Test whether mixed policies remain beneficial after changing budget, novelty prior, group communication and costs. In general there will not be a universal optimal exploration ratio.
