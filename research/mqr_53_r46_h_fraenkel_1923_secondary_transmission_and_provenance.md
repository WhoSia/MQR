## Identified primary-in-origin, secondary-transmitted evidence

Kati Kish Bar-On, 'From philosophical traditions to scientific developments: reconsidering the response to Brouwer's intuitionism', *Synthese* 200:521 (2022), source Research OS Drive `1Qp1YwuFje-6Ei4eNVWxNEdECqZSdyF10`, §4.1 printed pp.16–18, cites Fraenkel's **18 April 1923 letter to Brouwer**, reproduced via Hesseling (2003), p.150. The cited letter says intuitionism was still intellectually active and the questions continued to develop in Fraenkel's mind. **We have read Kish Bar-On's quotation, not the physical 1923 letter or the Hesseling volume.** Do not claim independent original-language verification.

The same study reports:
- Fraenkel's *Einleitung in die Mengenlehre* (second edition, 1923) discussed intuitionistic existence and excluded middle more extensively than the 1919 first edition.
- Fraenkel's *Ten Lectures on the Foundation of Set Theory* (1927), especially p.58, examined restrictions of intuitionism; Kish Bar-On reports Brouwer's objections and correspondence in January 1927, following van Dalen (2013). The 1927 first-person work is **not** directly on hand by title in the current Drive search.
- Fraenkel could sympathetically present a dissenting program without adopting its total mathematical commitments. Presentation, permission to investigate, endorsement, and eventual uptake are different historical acts.

## Distinct from Weyl

Weyl's 1921 first-person published foundation argument plus the 1925–27 follow-up are available in Mancosu (1998) translated anthology, ID `1x98toxjYnbbHUNJbJVaBKPofGvqKtCAI`. Brouwer's marginal notes and draft letters object to Weyl's restrictive reading of intuitionism. These are different document types: original journal article later translated, papers in Brouwer's estate later edited, and postwar later historical reconstructions.

A key question for MQR: How can the same scientific community have **textual transmission** of alternatives, **provisional pursuit**, **public argument**, and **collective non-adoption** without imposing one all-or-nothing adoption variable? BUT this distinction is directly foreshadowed by Kish Bar-On and historians. It is an audit schema, not original philosophy.

## Specific evidential caution

A 1923 letter of encouragement is not a definite endorsement of Brouwer's entire logic. A 1927 discussion of restrictions is not proof Fraenkel considered intuitionism irrational. A 1930s decline in the foundational debate is not proof all further constructive mathematics was unsuccessful. Do not use the later outcome as knowledge available in the earlier actor's information set.

## More decisive hypothetical challenge, not yet historical proof

If a prior-art account predicts that actors will reject all practical application of a framework when they reject its foundational commitments, Fraenkel's sympathetic ongoing exposition would be a counterexample.
