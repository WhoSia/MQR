# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · R31 — A historical correction: the question came before the label, and elaboration gated its effect

**2026-11-22 KST.** Source level: published bibliographic abstracts (PubMed) + already-held Feest (2010) original full, no individual participants or full 1981/1985 numeric tables reanalyzed. MQR 5.4 OPEN. This is a prospective historical argument, not the discovery of a novel memory mechanism.

## 1. A strict timeline prevents anachronism
- **Jacoby & Dallas (1981)**, 'On the relationship between autobiographical memory and perceptual learning', *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 110(3):306–340, PMID [6457080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6457080/), DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.110.3.306. Already publicly located [author-hosted original PDF](https://larryjacoby.ca/images/jacoby%20%26%20dallas%20%281981%29.pdf), exact bytes NOT downloaded or first-page independently collated in MQR. Bibliographic abstract discusses recognition memory versus perceptual learning and alternative contributions to recognition.
- **Jacoby (1983)**, 'Perceptual enhancement: Persistent effects of an experience', *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 9(1):21–38, PMID [6220114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6220114/); original author-hosted PDF route in R26, not newly imported.
- **Graf & Schacter (1985)**, 'Implicit and explicit memory for new associations in normal and amnesic subjects', *JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 11(3):501–518, PMID [3160813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3160813/), DOI [10.1037/0278-7393.11.3.501](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.11.3.501). Source abstract explicitly reports *two experiments*, with larger same-context than different-context word-completion priming **only if study required elaborative processing of the word pairs**, in college-student and amnesic groups. The result is conditional on a task design; **do not present a generic invariant contextual-priming benefit**.
- **Roediger (1990)**, 'Implicit Memory: Retention Without Remembering', *American Psychologist* 45(9):1043–1056, PMID [2221571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2221571/). Its original abstract states that many dissociations may be explained by general principles that apply to both explicit/implicit tests, without requiring wholly separate memory systems; this is an alternative theory, not a refutation of all multicomponent memory accounts.
- **Feest (2010)**, 'Concepts as Tools in the Experimental Generation of Knowledge in Cognitive Neuropsychology', DOI 10.4245/sponge.v4i1.11938, held full PDF ID `1LePHrGcp10C4Lr5135YsMKzMc5A64a9A`, paragraphs around printed pp. 184–187 freshly examined. The concept 'implicit memory' was provisionally used as a target of investigation despite significant alterations of what counted as a paradigmatic instance. Feest notes that later authors interpreted early findings through modality-specific representations; that does NOT establish a unique ontology by itself.

## 2. The decisive logical error in a simplistic inquiry model
A tempting but invalid chronology: 'the field invented the label implicit memory in 1985, which suddenly made implicit/explicit tests possible'. Jacoby and Dallas's 1981 experiments predate Graf & Schacter's 1985 naming/interpretation. Thus a **concept label cannot be asserted to be a necessary prerequisite for the already-existing experimental contrasts**. Some concepts formalize, reorganize and communicate the interpretation of tasks that scientists already had. Historians must distinguish:
1. the oldest accessible **operation**;
2. the newest publicly articulated **contrast**;
3. the new or consolidated **label**;
4. the first **new predictions or experiments made possible** by that label/framework;
5. the stronger **existence/causal-mechanism claim**.

Missing any of these dates creates retrospective concept injection. The 1981 paper is a concrete **negative control** for any MQR claim 'no concept, no experiment'.

## 3. A novel proposal with a genuinely discriminating empirical test
Let a study design have factors (A) same vs different word-pair context, (B) elaborative vs nonelaborative encoding, and (C) direct recollection versus indirect word-completion criterion; groups might include amnesic and non-amnesic participants. MQR **does NOT have the underlying numbers**, and no interaction significance is estimated here. The 1985 abstract reports a contextual priming advantage conditional on elaboration.

Competing causal interpretations to check from the original methods/results:
- H_store: separate memory mechanism permits preserved priming in amnesia, with modality/context dependencies.
- H_process: encoding and retrieval operations jointly determine transfer; the task×encoding interaction can emerge without a unique segregated store.
- H_construct: 'implicit memory' as provisional research object is revised with accumulating operational cases; useful classification does not license H_store without tests.
- H_measure: task difficulty, baseline ceiling/floor, incomplete avoidance of explicit retrieval in indirect tasks, and group/response confounds may mimic dissociation.

**Prospective discriminator:** pre-specify *matched method factors* and cross-modal changes, test whether one explanation predicts a qualitative interaction or transfer pattern that rivals forbid. A single observed task dissociation alone does not force separate stores. The actual 1985 study may provide partial controls; claiming the controls were absent until reading the whole study would be unsound.

## 4. Strongest competing explanation and historical limits
Existing Feest/Steinle, Roediger and standard psychometrics already distinguish methods and ontology. MQR can claim a useful **cross-domain dated test protocol** (with Faraday 1821 correspondence), not that its concept-as-tool mechanism is unprecedented. A meaningful new comparative contribution would uncover an independently verified *post-label operation or question* made possible by the framework, then distinguish that from mere reclassification of familiar experiments. The historical case may legitimately REFUTE the narrow hypothesis that all conceptual innovation creates new operational abilities.

**Decision:** 1981 bibliographic anteriority and conditional 1985 effects: PASSED source-grounded claims. Claims of entire 1981/1985 original paper independent full-text analysis, original experimental quantitative replication, and robust neural mechanism: HOLD. No new standalone 'missing paper' group added; paper PDFs merely located online.
