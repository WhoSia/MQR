## Scoped identifiability argument
A simplified forward model for constant-current apparent height is I=C(V) rho_eff(x,V) exp[-2 kappa(z-h(x))], with positive rho_eff, fixed kappa>0, z apparent tip height, h a geometric height, and rho_eff an effective spectral weight (not the exact full Tersoff-Hamann model). Thus z=h+(log rho_eff+log(C/I))/(2 kappa). For any spatial delta(x), h'=h+delta and rho_eff'=rho_eff exp(-2 kappa delta) preserve the predicted z at every bias if rho_eff is otherwise unrestricted. This proves nonidentification only within this unconstrained simplified forward family. Multiple bias values do not alone fix absolute geometry; their contrast can constrain relative spectral weight when geometry, kappa, calibration are stable. Independent structural constraints and physical restrictions on electronic structure can remove or reduce ambiguity. This is elementary algebra, not a new STM theorem or verified calibration. Do not infer that true LDOS is arbitrarily rescalable for a fixed physical sample.

## Macro synthesis
Distinguish a new signal from an independently identified object, and a newly available question from a solved question. Instrumental genesis can turn what was initially a nuisance or discrepancy into a distinct research target: geometric and electronic contributions become separate objects of inquiry. The positive case is not merely more measurements but the coordination of distinct structural and electronic hypotheses with different prediction pathways. The electron-diffraction model and STM comparison have overlapping historical evidence ancestry; local cross-method success is not total independence nor proof of complete shared ontology. This extends the founding UV–Vis question, yet no claim is made that a common forward model is sufficient for all alien sciences or that every new instrument generates a new science. Compare Wilson formal nonclosure, Turing structural possibility, institutional capability selection and Rheinberger material inquiry; keep them distinct. The test of a stronger genesis hypothesis would require dated evidence of what questions were not previously executable and how material/theoretical changes opened them, including unsuccessful alternatives.
