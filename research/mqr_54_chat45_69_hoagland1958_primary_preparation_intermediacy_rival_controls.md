## Explicit original experimental question and independent route

Opening of original article: the investigators had used an established cell-free protein-synthesis system for years, and were probing still-unexplained intermediates between amino-acid activation and incorporation into protein. The authors' actual question was whether a *particular soluble RNA-associated fraction* could chemically carry the amino acid into nascent protein, not a simple yes/no vote on Crick's 1955 adaptor prediction. The term pH-5 RNA denotes the experimental fraction at the time, NOT a chemically or sequence-completely defined modern tRNA species. Identification requires subsequent work.

Three distinct levels of observation:
1. **Co-localization/association:** label from an amino acid can become associated with an RNA-rich fraction. This is *not enough* to prove a protein-synthesis intermediate: contamination, adsorbed material, or an incidental pool are rivals.
2. **Functional transfer and dependence:** the authors compare loss of RNA-associated label with appearance in microsomal protein, distinguish an ATP-linked activation/binding stage from a GTP-linked transfer stage, and test loss-of-function/inhibition controls. Table II provides indicative totals, e.g. complete system before RNA=478, protein=22 counts/min vs after RNA=182, protein=433 counts/min. These are different preparations, not a simple exact stoichiometric mass balance (sources of label, recovery, background, normalization may differ). Removing GTP in their design strongly reduces protein-associated signal; specific attribution still conditional.
3. **Mechanistic/natural-kind interpretation:** in the original DISCUSSION the authors explicitly admit: due to impurity of their enzyme fraction, they cannot assert whether RNA is naturally attached to the activation enzymes or whether additional enzymatic steps occur; they consider other possibilities for chemical linkages and recognize a **storage-site alternative**. The work did not settle every proposed molecular mechanism or confirm every detail of Crick's adaptor hypothesis.

## A stronger negative control than mere theoretical anticipation

Two opposing candidate mechanisms can produce early apparent RNA labeling. Only a downstream functional transfer plus perturbation discriminates the biologically operative intermediate from an inert bound label. The negative-control idea is **original authors' experimental logic**, not MQR's discovery.

The original authors' follow-up transfer experiments demonstrate preparation-dependence and a role for specific catalytic processes. They also show that apparent requirements can change with purification: trace ATP carried through one preparation masked a partial ATP dependence revealed in a cleaner RNA-associated fraction. This is a concrete **measurement preparation / hidden-carryover** counterexample to treating all reported labels as immediately identical physical kinds.

Their paper notes GTP absence can yield loss of intermediate label without appearance in protein, directly separating **label disappearance** from **productive transfer**. Therefore neither “RNA label decreases” nor “protein label eventually increases” alone establishes the directed intermediacy story. These patterns are compatible with different biochemical paths unless tested with controlled interventions.

## Verdict and severity

This source refutes the stronger simplifying claim that finding a label that fits a previously posited entity is the same as establishing a new biological kind. It does **not** establish whether the original chemists were *causally* ignorant of Crick's work without independent correspondence and dates. It also does not confer originality on MQR, since biochemical fractionation, causal experimental controls and history-of-science reconstructions already encode these points.
