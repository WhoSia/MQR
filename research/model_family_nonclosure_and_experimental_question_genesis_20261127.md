## Exact nonclosure witness
A finite equilibrium star graph has one hidden Ising spin t and four visible spins s_i in {-1,+1}, with dimensionless log-weight K t sum_i s_i. Summing t yields W(s)=2 cosh(K sum_i s_i). The log-weight on the visible spins admits a unique Walsh expansion C0 + C2 sum_{i<j}s_i s_j + C4 s1 s2 s3 s4; odd terms vanish by simultaneous reversal and all pair coefficients equal by permutation symmetry. C2=log(cosh(4K))/8; C4=[log(cosh(4K))-4 log(cosh(2K))]/8. For K!=0, write c=cosh(2K)>1; cosh(4K)=2c^2-1<c^4 since (c^2-1)^2>0. Thus C4<0 and no exact pairwise-only log-linear family on these four fixed visible spins can represent this positive marginal, even permitting arbitrary individual fields and all six pair coefficients. Normalization changes only C0. The obstruction is representational closure, not an impossibility of retaining a hidden spin or alternative coordinates.
The exact expression is standard statistical mechanics/Walsh algebra, not a new theorem. It illustrates generation of a missing interaction from elimination without changing micro laws. ## Macro interpretation and rivals
The initial scientific representation may be closed under its own operations but not under a scientifically motivated new transformation. The new term is expressible in the analyst's Boolean language, but is outside the stipulated pairwise model family. Distinguish new member of old family, expansion of family, acquisition of new access/representation, and genuinely new native primitives. A failure of closure is evidence to expand or reconsider a family only when the transformation and intended target are justified; approximation can be adequate and rival hidden-variable formulations remain available. Kadanoff/Wilson show formal reorganization; Rheinberger describes materially shaped inquiry. These mechanisms are not identical. MQR should investigate the relation between new representations and new empirical access, rather than claiming every scientific discovery is a failure of closure. A falsifier of a strong closure-driven genesis theory is a useful new question generated without mathematical nonclosure, or a closure failure whose added term contributes nothing at the target precision. Existing 5.4 joint-action and Turing cases remain relevant, and normative/institutional questions stay live.
