## Competing readings and falsifiers

| Claim | Author-level sources | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 'Maxwell's analogy is a concrete picture or a notational convenience, with no evidential force at all' | Maxwell's 1855/56 use of 'artificial notation' and admission that not proven physical fact | **TOO STRONG**. Nappo argues analogies play both an *illustrative* and defeasible *inductive* function, so the complete method cannot be reduced to a powerless picture |
| 'Maxwell confirmed the literal mechanical vortex medium' | Maxwell 1861–62 author treats molecular vortices as useful system | **TOO STRONG**. Maxwell explicitly calls such model provisional/temporary; even if mechanism yields predictions, success is not proof of material constituents |
| 'Faraday-to-Maxwell was a simple linguistic translation of a fixed known object' | Faraday 1831 named a physical condition, 1852 suggested magnetic lines' physical existence, Maxwell 1855/56 defined component functions | **TOO STRONG**. Empirical target, mathematical specification and inferential use changed; Thomson and Stokes also fed the mathematical development |
| 'The new MQR account of analogical inference exceeds all prior literature' | Steinle (1997/2016), Nersessian (2008), Nappo (2021), Cat (2001) | **UNSUPPORTED**. Nappo's double-function treatment substantially reduces novelty headroom |

Nappo's *claim* that the physical analogy can provide defeasible inductive support is an interpretation defended by a present scholar, **not a theorem or uniquely accepted historical judgment**. The article itself addresses competing historical reconstructions by Achinstein, Siegel, Harman and Nersessian; disagreement remains legitimate. It specifically distinguishes analogy used to **understand/reinterpret existing mathematics** from analogy used to **suggest plausible new relations in the target domain**. Both should be assessed separately, without treating inductive plausibility as empirical confirmation.

## Useful three-stage chain for MQR Q2/Q4/Q5/Q7

1. **Representational articulation**: mapping of familiar fluid quantities into novel electro-magnetic variables may make implicit mathematical dependencies available for reasoning. It can be useful even if no material is actually flowing.
2. **Defeasible hypothesis proposal**: a *source-domain* fluid mechanic relation suggests a *target-domain* electromagnetic relation. It is not deductively forced by the analogy; source and target differences are an important negative control.
3. **World-facing discrimination**: calculate a measurable contrast, state apparatus assumptions and test against actual electromagnetic evidence. Only this determines empirical warrant to the tested scope. Old mathematical identities, new testable implications, and physically real mechanical devices are three separate targets.

**No inevitable chronology:** an experiment can create conceptual distinctions before formal math, and a new formal function may merely reorganize known experiments. Faraday and Maxwell cannot be treated as different civilizations with separate independent scientific origins; they shared knowledge and historical contact. They are a **bounded model of partial translation and conceptual co-development**, not a solution of the founding alien-science question.
