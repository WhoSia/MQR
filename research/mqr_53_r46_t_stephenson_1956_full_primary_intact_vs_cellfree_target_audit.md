## 2. First-person original motivation — a specific protein, not merely a fraction

The authors explicitly chose tobacco (including TMV infection) because a **pure virus protein** could be isolated and provide a more stringent endpoint than generic radiolabel incorporation. They explicitly described the goal as finding a *cell-free* system with recoverable pure labeled product. This strengthens the evidential discrimination absent from 1954 liver fractions.

At the same time they state that the chloroplast fraction is an **operational centrifugation category, not a fully purified organelle**. They did not claim to have purified every fraction; small chloroplast fragments can enter other fractions and microsomal RNA-rich particles may contaminate chloroplasts. The authors explicitly say the RNA in the chloroplast fraction might be intrinsic or due to microsomal contamination and at the time they **cannot decide between the alternatives**. These are historically contemporaneous, written limitations—not hindsight criticisms imposed by an analyst.

## 3. Direct comparison of distinct experimental endpoints

**The most important result:** Radioactive TMV was recovered from **intact infected leaf disks**, while virus recovered after incubating **cell-free material from infected leaves** showed no radioactive label even though other protein fractions were labeled. Separate control: adding existing purified virus to a normal leaf homogenate before incubation gave no label in the recovered virus, supporting a lack of simple exchange or spurious residual labeling of the pre-existing virus under their conditions. These are separate preparations and tests, not binary contradictions.

- **Table II, p.199:** After incubation of *intact* infected disks, labeled fractions included the recovered **tobacco mosaic virus at 81 counts/min/mg protein**. Other fractions were also labeled; do not compare the absolute signal of TMV versus mixed fractions without addressing product abundance and pre-existing virus dilution. This result is evidence of radioactivity recovered in virus from *intact cells*; it is not independent proof of virus assembled in cell-free extracts.
- **pp.207–208:** In a normal-leaf cell-free mixture with added preformed virus, virus remained unlabeled while other fractions labeled; in further cell-free preparations from **infected** leaves, no labeled viral product was detected, even while other protein fractions showed label. Conversely, intact infected-leaf disks consistently yielded labeled virus. The negative conclusion is explicitly scoped to the examined cell-free conditions, not an assertion that viral protein synthesis is biologically impossible.
- **Table III, p.200:** Post-fractionation incubation at a fixed one-hour endpoint shows higher *chloroplast-fraction* label than some other fractions; however the narrative warns that the initial rates can be similar while chloroplast activity persists longer. The temporal shape of incorporation matters as well as one-time cpm/mg.
- **Table IV, p.203:** A bactericidal treatment changed viable bacterial counts substantially, while measured protein label remained approximately stable: untreated **29.7, 35.0** cpm/mg vs treated **29.1, 30.8** cpm/mg (two experimental entries, not replicate population means). The authors therefore argued their measured signal was not attributable to viable bacteria in that comparison. The readout/viability control does **not** logically guarantee complete purity, causally identify each chloroplast component, or immunize every later species/protocol from bacterial contamination.
- **Table V, p.204:** Chloroplast fraction under oxygen **39.0** vs nitrogen **13.8** cpm/mg, while other fractions also showed oxygen-related differences. The contrast is a descriptive source report in a specified condition, not an independently estimated pure chloroplast causal parameter.
- **Table VI, p.204:** Chloroplast fractions in light oxygen **45.7**, light nitrogen **18.6**, dark oxygen **27.6**, dark nitrogen **15.5** cpm/mg. Oxygen and light have separable and combined descriptive contrasts in this particular matrix. Without independent replicate errors, don't present significance tests, and do not infer a complete photosynthetic energy mechanism from these counts.
- **pp.197 and following:** the authors saw labile surface-associated/nonpeptide contamination during purification (reported decreases around 20–30% in preliminary treatments). This directly prevents treating all precipitated counts as an unproblematic singular target.

## 4. One case, four possible philosophical verdicts — no invented identical-question disagreement

Define:
- O = radioactive label in a processed fraction;
- B = convincing evidence of peptide-bond incorporation;
- P = purified specified labeled protein recovered from that preparation;
- V = labeled TMV protein recovered after *cell-free* incubation of infected tobacco leaf material.

Neither O nor B logically entails V. The authors observed O in many preparations, **and observed V only in the intact infected-disk procedure**, not in their tested cell-free procedures. This is a genuine within-paper target-specific discriminant, unlike the wholly synthetic binary models in R46-K/Q.

**Green 2001:** The target of the strict semantic-identity criticism is *concept equals a single operation*. The 1956 authors did not assert O is definitionally identical to V; they described incomplete/potentially contaminated fractions and explicitly distinguished endpoints. This historical case does not directly refute Green.

**Feest 2005/2010/2012/2025 intro:** The provisional 'chloroplast fraction', labeling and alternative TMV endpoint were legitimate investigative *tools* that progressively formed and limited knowledge even when the desired target was not achieved. A qualified *interpretive application*, not a claim that Feest discussed this historical paper. Novelty strongly pre-empted by Feest.

**Borsboom et al. 2004:** Specific target property = TMV synthesis in cell-free infected leaf system; claim of its causal influence on virus-specific labeled product requires evidence of such an attribute. The reported **negative viral endpoint** fails to establish that proposed cell-free property in the examined setup. It does not invalidate the more modest observation O of labeled fractions or the value of searching for mechanisms. Qualified application, not the authors' written verdict on Stephenson.

**Rheinberger 1997:** Historiographically already describes the plant-system failed target and research redirection to chloroplast fractions, pp.124–127. Current direct 1956 paper **confirms** the critical intact-disk vs cell-free viral contrast and contemporaneous instrumental uncertainty, but correcting our earlier reliance on a retrospective description is **source improvement**, not a philosophical theory novelty.

**1956 experiment versus 1964 spinach:** App & Jagendorf (1964), *Plant Physiology* 39(5):772–776 DOI `10.1104/pp.39.5.772`, later species/protocol and microbiological contamination; must not be imposed as causal disproof of the 1956 tobacco bactericidal controls. Same target/operator/procedure condition fails.

**Outcome:** No genuine **same-question** opposed verdict has been demonstrated among Green, Feest and Borsboom. Their frameworks address semantic coherence, investigative permissibility and actual causal-target validity. **MQR R46-T NOVELTY HOLD**. An artificial contradiction would depend on covertly switching O, P and V.

## 5. The more demanding research target

Not simply “Can we find experimental target mismatch?”—the 1956 authors and later Rheinberger already found it.

Try to specify an *evidence-to-target transport warrant*: which controls justify promoting an operational fraction signal O to a causal attribution in target P/V, how does target continuity survive a changed preparation, and when must a previously informative label be retired? Compare the strongest preexisting accounts (Garner 1956 differential tests, Campbell–Fiske 1959, Feest, Borsboom, Rheinberger) before introducing any new MQR term or principle. An original answer needs a source-grounded case with an explanatory or prospective prediction those existing accounts lack.
