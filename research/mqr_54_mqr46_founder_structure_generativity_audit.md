## What the actual document says

- Explanatory models project/reconstruct chosen variables and a space of possible trajectories, attractors, bifurcations, structural constraints—not merely a single observed output. World-model correspondence is distinct from identity.
- Successful prediction does not on its own reveal internal causation or ontology. The essay asks how structural explanation and world-constraints can be distinguished from prediction and computational control.
- Complex, biological, historically dependent and adaptive systems provoke questions about whether fixed model variables and current action vocabularies exhaust future inquiries.
- The most useful next hypothesis is that scientific theories can **change the space of questions and feasible interventions**, not just optimize predictions within an already fixed menu.

## Treat three sweeping inferences critically

1. Gödel's incompleteness does **not** logically refute all mathematical Platonism, every formal science or realistic physical explanation. The precise incompleteness hypotheses concern particular formal systems.
2. Chaos, nonlinearity, emergent behavior and non-Markovian dynamics do **not** entail the impossibility of mathematical explanation. Nonlinear dynamical systems, stochastic processes, explicit history variables, network models and interventionist tools are mathematical. Particular models can fail when omitting pertinent variables.
3. High-performing machine learning does **not** always lack explanatory structure; nor does prediction establish a unique causal model. Test those claims by specified criteria and held-out interventions rather than broad historical generalization.

**Preserve the promising core:** data-fit or structural isomorphism on selected observations does not imply ontological identity. A model may carve a new action/possibility space; prospective world-resistance, not textual sameness, must evaluate its scientific import.

## Four levels of change that MQR must NOT conflate

- **L0: pure relabeling.** Different native names but same feasible questions and behavioral distinctions. Graph matching and language translation live here.
- **L1: new discrimination with existing primitives.** A previously unused physically feasible experiment splits at least one class of states previously equal under every performed observable. The resulting observable partition is a strict refinement. This is known finite partition algebra, already present in earlier MQR 42, not a new theorem.
- **L2: new operational composition.** Build or compose a manipulation that was **not available under the old action grammar**, then physically execute it and discriminate rival hypotheses. The repertoire of possible experiments expands; this is stronger than relabeling or reading a second already-installed sensor.
- **L3: conceptual architecture revision.** Existing types of preparation, entity or observation cannot formulate the new successful counterfactual predictions; the theory and operation grammar jointly change. A claim of L3 requires a precisely articulated old-language inexpressibility result, not just novelty of terminology.

If E_old is the partition induced by the old executable experiments, L1 is captured by an observed new experiment a_new with strict refinement E_new≺E_old. **L2/L3 do not follow from this partition condition alone**. A new operation must be constructed (not just selected) and must generate independently checkable consequences.

## New severe counterexample

Two laboratories can study the *same four stable physical preparations* while one has C4 allowed operations and the other only P4 operations. Their local graphs are nonisomorphic; the MQR 46 finite matcher returns 0 under the assumption of a common isomorphism. This is NOT a world-disagreement result. It indicates their action grammars differ. The scientific response is to seek a **new shared manipulation**, not to conclude they inhabit different worlds. This is a concrete route back to the founder's structure-generation ambition.

**Research directive:** move beyond finding a bijection. Model how one agent's experiment leads the other to learn/build a new primitive *that could not previously be executed*.
