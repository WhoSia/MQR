## Estimand and sharp constraints
Closed finite cohort, fixed binary baseline group X, exact knowledge of group membership and observed binary measured-regime outcomes; unobserved untreated outcomes unrestricted. For a group with n observed, m unobserved, p measured positives, c untreated positives, at most k measured individuals differ between regimes. A possible number t of untreated positives among measured units satisfies 0<=t<=n, p-k<=t<=p+k, t<=c<=t+m. Hence max(0,p-k)<=c<=min(n,p+k)+m. Conversely choose t=max(0,p-k,c-m), and assign observed positives/negatives with minimum mismatches |t-p|; add c-t missing positives. This proves every integer in the interval attainable. Minimum cost d(c)=max(0,p-c,c-p-m), subject to 0<=c<=n+m. For two groups, total budget k gives d0(c0)+d1(c1)<=k, not k separately in each group. Signed contrast D=N0*c1-N1*c0; covariance D/(N0+N1)^2 and conditional mean difference D/(N0*N1) for nonempty groups. To minimize D, set missing positives to 0 in group 1 and all missing positives in group 0; allocate possible flips in descending coefficient magnitude (N0 for reducing group 1 positives; N1 for increasing group 0 positives), capped by eligible observed outcomes and total budget. Maximize symmetrically. This is an elementary two-variable integer allocation; only extrema guaranteed for D, not every integer in between. Unknown outcome under nonmeasurement is a counterfactual, not a noisy read of the measured outcome. Budget k is an externally justified assumption, not a quantity estimated here.
Average invariance counterexample: X=(0,1), Y(0)=(1,0), Y(1)=(0,1); equal mean 1/2, covariance -1/4 and +1/4. Exact equal binary margins may hide arbitrary opposing changes; if one-way monotonicity holds, equality of counts instead rules out changes. If two binary regime totals A,B are known for the exact same N units but linkage is unknown, attainable Hamming distances are |A-B|,|A-B|+2,...,min(A+B,2N-A-B), from the feasible joint-positive table; not a population identification guarantee from separate samples.

## Aggregate untreated count as a new independent constraint
If B=c0+c1 is independently known exactly for the same cohort, z=c1 lies in [max(0,B-N0),min(N1,B)]. Let intervals [l,u]=[p1,p1+m1] and [a,b]=[B-p0-m0,B-p0]. Minimum required total mismatches is distance(z,[l,u])+distance(z,[a,b]). This is <=k iff all nine pairwise sums of {0,l-z,z-u} and {0,a-z,z-b} are <=k. Thus require k>=max(0,l-b,a-u) and
L=max(0,B-N0,l-k,a-k,ceil((l+a-k)/2)),
U=min(N1,B,u+k,b+k,floor((u+b+k)/2)).
If L>U or gap constraint fails, no compatible model. Otherwise all z in integer [L,U] attainable, D=(N0+N1)z-N1B. Distinct from calibration by mean equality across different samples, or an aggregate at a different measurement regime. No claims about real biology.

## Coverage and evidence replacement

At fixed absolute k, newer feasible count set is contained in the older set (also true jointly with shared k because each group's minimum cost cannot decrease). If a changed protocol replaces prior budget, coverage alone does not guarantee this.
