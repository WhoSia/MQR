## A. Weyl in 1921: genuine alternatives, not generic praise

In the opening, Weyl regards set-theoretical antinomies as symptoms of a foundational crisis, contrasts Brouwer's line with his *own independently developed* 1918 `Das Kontinuum`. In the final pages (1998 translation circa pp.116–117) he treats intuitionistic construction of the continuum and the logic of universal/existential assertions as connected and welcomes Brouwer's intuitive approach. Importantly, this does **not** establish that Weyl's complete mathematical ontology or set conception was identical to Brouwer's.

## B. Brouwer's actual objections: primary in origin, editorially recovered

The editorial notes identify three estate documents commenting on Weyl 1921. In marginal and draft-letter material, Brouwer argues against restrictions that would exclude discontinuous functions and objects he understands through species or set-law notions. He describes Weyl's restriction to lowest-order mathematical entities as unjustified and questions whether Weyl sufficiently grasps intuitionism. The genuine disagreement concerns **admissible mathematical objects, laws and levels of generality**, not simply whether inquiry is rational or whether it should receive institutional support.

This constitutes a source-supported **object-level mathematical-foundations dispute**. It is NOT yet a contradiction among Dewey, Feest, Kitcher, Chang or Kish Bar-On, who assess different historiographical/philosophical questions.

## C. Weyl in 1925–27: a real plural appraisal

At the end of 'The Current Epistemological Situation in Mathematics' (1998 translation around pp.140–142), Weyl recognizes Brouwer's emphasis on intuitive meaning but explicitly argues that Hilbert's symbolic direction must also be pursued. His argument invokes a demand for theoretical representation not exhausted by phenomenological intuition, and discusses mathematical theory in connection with physical theorizing.

This weakens the simplistic narrative '1921 pro-Brouwer → later anti-Brouwer'. Instead the actor articulates multiple standards: intuitive transparency, mathematical construction and expressive/theoretical need. Whether he changed a particular formal thesis must be decided **claim by claim**, not from an untyped conversion label.

## D. The nontrivial historical research question

What counted as a justified foundational mathematical transformation for Weyl and Brouwer, when one evaluates (i) what objects may legitimately be admitted, (ii) what methods can establish an existence theorem, and (iii) what explanatory/representational work a mathematical system ought to perform?

The historical actors *do* dispute some of these. Distinguish actual mathematical controversy from a model of social uptake and another model of ex-ante resource allocation.

## E. Minimum evidential test

A source-grounded historical actor disagreement requires an individually located proposition P, two truly opposing stances on the same proposition, contextual date, and editorial transmission. Marginal notes are evidence of Brouwer's views but not evidence that every reader of the public 1921 paper encountered them. The two Weyl essays differ in publication period and rhetorical objective.
