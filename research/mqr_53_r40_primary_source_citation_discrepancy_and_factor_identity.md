## 1. Source identification and bibliographic inconsistency

- Philip Siekevitz (1952), **"Uptake of radioactive alanine in vitro into the proteins of rat liver fractions"**, *JBC* 195:549–565. PMID 14946165; DOI 10.1016/S0021-9258(18)55763-X. Original 1952 article text available in a publicly indexed copy at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8704809_Uptake_of_Radioactive_Alanine_In_Vitro_into_the_Proteins_of_Rat_Liver_Fractions ; bibliographic check https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14946165/ .
- Keller, Zamecnik & Loftfield (September 1954), *The role of microsomes in the incorporation of amino acids into proteins*, *J Histochem Cytochem* 2:378–386, DOI 10.1177/2.5.378. Publisher original with extracted page/paragraph anchors: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2.5.378 .
- **Real error of bibliographic transmission:** the original 1952 title says **alanine**, whereas the September 1954 article's reference 19, printed p. 386, says **valine** for Siekevitz 1952 with identical volume and page range. The September 1954 article does employ valine as a labeled amino acid in its own *in-vivo* table, but that must not be retroactively attributed to the 1952 paper. The correct title has independent PubMed and open-index confirmation. This is a source-level discrepancy, **not** an established error in the underlying experimental inference or a previously unreported finding. Do not overstate it as an original historical discovery.

## 3. The most useful physical-characterization comparison

**Siekevitz 1952** original's conclusion (printed p.563): the preparations support a transferable factor associated with the older metabolic/fractionation system. The author says the factor is **stable under substantial heat treatment**, and says the factor probably is not ATP, though it could be derived from it. Crucially, the paper *does not* present a chemically isolated, molecularly identified entity; even the attribution of residual labeling in controls is treated cautiously.

**Keller et al. 1954** original (printed p.383–384) says the later reduced cell-free assay requires a **heat-labile non-dialyzable** component from the soluble fraction, even with energy-regeneration support. Authors explicitly state that the new factor's chemical identity, possible enzymatic role and precise function are under investigation.

These are different experimental criteria and thus **defeat the unargued inference that the two similarly named 'soluble factors' are the same identified object**. However they do **not by themselves prove molecular non-identity**: preparations, fractions, treatments and measurement domains differ. Rheinberger 1997 chapter 6 **already makes the historical distinction between the old and new soluble factors**; MQR has not discovered a new historiographic distinction here. The historical comparison yields a sharp source-anchored negative inference.

## 4. What the original 1952 text actually argues

The openly indexed original 1952 article includes Tables I–IV and a five-part Summary. In the reproduced OCR portions, author draws a correlation between phosphorylation and labeled-alanine uptake but explicitly notes discrepant inhibitor results and low residual counts in metabolic controls. The historical actor's position is therefore *already more cautious than a simplistic theory-assertion narrative*. Because full page-image inspection and aligned Table I–IV cells could not be performed, no individual 1952 numeric table cell is promoted here.

Zamecnik & Keller (July 1954), *Relation between phosphate energy donors and incorporation of labeled amino acids into proteins*, *JBC* 209:337–354, PMID 13192089, DOI 10.1016/S0021-9258(18)65561-9: bibliography and cross-references verified, **full original tables and paper remain unavailable for direct, reliable collation**. Do not import data from the September article into its tables.

## 5. A newer and extremely relevant novelty attack

Michael Fry (2022), "Crick's Adaptor Hypothesis and the Discovery of Transfer RNA: Experiment Surpassing Theoretical Prediction," *Philosophy, Theory, and Practice in Biology* 14:11, DOI 10.3998/ptpbio.2628. Full open article https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ptpbio/article/2628/galley/752/download/ . The author explicitly reconstructs the experimental path from early cell-free studies to amino acid-activating enzymes and transfer RNA, comparing it to Crick's adaptor hypothesis and contemporary uptake of predictions. Thus MQR's *very same historical research program* has undergone **specialized history-of-philosophy treatment beyond Rheinberger**, and an originality claim based on "experiment produced concepts despite poor predictive theory" is even less defensible. Published article text read in introduction and section 3.1; not a complete line-by-line book study. The article itself argues about the historical impact of novel prediction, and should be examined adversarially rather than accepted uncritically.

## 6. Novelty verdict and next work

- **Confirmed with qualification:** separate thermal-characterization claims for old and new soluble activities, which resist unargued object identity. This is already Rheinberger's central historical claim.
