## 2. Historically separate physics evidence: October 1822 Faraday original letter
**Faraday to De La Rive, 9 October 1822, Ɛpsilon Faraday0182**, https://epsilon.ac.uk/view/faraday/letters/Faraday0182, contemporary letter scholarly transcription from BPUG MS 2311 ff.65–66 directly read in full, not physical manuscript imaging. He explicitly acknowledges Ampère's theoretical ambition and substantial empirical fit, yet says additional facts are needed before untested parts deserve full confidence. He regards De La Rive's new experiments as correcting generally accepted opinions and asks for their full account. His remark about waiting for experimental testing when mathematicians disagree shows **stated contemporaneous standards of inference**, not proof that formal mathematics was not useful to electrodynamics.

**Nuance:** the letter says Faraday welcomed a report of De La Rive's experiments but also states he awaits the *full account*. Thus one must NOT allege independent replication or possession of all experiment details by Faraday on 9 October. In his footnotes the editors cite De La Rive letter 181 and preceding 179; those prior exact transcript texts remain bibliographic leads rather than directly checked letters. His discussion also shows a later convergence with Ampère on his 'apparent loss of weight' effect: **not all experiments caused interpretive divergence**. ## 3. Cross-domain argument in a typed form
Let:
- **O** = an experimental result reproducible under declared preparation and measurement;
- **Q** = operationally formulable and executable questions about that result;
- **I** = an adequately discriminating inferential comparison between rivals;
- **N** = an ontological/natural-kind claim (or a specific causal mechanism).

Neither O nor (O and Q) entails I or N. A study can enable new empirical contrasts while multiple underlying mechanisms still fit the current observations. Likewise two groups can share O and Q but disagree on I and N. This is a logical decomposition, **not** a four-stage temporal law that all sciences necessarily traverse.

**Physics:** electromagnetic rotation reproduced within early apparatus families; disputed microcurrent implications. **Psychology:** priming-vs-recognition dissociations and their later variants support new experiments; the claim of a unique memory module needs independent theoretical and cross-method support.

## 4. Three rival accounts to retain
- **R_C (construct stabilization):** a provisional research concept becomes better specified through expanding experimental comparisons; real scientific progress can occur without a fixed ontology.
- **R_O (object discovery):** experiments in fact identify a stable separate underlying mechanism; MQR's ontological reserve is overly conservative in that specific case.
- **R_T (task confounding):** operational dissociation partly reflects test-specific procedures/response rules; apparent new object need not persist under cross-task matched tests.

Different experiments and sources can vindicate different rivals at different scopes. To discriminate use independently designed tasks/instruments, manipulations that target proposed shared versus separate mechanisms, held-out conditions, and dated original actor statements. **Do not assign any participant statistics** not read directly.

## 5. Specific NEW prediction and a failure criterion
Predict: a new method can widen **the class of answerable questions** before confidence increases in a unique ontology. This is supported as a descriptive reconstruction in the two source families but does not prove a unique causal pattern. Countercase sought: a historically introduced instrument that immediately confirms a single already-specified ontology independently under controls; also seek an 'innovative' new test whose results disappear after adjusting for task confounds. If all such cases fit already-known active learning or construct validation without an additional discriminating claim, MQR records a useful synthesis and **no new master mechanism**.
