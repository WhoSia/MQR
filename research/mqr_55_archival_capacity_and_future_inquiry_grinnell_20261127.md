## Scientific inference and its archival preconditions
For a simplified repeated binary detection model, assume occupancy Z~Bernoulli(psi), constant conditional detection probability p, no false positives, conditional independence of two visits and closure during visits. With only one observation, Pr(Y=1)=psi*p, so occupancy and detectability are nonidentified. With two visits, Pr(11)=psi*p^2, Pr(10)=psi*p*(1-p); when psi*p>0, p=Pr(11)/(Pr(11)+Pr(10)), psi=Pr(11+10)/p. This is textbook occupancy identifiability in the restricted model, not an estimator for the actual historical survey. Site heterogeneity, false positives, changing occupancy, dependent detections and sparse records require separate treatment. The authors use more complex detection models and model averaging, not this homogeneous example. The scientific distinction is that retention of individual observation histories rather than only a collapsed ever-detected indicator can make a future inferential question identifiable under stated assumptions. This is a concrete link from earlier MQR observation quotient and policy-level reasoning to future inquiry capability. It does not show preservation is universally sufficient, optimal, or that the question was literally inexpressible in 1910.

## Competing explanations and limits

The later paper supplies a positive use witness, not the causal effect of museum design versus a hypothetical alternative archive; compare actual data-poor sites and non-recoverable records before claiming necessity. FinBIF's earlier monotone missingness analysis illustrates the converse: presence labels without effort and source-form provenance do not identify biological occupancy.
