# R37 research claim audit — observational continuity is weaker than inferential continuity

2026-11-04. Philosophical reconstruction only; *not* an original theorem.

## Claim types

For historical record r produced by procedure p and contemporary interpretive model h, distinguish:

- **Trace-level:** an instrument registered a repeatable response under a specified configuration.
- **Operational:** a specified procedure reliably partitions or transforms the subject being studied.
- **Interpretive:** a scientific entity, causal mechanism, or stable concept is responsible for the record.

Later investigations can leave a trace-level report intact while withdrawing an interpretive claim. This distinction is familiar in philosophy of experimentation and theory-ladenness; it is not evidence that the lower-level report is infallible.

## Two opposite failure modes

1. **Premature continuity:** archival labels that appear unchanged are used to infer that the referent, method, and evidential role were unchanged. Rheinberger's historical account of evolving experimental fractions supplies a warning.
2. **Premature discontinuity:** a later model rejects an earlier explanation, so every earlier observation is treated as invalid. Steinle's distinction between empirically generated regularity and speculative explanatory theory supplies a warning.

Both judgments require contemporaneous textual and material evidence. The three claim levels are *analytical questions*, not rigidly separated entities or a newly discovered universal chronology. Each can depend on assumptions in the others.

## Research test

Choose a dated original publication and its successor, identify explicit statements and the apparatus or notation that licenses each, and assess which original statement the successor actually contradicts. Independently compare Feest, Schickore, Nersessian, Leonelli and Rheinberger. Without a verified discrepancy or unmet explanation, retire this candidate as ordinary critical historiography.
