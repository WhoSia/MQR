## 2. Three distinctions now nonnegotiable

**(A) Shared data is not shared evidence.** Without same sample, state preparation, processing provenance, and a valid linking model, data that appear to agree are not an interfield discovery. Marginal distributions alone do not identify joint correspondences.

**(B) New evidence use is not necessarily new physical operation.** Weiskopf already established a rich philosophical account of evidence unification through cognitive-neuroscience model embedding; Galison and Star/Griesemer already described local scientific coordination. Engineering a new port is one route, not necessary for every case.

**(C) New integrated model is not guaranteed to preserve original targets.** R10's compressed one-bit example, R11's distribution-mismatch and R12's frozen transport demonstrate three different failures. A joint program must test both old tasks and proposed new task. The target should be explicit and time-indexed.

## 3. A sharper research question, careful against prior art

A scientific practice P consists not merely of equations and datasets but an evolving set of **questions that actors can reliably and legitimately answer**, with its instruments, preparation conventions, routines and criticism. For two practices P and Q, interfield progress might be analyzed as a change in:
1. the set of materially executable and analytically evaluable questions under relevant costs;
2. the preservation or loss of each community's *existing, target-specific* error guarantees and criticism procedures;
3. the provenance and transport of the new evidence link to new specimens, times and experimental regimes;
4. the ability to contest *which problems matter* without letting one dominant community settle its own success criterion.

None of these variables should be added into a universal scalar. This formulation is not first-discovered MQR theory: Mitchell (2023), Friedman (2001), Galison (1999/2010), Star & Griesemer (1989), Weiskopf (2025), Fagan (2016), Nersessian (2008), Chang and Longino already tackle major components. Its current value is an **auditable interdisciplinary question and adverse-test design**.
