# MQR-5.3 R46-B — First primary-paper chronology and claim boundary

## Primary documents located and read from Research OS Drive

1. Siekevitz (1952), *Uptake of Radioactive Alanine in Vitro into the Proteins of Rat Liver Fractions*, JBC 195:549–565, Drive ID `1PSYEIxAwLdAiKVUCvIvUQwj0MHbkPUjU`. The manuscript reports **received July 11, 1951**, and earlier preliminary results presented May 3, 1951. It explicitly acknowledges alternative explanations for weak radioactive amino-acid uptake, including adsorption and non-peptide linkage, and describes controls. It investigates separate liver fractions and oxidative-phosphorylation relationships; the summary suggests inter-fraction synergy but should not be converted into a complete protein synthesis mechanism.

2. Zamecnik & Keller (1954), *Relation Between Phosphate Energy Donors and Incorporation of Labeled Amino Acids into Proteins*, JBC 209:337–354, Drive `1FJx6ghWM3QF4wpmQQlAdgDZcZxJlxp6n`. **Received January 27, 1954.** The introduction reserves judgment on whether measured *incorporation* is alpha-peptide bonding of protein synthesis. The experimental procedure details preparation sensitivity: gentle homogenization, cold immediate centrifugation, modified energy-support medium. Important: enhanced uptake is not, by itself, complete confirmation of the molecular mechanism.

3. Keller, Zamecnik & Loftfield (1954), *The Role of Microsomes in the Incorporation of Amino Acids into Proteins*, Journal of Histochemistry and Cytochemistry 2:378ff, Drive `1pWpuTvbXUaon6Gz3rFdebaPLjfHfcefC`. **Received June 4, 1954.** The authors explicitly contrast whole animal, liver slices, and cell-free fractions and acknowledge an exchange-labeling alternative. They examine microsomal participation and experimental pathway differences. Original figure interpretation requires visual/page-level collation.

4. Hoagland (1955), *An Enzymic Mechanism for Amino Acid Activation in Animal Tissues*, BBA 16:288–289, Drive `1sIkKZW7jKB7-iSQtnddV5GxVPDF9VaoX`. **Received December 4, 1954.** The preliminary paper observes amino-acid-dependent PP–ATP exchange in soluble fraction and hydroxamate formation with hydroxylamine, then proposes an explicitly tentative activation mechanism. Its wording does NOT establish proof of each step of protein synthesis; image/chemical formula transcription is distorted and must be checked visually before quantitative reuse.

5. Hoagland, Keller & Zamecnik (1956), *Enzymatic Carboxyl Activation of Amino Acids*, JBC, Drive `1G6m-pB4PG3RU-2oPuOQRXdg6sMBrKOrT`. **Received June 3, 1955** though published in 1956. Opens by describing the earlier soluble-fraction requirement and amino acid-dependent exchange evidence; expands partial purification and mechanism-testing. It must be kept separate from what was available to earlier experimenters in 1951/1954.

## Critical historical-philosophical observation

The scientists themselves divided observed incorporation, counter-hypotheses (adsorption/exchange/non-peptide bonding), dependence on preparation and soluble fraction, and a proposed chemical activation mechanism. These cannot be represented as a single monotone 'knowledge gain' without destroying the contemporary inferential distinctions.

This is especially promising as a *source-grounded basis* for assessing pursuit (rational to experiment while uncertain), acceptance (which specific molecular conclusion was warranted), and progress (what new instrument/assay/relation became investigable). It does NOT yet demonstrate that Dewey, Chang, Kitcher or Feest disagree.

## Adversarial cautions / next DFS

- Do not attribute historical numbers or specific errors to experimenters without exact original table/figure page collation.
- Distinguish **received** dates from published dates and first performed experiments.
- The 1956 paper is not admissible as 1954 contemporaneous evidence.
- 'Synergy' requires denominator/normalization and control checks; sum of counts per mg protein is not automatically a valid mixing interaction.
- Historical language is source-dependent; modern molecular descriptions must not be read back into the primary paper.
- To test the rationality of pursuing a given question, reconstruct realistic alternatives, resource costs, available controls, uncertainty, *and* contemporary opposing arguments. Retrospective success cannot establish ex ante rationality.
