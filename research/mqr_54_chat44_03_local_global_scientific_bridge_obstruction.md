## 1. A mathematical test of the local-to-global inference
Let A,B,C be binary observables. Available two-variable contexts are AB, BC and AC. On **every** context impose the anti-correlated probability table:
  p(0,1)=p(1,0)=1/2; p(0,0)=p(1,1)=0.

Each one-variable marginal is uniformly (1/2,1/2). Thus the contexts agree on all their shared variables; local compatibility passes.

If a single global joint distribution p(A,B,C) reproduced all three pairwise tables, it could assign positive weight only to triples satisfying simultaneously A!=B, B!=C and A!=C. The first two imply A=C, contradicting the third. There are no such triples. Hence no global joint probability exists.

This **does not** say the event-assignment presheaf lacks all gluing: its unrestricted assignments can glue. Rather, the given compatible *family of contextual probability distributions* has no global probability distribution agreeing with its marginals. Confusing these objects is a serious categorical error.

## 2. Controls distinguish obstruction from notation or overlap failure
- Negative control I, open chain AB, BC with the same anti-pair tables: compatible; exactly two deterministic global assignments (0,1,0) and (1,0,1). Their equal mixture reproduces both pairwise tables.
- Negative control II, even 4-cycle AB, BC, CD, AD: compatible; exactly two alternating global assignments; equal mixture reproduces all edge distributions.
- Negative control III, mismatched A marginal across AB and AC: local compatibility fails already. It is *not* a local-to-global obstruction under compatible overlaps.
- Positive obstruction, odd triangle AB, BC, AC: local consistency yet no global distribution.
- All tables are rational and finite. Source: [exact executable](../language/real/experiments/mqr_local_global_gluing_v54.py). Code includes explicit support enumeration, overlap-marginal checks, and controls.

## 3. What this establishes for possible-sciences inquiry
Let worlds/practices P_i share overlap domains O_ij and finite context-specific distributions p_i. Overlap agreement is a necessary condition for a global joint theory over the designated observables, but not sufficient on cyclic covers. Therefore:
  (all pairwise local interface checks PASS) does NOT imply
  (there exists a single joint distribution representing all interfaces).
The counterexample is mathematical and already part of contextuality literature. It does not show that real human or alien sciences instantiate these particular tables, that quantum mechanics is necessary, or that global conceptual translation is generally impossible.

Even existence of a global joint probability is weaker than semantic identity, physical explanatory equivalence, or common ontology. And absence of this particular joint representation does NOT eliminate other higher-level theories with changed variable types, context-indexing or different assumptions.

## 4. A scientifically ambitious *but bounded* bridge
Four separate questions must be investigated, not collapsed:
1. **Empirical bridge:** do two practices agree on shared outcome statistics at a stated resolution and provenance?
2. **Inferential bridge:** do they preserve the same experimentally accessible intervention/proof consequences?
3. **Semantic bridge:** are translation maps partial, context-dependent, and composable on declared domains?
4. **Global synthesis:** can compatible local bridges jointly embed in a common higher-level representation without altering the observation or intervention obligations?

MQR may use sheaf/event-structure mathematics as a diagnostic tool for the fourth, provided it formally establishes the conditions under which scientific interface data match the required mathematical objects. One cannot treat Kuhnian incommensurability as physical contextuality just because both admit local/globally inconsistent descriptions.

## 5. A stronger next question
Instead of searching only for successful bilingual translation rules, study *obstructions to scientific unification* under three independent threat models:
- finite-data incompleteness/epistemic ambiguity;
- conflicting causal interventions under observation-equivalent models (MQR44 record02);
- genuine joint-distribution obstruction in declared context covers (this record).

An ambitious research claim would demonstrate, with documented historical cases and independent model choices, that changing question repertoire or intervention access yields new admissible bridges or removes old ones. A legitimate result could equally show that the proposed MQR categories collapse into already known contextuality, causal identification or science-history theory; that is an explicit risk, not a failure to be hidden.
