# R45-A — Dewey: problem institution is already epistemically transformative

**Primary source**: [Dewey (1938), *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*, chapter VI, printed pp. 105–115](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-Tp4k31ObntyoS5TcXBk8BoI-pI4_yCv/view), verified original scan and extracted text.

Dewey separates **indeterminate situation** (not yet an intellectual proposition), recognizing a **problematic situation**, instituting a **problem** (already a partial transformation by inquiry), and determining a possible **problem-solution**. His explicit argument is that how a problem is put determines which suggestions, data and hypotheses are relevant. An ill-formed problem leads to inquiry that is irrelevant even if locally busy. He distinguishes logical inquiry from a pre-given school exercise.

**MQR novelty kill:** "scientific inquiry first creates the question and its relevance criteria" is not new; Dewey's 1938 argument explicitly contains it. We must not falsely attribute to him a completely fixed research-stage sequence; processes recur and problem statements can be revised.

**Open criticism:** Dewey's methodological story does not by itself settle institutional allocation of expensive exploration across rival researchers. But Chang, Longino and Kitcher are already competitors for that missing norm. Need historical decision to differentiate.
