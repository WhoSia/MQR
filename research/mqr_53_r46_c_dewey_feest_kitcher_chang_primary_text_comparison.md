## Primary philosophical sources and carefully limited readings

**Dewey (1938), Logic: The Theory of Inquiry**, chapter VI, printed pp.107–109, Research OS Drive `1-Tp4k31ObntyoS5TcXBk8BoI-pI4_yCv`. Section 'Institution of a Problem' identifies the transition from a pre-cognitive indeterminate situation to a problem through inquiry. Problem formulation alters which data and conceptual suggestions are relevant. This is a theory of problem constitution, not a Boolean approval of every exploratory project.

**Feest (2012), 'Exploratory Experiments, Concept Formation, and Theory Construction in Psychology'**, in Feest & Steinle (eds.) *Scientific Concepts and Investigative Practice*, printed pp.167–189, Drive `1WJyJHhYFi3R27QNfNOu5Jr4pmsdq5Ua9`. Intro and conceptual argument: experimental operations are not restricted to testing finished theories; operational definitions can serve as research tools; concept formation and theory construction may be entwined. Direct case domain is psychology. Transferring its analysis to 1954 biochemistry is an analyst's extension, **not** Feest's own verdict on this historical episode.

**Kitcher (1993), The Advancement of Science**, section 'Significant Questions and Accepted Statements' and immediately preceding account of scientific practice, Drive `1-dqXnIObLoPTVXFyeRAKCG0Astd9oqM-`. A scientific practice includes the significant questions one identifies, accepted statements, tools/instruments, and standards. Not all answerable questions are of equal significance. This does not itself settle which 1954 fractionation project was the optimal funding decision; a complete assessment needs alternative projects and contemporary weighting.

**Chang (2012), Is Water H2O?**, chapter 5, section 5.1.5 'Can We Afford It All?', printed pp.266–268, Drive `1ZZPVnMOCco8J2ORdtJxs5fC27kQ9sE15`. The impossibility of funding every inquiry does not entail funding a single line alone. Weak preservation of alternative paths differs from intensive support. This is not a universal 'always fund exploratory work' rule.

**Lari (2024), 'What Counts as Relevant Criticism?'**, Studies in History and Philosophy of Science 104:88–97, Drive `1w_d53dSeovNArYQ69kna_a1NjpwMKCHO`. Distinguishes first-level scientific discussion (goal-relative relevance of criticism) from meta-level criticism of the research goals themselves. The potential tension is explicitly addressed by Lari; claiming its discovery for MQR is invalid.

## Explicit comparison at one fixed question

Question P: 'Was continued experimentation on fractionation/incorporation rational in January–June 1954 even while the peptide-bond and mechanism interpretation remained unsettled?'

Dewey: allows problem formation to be intellectually meaningful without the final solution being known. Does **not** render a definite YES on the actual resource/practice details.

Feest: shows exploratory concept-building experiments can generate understanding in the absence of completed theories. Domain transfer is provisional; no historical case-specific YES/NO supported.

Kitcher: significance of a question is a dimension of practice; historical priorities and alternatives must be reconstructed. No determinate verdict without those.

Chang: preservation of alternatives can be valuable even under resource limitations, but scale of resource commitment is independent. No determinate verdict on this laboratory's actual funding.

Thus any four-row table of YES/NO verdicts would be fabricated. Present disposition is INTERPRETIVE COMPATIBILITY + HISTORICAL DECISION UNDERDETERMINED.

## New argumentative fork

It is necessary to separate:
- **Pursuit permissibility:** a line of inquiry is rationally admissible.
- **Pursuit priority:** it deserves resources relative to alternatives.
- **Claim acceptance:** observations support the proposed mechanism.
- **Scientific progress:** actual improvement relative to a specified scientific baseline.
- **Meta-criticism:** established criteria themselves are open to challenge.

The first two are not equivalent. In particular, an exploratory instrument may be a rational experiment but not a preferred high-cost program. Present philosophic work already contains the relevant distinctions, so their taxonomy is not original.

## Adversarial countercase

Consider two *constructed* projects with equal plausibility but different opportunity costs: cheap preliminary fractionation vs costly replication using an unvalidated measurement device. They might both be permissible pursuits while only the cheap one receives priority. A counterexample built this way is a logical stress test only; no historical cost data have been recovered for 1954. Do not claim actual funding priorities.
