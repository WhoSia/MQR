## 1. Return to origin
The founder's question asks why science can produce new forms of inquiry and explanation, including concept systems with partially incompatible question spaces. A static evidence accumulator or even a decision-theoretically optimal experiment selector under a fixed hypothesis class omits some changes to the **available distinctions, actions, concepts, validation conventions and evidence-producing community**. But this observation has extensive prior art, including Steinle 1997/2016, Feest 2005/2010/2025 intro, Nersessian 2008, Rheinberger 1997, Secord 2004 and Kane 2013.

## 3. A precise common grammar and its severe rivals
Three distinct meanings of 'productive experiment':
  E0 = produce a repeatable trace / observation;
  E1 = discriminate a hypothesis within a currently listed explanatory space;
  E2 = make a previously inaccessible **question or contrast** answerable by changed tools/procedures/representations.

E0 does not entail E1 or E2; E1 does not entail E2; E2 does not entail an ontologically real natural kind. Additionally, under conflicting method metrics **E2 may shrink** despite greater measured yield or signal-to-noise for a different measurand. This is a **classification and research conjecture**, not a new nontrivial information inequality.

Possible prior-art complete explanations:
- Rheinberger's epistemic/technical thing transformation already characterizes question-materializing systems.
- Feest's provisional research concepts already make operationalized conceptual work non-ontological.
- Kane/Messick validity theory already separates outcome, interpretation and licensed use.
- Standard causal identification already shows observational equivalence of rival latent mechanisms; the exact R32 countermodel is a familiar illustration.
- Scientific communities, institutions and acquisition costs mediate access as studied by Secord/Zollman/Avin/Gross & Bergstrom.

If no new discriminating power remains, MQR earns value through **careful integrative history, original-source comparison and reproducible adversarial calculations** rather than novelty claims.

## 4. A publishable research question, conditioned on stronger evidence
**Question:** Under what historically observable conditions does a change in experimental technique **expand, contract or merely relabel** the range of distinctions a scientific practice can reproducibly test?

Prospective operational test:
1. Date baseline task and target outcome, instrument, materials and scientific community.
2. Date the technical change and contemporaneous stated performance metric.
3. Identify one new possible contrast or removed sensitivity; state an independent, world-facing observable as a prospective discriminant.
4. Test whether the difference is only a language shift/relabeling, a method confound or a genuinely new accessible experiment.
5. Evaluate theory/ontology claims separately with independent methods and cross-setting transport.
6. Seek countercases: more precise technique with preserved all relevant signals, labels that don't change available tests, and new technical objects that reduce rather than increase interpretive access.

**False-positive stop:** a historian's retrospective phrase 'new questions' is not independent evidence of historically unavailable operations. Demonstrate changes in actual design repertoire and actors' dated predictions. Do not seed future variables into the model's historical agent without a source.
