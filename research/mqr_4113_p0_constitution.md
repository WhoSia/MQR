## P0-A: Competing mathematical and philosophical explanations

Define a finite *declared* latent state/target space \(\Theta\), a measurement experiment \(A: \Theta \leadsto X\) as a row-stochastic Markov kernel, and another experiment \(B: \Theta \leadsto Y\). A state-independent stochastic garbling is \(K:X\leadsto Y\), with \(B=AK\). This **Blackwell dominance** is a statistical experiment order: the randomness and state index must be modeled and held constant. For two binary states and binary signal alphabets, \(A\) and \(B\) are 2×2 stochastic matrices and the existence of a valid 2×2 row-stochastic \(K\) can be decided directly.

A directional deficiency under the fixed shared-state convention is
\[
\delta(A\to B) = \inf_{K\text{ row-stochastic}}\;\max_{\theta\in\Theta}
\operatorname{TV}\bigl((AK)_{\theta,\cdot},B_{\theta,\cdot}\bigr).
\]
For binary output, \(\operatorname{TV}\) equals the absolute difference in probability of the first category. **\(\delta(A\to B)=0\)** iff a feasible Blackwell garbling exists (for these finite exact inputs). In general \(\delta(A\to B)\ne\delta(B\to A)\). This is **classical Le Cam-style directional deficiency**, not a novel 4.113 theorem, and a zero statistical deficiency does **not** establish that \(A\) and \(B\) measure the same empirical quantity, use independently calibrated apparatus, or have the same history of sample exclusions.

**Representational rival:** two channel matrices can be statistically equivalent even if the instrument's operation–quantity correspondence is not established. Conversely, identical numerical values can express different physical quantities. For 4.113, defend an inference only after separately establishing statistical transport, operational grounding, calibration/genealogical transport and source/selection compatibility, without treating all as a universal mandatory independent-standard checklist. Distinguish mathematical sufficiency from *external* physical-world authorization.

## P0-C: Original theoretical competition (Drive originals consulted)

- [David Blackwell, *Comparison of Experiments* (1951)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1AabK3bac9aNuX5ASeEqkRDRucQSDZ7JB/view) explicitly develops comparison by decision-risk sets and sufficient statistical simulation. Mathematical experiment ordering is prior art.
- [Erik Torgersen, *Comparison of Statistical Experiments* (1991)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/112DN9KJ-JdKpRhLIbIDeusmejZ99msAX/view) systematically discusses decision risk, randomization, deficiencies, comparison. Direct prior art for the quantified gap.
- [Eran Tal, *Calibration: Modelling the Measurement Process* (2017)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1P5guGX-EJBfjr0fKigoSyTOghk5q8Ff2/view) locates measurement objectivity in a modeling and testing process, not in conversion-table algebra.
- [Elina Vessonen, *Representation in Measurement* (2021)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1jkafhd23yHYGUZYzXZtd32Pmy76fcTLu/view) argues for representation minimalism and distinct empirical relationships behind number systems.
- [van Fraassen, *Modeling and Measurement* (2012)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1hP92t9SNeHh-gI6IIDA_o0m8cK_69xGr/view) asks for theory-dependent criteria that operations count as measurement of targeted quantities.
