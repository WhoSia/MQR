# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · Record 19 — Direct participant witness before Paris meeting: Hachette and Ampère's reproduction on 19 Nov 1821

**2026-11-22 KST.** Actual document discovery: Ɛpsilon contemporary letter transcription [Faraday0159](https://epsilon.ac.uk/view/faraday/letters/Faraday0159) directly read; letter dated 19 Nov 1821. MQR 5.4 OPEN. This improves **R16**, not a new original physical discovery.

## 1. What is newly established, and how
- **Hachette → Faraday, 19 Nov 1821:** Hachette explicitly says that he and Ampère **repeated Faraday's conical electromagnetic wire-around-magnet rotation experiment successfully** and that Ampère would report it to the Académie des Sciences that same day. Hachette states he plans to add a diagram and description of Faraday's supplied small instrument to the forthcoming French publication. This is an actor's firsthand report of participating in the experiment, not merely the later 12 December assertion that a learned society saw a repetition. The source is Ɛpsilon scholarly transcript of IEE MS SC 2; **not** a direct visual inspection of the manuscript. The letter's editorial notes point to Annales de Chimie volume 18, p.397 and Faraday's journal article volume 12, pp.74–96.
- **Hachette → Faraday, 12 Dec 1821:** [Faraday0160](https://epsilon.ac.uk/view/faraday/letters/Faraday0160) retrospectively reports the 19 November Académie discussion and 24 November Société Philomatique repetition; also describes the sent 18 October explanatory letter and apparatus, plus Ampère's own differing apparatus. Original 19/24 November **meeting minutes remain unexamined**, so do not label the record independently corroborated by those minutes.
- **Faraday → Ampère, editor dated 2 Feb 1822:** [Faraday0165](https://epsilon.ac.uk/view/faraday/letters/Faraday0165). The letter internally prints “Feby 2nd. 1821”; editorial date 1822—retain conflict openly. Faraday reports repeating Ampère's magnetic rotation experiment and queries whether it discriminates *current about axis vs about constituent particles*. He does not assent to the proposed hypothetical internal currents. This is firsthand evidence that empirical agreement/reproduction **did not entail agreement about the inference that the experiment licensed**.
- **Ampère-side historiography independent author interpretation:** [Blondel and Wolff, *Faraday, Ampere, and the mystery of continuous rotations*](https://ampere-archives.fr/histoire-electricite/parcours-historique/lois-courants/rotations-continues/eng) distinguishes Faraday's rotation as basic phenomenon from Ampère's forces between current elements. It notes apparatus innovations, terrestrial magnetic action, and an Ampère demonstration using current/current interactions without a magnet. This is a modern **secondary synthesis**, not substitute for Ampère's autograph primary source.

## 2. Two distinct kinds of 'independence'
The participant letter is **independent experimental participation** relative to Faraday's original London observation; it is not an independent acquisition of all the written documents or a double-blind confirmation. Subsequent Hachette letters come from the same author and thus do not form two independent documentary lineages. Claim precisely:
(a) physical repeat reported by participant, (b) Paris community communication, (c) divergent interpretation subsequently witnessed. The 19 Nov letter materially upgrades (a) from secondhand report to participant testimony, while no experiment is performed by MQR here.

## 3. Faraday's correspondence as a bounded historical experiment
Candidate bridge contracts:
- Phenomenon bridge: does a current/magnet arrangement exhibit sustained motion/rotation with altered geometry? Evidence: Hachette 19 Nov first-person report. Supported within the described apparatus.
- Method bridge: can a recipient assemble and operate a sufficiently similar apparatus from transmitted text/diagram/device? Evidence: physical device received, reported success. Supported in a bounded historical sense; not a modern reproducibility protocol.
- Inference bridge: is the same motion competent to choose between Ampère's internal-current hypotheses? Faraday expressly disputes that licensing in Feb 1822; the competing full original Ampère argument is still a source gate.
- Ontological bridge: can both sides share one fully agreed primitive concept of force/current? No; differences are historically alleged, but formal noncommensurability NOT established.

## 4. A serious competing explanation
The community might share full operational and mathematical vocabulary, differing only in inference threshold. The historical record **does not yet require** a Kuhnian incommensurability diagnosis. A stronger test must reconstruct the exact rival mechanisms and their predictions under experiments accessible at 1821–22, and identify an experiment on which they make genuinely different predictions. Evidence of different *stated standards of proof* is not proof of different *world descriptions*.

A parallel warning: publication dates, editorial dates, letter writing dates and session dates are different metadata. Do not conflate Faraday's 11 Sept original paper timestamp, its published journal issue, 19 Nov report, and later 12 Dec summary.

## 6. Research proposition and next gate
The source-tested historical proposition is: **the same apparatus-based phenomenon circulated and was reportedly repeated across London–Paris while the theory-discriminating meaning remained explicitly contested.** This supports treating experimental reproducibility, instrumental affordance transfer and theory inference as distinct bridges. It does not demonstrate that a mathematically nonbijective quotient was literally realized or that translational failure is universal.
