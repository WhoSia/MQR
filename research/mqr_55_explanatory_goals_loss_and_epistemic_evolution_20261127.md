## Main correction
Kitcher's target is more specific than Kuhn's explicit argument about the bounds of inquiry. Loss of a correct explanatory schema, disappearance of a legitimate explanatory goal, loss of a practical capability, and loss of a promising but unrealized line of investigation are distinct. A schema may have been incorrect while its question was worth pursuing. The converse also holds: an apparently recurring goal may be a different question under a different ontology; lexical similarity is not identity. Kitcher's detailed analysis is a serious obstacle to any claim that Einstein restored an already correct Aristotelian gravitational explanation. It does not automatically establish retention of explanatory goals. This is not a claim Kitcher has no resources to discuss significance or aims: his practice framework already includes significant questions (pp.74–81 previously read) and his book discusses explanatory goals. Chang already makes the distinction between actual and counterfactual losses; no originality for a generic loss taxonomy is claimed.

## Discriminator without another arbitrary toy
For a specific dated transition, record separately (a) a contemporaneous stated goal, (b) an available explanatory schema and what it actually predicted or unified, (c) an independently justified retained/lost capability, (d) changed evaluation/acceptance standards, (e) later question and evidence for continuity of its meaning. Compare evidence of cessation, rejection, transfer to another field, replacement by a superior answer, or unchanged activity under new terminology. A claim of historical goal loss fails if contemporaneous records show the goal remained active in the relevant community. A claim of correct-schema loss fails if the old schema was false or preserved in the successor. A claim of counterfactual opportunity loss requires explicit assumptions about viable resources and alternative trajectories, not an observed difference alone. A claim of goal restoration fails if the old and new questions differ in substantive explanandum rather than just vocabulary. These are typed evidence requirements, not a new universal ontology or automatic scalar score. The old 5.4 record 10 on forced one-bit integration and record 29 prospective discriminators were reread; their fixed-target information loss cannot establish changed epistemic norms. Avoid repeating them.

## Positive and negative implications for possible sciences
A scientific transition may improve measured predictions, reject an incorrect ontology, and simultaneously close a legitimate research question. Alternatively it may discard a question because the question is incoherent or already solved. Both possibilities must remain live. Cross-science contact may revive an abandoned investigative goal by importing a new representation or tool without restoring the old ontology. This is a research hypothesis; no case here demonstrates alien communication. In Kuhn's own historical narrative general relativity and quantum mechanics re-open some old explanatory ambitions; Kitcher's critique blocks equating these with old correct schemata. A new research program should not require full prior convergence or a fixed universal menu of worthy questions. Preserve negative and positive outcomes rather than assume every former goal merits indefinite investment.
