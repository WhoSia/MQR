## 1. The central research correction

The six-permutation active learner on three latent states cannot construct a new scientific **question** out of a failed candidate match; its best-query objective is frozen and its output type fixed in advance. The new anomaly register repairs the loss of a possible reason to reopen that vocabulary but **does not generate revised concepts or certify evidence**.

Historical comparisons imply at least three distinct open-world transitions:

1. **New candidate within an old language** (e.g., a different latent B transition map after an unexpected native result). This can sometimes be solved by ordinary counterexample-driven learning.
2. **New operative question or observational representation** (e.g., the research utility of a spatiotemporal statistic, or a concept of genetic function indexed to investigative aim). The physical raw data need not contain additional bits; new utility is not automatically ontological novelty.
3. **Reorientation of epistemic purposes and standards**, including a formerly useful concept's loss of explanatory priority and previously neglected findings becoming new problems. This cannot be inferred from a syntactically enlarged state-machine alphabet alone.

This is a **working contrast of research tasks**, not a proof that these are exhaustive or necessary stages of science.

## 3. Epistemic paradox to retain rather than prematurely solve

A scientist may rationally construct an **unexpected question** from the same measured data, and another scientist may rationally use a *different* classificatory system for a different aim. A single universal category dictionary would erase some of these differences; but unlimited category relativism would erase the ability of one world's physical results to contradict claims.

**MQR candidate direction:** treat cross-science translation as a **partial, purpose-indexed and defeasible bridge of interventions, inferential roles and world-facing constraints**, not an automatic total equivalence of hidden ontology. The required realism is the possibility of discovering **where** a translation fails and revising that claim without pretending that any one representation equals reality. This has clear antecedents in [Brigandt](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~brigandt/The_epistemic_goal_of_a_concept.pdf), [Andersen (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110253610.271), perspectival realism, and active learning, so it is **an MQR working synthesis, not a first-discovered philosophical theorem**.
