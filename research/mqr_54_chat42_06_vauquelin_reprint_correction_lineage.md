## The missing registry's old mistake

Previously group #17 was “Vauquelin (1799), Nicholson (1800), Oppenheim (1807) exact first imprints,” an undifferentiated package. **Nicholson here denotes William Nicholson as editor of the English journal, *not* necessarily the author of Vauquelin's source data**; the 1800 journal is a *republication*, not the origin of a Nicholson-only measurement. Oppenheim 1807 is a **French book** rather than a separately authored peer-reviewed pyrometer paper. The 1799 original is a distinct French journal note; 1799 English and 1800 English are additional witnesses. Even the German 1799 entry must be distinguished from Scherer's different 1799 explanatory pyrometer article.

## The actual historical substantive question

Chaldecott 1975 reports that numbers **attributed** to Vauquelin in 1799 diverged substantially from Guyton's results, that Guyton commissioned a check using Wedgwood's pieces, and that Vauquelin said the published 1799 values did **not** match his earlier laboratory notes. A further composition was presented via Guyton in **1810**. The inaccurate published values had crossed at least German, British and French channels; the chain was not merely a translation of *words* but the re-use of **measurement authority**. This secondary reconstruction is well evidenced, but the exact causal path from each early printing to later decisions cannot be independently established without the primary texts, dates and original analytical tables.

**Original-source limit:** Chaldecott's PDF text extraction renders its original Table 2 badly; do not overconfidently map numerical rows to silica, alumina, lime, water etc. If the research needs quantitative source fidelity, read the table visually or acquire photo-facsimile of the article and three primary print versions, then compare column alignment, transcriptions and sums. Preserve the odd apparent >100% total as an **audit target** until source-table alignment and rounding are checked, not an established new discrepancy.

## Research implication for possible sciences

This is a concrete case of the distinction between **stable propagation of a representational symbol** and **reproduction of an instrument's validated result**. Native terms and tables can circulate intact across a research network while the original preparation, raw analysis and reference checks are unavailable. Formal mapping of results, even if perfectly reproducible, is weaker than reproducibility of the **world-contact process** that generated them.

The analogy to [record06 sequential measurement](mqr_54_chat42_06_sequential_contact_and_order_effects.md) is explicit and **limited**: the historical case concerns *human archival reclassification, translation and erroneous data attribution*, not physical quantum/classical measurement disturbance. Both warn against treating agreement of records as sufficient evidence of shared experimental identity; they do **not** have the same physical mechanism.
