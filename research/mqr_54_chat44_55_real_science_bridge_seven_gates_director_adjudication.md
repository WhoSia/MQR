## 2. R53 makes a new and uncomfortable adversarial point
Let true population bits A and B be independent, with four states each probability 1/4. If a community publishes only matching A=B reports, the retained data exhibit **perfect positive association** and covariance 1/4. If they collect cases in which at least one channel is active, the retained data exhibit **negative covariance -1/9**. These are exact conditional probability results. When positive inclusion probabilities are known and independent conditional on observed world state, inverse probability weighting repairs; when some states are deterministically excluded, association may become structurally unidentifiable. See R53 exact executable.

This hazard is standard **collider/selection bias**, not proof LIGO or single-cell researchers mishandled their analysis. The new operational requirement for cross-science bridges is **registration of detection, inclusion and reporting process** alongside pairing/calibration. Simply checking that reported observations match can make a false scientific consensus more likely under conditional publication.

## 3. R54 distinguishes a newly measurable variable from a newly identified physical quantity
Two messenger arrival delay can be written in a simplified model `d_obs = e_source + D*k` (emission lag plus propagation slowness contrast). Even with flawless GW–gamma source matching, a single event yields only one constraint on two physical components. Two events at different D identify k **only if the intrinsic source delay is shared**, or otherwise tightly constrained by astrophysics. Unknown event-specific lags defeat this extra identification. Standard inference/astrophysics already recognize the issue. The real 2017 paper derives conditional bounds on speed differences with stated physical assumptions, not a model-independent speed measurement from raw 1.74s.

## 4. Current failure ladder (now seven gates, NONE universally mandatory)
1. Separate A/B archives: no joint relation from marginal data alone (R44).
2. Same target linking: valid pairs unlock observational joint relations (R44).
3. Linkage uncertainty: `q=(1-pr)/2` yields only product if both unknown (R46).
4. Disturbing assays: accurate specimen IDs do not guarantee preserving the initial joint (R48).
5. Selection/triggers: reported pairs can show association entirely from conditional inclusion (R53).
6. Latent confounding: paired observation does not uniquely determine causal direction (R44/R32).
7. Physical nuisance lag: even a matched physical event + precise delay cannot isolate separate mechanism parameters without source/emission assumptions (R54).

These are limitations conditional on model assumptions; do not portray them as seven stages every scientist must sequentially encounter or as unique MQR mathematics. Their usefulness is an **assumption checklist** with source-specific controls.
