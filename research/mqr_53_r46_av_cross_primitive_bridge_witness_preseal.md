# MQR-5.3 R46-AV — First cross-primitive shared-world witness, with positive and negative bridges

Origin-first small demonstrator, 2026-11-10. Not a new theorem; a sanity-check experimental design before expanding to biology, physics, or actual historical scientific paradigms.

## Shared environment and distinct native observations

Unknown-to-agents environment W={00,01,10,11}, represented for outside verifier as two-bit state (h,l). A scientist A has only primitive signal a=l, a scientist B has primitive signal b=h. Their primitive instruments refer to distinct environmental degrees of freedom. This is a **toy** for divergent conceptual/measurement articulations, NOT a strong incommensurability claim about genuine alternative logic or language.

Both observers experience passive initial uniform states. Each sees binary 0 and 1 half the time; passive marginal histogram P(a=0)=P(b=0)=1/2. Thus simply matching the marginal probability vector is too weak to establish their observables are the same variable.

Intervention: action L toggles low bit l to l⊕1, with h fixed. Under identical shared-world operation, A's before/after observation must change with probability 1, B's before/after observation must change with probability 0. Even though passive marginals match, the joint time-series laws differ under L: P_A(Y_after≠Y_before|L)=1 versus P_B(...|L)=0. This formally refutes the simple identity bridge a↔b under L; it does not refute the scientists' claims in their respective intended domains.

## Positive control

Define alternate scientist B' with signal c=1−l rather than h. Both passive marginals still uniform, but under intervention L both A and B' flip; their paired observations always satisfy c=1−a. A complement map ψ(c)=1−c makes paired responses identical for every accessible sequence of L and no-op actions. A real bridge exists in this toy and can be found given paired synchronized measurements (but not merely independent marginal histograms). Native descriptions differ only by simple encoding, so this positive control is intentionally trivial and MUST NOT be cited as proof that all alien science is translatable.

## Identifiability and feedback

A finite experiments E that observes only passive marginal outcomes cannot discriminate B from B'; no choice of estimate uniquely identifies a bridge class from those data. Adding synchronized **paired pre/post** experiments with actual intervention L distinguishes them. Data from each measurement channel alone still do not identify the ontological total state W; paired action/outcome comparability is supplied from outside the toy as an engineering fixture. A genuine challenge must later endogenize how agents build that fixture using their own theory-dependent technologies.

## Source and anti-hype standards

Existing field already includes observational equivalence of automata, bisimulation, causal identification, and active design; novelty claim is NONE. This witness is a conceptual QA test against MQR's own drift: if work on Wedgwood or metrological legitimacy does not answer how alternative observers align their intervention/readout structures, it cannot advance this original-explanandum branch.

Proposed future scorecard for a less-trivial simulation: (a) no shared labelled state; (b) distinct symbolic primitives or action grammars; (c) accessible joint physical interface not given as oracle; (d) costs and failures of synchronization; (e) zero-shot transfer and held-out novel interventions; (f) positive recovery versus rival disconnected systems; (g) comparison to automata learning / causal model abstraction / system identification baselines; (h) invalid overclaim detection (observational match != same ontology).
