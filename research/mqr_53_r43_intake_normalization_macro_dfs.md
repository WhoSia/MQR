## 2. New close-reading disputes

**Schickore 2016**, publisher-in-press article, explicitly argues that exploratory experimentation as a general philosophical *category* is too vague to bear the normative role claimed for it. It links Steinle's search for regularities to Burian's triangulation and distinguishes rival goals/strategies. The category provoked better questions about theory, instrument and experimental practice but cannot substitute for careful specification.

**Feest 2010**, experimental neuropsychology, describes concepts as research *tools*, not fixed labels passively applied to given objects. The absence of a stable concept does not imply absence of disciplined inquiry.

**Feest 2011** distinguishes surface (data-pattern) regularities from underlying regularities; neither can be stabilized independently, because even visible patterns need validation and invisible mechanisms are inferred through constructed patterns. The resulting fit is the stabilization target. This directly attacks an MQR generic 'raw data is safe and interpretations are revisable' doctrine.

**Woodward 2000** argues data-to-phenomenon support requires appropriate patterns of counterfactual sensitivity across production and interpretation. A record's presence by itself is not sufficient. **Apel 2011** challenges reduction of phenomena to data patterns and proposes a contextual interpretation of their epistemic role. This is a live clash; MQR needs to identify exactly what is at stake.

**Tal 2016**, standardization of time: top-down conceptual/statistical adjustment and bottom-up standard artefact modification. The calibration/mutual-grounding topic already belongs to the source literature. R34–35 is a historical illustration, not theory priority.

**Zamecnik & Keller 1954 July** now directly available as original PDF in canonical Drive; its opening explicitly restricts 'incorporation' to bonding of labeled amino acid to protein without asserting alpha-peptide bonding of net synthesis. Direct period evidence supports the already noted caution and resolves a formerly unavailable primary source. The later September Keller et al. article is a different publication and remains separately indexed.

## 3. MACRO — Longino × Chang × Steinle and the pre-articulation problem

Primary book reinspection:
- [Longino (2002), *The Fate of Knowledge*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/14AVrxujqX41GbDmiS4dlA5cKwQVdlFdu/view), especially chapter 6 on effective/transformative criticism: public venues, response/uptake of criticism, recognized standards, appropriately tempered equality of intellectual authority. This is a well-established alternative to a simple consensus theory. Longino explicitly limits attention to relevant criticism and does **not** require all voices to be equally funded or endorsed.
- [Chang (2004), *Inventing Temperature*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RwuRfY9Ifz6iEAUZZm57z4EzHX8CP0CV/view), chapter 6 complementary science and epistemic iteration; [Chang (2012), *Is Water H2O?*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ZZPVnMOCco8J2ORdtJxs5fC27kQ9sE15/view), sections 1.2.4 and 1.3 on premature death of phlogiston and rational alternatives. The afterlives of rejected systems are already extensively investigated.
- [Steinle (2002)](https://addi.ehu.es/bitstream/handle/10810/40381/Theoria%2044%20291-316.pdf?sequence=1), historical reconstruction of Ampère's transition from battery-pole descriptions to the *current circuit* as a novel language for expressing experimental regularities. This is exactly a case of new questions becoming linguistically expressible.

**Macro-level tension, not a contradiction established:** Longino's public criticism requires sufficient shared standards to make criticism relevant; Steinle shows that exploratory work can produce the very language needed to articulate new problems; Chang shows why past dismissed research may warrant reconsideration. A worthwhile next problem asks about justified *pre-articulation exploration*, before the rival viewpoint can meet established discursive standards. However Longino may well have resources to accommodate this, and Friedman, Kitcher, Dewey, Chang and epistemic pluralism may already resolve it. A new universal 'pre-critical admission gate' would repeat MQR's own meta-language self-sealing mistake.
