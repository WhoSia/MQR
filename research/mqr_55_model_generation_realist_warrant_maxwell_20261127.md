## Result: distinct roles and distinct warrants
The primary record supports Nersessian's narrower claim that the actual published reasoning actively used a mechanical model. It also supports Kitcher's narrower claim that success of the retained relations does not independently establish a unique underlying medium or mechanism. A stronger classification of all ether-related assumptions as merely presuppositional in this particular derivation is inadequate; distinguish the general existence of a medium, the specific mechanical model, the constraints on that model, and the mathematical relationships retained. Conversely a productive model is not proof of the physical reality of its parts. Maxwell's disclaimer pertains to the specific rolling-contact connection, not a blanket rejection of the ether. P.491's model-independence remark is significant counterevidence against treating the whole development as logically inseparable from a particular mechanical realization. Do not confuse stated independence of relations with historical access to them before discovery.

## Mathematical check on the rival consistency narrative
Modern SI vacuum illustration, not Maxwell's original notation: assume smooth fields, div E=rho/epsilon0, continuity div J=-partial_t rho, and curl B=mu0(J+C). Taking divergence gives div C=partial_t rho. The standard C=epsilon0 partial_t E satisfies this, but so does C'=epsilon0 partial_t E+curl A for any sufficiently smooth vector field A with appropriate units. Therefore consistency alone fixes a divergence, not a unique vector correction. E.g. A=(0,0,x^2/2) in scaled coordinates gives curl A=(0,-x,0), nonzero and divergence-free. This is a local algebraic counterexample, not a viable alternative complete electrodynamics; boundary conditions, locality, symmetries, constitutive assumptions and independent empirical constraints can rule out alternatives. It also does not prove a specific mechanical model was necessary to discovery. No claim of new theorem, no actual experiment.

## Return to founding question
A scientific architecture can retain functional/structural relations while changing the resources that generated them. Distinguish four questions: actual generative use, contemporaneous alternative accessibility, retained explanatory/predictive content, and entity-level realist warrant. A single object can have different roles in different episodes. For possible sciences, shared surviving relation need not imply shared historical scaffolds or complete ontology; test bridge, scope and ancestry. For epistemic evolution, removal of a scaffold may mark a gain in generality while losing an accessible reasoning method. Kitcher p.174's actual discussion of explanatory loss offers a subsequent distinct normative branch; do not conflate it with the erroneous pinpoint.
