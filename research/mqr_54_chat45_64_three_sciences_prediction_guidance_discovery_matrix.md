## Strict three-axis decomposition

Let T = theoretical expressibility of a candidate, G = historical prediction-guided selection of experiment, and C = empirical corroboration after observation. Neither T∧C → G nor G → C is valid as a logical law.

- Dirac/Anderson and Crick/tRNA support T and subsequent C while challenging G in these particular experimental histories.
- Bell–CHSH provides a much clearer historically guided experimental transition, yet its experimental validation is conditional on details of selection, setting independence and protocol interpretation.
- The *existence* of a named theoretical entity should not be equated with experimental individuation of a natural kind. The same latent state may admit competing models, and multiple concepts may be operationally compatible until discriminating experiments become available.

## A genuinely risky test for MQR

If independent history of science can explain every difference solely through documented choices, local heuristics, established confirmation theory and causal inference without an extra theory of 'question generation', then MQR should remain an explanatory synthesis, **not** claim a new universal constitutive law. To progress, select a real case where a new mathematical question changed the *feasible experiment menu* in a way existing histories cannot capture without supplemental assumptions; register its instrument evidence and a near-miss negative control. Compare actual actor testimony and changes in protocol, not only chronology or dates of theory and discovery.
