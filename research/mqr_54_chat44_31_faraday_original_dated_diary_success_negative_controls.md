## Dated primary actor diary entries, editorial 2008 preview pagination

**29 August 1831, Diary Vol. I printed pp.367–368, edition PDF zero-based pages 36–37**, original numbered diary §§1–13. The iron ring A/B coil preparation is recorded as direct laboratory evidence at §§1–2. At **§3** primary circuit make transiently deflected needle, settled at original position, and break gave another disturbance; at **§7** momentary attraction on primary contact, subsequent rest and reverse movement on break; at **§8–9** Faraday explicitly identified transient and reversed response. At **§11** he tried to elicit chemical action from the transient using plated contacts and solutions but *found none*. The latter is a negative probe, not proof that induced electrical current is incapable of electrochemical effects in principle.

**30 August 1831, Diary Vol. I printed p.369, edition PDF page 38**, **§14**: With the primary A side still connected to the battery, breaking/closing B-side connection to the indicating flat helix generated **no detectable needle effect**; Faraday attributed the transient to changes at the **battery side**, not to a persistent special state of the secondary wire. **§15**: Attempt at detecting a spark on the indicator side was **negative**, with explicit reservation about possible stronger battery. **§18**: Iron cylinder alternative gave weaker but qualitatively same effect, so **closed iron ring geometry is not a necessary condition** for the observed effect in these trials. These were real counterevidence-seeking physical variation tests, not an abstract logical falsification machine.

**24 September 1831, Diary Vol. I printed pp.371–372, edition PDF pages 40–41**, **§§21–32**: Several alternative close-spaced copper/iron coils and loops failed to show measurable induced needle response under attempted source operation, including the remark that contacts were apparently perfect (§21). **§33**: a particular iron cylinder/winding placed between magnetic poles induced **momentary** galvanometer response each time the magnet contact was made or broken; when the electric indicator connection was interrupted no such response was observed. Faraday labels this an explicit conversion of magnetism into electricity. This is contemporaneous **positive/negative within the same date**, not just a historian's compressed retrospective successes.

## Source/claim matrix and real discriminants

- **What is the new empirically answerable question?** Which time-indexed change in a primary electrical or magnetic arrangement yields a response in an otherwise separate indicating coil?
- **What rules out the easiest artifact?** 30 Aug §14 does not show the same response from merely making/breaking B detector connection while A remains powered. 24 Sept §33 response vanishes on breaking measurement path: a detector/circuit integrity control.
- **What remains a rival?** Contact transients, galvanometer hysteresis and other arrangement-specific effects are not exhaustively ruled out by any one 1831 entry; broader replication in later Faraday reports supports a more general physical mechanism.
- **Which new experimental family emerged?** Coil shape, winding, iron/copper, magnet contact, primary circuit make/break, indicator connectivity and different geometries become independent factors. This is evidenced in actual dated variations, not a fixed symbolic operator alphabet gifted in a synthetic toy.
- **What changed conceptually?** An ongoing *magnetism/electricity conversion* question joined the old *magnetic needle under current* question; modern induction/flux differentiation is a later theory and must not be projected back as a direct Faraday oscilloscope waveform.
- **What concept-genesis prior art preempts MQR novelty?** [Steinle 1997](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rl444ab9HV-Q285EwbPq1QBR2z5g3JcR/view), [Steinle 2016](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-kviuErWDAEJMRlcbACBX7FbLR55hV1G/view), existing [MQR41 Ørsted note](mqr_54_mqr41_ersted_maxwell_trna_question_genesis_historical_episode_ledger.md).

## Why this source is much more probative than an additional model

The 1831 diary actually records **historical operations, what did NOT work, the next different operation and the actor's stated provisional interpretation**. This satisfies more of MQR's chronological question-genesis requirements than merely proving a finite observability partition theorem. Still it cannot isolate uniquely whether the actor's original expectations generated the experiment or arose in feedback; the actor describes an intention in §1, so 'wholly theory-free exploration' is inaccurate. The crucial epistemic problem is how theory-guided **and** exploratory work together produce new conditions of question relevance, a live non-exclusive rivalry.
