## Analytical consequence of relaxing the simplifying assumption
For two methods A,B pursuing the same recognizable success criterion, at fixed allocation let marginal success probabilities p,q and intersection s be given. Then P(A union B)=p+q-s, with max(0,p+q-1)<=s<=min(p,q). Therefore max(p,q)<=P(A union B)<=min(1,p+q). Sharp bounds by joint Bernoulli tables (s,p-s,q-s,1-p-q+s). No need to assume independence or exclusivity. If alternative allocation with success c is considered, splitting is strictly better for every compatible dependence iff max(p,q)>c, strictly worse for every compatible dependence iff min(1,p+q)<c. Equality cases permit ties. Otherwise no uniformly strict preference, and if c lies strictly inside the interval both strict rankings are possible. This holds for stipulated known marginals at fixed budget and same target only. It does not identify actual success probabilities, learning value or instrument creation.
Illustration not Kitcher's empirical data: concentrated success c=.65; split marginals .50,.40. Union range [.50,.90]; independent assumption gives .70, mutual exclusivity .90, nested success .50. Split beats concentration iff overlap s<.25. Merely counting two routes or assigning each a score cannot determine preference. Marginal incremental success from B given A available is q-s, bounded [max(0,q-p),min(q,1-p)]. Different observed response vectors or names do not alone determine overlap. This is standard elementary probability/Fréchet bounds, not new math or claimed improvement over Kitcher; Kitcher already includes overlap in objective.
