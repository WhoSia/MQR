## Genuine opposed philosophical assertions, fixed referent

**Grace 2001** (publisher abstract) attributes naive early operationism's problem to treating a single operation as sufficient to define unobservable constructs, and holds convergent operationism to be the enduring methodology for valid inferences to hidden psychological phenomena. He rejects reading naive operationism's defect as a disproof of the positivist philosophical orientation.

**Green 2001** (author-hosted text, §§ opening–criticism) argues a more fundamental problem: If a concept is *identical* with one operation, different operations necessarily define different concepts and there is, strictly speaking, no same referent for multiple operations to converge on. Green locates this critique already in L. J. Russell (1928), distinguishes a useful methodological rule of thumb from an original anti-metaphysical doctrine, and argues no number of converging operations alone can repair a **strict definitional identity thesis** without relaxing that thesis.

**Rogers 2001** (publisher abstract) objects to Grace's method-history account: multiple-measure practices predated 1930s psychological operationism by decades; the 1950s adoption of several measures is not proof it originated then or that it 'saved' logical positivism. Instead real scientists' prior investigative practice must be reconstructed.

**Feest 2005** (direct primary) adopts a methodological reconstruction rather than attributing to Stevens/Tolman semantic identity as an a priori definition. She accepts a residual circular validation problem and discusses multiple-method strategies without claiming complete solution. Feest's answer potentially dissolves Green's *strict identity* challenge for the instrumental definition case; it does not settle whether some operational constructs are causally/ontologically real.

## Conflict typing

P1. Does the adoption of multiple independent operations by itself solve the philosophical semantic identity problem under strict `concept = one operation`? Grace: defends the new method's rehabilitating ability (qualified, from abstract). Green: no, the strong identity hypothesis must be abandoned. **Actual philosophical disagreement**, but do not flatten to blanket "Grace says all problems solved"; Grace distinguishes programmatic usefulness from metaphysical solutions.

P2. Did sophisticated multi-measure practices first emerge as a methodological innovation in 1950s convergent operationism? Rogers: no, important earlier precedents (publisher abstract). Grace's historical account emphasizes 1950s correction, but does not necessarily explicitly deny every earlier multi-measure precedent. **Historical priority dispute with possible formulation mismatch**. Keep original paper date evidence separate.

P3. Must operationism amount to semantic reductionism? Green criticizes a strict semantic thesis; Feest's 2005 historical interpretation finds Stevens/Tolman had primarily provisional operational indicators instead. **Potential shared target mismatch**. A philosophical objection to strict semantic identity and a historical denial that actors asserted it can both be true.

## The crucial repair to MQR R46-N

Garner (1956) already teaches alternative-eliminating experimental operations, so R46-K's simple identification intervention is not original. BUT that fact neither **establishes** unique psychological ontology nor ends the Green/Grace debate. Do not misreport a methodological theorem about differential predictions as an ontological theorem about real constructs.

**Novelty verdict:** the 'find a dispute between operationism and convergence' problem was already openly argued in 2001; MQR has not added a novel solution. It has gained a more precise three-way adversarial baseline: historical semantics (Feest), methodological discriminations (Garner/Grace), and target/reference challenge (Green), supplemented by Rogers's practice-historical critique.
