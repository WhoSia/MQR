# MQR 5.4 — An experiment may require a translation before it can test one

2026-11-17 KST

## Return to the founding question

The [MQR 1 original archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/190Sh9592ogYTcaYEGtMNksdbqYzDhsTS/view) asks whether very different sciences can communicate when the physical means of communication embody their own theories. The [November 14 experiment](mqr_54_protocol_frontier_and_waveguide_design.md) held action and observation meanings in common and varied allowed sequences. The earlier [bridge checker](../language/real/experiments/OPEN-HORIZON-BRIDGE-V54.md) started from paired results and declared witnesses. This round treats the action dictionary as a live hypothesis. It keeps a shared output alphabet, known initial preparations and stated native protocol rules. Screening requires that each initially executable left action have an executable right counterpart with a matching trace. Subsequent experiments are restricted to the common admissible domain.

## Two dictionaries and one distinguishing experiment

Use polarized light prepared with unit horizontal amplitude. Let R rotate the polarization by 90 degrees, and let P transmit only the horizontal component. Measure transmitted intensity at the output of each prefix on fresh preparations. R alone and P alone both give unit intensity. R followed by P gives zero, while P followed by R gives unit intensity. Repeat operations RR and PP also give unit intensity. This follows from matrix products PR and RP acting on the same horizontal vector. It has been checked by exact integer Jones-vector calculation on all 255 words of length 0 through 7. The finite model folds signs of pure horizontal and vertical vectors into the respective states, since the available transformations and intensity output preserve that quotient. Neither simulator data nor the published calculus establish how a particular real apparatus performs.

The left laboratory calls its operations a and b. The right laboratory calls its operations x and y. Each has the rotation and horizontal polarizer. On initial and single-operation intensity records, both bijections survive. If both laboratories can put both modules in either order, translating ab as xy preserves the 1,1,0 trace, while translating it as yx yields 1,1,1. The first separating test costs two operations under unit costs. A budget of one leaves a known, unaffordable separating test. In the symmetric control in which both actions flip the same observed bit, no sequence distinguishes the candidate dictionaries.

## The experiment can depend on the dictionary

Make the right protocol more restrictive: both operations can be done singly, but xy is the only legal ordered pair. Neither aa nor bb is permitted in the right protocol. On the left, ab has a right realization under the straight dictionary, while ba has a realization under the swapped one. If a jointly scheduled experiment must be specified in left operations and executable under **every** live dictionary, its common domain contains the empty word and single operations only. Choosing one dictionary on independent grounds expands this domain and reopens a test. Thus the set of executable tests can depend on which translation hypotheses remain. A common-protocol failure alone does not establish that the rival dictionaries predict the same behavior on all protocols.

The hypothesis to carry forward is that the data needed to start comparing two sciences can include the means of aligning their actions, and that these means can change which comparisons they are able to run. The strong limit is explicitly conditional on common executability. If refusals to execute are themselves interpretable observations, if the right laboratory can choose its own native sequence and share a grounded trace, or if a physical anchor gives one pairing, the current obstruction can disappear. These are rival experimental arrangements. Different costs per dictionary, uncertain output scaling, physically unavailable resets and non-bijective dictionaries are outside this implementation.

Construct the product of the left protocol and one relabelled right protocol for each surviving dictionary. Readouts are compared in that common protocol using the existing minimum-cost finite search. The construction gives the intersection of the admitted word sets, not an intersection of empirical evidence. Removing a dictionary can only enlarge this common domain because one conjunct is removed. If deterministic trace predictions agree throughout a fixed common domain, an adaptive choice based solely on those traces cannot distinguish them. Inductively each permitted observed prefix is the same, so the next chosen branch stays the same. This statement concerns a fixed common domain, and does not preclude learning via the additional channels above.

## Literature and implementation scope

Moore (1956), §II, already distinguishes sequential machines through experiments. Angluin (1987), §§1.1 and 2, asks a Teacher for membership and equivalence information over a fixed known alphabet. Our finite use of products, permutations and counterexamples is standard mathematics. The change in the MQR question is to make the cross-lab pairing uncertain and examine the feedback between uncertainty and access. The examiner enumerates equal-sized action bijections and assumes both machines are declared; it does not learn unknown physical transition laws or certify a shared meaning from labels. Factorial dictionary enumeration and a protocol-product state space restrict this research utility to small candidate sets. Galison's waveguide narrative motivated the attention to objects and joint work, and does not validate the optical model or the inference about cross-civilization translation.
