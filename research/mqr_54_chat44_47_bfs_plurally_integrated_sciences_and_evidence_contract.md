## 1. Substantial prior art limits MQR's claims
Mitchell argues scientific representation is intrinsically partial and that methods, theories and observations select different features of the world; a plurality of perspectives may provide stronger constrained interpretations without reducing every method to one. In her paper's X-ray/NMR protein-structure discussion, two physical modalities access different relevant interactions, and their combination requires reliability and careful integration. One must not state that X-ray crystallography and NMR measure literally identical elementary signals or that they always produce compatible models. Mitchell already supplies the intellectual rationale for *integrative* pluralism.

Chang's active realism and conservationist pluralism advocate retaining practices still empirically valuable rather than deleting them when a new paradigm is dominant. This is an existing philosophical project; MQR has no right to priority for 'plural sciences coexist'.

## 2. R44/46 pairing formalizes a narrower problem hidden in loose pluralism language
Community P observes A, community Q observes B, each in its own experimental language, with flawless single-variable instruments. Without **same-target linkage**, perfect local measurement plus bilingual naming can fail to identify even P(A XOR B). Under accurate pairing the joint becomes identifiable; under unknown linkage probability p only pr is identified, so seemingly integrated records may hide fundamentally different scientific association strengths. Even full joint measurement cannot establish direction of causation without further assumptions or intervention (R44 fork vs chain).

This yields a conditional 'integration contract' with *four independent obligations*:
- TARGET: do both methods refer to the same individual/event/scale/temporal interval?
- LINKAGE: are paired observations correctly associated, and with what verified error model?
- MEASUREMENT: do each method's outputs validly track its declared quantity across contexts?
- INFERENCE: what descriptive/causal/ontological proposition is licensed by the combined data?

All four can fail separately. A common formula or ontology is neither necessary nor sufficient for all four.

## 3. Why not demand a global one-to-one dictionary?
Different methods may have complementary and intentionally **nonbijective** abstractions. One produces a spatial structure; another a dynamical process. A restricted interface can pass observable constraints between them for a task without a universal identity of every primitive and intervention. This echoes MQR R15–20 and is compatible with existing philosophy of science, category/model comparison and multimodal data fusion. But a common target and auditable linkage are unavoidable whenever the *question is a joint relation between measured facts*.

## 4. Two adversarial counterexamples
1. **No added insight:** P and Q have instruments but a joint scientific question is already fully answerable from P's records alone; elaborate integration provides no new relevant constraint. More disciplines do not imply more discovery.
2. **Illusory agreement:** P and Q both use a label 'temperature' but one reports a transformed/incompatible proxy in another material regime. Even identical numerical readings cannot guarantee transferable ontology or a stable joint target. The appropriate approach is instrument-specific calibration and testable agreement, not rhetorical unification.

## 5. New nontrivial thought-experiment extensions to prioritize
- Different individuals vs different times on same individuals: introduce a temporal matching operation and test whether the pairing contract really preserves causal time order.
- X-ray/NMR complementary constraints: build an abstract two-representation inference problem where a feature of the target is only identified jointly; then seek a source-grounded protein example, **not fabricated experimental structural data**.
- Finite cost/availability: an instrument may be individually exact but destructive and prohibit paired use, forcing inference from matched but nonidentical samples. This is an authentic empirical distinction rather than merely more Boolean algebra.
- Shared external-world intervention: add an intervention that perturbs a declared target, and verify whether both perspectives predict the change without assuming an artificial universal 'master' ontology.

## 6. What fails if the philosophy adds nothing new?
If Mitchell/Chang plus Bayesian data fusion, causal inference and standard calibration already explain all outcomes, MQR is a disciplined **cross-disciplinary adjudication of existing insights**, not a new philosophical law. A publishable original contribution needs an independently dated or empirically realized example where a specific linkage/translation obligation was wrongly assumed and caused measurable inferential failure, not merely a pretty four-world diagram.
