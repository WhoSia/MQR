## Macro inference: a possibility space is not actual history
The mathematical model does three different jobs: construct a conditional possibility, derive discriminating consequences, and suggest questions for instruments and experiments. Evidence from one realized system can support a particular mechanism without proving that all systems with a similar pattern share that mechanism. A similar mathematical structure can be a productive bridge even when its physical interpretation differs. A bridge may combine rival ideas rather than collapse them. Distinguish mathematical existence, empirically realized possibility, causal identification, and disciplinary uptake. Same local law but different domain produces different modal affordances; same visible pattern can arise through different mechanisms. Neither direction warrants ontology identity. A new science may emerge through combination of old concepts, which is weaker than invention of a genuinely new primitive; Turing is a strong witness of the former, not proof of the latter.

Prior 5.4 capability-generation and joint-action papers already caution against analyst hindsight embedding; Hiscock/Megason and Green/Sharpe anticipate pattern-to-mechanism and complementarity. Macro contribution is synthesis and explicit separation of conditions, not claimed priority. Preserve medicine as comparison: institutional allocation can restrict available instruments/people, whereas geometry can restrict physical modes, but these are categorically different mechanisms and not equivalent mathematical causes.
