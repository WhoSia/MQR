## Historical discrimination rather than a new taxonomy
Eilenberg & Mac Lane (1945), General Theory of Natural Equivalences, Trans AMS 58:231–294, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-1945-0013131-6, public university-hosted article text pp231–237 and section 7 read at https://people.math.osu.edu/cogdell.1/6112-Eilenberg%26MacLane-www.pdf . Authors explicitly describe a problem in their 1942 homology research leading to the general theory. Every finite-dimensional real V is isomorphic to V*, but this does not yield a choice of isomorphisms compatible with all linear isomorphisms (even scaling by 2 forces a putative invariant bilinear form B to satisfy B=4B). In contrast the evaluation map eta_V(v)(phi)=phi(v) gives a natural isomorphism V→V**; for f:V→W, f** eta_V = eta_W f by evaluation on each phi in W*. This is standard linear algebra/categorical naturality, not a new theorem. The conceptual advance is that compatibility across mappings becomes a question in its own right.

## Method necessity is not identified by chronological association

Explicitly recognizes fusion/recombination and that not every method produces a field, so category-theory latency alone cannot defeat his claim. Argues unintended tool use helps isolate causality. Separate proposition: a particular tool token is indispensable in a specified feasible historical context; at least one type of tool from a specified family is necessary; a method is new to a local practice; a productive arrangement is novel; a method's appearance is sufficient for autonomous field formation. These are not equivalent. If “new method” includes every new productive intellectual arrangement, necessity becomes difficult to falsify; freeze classification prospectively.
Construct two causal models on binary context U, complementary readiness R, method M, field outcome F. Both have factual assignment M=U; model A: F=M*R; model B: F=U*R. For all four contexts (U,R), observed F=U*R in both, and any factual F=1 has M=1. Yet under hypothetical do(M=0) with U=R=1, A predicts F=0, B F=1. Knowing that tools were not invented with the later field in mind does not alone exclude common causes or shared opportunity. This is a generic nonidentification witness, not a diagnosis of actual Krauss data; U is not estimated or historically asserted. A second example F=(A or B)*R demonstrates necessity of a family vs a specific member; if both absent no outcome, either alone with readiness succeeds. Do not equate all physical means of inquiry with a recently invented method. Counterfactual feasibility in actual history remains to be earned.

## Revised explanatory architecture
A knowledge-generating system can change in at least two directions: (1) changing resources for the same target, (2) making a former resource, its organization, or relation into a new object of inquiry. The second is target reversal (descriptive local label, no priority claim). It can occur within a discipline, establish a new program, or later a specialty. Do not require that new question is logically unexpressible in the old language. The historical distinction is what was recognized, achievable and pursued. A useful concept is insufficient by itself for institutionalization. A common method can serve distinct domains; different methods can serve a common target. Provisional test for disciplinary autonomy: sustained native questions not reducible to service tasks assigned by host fields; transmissible competence and criticism; evidence of uptake beyond founders. These dimensions need not be necessary universally (external demands may remain central), and institutions are separate.
New possible sciences do not all need new natural phenomena. Mathematical or artifact-generated objects, and social institutions, can become targets. New inquiry may arise because existing questions are generalized, reclassified, turned on their own means, or recombined, and can produce its own future resources. The central research task is to establish when such transitions happen, not define them into existence. No global scientific field count, prediction or fixed lifecycle.
