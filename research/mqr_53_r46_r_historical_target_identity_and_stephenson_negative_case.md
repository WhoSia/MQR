## B. Four propositions must not be collapsed

Let `S` be the operational radioactive count associated with an extracted protein fraction; let `B` denote radioactively labeled alpha-peptide bonding; let `P` be recovery of a fully characterized radiolabeled protein product; let `T` be synthesis of the target virus protein in a cell-free infected-leaf system.

`S` does not entail `B`; the 1954 paper **actively investigated** evidence for `B`, e.g. hydrolysis and provisional peptide peaks; those additional controls are stronger than the mere count `S`.

`B` does not entail `P`, and `P` does not automatically entail `T` in a different preparation. The actual authors emphasized that `P` remained outstanding to infer their cell-free system synthesized protein. The negative TMV result concerned **one specified target under a particular system and time**, not every biological protein synthesis process.

Even `S`'s mapping to 'microsomal' or 'chloroplast' may shift with centrifugation procedure and chemical treatment. Rheinberger's historical argument shows fraction labels were stabilized operationally before molecular identity was characterized. A fraction is an instrumentally produced sample class, not automatically a single molecular kind.

## C. Real competing assessments at the SAME inferential step

**Green (2001) semantic identity challenge:** If strict semantic operationism equates a construct with one operation, the alleged convergence of independent operations on the *same* concept cannot be explained without relaxing identity. **But** 1954 Zamecnik & Keller explicitly *do not* define 'protein synthesis' as the isotope assay. Therefore treating this paper as a direct counterexample to Green is a **category mistake**. Green's diagnosis applies conditionally to a strict semantic-reduction thesis the actors themselves did not assert.

**Feest (2005/2010/2012, 2025 Introduction) investigative tools:** Radioactive incorporation and protein fractionation can be legitimate *tools* for initiating/redirecting a program with incomplete concept/object identity. Stephenson's chloroplast result with the non-result for target TMV synthesis illustrates productive investigative divergence without validated original target. Feest's account is a strong explanatory prior; no Feest-specific dated judgment on this 1956 experiment has been located.

**Garner–Hake–Eriksen (1956) discriminating operations:** Protein washing/precipitation, ninhydrin, peptide hydrolysis, energy source and differential fractions target some alternatives; they are not all fully independent. Repeated isotope counting does not distinguish peptide formation from alternative binding. Later addition of more discriminating operations is a classical response, not MQR innovation.

**Borsboom–Mellenbergh–van Heerden (2004) causal attribute validity:** If the attribute measured is precisely 'cell-free synthesis of a defined target protein', the authors' causal criterion demands the attribute exist and causally influence the readout. A label in mixed extract under oxygen/light conditions does not, by itself, demonstrate that defined target; the failed TMV recovery directly resists that inference. But **not** every strong non-TMV incorporation signal is invalid as a measurement of that more modest target. Causal validity does not require that *no* provisional investigation occur without a confirmed attribute.

**Rheinberger's distinct explanatory level:** Laboratory fractions are operational and shift with the investigative setup; unsuccessful trials are often necessary for exploration, and epistemic things are historically and materially constituted. This very history has already been interpreted in a published sophisticated epistemology. Treat 'dynamic referents in experimental science' as **prior-art owned**, not new MQR theory.

## D. Strongest actual historical negative and method-stress case

Stephenson (1954–56) intentionally aimed to generate/identify a particular product: tobacco mosaic virus protein in a disrupted-leaf preparation. The 1956 article's publisher abstract directly confirms that cell-free synthesis of virus was not established despite incorporation into other fractions. Rheinberger's history reports that signals in chloroplasts led to new investigative possibilities, then the line was not pursued as the central laboratory mechanism target.

Two different rankings are logically consistent:
- **Pursuit permissibility**, ex ante: testing cross-organism transfer and a clearly identifiable TMV target could be informative under uncertainty.
- **Target confirmation**, ex post: no viral protein was synthesized in the investigated cell-free conditions. Explicit negative evidence.
- **Discovery of alternative phenomena**, ex post: incorporation into leaf fractions/chloroplast systems was not globally absent.
- **Allocation priority**, ex ante: exact time/resource and contemporaneous alternatives not sufficiently evidenced to rank plant work against the established rat-liver program.

We do NOT infer that researchers in 1954 *knew* it would fail in 1956 or that their 1954 pursuit was irrational.
