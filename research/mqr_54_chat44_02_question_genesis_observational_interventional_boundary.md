## 2. A concrete, mathematically checkable gap: observational equivalence does not fix interventional questions
Consider binary U ~ Bernoulli(1/2), measured X and Y, and two deterministic structural causal models:

A (direct cause): X := U; Y := X.
B (common source): X := U; Y := U.

Under passive observation both return exactly P(X=0,Y=0)=P(X=1,Y=1)=1/2, other outcomes zero. Hence no observational sample size distinguishes A from B under this specification.

Under surgical intervention do(X=1):
- A returns P(Y=1 | do(X=1))=1.
- B returns P(Y=1 | do(X=1))=1/2.
Likewise under do(X=0): A returns zero, B returns 1/2.

The distinction is **not a newly invented theorem**; it is a deliberately elementary synthetic illustration of a standard point in structural causal inference (Pearl; [SEP Causal Models](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/causal-models/)). The actual physical implementation of a modular intervention remains an empirical question; no randomization was performed here.

Exact Fraction arithmetic and all combinations are encoded in [executable Python](../language/real/experiments/mqr_question_genesis_causal_counterexample_v54.py). Assertions explicitly test identical observational distributions, both interventional contrasts, and probability normalization. This proof is mathematical, not evidence that either model describes an actual device.

## 3. What follows, and what does not
Let P be a scientific research practice with language L, question set Q, intervention repertoire I, aims G, success criterion S, evidence E: P=(L,Q,I,G,S,E). These are **comparative dimensions**, not a claim that practice ontologically decomposes into independent boxes.

Claim QG1 (supported by example): equality of passive-observation distributions does NOT entail equality of answers to all potential causal intervention questions, even in an identical observational vocabulary.

Claim QG2 (limited): a research transformation can retain measurements but change the experimental *intervention repertoire* and thus the accessible space of discriminating questions. This is an epistemic and practice-level change; it is not the logical creation of an inexpressible proposition. If the intervention operator was in the prior language and executable, the conceptual novelty claim requires independent history of adoption.

Claim QG3 (not established): different possible intelligences using different primitive structures inevitably or uniquely create incomparable sciences. Neither the example nor Kuhn, Friedman, Masterman, Galison, Lakatos, Nersessian, or Blackwell proves this.

A negative control: programs A and B may be observationally identical **and** have identical interventional responses yet differ in preferred ontology, explanatory aims, or research priorities; an invariance or interventional score alone cannot certify identical practices. Conversely different textual questions need not yield non-equivalent prediction or action. Therefore MQR cannot simply use the count of syntactically novel questions.

## 4. Ambitious but falsifiable research program: question-generative reach
For a specified bounded scientific practice P at time t, define the **accessible discriminating query family** D(P,t) as the set of explicitly constructible experimental/mathematical questions q for which (i) an admissible operation/proof is available, (ii) at least two live models/claims differ on a warranted outcome, and (iii) the practice has a stated success rule. This is a proposed operational construct, NOT a general mathematical invariant of every scientific tradition.

One can compare expansions in D along distinct axes:
- *Algebraic*: revised invariants and constraints (Galilei → Lorentz); does not by itself establish historical priority.
- *Proof-theoretic*: new counterexample-controlled quantifiers (pointwise → uniform convergence).
- *Interventional*: observation → do-operator, as above.
- *Practice/historical*: new exemplars, instruments, meanings, and institutional coordination (Masterman, Nersessian, Galison).

Proposed longitudinal study: at dated t0 and t1 identify explicitly sourced live alternatives, available procedures, and documented scientific uses. Blind historical coders should label whether a question was used/recognized before t1. Test whether pre-registered D(P,t0) predicts documented newly discriminating inquiries at t1 better than simply counting renamed predicates. Crucially, an unrestricted t0 language can encode arbitrarily many future questions; without resource/practice constraints this purported metric collapses and has no novel information.

## 5. Prior-art adversarial boundary
- Nancy Nersessian, *Creating Scientific Concepts* (2008) already centrally discusses model-based scientific conceptual innovation. [Publisher](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262515078/creating-scientific-concepts/).
- Blackwell statistical experiment comparison orders informativeness relative to a fixed state/decision structure; see [1979 comparison and information paper](https://doi.org/10.1214/AOS%2F1176344790). A change in actual aims/question sets does not automatically constitute a stronger experiment in the Blackwell order.
- Worrall's 1989 structural realism concerns structure retained across scientific theory change; it does not trivially give a measure of newly enabled questions. [SEP structural realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/structural-realism/).
- Lakatos, Masterman, Poincaré, Friedman and Galison are **predecessors to learn from and competitors**, not names that confer novelty. See MQR43 records 19–23.
