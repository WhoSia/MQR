## 2. Feest 2005—direct source argument

Uljana Feest (2005), 'Operationism in Psychology: What the Debate Is About, What the Debate Should Be About', *Journal of the History of the Behavioral Sciences* **41(2), 131–149**, DOI `10.1002/jhbs.20079`, Research OS file `1JXJyWfHOAVS3Uozgf8IsL8QQ9TDlqi0V`. Complete article text and conclusion examined.

- **pp.132–134** separates positivist **semantic** view (meaning of a term exhausted by observation operations) and epistemic reductionist reading from a **methodological** view: tentative empirical indicators let scientists investigate a presumed object, without assigning analytical truth or exhaustive meaning.
- **pp.135–136**, Stevens: assumptions linking auditory discrimination to perceived tonal volume/density guide a variable-control experimental design. The observational indicator does not constitute the final identity of subjective auditory experience.
- **pp.136–138**, Tolman: posited intervening variables (e.g., demand/hunger) were linked to deprivation and observed behavior, allowing subsequent inquiry; behaviorally accessible 'defining experiments' did not make the existence/meaning of the state self-certifying.
- **pp.142–145**, historical sources: Stevens and Tolman had substantial methodological commitments before the rhetoric of logical positivism entered their work; this is the author's historical interpretation, bounded by her selected cases (Stevens/Tolman not all operationists).
- **pp.143–145**, potential circularity: validation of a theoretical object by the same operations that fixed the initial indicator remains problematic. Feest discusses converging operations (Garner, Hake & Eriksen 1956) and multitrait-multimethod approaches (Campbell & Fiske 1959) as proposed resources but explicitly does NOT establish their full success.
- **pp.145–146**, disagreement is better framed in terms of adequacy of concepts, operations and validation rather than a facile 'operationism versus science' binary.

This essay alone defeats any MQR attempt to claim novelty for 'operational definitions are revisable research tools, not definitions of meaning'.

## 3. Feest 2025 published introduction—direct reading, NOT entire 2025 book

Uljana Feest (2025), *Operationism in Psychology: An Epistemology of Exploration*, University of Chicago Press; author's freely accessible **Introduction: Toward an Epistemology of Exploration in Psychology**, printed pp.1–29, Drive excerpt file `1mHLsGGz88TDiXy9suW-Kizyd2h1eqbNl`.

- **pp.1–7**: implicit-memory dissociations and conceptual uncertainty motivate ‘objects of research’ as epistemically blurry clusters of phenomena. Experimental paradigms require tentative operational definitions which do not exhaust concept meanings.
- **pp.8–16**: research process and results are distinct. Formal universal theory and domain-specific, historically grounded normative inquiry are *not logically opposed*. The 2025 project explicitly uses scientists’ own methodological norms as a foil for dual assessment: the norms themselves versus how actors implement them. This directly preempts another possible MQR novelty claim about evaluating moving standards from historical agent perspectives.
- **pp.18–19**: investigative tools can be useful even where the postulated research object fails to exist or changes. 'Pursuit tool' and 'valid stable referent' are not equivalent.
- **pp.21–22**: replicability concerns must be supplemented by scrutiny of whether data appropriately speak to the putative object and at what conceptual generality.
- **pp.24–29**: chapter plan situates operational analysis, validation, converging operations (with manipulation/measurement/individuation challenges), phenomenon clusters and a relational account of psychological kinds. THESE ARE PROGRAMMATIC SUMMARIES of absent chapters, **not direct verification** of those chapters' arguments.

Crucial stronger prior-art finding: the 2025 introduction directly discusses history-grounded, actor-normative, process-sensitive appraisal. Do **not** propose the mere combination of ex-ante norms, iterative concepts, negative cases and source criticism as novel MQR theory; Feest+Steinle+Chang+Dewey already provides much of this.

## 5. Precise philosophical problems that survive this audit

The plausible remaining MQR research question is **not** simply 'how are concepts formed?' or 'how can a program be rational before adoption?'.

One candidate: Given multiple operationalizations (O_1,O_2) of a target (T), a shared regularity in measured outcomes is often compatible with (H1) one robust latent phenomenon, (H2) two distinct context-dependent kinds, or (H3) a shared measurement artifact. What extra **prospective design discriminates** H1/H2/H3 in a way that Feest’s converging operations, Campbell–Fiske validation and Steinle’s experimental-history account do not already provide? Do not declare this an original contribution until those prior methods fail on an independently specified test.

An experimentally identified signal (D) under operation (O) does not logically imply a stable reference `Ref(T)`; nor does a successful operation justify the strongest framework. The failure of that implication is strong old prior art, not new.

Proposed next R46-K gate: original Garner, Hake & Eriksen (1956) paper, Campbell & Fiske (1959) paper (existing Drive `1rpeKl0VfcGR0PYi4nqEmDNCLFdbKfX03`), Feest (2011) and published 2025 introduction; construct a fixed same-observation underdetermination counterexample with competing intervention predictions, then allow novelty only for actual additional discriminating evidence.

**Novelty verdict:** STRONG PRIOR ART / HOLD. Full 2025 Feest monograph remains missing. Steinle 2016 and Feest 2005 now held, correctly classified.
