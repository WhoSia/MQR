# MQR-5.3 R46-AN — A missing calorimetry proviso in 1783 knowledge transit: case and adversarial audit

## Core source chain (source-specific time and editions)

(1) Antoine-Laurent Lavoisier and Pierre-Simon Laplace, Mémoire sur la chaleur: Academy reading 18 June 1783 (the *separate 1783 print*'s title misdates it to June 28, as Chaldecott p.9 n.38 describes), 56-page Paris 1783 Imprimerie Royale historical imprint per Bibliothèque nationale de France https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30739046p ; 1784 Academy memoir in a *volume nominally for 1780*, pp.355–408. Modern transcription of 1894 collected Laplace book https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_compl%C3%A8tes_de_Laplace/M%C3%A9moire_sur_la_chaleur. In Article I, near the end of apparatus instructions, the authors explicitly warn atmosphere and **experimental ice must not be below zero on their melting-ice thermometric convention**. It details preparation of the ice should it be too cold. **This proviso has been directly checked in the later reproduced French source**, not in the physical 1783 first printing.

## Why this source chain differs from a slogan about trust

Transmission changed actionability: 'use an ice calorimeter' is insufficient without boundary conditions preventing secondary refreezing, which affect signal independence from apparatus thermodynamic history. Source text -> editorial abstract -> reconstructed device -> anomalous observations -> rejection of calibration path. This is a testable **causal candidate**, not a new philosophical theorem; the relevant historical sequence is largely described by Chaldecott (1975) and Lodwig & Smeaton (1974). To argue that *abstract omission itself* caused the failure, need 1783 abstract actual page comparison AND Wedgwood's notebook showing which instruction he followed. It could be a conjunction of missing caveat, independent design changes and other experimental issues.

Illustrative energy balance: Q_hot = m_melt L_f + Q_ice_warming + Q_device + Q_losses + Q_other. If ice began below 0°C, Q_ice_warming>0 before melting at zero; if meltwater refreezes, net collected m_melt no longer measures heat transferred without accounting for refreezing/drainage. This is contemporary thermodynamics used to explain the confound, not a historical calculation Wedgwood necessarily made. No hazard instructions or experimental procedural claims.

## A second provenance failure outside the 1783 abstract

Chaldecott pp.11–12 cites Guyton's 1799 clay analyses and figures falsely attributed to Vauquelin in 1799, published widely in Germany and Britain. The 1810 genuine figures differed; French readers reproducing the wrong composition suffered bad outcomes. This is an even stronger source-transmission failure candidate: **falsely published composition figures** -> independent fabrication failures -> criticism of measuring instrument. Current evidence is historian's account, not original 1799 and 1810 table comparison. Competing causes are regional clays, technique transfer and loss of original pyrometer pieces under Franco-British conflict; avoid mono-causal claim.

## Reproduction & instrument governance (separate 1784 pamphlet)

Science Museum Group accession 1997-349 https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co422422/description-and-use-of-a-thermometer-for-measuring-the-higher-degrees-of-heat catalogs Wedgwood's 1784 London pamphlet/offprint 26pp, **from his 1782 thermometer paper** (not the 1784 silver-bridge paper). Chaldecott 1975 p.11 quotes its pp.22–23: initially Wedgwood expected other people to make gauges from published instructions, then promised to supply standardized pieces because third-party manufacture might damage the method's reputation. In one 1786–1787 ledger forty sets were sold or billed (seven billed to Wedgwood, maybe factory use or gifts). Whether a method is publicly specified and whether it is materially reproducible by independent workshops are separate criteria. This is an empirical precursor to problems Shapin 1994 ch.8 and Porter 1995 discuss, with historical priority already held by Chaldecott.

## Comparison to MQR's main question

**Operationally productive:** 1782 clay shrinkage readings could compare furnace operations. **Epistemically unestablished:** temp-only invariance, nonlinear shrinkage, manufacturing batches, calibration validity, microconditions. **Publication-mediated actionable knowledge:** 1783 Monthly Review conveyed an instrument idea but possibly dropped essential thermal boundary condition; 1799 circulation of mistaken elemental composition provides possible later bad replication. **Collective criticism:** readers may reasonably critique a method even when it was worth experimenting with, but exact record of 1784 Society editorial decision is not recovered.
