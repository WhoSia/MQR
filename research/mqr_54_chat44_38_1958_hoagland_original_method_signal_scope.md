## 1. What the 1958 authors themselves actually documented
The original author paper describes **two different RNA isolation/analysis pathways** with substantially different recovery yields and different analytical roles. It explicitly warns that a change in a solution's chemical state during one extraction is essential to preserving a labeled signal: the isolated RNA may retain little or no radioactivity when a different state is maintained. This is **the authors' own printed method-specific qualification**, not solely a later historian's description.

This is not a general effect of 'more sensitive instruments seeing less': it is a sample-preparation and analyte-survival/partition issue, with a potentially unstable target readout. The mechanism of physical disappearance, loss or altered partitioning of label **is not uniquely established by that textual warning**. The original printed data establish dependence of observed measurement signal on protocol conditions.

Quantitative comparisons of recovery in the paper apply to different measurement procedures and source material, rather than proving an ideal paired randomized protocol contrast for fixed identical specimens and an independent reference. **Do not calculate a meaningful clinical or biochemical effect size** from comparing those stated yields. To respect safety and relevance, this research note intentionally omits procedural instructions, reagents, concentrations and temperatures; it analyzes epistemic comparability.

## 3. A severe causal ambiguity
Several mechanisms could create 'more recovered RNA / less radioactivity': altered fraction composition, label-carrier dissociation, differential recovery of labeled subset, procedural degradation, or readout threshold changes. The presence of an observed signal drop does not by itself uniquely select one. It also does not establish that the 'improved' procedure Blackwell dominates the original target-measurement channel; indeed, if a formerly observable target signal is lost under substitution, its outcome channel may become *less* informative for that target.

The 1958 paper provides a contemporaneous qualification **supporting the fact of method-conditioned signal visibility**, not MQR's speculative full mechanistic account. A legal full-text scan is publicly available, and no new acquisition to Drive is required to continue.
