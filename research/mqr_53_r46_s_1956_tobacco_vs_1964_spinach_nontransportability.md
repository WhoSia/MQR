# MQR-5.3 R46-S — Later contamination evidence as an anti-hindsight stress test

Date 2026-11-05. Source methodology: historic papers must be compared at **identical tissue, fraction, protocol, time and proposed target**, or a contrast is only thematic.

## New contemporaneous source locator

**Stephenson, M. L., K. V. Thimann and P. C. Zamecnik (1956)**, 'Incorporation of C14-amino acids into proteins of leaf disks and cell-free fractions of tobacco leaves', *Archives of Biochemistry and Biophysics* 65(1):194–209, November 1956, DOI `10.1016/0003-9861(56)90187-4`, PMID 13373418. DOI and pages confirmed by PubMed; ScienceDirect's author listing reproduces publisher abstract. Research OS exact-title search on 2026-11-05: **FULL 1956 ORIGINAL NOT HELD**. This is the first priority missing PDF for the new independent negative historical case.

Rheinberger (1997), ch. 'The Activation of Amino Acids, 1954–56', pp.124–127, supplies careful historical interpretation, including author's source on paper publication and Stephenson retrospective interviews. This is not the same evidential grade as direct per-table experimental reconstruction.

The abstract states (a) radioactivity in proteins of plant disks and cell-free preparations, (b) high early rate in microsome fraction, (c) longer continuing chloroplast activity, (d) oxygen/light effects and stated bacterial-exclusion evidence for the 1956 **tobacco** system, and (e) failure to synthesize the *virus protein* in the virus-infected cell-free extract. Do not paraphrase (e) as zero protein synthesis in all plant preparations.

## A new later challenge, not an anachronistic dismissal

**App, Alva A., and A. T. Jagendorf (1964)**, 'C14 Amino Acid Incorporation by Spinach Chloroplast Preparations', *Plant Physiology* 39(5):772–776, DOI `10.1104/pp.39.5.772`, original freely indexed https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC550164/. Its published opening/summary identifies sedimenting contaminating bacteria as an explanation for apparent labeling in **some spinach chloroplast preparations**, and discusses the effect of light and microbial contamination. It is a separate organism/protocol/time, **not** proof that Stephenson's reported 1956 tobacco controls failed. In the 1956 author abstract, bacterial contamination is explicitly addressed as an alternative.

There are two defensible comparisons:
1. Philosophical: an operational label 'chloroplast fraction' is not a guaranteed causal attribution; compare independent contaminant tests, organelle purification and target-specific products across preparations.
2. Historical: the 1964 finding can inform a 2026 retrospective reliability analysis **only**, not a rationality judgement of what scientists knew in 1954 or 1956 unless contemporaneous knowledge and access are independently demonstrated.

### Scope constraints

- 1964 App & Jagendorf paper cannot be counted as verification of Stephenson's 1956 data, because distinct preparations, species and source provenance.
- An apparent discrepancy between (1956) 'not due to bacteria' and (1964) 'bacterial contamination' does not constitute two mutually exclusive judgments of the same prepared material. Nonmatching samples are not a logical contradiction.
- New historical document is a **stress test** of how observations are transported across contexts, already treated by Steinle, Rheinberger, Feest and broader measurement theory. Strong PRIOR ART; MQR NOVELTY HOLD.
- Direct 1956 article and Stephenson dated notebook/dissertation still needed for a fully supported actor-level case.
