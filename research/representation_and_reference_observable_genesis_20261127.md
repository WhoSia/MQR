## Exact computation and discriminating observable
For equal unit scatterers at finite points P, F(q)=sum_x exp(2 pi i q·x), I(q)=|F(q)|^2=sum_{x,y} exp(2 pi i q·(x-y)). Equal ordered difference multisets imply equality for every q, not merely a finite sampling grid. The 15-point pair from Grimm–Baake Eq.1 has identical integer difference multiplicities (52 nonzero vector types), but different multisets of per-point squared-distance signatures, excluding any Euclidean congruence. Executed 256 reciprocal grid samples and 7 checks, all pass. Maximum intensity discrepancy 7.105427357601002e-14 numerical roundoff; exact proof by differences independent of numerical tolerance.
A calibrated coherent reference r changes I to |F+r|^2, including 2 Re(F conjugate(r)). With r=1 and r=i, separately subtract I+1 and divide by 2 to recover Re(F), Im(F). At q=(1/4,0), F_A=-2+i, F_B=-1+2i, both I=5. With r=1, intensities 2 and 4; with r=i, 8 and 10. The 256-grid recovery max error 1.0658141036401503e-14. Zero-frequency negative control: reference cannot distinguish two equal total scatterer counts at q=0. Assumes same phase origin, stable sample, known coherent complex reference; no physical protocol, instrument feasibility test, actual crystallographic phasing reproduction, or proof of all-structure uniqueness. Independent reference changes observable; invertible re-expression of old intensity does not. In ideal complete complex Fourier data, inversion can identify the finite point measure; finite sampling alone does not prove this.
