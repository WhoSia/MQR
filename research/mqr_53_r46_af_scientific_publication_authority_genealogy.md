# MQR-5.3 R46-AF — Scientific publication and the historical making of paper authority

Date: 2026-11-05 KST. STATUS: research proposal; no new empirical historical discovery claimed.

## Problem
How did a research report, manuscript or journal article become accepted as a credential of scientific legitimacy? Distinguish the epistemic warrant for a claim, the credibility of its authors, the procedural acceptance of a paper, registration of priority, the institutional reputation of a learned society, and its later reputation. Publication of a claim does not entail its truth.

## Rival explanatory hypotheses
E: independent expert scrutiny improves the warranted credibility of published claims.
I: editorial acceptance confers an institutional endorsement and manages reputation and cost.
P: the journal serves as public record and priority registration, independent of content truth.
C: academic career professionalization transforms publication into a credential.
R: postwar legitimating rhetoric retroactively projects modern peer review onto early editorial systems.
These can jointly operate; discriminating research must compare roles across sources and periods, not assume mutual exclusivity.

## Research design
Within each period compare (1) manuscript or reading record, (2) printed publication, (3) any editorial committee notes/referee report actually held, (4) documented author revisions, (5) priority claims, (6) reception and disputes. Unknown editorial records are not evidence that no review occurred. Compare only what could have been known at actor time; do not use modern pyrometer calibration failure to dismiss an earlier article's legitimate priority claim.

## Novelty kill
The history of refereeing, the gap between publication and truth, and the later expansion of expert-review authority are well covered by the above literature. Unless the Wedgwood source-to-paper genealogy produces a real case where rival historical-philosophical explanations diverge and MQR adjudicates the difference, the output is an annotated historical case, not a new theory.
