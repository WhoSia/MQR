# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · Record 04 — Question genesis is not the same as more options

**Research date:** 2026-11-21 (KST). **Status:** exact synthetic negative control + scoped historical source audit; no new physics, original biological experiment, or universal philosophical theorem. Version **MQR 5.4 OPEN**.

## I. Research problem inherited from MQR 43 and the Harvest genealogy

MQR44 record03 rejected a scalar count of the questions a practice can formulate: arbitrary linguistic duplication, arbitrary hypothesis menu, and confusion of a proposed action with an executable one compromise interpretation. We now sharpen the objection by separating:
- *formal expressibility* X: syntactically representable queries;
- *technical affordance* A: feasible proof/experiment/action with resource, instrument, and logical conditions;
- *recognized inquiry* Q: a problem that participating scientists actually treat as worth investigating;
- *warranted adjudication* W: evidence/proof that can justify a claim relative to a specified standard.

Neither X⊆A nor A⊆Q is generally an appropriate type-consistent inclusion: the objects and relations differ. An action can become feasible yet unused, and a central question may persist despite lacking an executable experiment. A historical transition should therefore be represented with typed, dated arrows rather than a scalar innovation score.

This is **not** novel as a philosophical taxonomy: Brigandt's epistemic aims, Kitcher's significant questions, Nersessian/Clement's model construction, Rheinberger's experimental systems, and Masterman/Friedman's frameworks are substantial prior art. Our research task is to test whether a concrete comparison exposes a remaining unexplained mechanism.

## II. Exact counterexample to naive 'more questions automatically improves inquiry'

Unknown binary state θ has fair prior. Available query **good** gives the true bit with probability 3/4, independently conditional on θ. Query **decoy** always returns the same label, independent of θ.

For one query followed by a Bayes-optimal classification, expected accuracy is

`A(e)=Σ_y max_θ P(θ)P(y|θ,e)`.

Hence A(good)=3/4 and A(decoy)=1/2.

If the initial menu contains only good, both optimal experiment selection and a fixed salience-based heuristic achieve 3/4. Expanding the menu to {good,decoy} preserves optimal achievable accuracy 3/4 by option inclusion, **but** an illustrative heuristic that selects the higher-scored novel/attention-grabbing option chooses decoy and realizes 1/2. Thus:

1. **Opportunity monotonicity:** for optional actions with unchanged costs/conditions and an optimizing agent, enlarging the action set cannot reduce the optimal value.
2. **Realized-inquiry nonmonotonicity:** an agent with a demonstrably imperfect, fixed selection policy may do worse after a useless option is made available.
3. One must not claim that the extra option *physically causes* scientific decline in general; this conclusion depends on the precisely specified policy/attention budget. A rational optional-action optimizer need not suffer.

Executable standard-library-only exact Fraction witness: [question-menu counterexample](../language/real/experiments/mqr_question_menu_salience_counterexample_v54.py). Its assertions check 3/4 versus 1/2, optimal-menu monotonicity, realized degradation under the declared heuristic, and the simple polyhedral combinatorics below. Arithmetic independently checked in this Chat (3/4, 1/2 and 4). No empirical behavioral data.

### Negative controls
- **Pure renaming:** replacing 'good' with a second label for the *same conditional kernel* changes textual query count without changing attainable evidence; do not credit it as a new experimental affordance.
- **Measurement improvement without novel object:** increasing likelihood precision may improve Bayes accuracy without changing the target question, ontology, or standards.
- **Failed theory-to-experiment bridge:** a novel written `do(X)` operator without an executable modular intervention changes a formal menu but not demonstrated physical capability.

## III. Three historically distinct pathways; original-witness correction

### A. Lakatos: mathematical proof criticism
Previously held [Lakatos, *Proofs and Refutations*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qpRtwZLjZBgio5W6ptQorzu1MY7l970G/view) was read in MQR5.3 R9 (2015 Cambridge reprint of 1976 book). A hollow solid between two nonintersecting cube boundaries has **boundary-surface** cell counts V=16, E=24, F=12, so χ=V−E+F=4. The exterior cube boundary and interior cavity boundary are each topological spheres, each χ=2. This is a counterexample to a broad, underspecified 'all polyhedra have χ=2' claim; it is **not** a statement that the solid three-dimensional body itself has Euler characteristic 4. For a connected sphere-like boundary χ=2, with two sphere boundary components χ=4. Terms such as connectedness, surface genus, holes and what counts as a polyhedron are mathematically load-bearing.

A counterexample may transform an explanandum and the domain of a theorem without a new detector, and its mere retrospective formal expressibility does not erase the epistemic invention. Yet both Lakatos's proof-generated concepts and the topology are established prior art. Note: Lakatos's dramatized dialogue is not a literal stenographic history of Euler.
