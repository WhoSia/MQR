# MQR 5.7 — Unpaired structural bridges and nonunique maxima
2026-11-28 KST · MQR 48 · two synthetic exact search witnesses. No physical experiment, independent replication or mathematical priority claim.

## Search problem
Given complete deterministic local transition tables T_A and T_B with equal finite action-set cardinality, enumerate canonical set partitions p of each state set. A partition is admissible iff p(x)=p(y) implies p(T_i(x))=p(T_i(y)) for every local action i. Each induces a quotient Q. Enumerate quotient-state bijections s and action bijections r, retaining pairs satisfying s(Q_A,i(c))=Q_B,r(i)(s(c)) for every c,i. Search all admissible partitions and all bijections, retain greatest common quotient cardinality. This is exhaustive under the stated finite, deterministic, fully observed table and global action-bijection assumptions: the kernel partition of every surjective deterministic homomorphism is enumerated, and the quotient maps are determined by that partition up to naming. Does not cover partially observed agents, stochastic transitions, action compositions, unknown operation cardinality, experiment costs, or actual autonomous scientific discovery.

## Co-reference obstruction
For any local-table-only procedure F, a world where the tables arise as two views of a common system and a disjoint-world construction with the same two tables yield identical input to F. Therefore no decision rule on those inputs alone can universally decide physical co-reference. A unique structural alignment under an assumed quotient class is not evidence that the two systems share a physical substrate. Correlation, shared calibration and independent source/coupling evidence may narrow candidate world classes; a correlation alone is not a universal identity certificate. Distinguish (1) existence of a bridge, (2) uniqueness of correspondence conditional on quotient type, (3) uniqueness of bridge type, (4) empirical co-reference and (5) discovery from actual native observations. Maximal information by cardinality does not impose a unique structure.
