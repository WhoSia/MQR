## A. Primary historical comparison

Keller, Zamecnik & Loftfield (1954), *The role of microsomes in the incorporation of amino acids into proteins*, *J Histochem Cytochem* 2:378–386, DOI 10.1177/2.5.378. [Full publisher article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2.5.378); [publisher PDF](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2.5.378). The original paper's text and table extraction were read; **visual PDF screenshot verification failed because the provider screenshot request returned a cache miss**. Verify table pixels before treating this as a critically edited historical edition.

In table II, recorded cpm per mg washed protein for supernatant additions 0, 0.1, 0.4 ml and creatine kinase amounts none, 10 gamma, 25 gamma are:

| Supernatant | CK none | CK 10 gamma | CK 25 gamma |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| None | 15 | 29 | 28 |
| 0.1 ml | 31 | 48 | 46 |
| 0.4 ml | 130 | 156 | 172 |

This supports a relative association between the supernatant preparation and labeled-amino-acid incorporation at the tested conditions, beyond what adding creatine kinase alone achieved. It does **not** identify a molecular species or establish net complete protein synthesis. It was not our experiment and must not be reinterpreted as a controlled factorial design with clean biological-effect separation.

Critically, **the 1954 authors themselves expressly limit their terminology to incorporation rather than stronger protein-synthesis language for cell-free observations** (printed p. 383). They discuss competing interpretations, incomplete synthesis, and the unknown additional active constituent of the soluble fraction. Thus the proposed MQR observation-versus-interpretive-claim distinction was already practiced by historical actors, as well as analyzed by Rheinberger. This defeats any claim of originality based merely on discovering that distinction.

[Zamecnik & Keller 1954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13192089/), *Relation between phosphate energy donors and incorporation of labeled amino acids into proteins*, *JBC* 209:337–354: bibliography and references confirmed; original complete tables not collated in this research unit. ## B. More stringent philosophy prior art

**Feest (2025), *Operationism in Psychology: An Epistemology of Exploration*, introduction pp. 1–29**, read from [author-hosted first-chapter PDF](https://www.philos.uni-hannover.de/fileadmin/philos/Publikationen/Feest/Feest_U.__2025_._Operationism_in_Psychology._An_Epistemology_of_Exploration._First_Chapter.pdf). Its critique of experimental inference explicitly separates three challenges: individuation of an object, adequacy of manipulation, and validity of measurement. It insists operational definitions are tentative investigative tools and identifies assumptions that support an inference from local data to a broader scientific target. **Consequently MQR's prior proposed three-level distinction between signal, procedure and object identity is substantially anticipated**, not novel. Introduction fully available; chapters 2–7 were not read.

**Haueis (2023), *Exploratory concept formation and tool development in neuroscience***: read accepted manuscript sections 2–4 from [PhilSci Archive](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/21007/1/Haueis_2022_Exploratory_Concept_Formation_Tool_Development_Neuroscience.pdf), DOI 10.1017/psa.2022.79. Section 4.1 gives operational conditions AC1 (appropriate experimental conditions), AC2 (real-world functional significance), AC3 (sufficient tool precision for reference fixing), plus open-endedness O1 (cross-context applicability) and O2 (adaptability). Partial success and failure are explicit in its 'default mode' and 'default mode network' comparison. Both a formal norm of reference acquisition and a portability criterion are **already provided**. No new MQR checklist should be proposed as if these conditions were missing.

**Beauchemin & Staley (2024)**, *The epistemological significance of exploratory experimentation: A pragmatist model of how practices matter philosophically*, DOI 10.1007/s13194-024-00620-6; [open preprint](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/23945/7/Beauchemin_Staley_EE.pdf) located. Shows experimental exploratory status is aim- and context-dependent; uses LHC examples to answer Schickore's normative critique. This challenges any supposed novelty based on giving exploratory experiments a warrant structure. The full preprint still deserves line-by-line reading.

**Beauchemin & Staley (2025, 2026)**, [2025 Synthese preprint](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/26080/1/Beauchemin_Staley2025.pdf) and [2026 Measurement paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0263224126000394): measurement uncertainty, sensitivity, usefulness and epistemic transport of outcomes are explicit. General evidence-lineage transport cannot be claimed as original.
