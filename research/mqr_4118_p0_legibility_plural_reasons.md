## P0 principal research question
What makes an event, objection or scientific practice *legible as evidence* within an institution, and who is allowed to contest the terms of legibility? A disagreement over conceptual vocabulary is **not yet incommensurability**. Test failed mutual translation by attempting shared falsifiers, common consequences and cross-encoding loss audits before making that label.

## Five distinct judgment authorities
Formal correctness (deductive/measurement transformation), empirical grounding (actual source/custody), historical interpretation (what actors did and understood), conceptual adequacy (which questions remain expressible), and normative legitimacy (who may set/revise admission rules). These do not substitute for each other, and no numerical PASS can by itself dismiss a normative objection.

## Non-equivalence caution
One encoding may fail to recover a claim because it never stored the right information. This proves claim-relative non-identifiability from that encoding (conditional on a specified predicate), but does not entail Kuhnian incommensurability of scientific communities. Translation and historical contact may be partial, contested or revisable.
