Date: 2026-10-17. ACTIVE 4.118, not 4.119.

## Source status and historical case

The phlogiston–oxygen controversies around eighteenth-century chemical theory provide an external case in which material classification, explanations of calcination, and rival questions were disputed. The secondary historical study Carleton Perrin, "Early Opposition to the Phlogiston Theory: Two Anonymous Attacks" (British Journal for the History of Science; Cambridge Core) describes metal mass gain upon calcination as requiring reconciliation with alleged phlogiston loss. A scholarly study "Theory comparison and choice in chemistry, 1766–1791" (Foundations of Chemistry) describes how theories of gases were compared and historical counterarguments were posed. Lucía Lewowicz (2011), "Phlogiston, Lavoisier and the purloined referent", explicitly offers a contestable reinterpretation of phlogiston reference. These are secondary scholarship, and the original eighteenth-century source pages, complete translations, and their exact passage-level alignment HAVE NOT been collated in this stage.

## P2 case-level cross-encoding loss ledger

**Common challenge:** An observed material transformation and a mass change can be reported in public experiments. What inference from those observations is legitimate?

**L — inferential reconstruction:** Put mass observations, experimental conditions, rival balance assumptions, and theory-specific predicted transitions into explicit premises. The need for a bridge premise is visible: observing a mass change does not by itself determine what the named substance or explanatory entity denotes. Avoid importing modern chemical conclusions as premises while describing historical reasoning.

**P — historical practice/legibility:** Record which experiments were replicated, which gases or materials were available as experimental categories, the methods of weighing and isolation, who answered whom in print, and the public standards of acceptable explanations. Such descriptions require period-specific texts, not imagined anecdotes.

**Candidate divergence:** A single simplified diagram with one arrow labelled phlogiston → oxygen suppresses the dispute about *what the arrow relates* (entities, theoretical roles, names, experimental practices). A practice/history description is richer regarding contestation but cannot independently establish all calculations or experimental accuracy.

**Current result:** SOURCE-LIMITED CANDIDATE, NOT CERTIFIED INCOMMENSURABILITY. Shared tests and translations have not been exhaustively audited.
