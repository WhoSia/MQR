# MQR R44 — Before the question: macro–DFS–macro review and novelty kill
**2026-11-05 | Research status:** targeted primary-text analysis, adversarial philosophical comparison, no new general theory.

## 1. Recovered macro question
MQR asks how the conditions for identifying *what counts as a significant scientific problem, legitimate evidence and valid criticism* can themselves evolve. R43's provisional thesis was whether an exploratory practice deserves provisional standing even before its goals and concepts are expressible within prevailing public standards. The following sources threaten the originality of that question.

## 2. DFS I: Longino and Lari on criticisms of a goal itself
[Longino (2002), *The Fate of Knowledge*, ch.6](https://drive.google.com/file/d/14AVrxujqX41GbDmiS4dlA5cKwQVdlFdu/view) was directly revisited. Her conditions of transformative criticism include public venues, uptake, public standards and tempered equality of intellectual authority. A relevant criticism draws on some shared terms, principles or aims; the community must pay real attention but is not required to accept every criticism. Longino's theory does **not** require equal funding, nor does it hold the community's goals permanently beyond criticism.

**New strong prior art:** [Teemu Lari (2024), *What counts as relevant criticism? Longino’s critical contextual empiricism and the feminist criticism of mainstream economics*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1w_d53dSeovNArYQ69kna_a1NjpwMKCHO/view), *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science* 104:88–97, DOI 10.1016/j.shpsa.2024.02.005. Full publisher text was read in sections 1–4 and 6. Lari locates a tension between OGC (openness of goals to criticism) and GRC (criticism needs relevance to the community's existing goals). He distinguishes **first-level criticism** of methods relative to goals from **second-level criticism** that challenges the desirability of those goals. Existing goals cannot simply veto second-level criticisms as irrelevant; goals of science more general than the specific community's goals can make those criticisms relevant. Example: feminist criticism of the goals of mainstream economics.

**Kill:** An MQR thesis of 'the criteria of scientific relevance must themselves be criticizable' or even 'different standards should apply to methodological versus goal-changing criticism' is directly anticipated by Lari. No novelty claim survives on that basis alone.

## 3. DFS II: the problem's formation is already Dewey, Steinle and Feest
**Dewey (1938), *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*** distinguishes an initially indeterminate situation from the constituted/problematic situation and from the later tested resolution. Publisher bibliographic check and public selected excerpts were consulted; the complete 1938 monograph is NOT present in Drive's verified inventory and was NOT read end to end. Thus the generic claim that inquiry can be rationally initiated before a determinate problem exists is not unique to MQR.

[Steinle (1997), *Entering New Fields*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rl444ab9HV-Q285EwbPq1QBR2z5g3JcR/view) and [Steinle (2002), *Challenging Established Concepts*](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Ux1PayHiIr8shB0BOLqTBRvtC2rk04Zl/view) already reconstruct exploratory work that generates rather than merely tests a descriptive framework. **2002 PDF is still an image-scan/text and byte-matching HOLD**, although a public journal copy has been examined. [Feest (2010), concepts as tools](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1LePHrGcp10C4Lr5135YsMKzMc5A64a9A/view) likewise already licenses provisional concepts for inquiry before stable referents are secured.

**Kill:** 'exploration precedes explicit problem formulation' is not a new MQR general philosophical principle.

## 4. DFS III: preserving alternatives under finite resources is already Chang
[Hasok Chang (2012), *Is Water H2O?*, ch.5 §§5.1.5 and 5.2](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ZZPVnMOCco8J2ORdtJxs5fC27kQ9sE15/view), directly revisited. The *Can We Afford It All?* section explicitly distinguishes 'cannot afford all programs' from 'can only afford one', and discusses modest low-cost maintenance of alternatives, plural benefits from mutual toleration and fruitful interaction. [Chang (2004), *Inventing Temperature* ch.6](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RwuRfY9Ifz6iEAUZZm57z4EzHX8CP0CV/view) already treats historical recreation and complementary science. This forecloses an MQR 'preserve unfashionable alternatives but don't fund everything' claim.

## 5. Hard analytical test: three situations with different criteria
Proposed distinction for *testing prior art*, not an established original taxonomy:

- **A — Indeterminate investigation**: available materials or observations do not yet support a precise question. Rationality may require provisional manipulations whose results change the candidate concepts; compare Dewey–Steinle–Feest.
- **B — Articulated first-level challenge**: competing methods disagree about an accepted cognitive goal; compare Longino and Lari's first-level standard.
- **C — Articulated second-level challenge**: dissent challenges which cognitive goals a discipline should pursue; compare Longino, Lari and Chang.

A and C are **not equivalent**: an unformed problem may have no proposition to criticize; goal criticism is already propositional and contestable. Nor is the shift A→B→C necessarily chronological: practitioners may revisit A, challenge C before resolving B, or change goals and tools together.

### Resource counterexample
Suppose a team can allocate effort either to an exploratory method that cannot yet articulate a promising question or to a well-defined research program with strong evidence and urgent significance. The mere future *possibility* that exploration creates new concepts cannot justify unlimited spending on the former. Conversely, existing scientific goals alone cannot dismiss it, as those goals might be historically contingent. A rational choice requires situated cost, opportunity cost, expected learning and reversibility judgments. **This is a constructed thought experiment, not an empirical case or an original decision theory.** Chang and Kitcher already have accounts of finite resources, significant questions and alternatives.

### Failure conditions for a new MQR framework
1. If Dewey already explains A and Lari explains C, why would their combination miss anything?
2. Can an actually historical case warrant an **additional, discriminating norm** that both fail to capture, rather than renaming them?
3. Can it explain a counterexample where experimental freedom grows yet the resulting knowledge becomes worse?
4. Does the proposed norm become a new, self-sealing 'meta-paradigm', violating MQR's earlier self-criticism?
