# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · R42 — Scientific access is a partial order on question–method pairs, not a single rising score

**2026-11-22 KST. Director's adversarial synthesis.** Source-inspected Blackwell 1951, Hoagland 1958 published primary text, and held 1954/1956 and Fry 2022 originals; mathematical codes R39/R41 locally PASS. MQR 5.4 OPEN; no premature theoretical novelty or journal submission claim.

## 1. Corrected thesis
A scientifically meaningful claim 'technology T2 improves T1' must name a measurable target, allowed actions, preparation regime, instrument costs, transmission of raw signals, and governing inference/decision criterion. If T2 is literally more informative than T1 in Blackwell's sense, a fixed Bayesian decision task with optimally chosen policies cannot have worse maximum value. But improvements in one **technical objective** (mass recovered, setup convenience, nominal precision, reward of the researcher, or current-fit metrics) do not establish this dominance for a distinct **scientific question**.

The 1950s soluble RNA case is a published author-source witness of this distinction. The underlying practical problem involves sample treatment, target label signal, recoveries and possible hidden confounds. It is not a counterexample to Blackwell's theorem.

## 2. Two mathematical witnesses and exactly what each proves
**R39:** three binary-state signals (perfect, 3/4-symmetric and totally uninformative) form a garbling chain. For fair binary state and classification goal V*=1 ≥3/4≥1/2. Arbitrary higher declared yield metric attached to blind replacement is compatible with its lower target decision value; it is **not the same information-ordering metric**. Verified in exact fractions, all 101 sampled priors on a rational grid; proof of full monotonicity does not depend on that grid.

**R41:** four equally likely latent worlds (X,Y), one sensor reveals X and another reveals Y, neither can simulate the other's conditional observation for all states. For each relevant binary target, prediction success is either 1 or 1/2 in reversed order. The channels are Blackwell **incomparable**, not one absolutely superior. These two exact models are standard information-theory counterexamples, not parameter estimates or novel math.

**Important correction to rhetoric:** In an ideal Bayesian decision setting, simply supplying more raw observations while retaining the old ones cannot worsen optimal value for an unchanged target. A real laboratory may still suffer costs, sample destruction, calibration shifts, altered specimens, an inaccessible old method, cognitive policy error, methodological selection, or a changing question set. All should be modeled separately rather than smuggled into the word 'more information'.

## 3. Original 1954/56 and 1958 research plus Fry 2022: distinct creation routes
**Original Zamecnik/Keller 1954** direct source warns a labeled incorporation signal does not automatically prove chemical identity of protein synthesis. **Original Hoagland/Keller/Zamecnik 1956** explicitly posed a local amino acid activation hypothesis, so a 'theory-free exploration' label can at most mean no direct reliance on **Crick's specific abstract adaptor hypothesis**. **Original 1958 Hoagland et al.** shows preparation-dependent radioactive signal in a printed methodological qualification. **Fry (2022)** argues Crick's 1955 abstract adaptor idea predicted a class of molecule but did not strongly guide the biochemists' independent discovery; the actual causal extent of Crick's influence requires correspondence and manuscript reception evidence.

Three independent dimensions of scientific newness:
  - **predictive conceptual possibility**: an entity or intermediary imagined before experimental isolation (Crick).
  - **material operability**: the preparation, manipulation and measurement repertoire making a candidate tractable (Zamecnik/Hoagland).
  - **authority to infer ontology**: independent controls and successful exclusion of alternative mechanisms in source-era science.

These can be dissociated chronologically, causally, and socially. An 'experimental system' need not operate without hypotheses; a theory may be correct without being the historically causal discovery tool.

## 4. Relation to MQR's root
Scientific generation is not simply rising mutual information on a fixed state variable; the available questions, scientific *targets* and experiment access can change with new conceptual and material constructions. Yet a fixed finite formalization necessarily presupposes the later analyst already lists candidate variables. This is the central unsolved problem: identifying historical conceptual expansions without projecting modern states or intended applications backward into actors' beliefs.

MQR can begin with an empirical **partial comparison graph**, not a scalar score:
- nodes = (time-stamped target distinction, apparatus/procedure, possible readouts, valid domain, alternative ontology, sample provenance);
- edges = documented method transfer, translated observation, measurable dominance/garbling when applicable, loss of a necessary signal, category creation, retrospective reinterpretation.
- unknown edges remain UNKNOWN rather than falsely classified as success or incommensurability.

## 5. Falsifiable acceptance test for a paper
One strong paper should furnish at least two completely independently sourced historical method-transition events and one **negative example** where technology improves yet the relevant epistemic access remains intact. For each:
1. contemporaneous before-state capability and declared scientific target documented;
2. actual modification date, sample comparability and raw-readout differences established;
3. competing accounts (garbling, incomparable outputs, method bias, changed decision goals, unavoidable resource costs, new object) listed;
4. quantitative/qualitative signatures that distinguish accounts determined **before** reading later theory reports;
5. observable downstream impact of capability transfer: a newly asked question, a method lost, a changed construct, or no change;
6. source audit separating early publication, archival manuscript, historian's narrative and modern formalization.

**Negative controls:** 1950s RNA from historian vs author publication is good material, but not yet an independently recreated paired controlled experiment; historical 1958 text cannot prove the precise acid-neutral biochemical reaction without original notebooks and alternative mechanism tests. Faraday Paris replication and 1980s implicit-memory question evolution are meaningful comparison candidates, with their own nonidentity of apparatus/target issues.

## 6. Which novelty claims are defeated here?
- 'More information always reduces knowledge' — rejected by classical Blackwell dominance under fixed task.
- 'Technology improvement always creates a better scientific question' — rejected by objective mismatch and incongruent channels.
- 'Exploratory experimental practice is theoryless' — rejected as an unqualified description by 1956 original authors' hypotheses.
- 'Novel mechanisms must be conceived first by the discoverer' — rejected as a universal prerequisite by Fry's separate theory/experiment history.
- 'MQR has discovered question materialization itself' — rejected: Rheinberger (1997), Feest (2010) and Steinle are major prior art.

**Research opportunity still meaningful:** verifiable, source-critical rules for comparing *changing* question/experiment/translation domains and preserving evidence authority without introducing future concepts into past experimenters' language. This is a coherent integrated research program, **not yet an irreducible new physical or epistemic law**.
