## Constructed mathematical worked example A — one vs two wavelength spectroscopy
**Constructed example, not actual MQR 2026 measurement.** Assuming dilute Beer–Lambert behavior, known path length l, known absorptivity, no interactions:
A(lambda)=l(epsilon_1(lambda)c1 + epsilon_2(lambda)c2).
At one wavelength, rank of the 1×2 matrix <=1; absent additional independent constraint, the two concentrations are not separately identifiable.
At two wavelengths, E=[ [e11,e12], [e21,e22] ]. If det(E)!=0, point identification holds *within the ideal linear calibration model*. With near-zero det(E), uncertainty is amplified; algebraic uniqueness is not robust empirical identification. Unknown scattering/baseline b(lambda), stray light and chemical species equilibria create alternative extended models; adding a wavelength need not identify parameters when the nuisance dimension also grows.
This bridges earliest observation quotient and later rival-generation/measurement-model audits.

## Constructed mathematical worked example B — micro to macro
For illustrative micro populations of N absorbers each with state s_i in {0,1}, define macro fraction c = N^{-1} sum_i s_i (dimensionless; physical concentration requires density/volume conversion), mapping g:{0,1}^N -> {0,1/N,...,1}. Different configurations can map to same c: identity of macro summary != microstate identity. Under an ideal homogeneous linear response A=k*c, all configurations with same c yield the same A. But spatial clustering or scatter, nonuniform illumination, chemical interactions, and path-dependent kinetics can make A depend on configuration at fixed c, breaking the homogeneity bridge. Such observational splitting is a positive probe of scope, not proof that macro variables are unreal. One detector-derived reading used in both descriptions is not two independent experimental witnesses.
In practice, microstate specification depends on physical context; this is a purely constructed bridge illustration, not a validated absorption microphysics model.

## Constructed mathematical worked example C — local to global
Three binary propositions x,y,z with C12:x=y, C23:y=z, C13:z!=x are individually and pairwise jointly satisfiable, but all three cannot hold simultaneously. This is a standard finite CSP/compatibility obstruction, not a discovery. The scientific issue is identifying whether all three equations really refer to the same variables/regimes, and whether the bridges have defensible tolerances. If not, contradiction diagnoses bridge/translation/scope assumptions, not automatically physical impossibility.
Three spectroscopy calibration schemes may report pairwise compatibility on *different sample aliquots/standards* without forming a single global witness; direct analogy requires explicit sample identity, covariance and cross-bridge provenance.

## Constructed mathematical worked example D — representation invariance
Choose optical transmittance T=I/I0 in (0,1], and A=-log10(T). T and A are invertibly related (for T>0): using either display does not produce independent world contact. Algebraic inequality T1<T2 iff A1>A2, so ordering reverses under this transformation. Absorbance differences correspond to log ratios, not raw intensity differences. Calibration and noise geometry likewise transform: equal increments in T generally are not equal increments in A. Thus a 'progress' or uncertainty metric built on raw coordinate distances is not intrinsically invariant merely because the measurements are bijectively recoded. When zero or saturation occurs, invertibility at measurement support can also fail. Formal invertibility alone does not validate the physical Beer–Lambert bridge.
