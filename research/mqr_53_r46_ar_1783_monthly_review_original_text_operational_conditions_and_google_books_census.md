## Direct eighteenth-century source comparison (primary)

`The Monthly Review; or, Literary Journal`, vol.69, 1783, pp.568–579, `Abstract of a Memoir upon Heat, by Messrs. Lavoisier and De la Place`. Cover, printed pagination and table of contents explicitly match title. Abstract p.570: ideal hollow globe of ice **of the temperature of melting ice**, placed in warmer atmosphere. Apparatus p.572: middle cavity **filled with pounded ice, at temperature of melting point**; outside cavity **likewise with pounded ice at melting point**. That is THREE explicit or repeated statements of near-freezing ice as thermodynamic prerequisite. Prior MQR blanket statement '1783 English abstract omitted ice-temperature condition' is FALSE on the primary source, and must be withdrawn.

The same source *does omit* the French primary memoir's more specific preparatory safeguard if ice had already cooled below melting point: physical preconditioning, crushing thin pieces and exposure until temperature equalizes; there is no explicit warning what to do with sub-zero ice, and p.572 just says additional procedural particulars occur in original. We compare against original French text known through later 1894 Laplace collected-works transcription at https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Œuvres_complètes_de_Laplace/Mémoire_sur_la_chaleur ; the physical 1783 French 56p first print itself not directly examined. Thus distinguish `theoretical validity precondition communicated` from `implementation method to guarantee it omitted`. A secondary historian can be right about missing *detailed passage* even when too-literal shorthand obscures a precondition present in summary.

## Editorial double-filtering is explicit

Printed Monthly Review p.568 editorial footnote reports the editors received a **correspondent-provided abridgment** of the unpublished-to-Academy paper and could only insert **part** of that already abbreviated text due to page space. Hence transmission path is **French technical source -> unknown correspondent's abstract -> selected excerpts in review -> reader**. This does not locate who omitted preparation details because original correspondent's abridgment unavailable. Crucial context: the review p.572 explicitly states the source holds additional instructions about conducting the experiments, giving a reader a signal of incompleteness. Editorial significance vs reproducibility is not the same issue.

## Contrast with historical Wedgwood's modified instrument

Source text p.570/572 could have warned an attentive replicator of wrong initial condition.
