## Specific methodological inference

A transient observation on one device only becomes a reliable counterexample to a universal *steady-current-only* framing when the prior framing actually asserts that framing as a prediction, and the experiment excludes alternative instrument causes. We can safely say Faraday's material setup **makes a temporally indexed scientific question answerable** and that the outcome was historically unexpected or investigated under rival explanatory pictures. We cannot safely say *all historical physicists* thought constant current would yield constant induced current. An initial current-circuit establishment or interruption generates temporary flux variation in modern physics; the *historical source does not contain a continuous digitized dΦ/dt record*. The modern equation is a retrospective clarification of the physical mechanism, not the literal 1831 measurement protocol.

## The falsifiable genealogy test

A defensible MQR research record should contain: **pre-existing question/counterpart**, **source-date experiment**, **apparatus & controlled varied parameter**, **positive and negative observation**, **post-experiment distinction introduced by actors**, **independent corroboration**, **documented community uptake**, and **historiographical rival**. A single modern museum description can support apparatus date and material identity, not all eight columns. Steinle's book may fill some interpretive aspects but does not become independent confirmation of the physical object's later curator record just because it is a different file.

The current record supplies some but not all fields. The **original 1831 laboratory diary facsimile**, exact previous unsuccessful notebooks and independent early replication records remain relevant source gaps. They were not in the original official 17 missing groups; do not change its denominator automatically.
