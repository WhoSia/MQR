## B. Seven frontiers, without one compulsory giant formalism

1. **UV–Vis and computation:** What can be reduced to a digital output pipeline, and which scientific distinctions depend on the transduction and experiment design rather than the last ADC stage?
2. **Possible alien sciences:** Can sciences with different primitives, observation procedures, time standards and interventions find *operational* bridges without assuming a pretranslated common ontology?
3. **Paradigm of paradigms:** What generates not just theories but the rules for what constitutes a problem, observation, explanation and a legitimate experiment?
4. **Concept creation:** How can a scientist first formulate a question or predicate not expressible in the previous vocabulary?
5. **Why mathematics in engineering:** Evaluate competing mechanisms of selection rather than arguing mathematics is 'simply' intrinsic to reality or 'simply' social convention.
6. **Realism and successful contact:** What can persistence under independent interventions tell us, without claiming that the resulting representation is the final ontology?
7. **Unconceived inquiry:** Distinguish 'difficult to answer' from 'not yet expressible' and examine the material/social construction of new question spaces.

A successful DFS branch must feed a conclusion or a new challenge **back to one of these**.

## D. New candidate synthesis, not novelty claim

`concepts <-> tools <-> experiment design <-> observed distinctions <-> new problem vocabulary` is an *open exploratory loop*, not a one-directional sensor-to-data-to-proposition chain. The model from the companion MQR41 note proves only that specified interventions refine finite observational partitions. Haueis and Nersessian motivate a deeper question: **How are the vocabulary and protocol-generating operations themselves discovered?** Under a fixed experimental language, no finite-output theory can guarantee it has exhausted procedures not yet conceived. This is a scope/underidentification caution rather than an original impossibility theorem.

The 2026-02-05 HWPX genesis of structure-generating explanations meets this loop at concept creation. Historically known predecessors include Kuhn on lexicon learning, Nersessian on creative model construction, Haueis on tool–concept cycles; no 'first discovery' claimed.

## E. Computed toy cross-science dictionary witness (separate from historical claim)

Suppose agent A has four distinct local states and agent B has four distinct local states; each has an internally complete binary-pair experimental signature. There are `4! = 24` possible bijections between their **local labels** if no matched events have been established. If a common experimental event can independently and unequivocally certify a particular A-label/B-label pairing, successive *distinct* matched pairs reduce remaining bijections as:

`24 -> 6 -> 2 -> 1 -> 1` after 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 distinct matched states.

Python exhaustive enumeration of all 24 bijections on 2026-11-10 reproduced these counts. This is a combinatorial positive control **assuming an already-shared event correspondence**; therefore it is explicitly **NOT** a construction of communication from absolutely no common anchor. Removing the shared-event oracle creates the real negative-control problem (multiple admissible dictionaries). Note that full local state discrimination is a stronger assumption than the initial x-only sensor.
