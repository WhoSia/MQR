# MQR-4.121 P1B — Zong 2007/2010 and Sethna et al. 1993: Calibration Inversion, Uncertainty and State Memory

2026-10-18. Cross-source close-reading notes, separated by experiment type. All three original PDFs found in user Research OS/10_PAPERS and individually renamed without changing original Drive IDs. Not a claim of new empirical data.

## A. Zong, Brown, Lykke & Ohno (2007), CIE Session Beijing
Source 1kCcVlmzuiBZrWCtoAo2Q-VnEVebuj-7b; NIST official publication record https://www.nist.gov/publications/correction-stray-light-spectroradiometers-and-imaging-instruments . Original paper sections 1–4 and figures 1–4 read from available text.
A1 — Spectral and spatial stray light are distinct. The method uses empirical line-spread functions across wavelength for a spectral instrument and point-spread functions for an imaging instrument. Interpolated stray distributions generate a correction operator. Measuring a spectrometer under narrow line sources gives information not obtainable from an ordinary broadband sample. Calibration source spectrum versus unknown source spectrum mismatch is an important reason uncorrected stray-light bias transfers badly.
A2 — In the calibrated linear-response approximation, the published operator is Y_corrected = C_spec Y_measured. The imaging analogue replaces spectral with spatial calibration matrix. Correction is performed **on raw instrument signals**. The paper reports error reductions on the order of one to two powers of ten on examined hardware and uses LED/filtered sources and a black spot to probe near-zero spectral/spatial regions. Scope is the tested optical instruments; a universal error reduction for all device states cannot be inferred.
A3 — The matrix is stable only insofar as dispersion/scattering/optics stay comparable to calibration. Interpolating line-spread functions leaves uncertainty. Relevant figure-specific numerical claims require visual verification in this extracted PDF.
Margin: correct before computing absorbance, because wavelength mixing and taking the logarithm generally fail to commute. Do not treat matrix C_spec as an operator on already logged absorbance values without derivation and justified calibration.

## B. Zong (2010), CIE uncertainty audit
Source 1ZdD7HYmmyp24-AoEuuGj7Ov2DILWvwGr; official NIST record https://www.nist.gov/publications/analysis-uncertainties-stray-light-correction . Full text abstract, methods, error discussion and small comparison table surveyed.
B1 — Stray-light correction is not certainty creation. Uncertainty arises from SLSF measurement, response dynamic-range limits, narrow-source calibration quality, illumination geometry, interpolation between tested wavelengths, and instrument response drift.
B2 — The paper describes electronic bracketing as a technique to improve characterization dynamic range and compares four colored LEDs using the corrected array instrument and a double-grating scanning instrument. The printed reported chromaticity-coordinate differences are small within reported uncertainty; this is not physical independence from all calibration chains or proof of exact zero residual stray light.
B3 — Corrected-signal covariance must propagate instrument/source uncertainties. If Z=C Y, then with fixed known C, Cov(Z)=C Cov(Y) C^T. If C itself is estimated, add terms from its uncertainty and possible Y–C dependence. Uncertainty can be amplified by an ill-conditioned inverse or error in measured spread functions.
Margin: source-independent 'correction passed' is not a theorem of signal fidelity. Uncertainty must remain a recipient of the transformation rather than disappearing when an electronic matrix is applied.

## C. Sethna, Dahmen, Kartha, Krumhansl, Roberts & Shore (1993), Physical Review Letters 70, 3347–3350
Source 17ma_HtSdboFGJxVK8Dv-gd4Iohj8bb_D; DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.3347. Cornell/publisher publication information independently checked; original 4-page journal text has substantial OCR damage in the first page's author, formula and figures. Study concerns athermal slowly driven zero-temperature random-field Ising dynamics.
C1 — Context is a quenched disordered spin system with driven metastable dynamics under an external field. The authors analyze hysteresis, collective avalanches, disorder-related transition and **return-point memory**. This is a conditional dynamical physical model, not a blanket assertion that all materials with hysteresis have the same return memory.
C2 — A microscopic state is a configuration of individual spins, not just scalar magnetization. Two configurations can have the same total magnetization but differ in which spins are flipped, and thus differ under the next field history. A scalar observable alone need not be a sufficient dynamical state.
C3 — The paper develops a partial-order/no-passing argument under monotone driving and suitable ferromagnetic interactions, with a slow-drive (adiabatic) assumption; these particular rules can explain nested-loop return-point memory. This is a **positive law of limited scope** and does not refute the existence of a microscopic Markov state. On the contrary, microstate dynamics can be well defined while its coarse projection has memory.
C4 — One must distinguish a returned scalar magnetization, a returned full microscopic spin configuration under the special return-point conditions, and a general reset of all material state variables. The paper supplies special circumstances where return-point memory is real, not general equivalence of every matched observation.
Margin: MQR 1.15's useful philosophical correction is not 'history is a new fundamental substance'; it is 'the selected present state may omit variables needed for prediction'. Sethna offers a mechanism-rich, assumption-bounded case where this can be analysed with testable partial-order structure.

## D. New mathematical bridge (analyst formulation, not attributed to authors)
Two wavelength transmissions T1,T2>0 and normalized instrument weights w1+w2=1: A_band = -log10(w1 T1+w2 T2), whereas the average of component absorbances is w1 A1+w2 A2. These differ unless T1=T2 (or a trivial weight): log and spectral integration are not interchangeable. Hence even a perfectly calibrated logarithm cannot undo wavelength mixing. A spectral response calibration may be required **before** logarithm/chemical inversion.
For hysteresis define state x_t, intervention h_t and observation y_t=q(x_t). 'y_t suffices for prediction under policy class H' requires P(y_{t+1}|x_t,h_t)=P(y_{t+1}|y_t,h_t) across allowed hidden microstates consistent with y_t. This is a conditional Markov/lumpability criterion, not a global identity criterion. A pair with the same y_t but different next observed response under identical future field schedule is a concrete failure witness.
Scientific contrast: Griffiths & Potts show low measured spectral affine rank need not fix chemical species count; Zong shows a correctable mixed signal need not equal the original optical state; Sethna shows one scalar present reading need not determine future state under intervention. These three are genuinely different mathematical defects and cannot be replaced by one generic 'observation insufficiency' slogan.

## E. Experiment discrimination plan (analysis only, no new measurements)
Fix one chemically equilibrated solution and vary calibrated optical bandwidth while recording the actual wavelength response; separately vary measured narrow-line source calibration to characterize stray-light matrix; independently vary pH/temperature and obtain chemistry-specific evidence. Analyze interactions, not merely curvature. For state/memory, compare two intervention sequences that return to the same current scalar observable but yield different responses to a predeclared common future probe. No policy requiring intrinsically dangerous experimental activities; ordinary laboratory safety and qualified supervision apply.
