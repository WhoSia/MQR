## Main claim

**Formal distinguishability does not automatically entail experimentally licensed discrimination.** For deterministic bounded-error observations, robust separation requires that admissible nuisance/calibration/readout error sets do not erase the contrast. Independence and uncertainty bounds of calibration are evidential inputs, not conclusions that can be generated by the algebra itself.

## Verified bounded findings

1. **P0 paired scalar protocol.** With rival signal δ=3/10 and shared bias b, a paired intervention removes a stable common b. Under allowed bias drift η and per-readout absolute error ε, the scalar contrast prediction intervals are disjoint when δ>2η+4ε; at η=1/20, ε=1/100 the gap is 4/25. At η=1/5 the sets overlap. 2. **P1 multichannel nuisance projection.** Three-channel nuisance directions (1,1,1) and (0,1,2) admit witness w=(1,-2,1), which annihilates both and keeps signal g=(3/10,0,3/10), w·g=3/5. Bounded coordinate errors |e_i|≤1/20 leave gap 1/5. A freely ranging nuisance aligned with signal g can erase discrimination completely; restricting the signal-aligned nuisance independently to |t|≤1/10 leaves a gap 2/25.
