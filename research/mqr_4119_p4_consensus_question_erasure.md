# MQR-4.119 P4 — When Consensus Erases the Question

2026-10-17. 4.119 ACTIVE. P3 reviewed; philosophical countermodels, not observed reviewer decisions.

## The problem
Majority agreement can be meaningful evidence of inter-rater concordance, but it cannot independently prove that the case, admissibility regime or evaluation ontology is justified. More reviewers can agree on a question whose formulation precludes the relevant objection.

## Three constructed adversarial cases
**C1, shared blind spot:** three judges label a UV–Vis response wrong because it refuses to issue a concentration. A fourth identifies unresolved standard custody. If custody is needed for the claim, numerical refusal might be warranted; majority does not discharge the custody obligation.
**C2, genuine empirical uncertainty:** reviewers disagree whether an extra spectral band is analyte overlap or baseline drift. This is a contest about missing discriminating measurement; preserve as evidence-needed, not a normative revolution.
**C3, contest over the court:** reviewers accept only existing SOP-listed questions; a challenger questions who authorized the SOP and what records it excludes. This concerns authority and admissibility, not merely uncertainty about a signal.

## Non-substitution rule
Agreement about a response's conformity to a rubric is a statement relative to that rubric. Agreement about whether the rubric is scientifically appropriate needs further reasons. The latter still does not by itself settle normative legitimacy of who can set a rubric.

## Reciprocal limits
An outsider's objection does not become correct merely because it is dissent. Some objections fail on validated analytic grounds. Some apparent conceptual conflicts disappear after an additional measurement. Keep disagreements open to rebuttal, with specific evidentiary duties.
