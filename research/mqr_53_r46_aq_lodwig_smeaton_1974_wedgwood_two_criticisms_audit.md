## Lodwig and Smeaton, read sections pp.1–18

Chapter on first and second objections (~printed pp.5–7) explicitly bifurcates: FIRST, Wedgwood's concern that crushed ice holds meltwater by capillarity might have been substantially right *for the Lavoisier–Laplace apparatus itself*. Authors estimate meltage up to 6 pounds in an ice compartment holding ~30 pounds; original inventors' assumption constant capillary retention is not demonstrated. SECOND, Wedgwood's freezing-mixture solid ice calorimeter froze meltwater anew; this specific failure is explained by ice initially sub-zero in his modified apparatus, and thus should not invalidate the founders' normal-condition instrument. Inventors expressly warned against sub-zero ice in their memoir. Both scholarly histories (Lodwig/Smeaton 1974, Chaldecott 1975) say the warning was missing in Monthly Review 69:568–579 (1783), but the 1783 actual page scan NOT yet directly compared. There may be source-lineage dependence; two papers citing the same evidence is not double independent experimental confirmation.

Novel point of care: The first criticism is intrinsically about ice surface retention and capillary water, whereas the second is about initial sub-zero ice and regelation, a material-protocol conflict. Distinguish flawed device principle vs installation/condition mistake. Both can occur together, so eliminating one source does not prove the whole instrument unbiased.

## MQR formal measurement distinction (not novel theorem)

Let true heat Q, ice initially below 0°C absorb sensible heat Hs, apparatus heat sink Ha, environment net heat He (positive absorption), thawing ice mass mM and latent heat L. Q = L mM + Hs + Ha + He. Let observed drained mass md = mM − mr − mh + mi, where mr is refrozen melt, mh is retained liquid and mi is spurious external drainage. One md measurement is insufficient to identify Q and all latent terms, even with L fixed; infinitely many tuples yield same md. Repetitions at fixed nuisance states can assess apparent repeatability but not accuracy for different conditions; independent observation of ice temperature, ice type, melt retention, outlet clearing and energy losses changes identifiability. This is a toy structural model and does not reconstruct a unique 1784 working formula.

Longino's critical uptake is present: Wedgwood critique transmitted to other British authors, including Nicholson, Thomson (initially positive) and Irvine; later Accum 1816 recommended methods at/freezing point as useful. However exact significance varies by individuals; this is secondary article's reconstruction, not original social network archival census.
