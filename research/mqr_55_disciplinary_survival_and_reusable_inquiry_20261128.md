## Exact scoped cost model
Two routes deliver equally valid current answers and equally valid future tasks. Direct costs a initially and b+s per later task; reusable costs a+p initially and b per later task. Nonnegative integer parameters, s fixed, n homogeneous later tasks, skill remains usable, no option to switch midcourse, same outcome quality, no discounting or capacity constraints. C_D=a+nb+ns, C_R=a+nb+p. Reuse strictly cheaper iff p<ns, equality iff p=ns. For p,s>0 first strict reuse preference is floor(p/s)+1. At n=0 direct strictly cheaper when p>0. For p=s=1 and any regret threshold r, choose n=r+2; C_D>C_R+r. Thus a policy restricted to choosing the cheapest initial answer has no finite universal additive regret bound in this model; not a claim that shorter proofs always have lower transfer value.
If the future horizon is only known to be integer n in [L,H], direct's worst regret against hindsight best of the two routes is max(0,Hs-p); reusable's is max(0,p-Ls). Because the pointwise regrets are monotone in opposite directions, endpoint calculation is exact. Both are zero if p=s=0; ties/zero saving handled. Direct weakly dominates across the range if Hs<=p; reuse weakly dominates if Ls>=p. If strict preferences cross, a choice needs additional criteria (e.g., minimax regret); no universal ranking of approaches. This is elementary amortized cost/reuse and minimax regret, not a new mathematical theorem; no fitting historical data. Equal-quality outputs and fixed costs are simplifying hypotheses and cannot represent all scientific values. Uncertain skill transfer, changing questions, alternative routes and independent methodological value need separate models. Example p=5,s=2: direct preferred at n=0,1,2; reusable at n>=3. For horizons 0..4, max regrets direct 3 vs reuse 5; for 3..4 direct 3 vs reuse 0. The conditional cost criterion is not a default scalar research score.
