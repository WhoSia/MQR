## New source-bounded result

For each observation year define the sorted union of interval dates appearing in that year's archived Spring records. Seven slots in 2013–2017, eight in 2018–2024. Every document's slot availability is a prefix of that observed union: 2,272/2,272, zero intermediate missing slots followed by a later available slot. No duplicate document-slot records. The union is not independently certified to reproduce every historical official schedule. Monotone availability is a pattern, NOT a proof of MCAR, MAR, MNAR, dropout, form automation or completed field visits. Three documents have no interval records. Of 1,086 documents with at least one interval positive, 1,075 have one contiguous positive block and 11 have two blocks. The latter are counterexamples to a deterministic single-positive-block description.

## Partial identification, not imputation

Condition on the 131 archived 2024 documents. At a given observed annual schedule slot, p positives among n available labels imply sharp finite-cohort completion bounds [p/131, (p+131-n)/131], provided all missing cells may independently take 0/1. Endpoints are attained by all-zero and all-one completion. This is about a hypothetical binary **recorded status**, not biological occupancy, not a confidence interval, and not an inference to an external population. For April 20 to May 4 the contrast is exactly [27/131,50/131], strictly positive; May 4 to May 18 [-20/131,54/131] and May 18 to June 1 [-82/131,74/131], each contains zero. No robust peak date follows. Observed slots may also represent a report construction rather than actual field observations, so even these bounds cannot be interpreted as actual flowering without additional semantic assumptions.

Jacobusse & Jongejans define visits using area/date/observer and reported species, which is not equivalent to assuming each calendar slot represents a performed visit. No claim that entire papers or figures independently reaudited.

The [National Research Council 2010 chapter on incomplete clinical-trial data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK209900/) explicitly distinguishes monotone missingness pattern from missingness mechanism and describes a trial where protocol-defined withdrawal after a recorded intermediate outcome and other withdrawals produced missing values. Thus identical-looking longitudinal truncation can have different causes; the protocol is essential evidence. This cross-domain comparison is documentary, not clinical data reanalysis. It reinforces an established statistical lesson, not MQR novelty.
