# MQR 5.4 · Chat45 R62 — Dirac–Anderson: theoretical anticipation without discovery-guiding causation

**2026-11-23 KST · 5.4 OPEN · historical BFS (Q5 mathematical usefulness, Q7 genesis of scientific questions).** No new empirical physics, and no new first-hand archive facsimile acquisition. This is an explicit adversarial case to the facile "prediction leads scientist to find what was predicted" narrative.

## Timestamped historical constraints

1. **1928 theoretical equation.** Dirac published his relativistic electron equation with positive- and negative-energy solutions. The equation alone did **not** immediately establish a settled experimental positron concept, nor did its earliest interpretation straightforwardly identify the electron-mass antiparticle.
2. **1929–1930 disputed interpretation.** Dirac's original hole-based interpretation attempted to identify holes with protons; mass/symmetry and annihilation difficulties challenged that construction. Theoretical interpretation underwent correction; it did not grow monotonically from one uniquely fixed mathematical meaning.
3. **1931 reidentification.** Dirac explicitly proposed an unknown anti-electron with electron mass and opposite charge. Careful account of the Weyl mass argument and exchange/scattering analysis is required; their respective historical causal weights are contested.
4. **1932 observational discovery.** Anderson's cosmic-ray cloud-chamber observations yielded a positive electron. His contemporary Science announcement appeared in September 1932, while the fuller *Physical Review* article *The Positive Electron* appeared **15 March 1933** (DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.43.491). These are **different publications**; do not falsely date the full 1933 paper to 1932.
5. **Discovery causation vs retrospectively unified confirmation.** Anderson maintained that Dirac's theoretical prediction played no causal role in his accidental experimental discovery. Historical accounts discuss his awareness and later interpretation differently, but the strongest limited claim is: mathematics anticipated an entity that was experimentally encountered along a distinct research program. A successful prediction does **not** establish that the prediction caused the discovery.

## Three logical relations (the important result for MQR)

(A) **THEORETICAL ANTICIPATION:** the corrected 1931 framework contains an anti-electron candidate and testable features. (B) **ACTUAL EXPERIMENTAL CAUSATION:** the experimentalists' choice of apparatus, research program and data collection was driven by a particular prediction. (C) **RETROSPECTIVE EMPIRICAL SUPPORT:** post-discovery evidence strengthens the identification of a naturally occurring particle with the theoretically proposed entity.

These relations are NOT equivalent: (A) and (C) can hold while (B) fails. Maxwell→Hertz and Bell→CHSH→Freedman–Clauser have different experimental-theory interaction histories, so an all-purpose "formula directly creates discovery" account fails across the three episodes.

## Rebutting an overclaim

Even (A) requires careful attention to mathematical *interpretation*: Dirac's 1928 negative energy states did not logically, without further interpretive work, supply the unique accepted positron ontology. The 1930 proton-hole interpretation demonstrates a failed initial target, then 1931 revision. This is stronger evidence for interaction of mathematics, background physics and concept repair than for a universal mathematical discovery machine.
