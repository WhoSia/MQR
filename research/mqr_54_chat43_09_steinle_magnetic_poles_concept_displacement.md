## 1. A different kind of concept change: loss of explanatory privilege

The previous [Nersessian CAT case](mqr_54_chat43_07_nersessian_cat_historical_concept_genesis.md) describes the **introduction** of an investigative concept through physical/computational model-building. Steinle instead follows how a once-central scientific concept can lose particular explanatory roles as research goals and alternative representations develop.

| Historical moment, according to Steinle | What the concept was used to do | Epistemic distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Petrus Peregrinus, 13th century | A north/south-pole distinction helped state regularities of magnetic attraction/repulsion rather than only orientation. | The new concept made a **specific law-like question formulable**. Steinle explicitly says Peregrinus's own *motivation* for introducing it cannot be established from surviving sources. |
| Gilbert, 1600 | Magnetic poles appeared as an established and apparently real property, extended to conceptualizing the Earth as a magnet. | Useful conceptual tool becomes a more deeply ontologically committed explanatory picture. |
| Biot–Humboldt and geomagnetic analysis, early 19th century | More complex arrangements of Earth poles were invoked to accommodate inclination observations; abstract mathematical methods, notably Gauss's spherical-harmonic analysis, proved more productive for broader accurate data representation. | The decline of poles in *quantitative analysis* need not retroactively erase their earlier practical role. |
| Faraday's experimental electromagnetic research, 1820s–1850s | The concept of poles provided a reference frame for electromagnetic motions, but magnetic curves/lines of force progressively proved more useful for expressing relations involving electricity, magnetism and motion. | The replacement changes which directions and relations the theory treats as basic. |
| Faraday's 1832 private notes; later public work | Steinle cites Faraday's diary of 26 March 1832 for a proposed general formulation using magnetic curves independent of pole-defined directions, while stressing this was private and not immediately a public settled view. | A new idea's occurrence, public acceptance and eventual scientific authority are **different events**. |

Steinle's main philosophical point is that a scientific concept is not itself a proposition with a simple true/false status. Its adequacy is relative to epistemic purposes and research practices, even if those practices also require true empirical claims and successful prediction. Consequently, a concept may be successful for one activity yet poor for another; this is **not** equivalent to a claim that magnets have no observable poles in ordinary handling.

## 2. Comparing two historically different developmental patterns

| Comparative dimension | Nersessian neural-engineering episode | Steinle magnetic-poles episode |
|---|---|---|
| Main transformation | Burst as nuisance → burst as informative pattern; CAT and related measures introduced | Pole as law ingredient / reference → gradually displaced as privileged theoretical descriptor by fields/curves and mathematical accounts |
| Material or model practice | Physical and computational model systems co-develop, interacting with visualizations and control problems | Navigational, geomagnetic measurement, electromagnetism and mathematical representation pursue changing aims |
| New scientific question | How do activity patterns move, stabilize, and relate to control? | What direction or field relation best unifies electromagnetic phenomena without fixing the pole line as primary? |
| Novelty limitation | Much of CAT may be computed from time-resolved information; model validity needs physical target comparison | Lines-of-force/field thinking did not mean every practical use of poles became illegitimate |
| Evidence limitation | Ethnographic author account, not original apparatus data in MQR's possession | Steinle's scholarly historical narrative, not primary manuscripts collated here |

Both examples show a change in **explanatory and investigative roles**. Neither supports the crude statement that “newer language is automatically truer” or that any newly constructed algorithmic feature constitutes a new object in the world.

## 3. An internal adversary for the MQR 43 formal program

The existing MQR 43 active learner holds fixed: (a) a hypothesis menu, (b) possible native actions, (c) sensor alphabets and (d) a criterion for a 'best' experiment (minimum candidate count). But these historical cases involve change in **the very utility function and research target**. For Nersessian, bursts became candidate informative signals rather than nuisances; for Steinle, the useful explanatory role of poles shifted toward field/curve concepts. A learner that merely separates the six native-alignment permutations cannot discover why these questions mattered.

A more serious research implementation should distinguish:
- **Concept availability:** a representation or category can be constructed or named;
- **Concept viability:** it supports a new empirical research practice;
- **Concept authority:** its consequences survive repeated, independent confrontation with the world;
- **Concept displacement:** the old term remains serviceable for some activities while losing privileged place in others.

This is an analytical comparative vocabulary, **not an empirically validated universal taxonomy** or new mathematical theorem.

## 4. Where the proof burden sits

The philosophically stronger question is: if two apparently incompatible sciences agree on physical interventions yet use different conceptual systems, can they both be warranted without demanding an absolutely unique common underlying state description? The MQR 43 permutation-gauge residue already demonstrates **representational nonuniqueness inside a finite analyst-set model**. These historical episodes show that **the appraisal goals themselves change over time**. The conjunction motivates investigating *dynamic* epistemic objectives, but does not prove that two incompatible fundamental ontologies are equally true.

For validation: use specific, independently documented historical cases of which *new or anomalous empirical result* supported a changed scientific question. Compare rival historiographies and original notebooks/records, rather than accepting a philosophical interpretation solely because it fits a toy equation.
