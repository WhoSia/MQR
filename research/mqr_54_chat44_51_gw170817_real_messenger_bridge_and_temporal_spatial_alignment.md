## 1. What is actually established and what is only interpretation
Contemporaneous **Abbott et al. (2017)**, *Multi-Messenger Observations of a Binary Neutron Star Merger*, *Astrophysical Journal Letters* **848:L12**, DOI [10.3847/2041-8213/aa91c9](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/aa91c9), official [LIGO DCC publication record P1700294-v7](https://dcc.ligo.org/LIGO-P1700294-v7/public) inspected at abstract, date and journal bibliographic lines. It documents **17 Aug 2017** gravitational wave event GW170817 with merger at **12:41:04 UTC**, gamma-ray GRB 170817A **~1.7 seconds later** independently observed by Fermi GBM, an initial roughly **31 deg²** gravitational wave source localization, optical counterpart in host galaxy **NGC 4993 less than 11 hours** after merger, follow-on X-ray/radio observations detected ~9 and ~16 days later. Note LIGO's public **later/final sky-area** figure **28 deg²** is not the same edition/analysis as the 31 deg² initial value; do not paper over the difference.

Companion official [LIGO summary](https://ligo.org/science-summaries/GW170817GRB/) identifies additional information about sky coincidence and gravitational/gamma arrival delays. The summary gives an approximately **1 in 20 million probability of this close space-time coincidence if unrelated**, which is a **likelihood or null-chance claim**, not by itself a Bayesian posterior P(unrelated|data) without base rates and selection/trigger conditioning. The published companion [GW + gamma event article](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05834) contains the exact delay **1.74 ± 0.05 s** and a speed-difference bound; do NOT infer the speed difference by simply calculating 1.7 s divided by cosmic travel time, since astrophysical emission-time lag and detailed assumptions matter.

## 2. Why it gives MQR a serious world-facing test
Two previously distinct experimental communities independently measure **spacetime strain** and **gamma-ray photons**. They need not achieve semantic reduction of gravitational wave strain into photon intensity; their combination depends instead on event-time and sky-position localization, independently characterized sensor/background statistics, and source-association analysis.

Their joint match enabled new empirical **constraints** on neutron-star-merger origins of some short gamma-ray bursts, and gravitational-vs-light propagation. Yet these questions were already theoretically expressible before August 2017. This is **new empirical answerability / evidence**, not a demonstrated invention in 2017 of the concepts 'gamma-ray burst' or 'speed of gravity'. Very close fit to R44's new paired evidence, not evidence for a novel philosophical master law.

## 3. Falsification checklist
- **Temporal association**: both instrument clocks calibrated; exposure and data gaps properly accounted for; known background rates used.
- **Spatial association**: sky localization distributions considered, not merely single best-fit coordinates.
- **Multiplicity**: all candidate alerts and implicit look-elsewhere effects included; one cherry-picked coincidence cannot support an unconditional probability.
- **Common-source alternative**: independently observed electromagnetic transient in an appropriate host further tests joint-source inference.
- **Observation ≠ causation**: detecting correlated signals from a shared astrophysical event strongly supports a common progenitor only against specified rivals; not a controlled `do(GW)` intervention.
- **Negative evidence**: official original abstract reports *no* coincident high-energy gamma/neutrino candidates in relevant searches, rather than universal source emissions in every messenger.
