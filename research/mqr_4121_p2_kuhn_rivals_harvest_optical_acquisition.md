## IV. Instrument intervention versus chemical hypothesis: causal design and gate
For detector raw intensities i(λ), spectral stray-light correction C is an operator on RAW intensity channel before normalized absorbance. The finite bandwidth convolution B acts on intensity/transmittance before log. Proposed full observation generative path:
chemical species concentrations c_j(pH,T,h) → transmission T(λ)=10^[-ℓ Σ ε_j(λ)c_j] → illumination and spectral response convolution + stray coupling → raw intensity/dark/reference → calibrated spectral response → -log10 normalized T → analysis.
Note noncommutation (log after averaging vs average after log). Main competing explanations: H_C chemical species equilibrium, H_B bandwidth response, H_S stray light. Nuisance/hardware interventions can change more than one operator; no guaranteed 'orthogonal' knob without instrument documentation and independent calibration.
Rival controls: (1) same chemistry, change independently measured spectral slit line-spread function; (2) same chemistry, independently characterize or suppress stray light; (3) chemically perturb pH at fixed optical response, control dilution, ionic strength, temperature and path length; (4) repeated blank/dark/reference, reversals and sample-history blocks; (5) orthogonal chemical state check. A pattern counts as source of local model change only to the extent these controls hold. Chemical state unique identifiability and a Kuhnian revolution are **not** implied by residual spectra.
Prior toy 12-cell design=3 concentrations x 2 width x 2 stray regimes **only an initial screening**, not mathematical minimum or sufficient sample size. Revised practical design: precheck dynamic range; avoid selecting A≈4 where T=0.0001 unless instrument SNR supports it. Determine variance by pilot repeated raw intensities and matrix uncertainty; then predeclare contrast delta and its combined uncertainty with nuisance covariance. The familiar |Δμ|/σ ratio is not a substitute for experimental identification or calibrated rejection error.
