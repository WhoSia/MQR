## What the author's opening establishes

Weyl characterizes foundational contradictions as symptomatic of problems reaching beyond peripheral set-theoretic paradoxes. He identifies Brouwer's proposals, developed from 1907 into a concrete theory, **and separately** refers to his own 1918 *Das Kontinuum* as an independent attempt to ground analysis. The opening explains why real numbers and the continuum provide the testing ground for foundational reconstruction.

This is evidence for first-person *recognition of a foundational problem*, multiple intellectual routes, and a time-dated rationale for investigating them. It does NOT prove that Weyl had fully endorsed all of Brouwer's rules, that he ranked it highest after accounting for mathematical practice, or that he already foresaw later non-adoption. No binary conversion narrative should be forced onto the source.

## Direct historical comparison against R46-E

(1) Weyl 1921: first-person advocacy of investigating foundations plus recognition of an independent alternative.
(2) Kish Bar-On 2022: historical explanatory framework relating Weyl and Fraenkel's varying responses to Brouwer and the failure of dominant adoption.
(3) A theory of ex-ante pursuit cannot assume community-wide non-adoption was already known to Weyl in 1921.
(4) Intellectual merit, choice to pursue, explanatory justification, and collective adoption are separate. The 1921 opening is useful evidence for the first two, not a quantified choice between every rival available.
