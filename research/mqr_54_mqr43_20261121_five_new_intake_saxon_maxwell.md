## Important correction: the February 1945 Saxon memorandum exists *inside* the 1968 volume

The 1968 PDF table of contents specifically lists **“Memorandum on Schwinger's Lectures”** before “Introduction.” On the roman-numbered first pages after the editors' foreword, there is a stand-alone memorandum attributed to **DAVID S. SAXON** and dated **February, 1945**; it begins by explaining the wider distribution of the lecture notes and describes the scope of the omitted/planned parts. The 1968 publisher's preface confirms a memorandum and introduction were written at the time to supply background. Thus **Saxon's 1945 memorandum is now held as a 1968 published textual witness**, not wholly absent. The *distinct* 1945 mimeographed original/autograph is unconfirmed, if physical-original collation ever becomes material. The older “Saxon memorandum text unheld” description in the live registry must be amended. **Do not silently treat 1968 source as a 1945 facsimile with original typography**.

The same volume's Introduction describes a choice between engineering reduction to equivalent line/circuit elements and theoretical boundary-value methods. The author's contrast is significant to MQR: the transfer of a formalism can create a successful *external-phenomena description* without reproducing all internal fields or the same laboratory practice. A 1948 Kerns paper has already shown the related **transducer-as-terminal-relations** assumption. The 1968 volume supplies the longer original lecture perspective; this is a substantive new reading, not another unrelated document accession.
