## 2. A stronger, *fully read in sections*, philosophical competitor

Pierre-Hugues Beauchemin and Kent W. Staley (2024), *The epistemological significance of exploratory experimentation: A pragmatist model of how practices matter philosophically*, *European Journal for Philosophy of Science* 14:59, DOI 10.1007/s13194-024-00620-6. Full openly accessible author version:
https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/23945/7/Beauchemin_Staley_EE.pdf

The publicly accessible full-text PDF was examined in introduction, sections 3, 5 and 6, using page/line-identified excerpts. Screenshot rendering through web failed with cache miss; no visual-page image verification claimed. The distinctive arguments:

- Exploration is not a fixed kind of experimental apparatus or a method with its own intrinsic features. It is relative to aims, resources, tasks, criticism and context.
- The authors formulate a **normativity challenge for practices (NCP)**: it is not enough to narrate practices; show how they warrant judgments.
- Their model distinguishes inquiry in **use mode** from **critical mode**. Criticism examines resources, performance and what conclusions count as warranted.
- Their LHC comparison explicitly contrasts signature-based model-independent (SBMI) searches with searches optimized for specific beyond-standard-model (BSM) predictions. The SBMI search tries not to miss unanticipated signals, accepting a different tradeoff from tightly targeted exclusion tests. Yet it is still theory-laden in **constraining, reporting and complementing** roles.
- More important than whether a search is called exploratory: the same basic analysis procedure can have a different *epistemic role* because different goals justify its design and risk posture.

This rival directly preempts an MQR claim that exploratory practices need a separate justification framework, that preservation of alternatives is normatively important, or that exploratory apparatus is independent of theory. The authors' explicit inclusion of Schickore, Steinle, Karaca and Dewey further narrows room for a novelty claim through literature juxtaposition.

## 4. What could constitute an actual paper

A *specific* correction to a source-backed historical inference could be valuable: e.g. a previously overlooked textual and numerical contradiction across original 1952 and 1954 authors' claims that changes an accepted historiographic interpretation. This is an **example of a desirable type of discovery, not an allegation that such a contradiction exists**.

Alternatively, a philosophical paper would need a precise rival proposition (e.g. in Beauchemin–Staley's pragmatist framework), a historically warranted counterexample that their actual text does not already absorb, and a constructive replacement interpretation with independent explanatory gain. Neither exists yet.
