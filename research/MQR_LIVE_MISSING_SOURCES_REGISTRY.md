Society's 1752–1957 disclaimer explicitly disclaims certification of factual certainty or arguments' validity. Therefore 1784 Wedgwood publication was not equivalent to modern peer certification.

Journals and grants have distinct editorial decision rights; even NSF outside expert panel reports did not eliminate institutional discretion. 1975 NSF review modes: 44% ad hoc mail, 28% panel, 28% combined (in cited internal summary).
- Fyfe et al. (2020): **five-year sample years** from 1865–1965, **not every intervening calendar year**, 3,716 distinct authors and 1,015 referees. Page 5 reports 6,665 submissions with counts 787 _Transactions_, 5,214 _Proceedings_, 665 not published; **the components sum to 6,666**. Same numeric discrepancy occurs on publisher web version, so not mere Drive text corruption. **One-record reconciliation OPEN**, without deciding which number is wrong; do not present publication proportions as microdata-validated. Data counts only referee assignments/report metadata **not full report texts**; cannot infer referee intellectual rigor from counts.
