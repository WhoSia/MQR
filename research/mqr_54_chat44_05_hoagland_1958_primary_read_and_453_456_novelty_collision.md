## 2. Historical primary paper now newly contacted at the document level

**M. B. Hoagland, M. L. Stephenson, J. F. Scott, L. I. Hecht and P. C. Zamecnik, 1958**, *A soluble ribonucleic acid intermediate in protein synthesis*, *Journal of Biological Chemistry* 231(1):241–257, bibliographic [PubMed PMID 13538965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13538965/). Firsthand digital [institution-hosted PDF of the journal article](https://njc.rockefeller.edu/pdf4/Class4-HoaglandZemecnik1958.pdf) opened in chat44; original PDF textual body was directly consulted at printed pp.241–243, pp.246–247 and pp.253–256. **Original article page image/graph parity and original laboratory records were NOT independently authenticated or digitized, and the Drive copy's presence remains unverified.** The same HTML/PDF-hosting URL is not proof of an author's archived raw data.

Time-relative important fact: the article's own page 241 records **received September 27, 1957**, versus publication March 1958. Do not retrospectively claim an experimental finding only became thinkable in March 1958, or date exact experiment runs from this submission date.

### Distinct evidence categories that the original authors themselves separate

**Preparation and measurement** (printed pp.241–243): cytoplasmic preparations (microsomes, pH 5 enzyme fraction) and radiolabeled amino acids; pH 5 fraction RNA labeling is measured. For the NaCl extraction procedure, the authors report only about **30–35% RNA extraction yield** and explicitly note that total radioactivity extrapolation assumes the extracted RNA is representative of all RNA in the original mixture. This is a **measurement/representativeness assumption**, not direct quantified proof of random extraction. It creates a provenance-dependent warrant limit.

**Differential experimental requirements** (printed pp.243, 246): they report ATP sufficient for labeling the pH 5 RNA fraction, whereas GTP and energy-regeneration conditions matter for other stages of incorporation; ribonuclease inhibits RNA labeling but does not simply invalidate the separately characterized amino-acid activation reaction. These different conditional interventions support a process-stage distinction; do not collapse them into one scalar 'RNA pathway discovered'.

**Intermediate transfer** (pp.252–255): labeled pH 5 RNA can subsequently transfer labeled amino acid to microsomal protein under specified experimental conditions. Seven isolated-RNA-transfer experiments report **mean ~20% of label transferred** and maximum ~25% (p.254). GTP is again reported as necessary in that assay context. This is a concrete conditional evidence claim, not independent replication or a modern mechanism theorem.

**Competing interpretations remain alive** (pp.254–256): authors explicitly say pH 5 enzyme fraction impurity prevents certifying whether the RNA naturally associates with amino-acid-activating enzymes or whether other intervening enzymatic stages exist; the precise amino-acid–RNA bond was unresolved, and several chemical possibilities were entertained. They distinguish a proposed intermediate carrier role from complete structural determination.

**Heuristic proposal beyond the experimentally established scope** (p.255–256): authors advance a *working hypothesis* in which charged pH 5 RNA molecules polymerize in microsomes in an ordered fashion to explain peptide formation. This proposed pathway is **not implied by** the measured labeling/transfer experiments alone. It was presented as a hypothesis to guide further studies, not final molecule-by-molecule proof. A modern mechanistic comparison requires a separately sourced present-day account, not retrospective dismissal from a paraphrase.

### MQR inference
A dated research article can contain simultaneously:
(1) explicitly observable and conditional intervention signatures;
(2) acknowledged preparation and sampling assumptions;
(3) a useful newly hypothesized functional intermediate;
(4) several unresolved structural alternatives;
(5) a much more speculative mechanism generating new future experiments.

These stages have **different warrants within the same dated practice**. Neither all of them constitute the same 'new question' nor all are resolved by community uptake. This makes the history more precise than a staged path of 'question→experiment→truth', **but is anticipated by Rheinberger (1997), Feest (2012), and normal philosophy and laboratory methodology**. We have improved source-grounded specificity, not established new philosophical priority.

## 4. More demanding theory question: standards for standards, without circular self-certification

Using MQR R45-H and Lari (2024), define two different evaluands:
- **First-order criticism**: Does a proposed claim/method satisfy the community's declared local aims and available tests?
- **Second-order criticism**: Are those aims/validity criteria themselves defensible in light of accessible rival practices, world contact, and broader public scientific purposes?

A program may properly reject a first-order objection as irrelevant to its current stated target while admitting the same objection as a second-order challenge to its *target selection*. This is consistent with Lari's published resolution and therefore **not** a contradiction of that theory.

The unresolved, genuinely harder question is whether a second-order exchange can be *actionable* where no stable shared ontology/experimental anchor is yet available. Existing Kuhn (lexicon learning), Galison (trading zones), and MQR41 cross-primitive toy experiments must be tested before claiming that MQR supplies a novel answer. Merely proposing 'third-order criticism' is an infinite-regress label, not a solution.

The strongest near-term protocol: trace the ex-ante question, material procedure, warranted observation, explicit uncertainty and future proposed test in 1958 Hoagland; compare to a distinct actor-level first-person controversy such as Weyl 1921 (transmitted via Mancosu 1998); then put Lari/Kitcher/Brigandt's strongest possible explanation alongside, not weak caricatures. If their account covers all transitions, record a **negative novelty result**.
