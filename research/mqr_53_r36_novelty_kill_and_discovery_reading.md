## Prior-art kills from direct book/paper reading

### K1. "Choosing a reference changes quantity-concept application" — close / subsume

- Chang (2004), *Inventing Temperature*, ch. 1 and 5, directly treats unstable thermometric reference points, defense of fixity, standard justification and epistemic iteration. Drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RwuRfY9Ifz6iEAUZZm57z4EzHX8CP0CV/view
- Tal (2017), *Calibration: Modelling the Measurement Process*, makes calibration model-construction and testing. Drive: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1P5guGX-EJBfjr0fKigoSyTOghk5q8Ff2/view ; DOI 10.1016/j.shpsa.2017.09.001.
- Stronger and previously insufficiently included: Tal, *Making Time: A Study in the Epistemology of Measurement*, online 2014-12-05, printed *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* **67(1)** (2016) 297–335; DOI 10.1093/bjps/axu037, https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1093/bjps/axu037. The accessible abstract and section map (not a complete full-text reading) explicitly conceptualize standardization as legislative application of a quantity concept, with top-down theoretical/statistical model revision and bottom-up modification of material standards. This **directly subsumes the main aspirational novelty of R34/35**. The claim-relative identifiability algebra itself is ordinary statistical identification.
- **Disposition:** R34/R35 kept as teachable constructed example, not article central claim; no name-only relabeling as a new 'three-stage warrant theory'.

### K2. "Exploratory experiments invent concepts without pre-existing hypothesis space" — close / subsume

- Steinle (1997), *Entering New Fields: Exploratory Uses of Experimentation*, directly read from Drive PDF https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rl444ab9HV-Q285EwbPq1QBR2z5g3JcR/view : Ampère's 1820 variable manipulations led to empirical regularities and developing classification/concepts. The text explicitly discusses concept formation and difficulties of criteria for the appropriateness of classifications.
- Steinle (2002), *Challenging Established Concepts: Ampère and Exploratory Experimentation*, Theoria 17(44) 291–316, accessible published abstract https://addi.ehu.es/bitstream/handle/10810/40381/Theoria%2044%20291-316.pdf?sequence=1. Direct attention to historical lab practice and emergence of 'current circuit' defeats generic originality.
- Nersessian (2008), *Creating Scientific Concepts*, directly read sections from book Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/168Yv277pBhy--iQrNjhMWbYcFhVx3_KR/view : introduction / chapter structure / discussion of Maxwell's analogical model-building, hybrid intermediate models, and iterative bootstrapping. Her explicit goal is how model-based reasoning generates novel conceptual representations.
- **Disposition:** "hypothesis-space expansion is not the same as conceptual invention" may still be an educational distinction; it is not independently novel by itself. Historical study requires a question Steinle and Nersessian actually leave open, not an invented gap.

### K3. "An experimental system produces unforeseen epistemic objects" — close / subsume

- Rheinberger (1997), *Toward a History of Epistemic Things*, chapter 2 and 5–6 snippets read from the canonical 11_BOOKS source https://drive.google.com/file/d/1DDrn0i1jOZ79tx8VxftIgQmdTrNH4tr8/view. Epistemic things embody not-yet-known objects; technical conditions simultaneously produce and delimit them. Ch. 6, 'Defining Fractions, 1952–55', recounts protein synthesis fractionation, low resolution and contamination by intact bacteria as an alternative explanation of apparent incorporation. This is an actual source-based methodological ambiguity, not a made-up numerical example.
- **Disposition:** A theory in which anomaly + experimental variation + later stabilization yields a previously unseen research object is not uniquely MQR. Any new claim needs a materially distinct, case-specific explanatory failure of Rheinberger's reconstruction.

### K4. "Evidence travels and changes meaning across contexts" — close / subsume

- Leonelli (2016), *Data-Centric Biology*, chapters 1, 3, 4 and 7, directly reviewed by topic from Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qPm83qmzIATx-n_uBxr1hf02vMIoMlb5/view : data are claim-relative evidence; travels require decontextualization and recontextualization, metadata, material and tacit expertise. 'Evidential value' can vary with downstream investigative aims; no novel discovery from merely writing an 'evidence-lineage' pipeline.
- Staley (2004), *Robust Evidence and Secure Evidence Claims*, directly read Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1NguU7MEAb0RGYmoSeq9eVwogsmJHu1mH/view : security of evidence claims is distinct from evidence strength; robustness can be second-order evidence or backup against assumptions. Therefore R32's distinction between independent evidence and common error is not novel.
- Particularly powerful new rival: Beauchemin & Staley, *How uncertainty and underdetermination allow measurement to produce useful results*, **published 2026** in *Measurement*, DOI 10.1016/j.measurement.2026.120330 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0263224126000394 . Prior manuscript dated 2025-06-27 already in Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1z-EKpsT9cJr83Ip2KqPc8JC_KJHe1__G/view ; sampled introduction and substantive argument. It specifically connects measurement's original situational specificity to usefulness and warrant for subsequent inquiries through uncertainty and sensitivity. **Published-vs-draft collation is outstanding.**
- **Disposition:** Generic claim that 'transport requires local validation and scope-sensitive uncertainty' is **not** a viable original contribution.

### K5. "Local counterexamples can produce new scientific categories" — constrained / subsume

- Lakatos (1976), *Proofs and Refutations*, directly read Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qpRtwZLjZBgio5W6ptQorzu1MY7l970G/view : ch.1 explicitly distinguishes local (lemma) from global (conjecture) counterexamples, monster-barring, and lemma-incorporation. Mathematical criticism can reorganize the relevant class of objects and the conjecture.
- Bridging this with Rheinberger's experimental novelty is *interesting comparative work*, but not automatically a research advance. Mathematical proofs and material experimental systems differ in what makes a counterexample admissible; one must not flatten the history or transfer epistemic authority across domains automatically.

1. **First read historical disputes rather than another proof**: Steinle (1997) Ampère's 1820 research; Rheinberger ch.6 (1952–55 fractionation, alternative contaminant explanation); Nersessian ch.2 and 6 (Maxwell analogical models, representations and constraints). 2. **Compare without flattening:** For each case track object/target before; manipulations permitted; observed anomaly; what new object/concept became available; what contemporaneous alternative interpretation was considered; what later confirmation was independent. List missing primary texts as missing, not as conclusions.
3. **Ask the adversarial question:** Does an experiment that transforms what can be described necessarily destroy the warrant of earlier measurements? Find both a survival and a failure example. R36 does NOT answer it.
4. **Before any novelty claim**, explicitly compete against Steinle's exploratory experimentation, Nersessian's generative bootstrapping, Rheinberger's epistemic objects, Leonelli's relational data and Beauchemin–Staley's transport/usefulness analysis. If any rival supplies the complete explanation, close the proposed MQR contribution.
5. **Broaden philosophy, not bookkeeping:** MQR's original ambition concerns how a world that exceeds current representations contributes to novel inquiry. Formal identification/contrast examples remain tests and countermodels, not the scientific/philosophical destination.
