# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · R40 — Theory predicted an adaptor; experiment made it operational: the Crick–Hoagland/Zamecnik genealogy

**Date 2026-11-22 KST.** A new substantive held-original cross-check with an explicit adversarial correction to the phrase 'theory-free experimental discovery'. MQR 5.4 OPEN. Directly read selected passages from three held original scholarly texts: Zamecnik & Keller (1954), Hoagland–Keller–Zamecnik (1956), and Fry (2022); separate 1958 experimental article public text examined R38. None was newly downloaded/acquired or should appear in the missing-source list.

## 1. Source custody and publication order (read, no invented source gaps)
**Paul C. Zamecnik & Elizabeth B. Keller (1954)**, 'Relation Between Phosphate Energy Donors and Incorporation of Labeled Amino Acids into Proteins', *Journal of Biological Chemistry* original, held exact PDF Drive `1FJx6ghWM3QF4wpmQQlAdgDZcZxJlxp6n`; original opening and explanation examined. The authors repeatedly caution that amino-acid incorporation in an isolated preparation had not yet proven actual peptide-bond formation/protein synthesis. The experimental signal was real under described conditions, but its **ontological identity** remained partly unsettled.

**Mahlon B. Hoagland, Elizabeth B. Keller & Paul C. Zamecnik (1956)**, 'Enzymatic Carboxyl Activation of Amino Acids', *Journal of Biological Chemistry* original, held exact PDF `1G6m-pB4PG3RU-2oPuOQRXdg6sMBrKOrT`; authors' opening directly examined. They test a **specific local mechanistic possibility**: a component of soluble material enables amino-acid activation. They compare different activation-associated observations, demonstrating that their inquiry was exploratory but NOT literally devoid of theoretical assumptions, mechanistic hypotheses or prior work.

**Michael Fry (2022)**, 'Crick's Adaptor Hypothesis and the Discovery of Transfer RNA: Experiment Surpassing Theoretical Prediction', *Philosophy, Theory, and Practice in Biology* **14:11**, DOI [10.3998/ptpbio.2628](https://doi.org/10.3998/ptpbio.2628), *held full original publication* `1KVPJTOaL3eiu6_Z-ZCfZUw0seZ6CVOsV`. The original text's abstract and substantive historical passages were directly read. Fry's thesis: Francis Crick's **1955 adaptor conjecture** anticipated an RNA molecular intermediary (and charging enzymes) at a highly abstract level, while biochemical researchers developed experimentally powerful representations largely independently. The two groups operated in differentiated knowledge communities and experimental spaces, including communication barriers; this is **Fry's historical interpretation**, not a universally measured causal effect. **1958 Hoagland et al.** direct article text now publicly available (R38) documents concrete published experimental methods and their limits.

## 2. The four chronologies researchers must not conflate
1. Early 1950s original protocols that produced an uncertain observed incorporation signal (Zamecnik/Keller).
2. 1955 Crick adaptor idea predicting a kind of mediator (a conjectural *question/answer template* before direct object isolation; its original 1955 circulated letter manuscript not directly read here).
3. Mid-1950s experimental purification, functional comparison and local biochemical testing (Hoagland and colleagues), **with their own** hypotheses about activation rather than total explanatory vacuum.
4. Later established tRNA identity and transformation of unstable biochemical objects into routinely manipulable laboratory tools.

**Note:** publication year, experiment date, and later historians' description must be separately dated. A 1956 journal paper with a 'Received for publication June 3, 1955' line witnesses preprint receipt but does not prove all findings first arose exactly that date.

## 3. Which side generated the question?
A simple competition 'theory vs experiment' has no single winner. Crick's early abstract proposal supplied an in-principle molecular object/concept that could orient future questions **even where it did not directly guide these practitioners**. Hoagland/Zamecnik's program generated experimental constraints, measured intermediary activity and procedures that made the candidate answer materially accessible. Inference:
- `PREDICTION_CREATED` does not entail `APPARATUS_AVAILABLE`.
- `APPARATUS_AVAILABLE` does not entail `CORRECT_UNIQUE_ONTOLOGY`.
- `DISCOVERY_OCCURRED` does not entail `IT_WAS_CAUSED_BY_THE_MOST_ACCURATE_PRIOR_THEORY`.
- `EXPLORE_WITHOUT_CRICK_GUIDANCE` does NOT entail `NO_HYPOTHESIS_GUIDED_EXPERIMENT`.

This is a stronger adversarial historical example for MQR than repeated finite-state toy refinements. It directly blocks both 'all novel research questions arise from data' and 'the first successful prediction must have caused the eventual discovery'.

## 4. Alternative explanations and severe counterevidence
**Fry's strong rival** is already in the held corpus: theory can predict an entity before material discovery, and concepts need not await observation. Yet why did an apparently accurate prediction have limited immediate laboratory effect? Candidate explanations include communication networks, difficulty converting abstract representation into viable biochemical assays, different scientific interests, technical feasibility and naming/credit histories. The held Fry paper argues these possibilities but without controlled counterfactual evidence they remain historical explanations with varying strength.

**Challenge to Fry wording:** Original Hoagland/Keller/Zamecnik (1956) explicitly frames a testable biochemical activation hypothesis; 'theory-free' cannot mean theoryless/local-hypothesis-free, only more narrowly 'not guided by Crick's specific adaptor hypothesis'. A source-critical MQR treatment should preserve this contrast and avoid importing later tRNA ontology back into the 1954 paper.

## 5. Conditional, testable MQR thesis (not universal law)
When a scientifically promising abstract proposed entity and a separately developed experimental practice converge on a similar observed intermediary, **their epistemic and practical influence can be unequal**. The explanatory target is the actual chain from predictive representation → test design → detectable signals → reproducible procedures → independent interpretive authority, conditioned on communities and instruments. Specific evidence needed: correspondence or lab notes proving whether hypotheses reached the relevant scientists *before* design choices, dates of protocols and tests, and contemporaneous reception studies, rather than later first-person memory alone.

**Decision:** held-source prior-art conflict and 1954/1956 primary ambiguity audit PASS. Causal claims about Crick's hypothesis' impact and complete original 1955 RNA Tie Club letter text HOLD. MQR 5.4 OPEN.
