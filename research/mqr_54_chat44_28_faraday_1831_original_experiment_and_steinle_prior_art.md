## Four separately indexed chronological events (must not collapse)

- **1820:** Ørsted's electrical-current wire deflects nearby magnetic compass, provoking experimental study of the geometry and response; the phenomenon did not immediately supply a stable, generally agreed mechanism.
- **September 1821:** Faraday's exploratory **electromagnetic rotation** work (Steinle 2016 Ch. 6), generating questions about actual persistent circular motion and noncentral relations. This is NOT the same experiment as 1831 induction; the 2016 historical monograph chiefly analyzes 1820–21 origins, not a full primary history of 1831 induction.
- **29 August 1831:** Faraday's original surviving **iron-ring induction** apparatus, observed transient secondary galvanometer deflection when primary circuit made, rather than an uninterrupted secondary current under continued primary current.
- **24 Nov 1831 reading, 1832 publication:** Faraday's First Series explicitly reports repeatable changes on make and break with opposite deflection, and the lack of sustained secondary effect during steady primary connection. The exact discovery is historically earlier than his submission/read date; do not label 1832 a separate discovery.

## What new scientific question became materially answerable?

Rather than merely asking whether an established permanent electrical current creates a **constant** magnetic/electrical outcome, the controlled apparatus lets the experimenter investigate a **transient, event-indexed response**: how does a secondary circuit react when the primary circuit's state changes, and with what sign upon establishment vs interruption? With the circuit continuously closed the galvanometer returns to rest; on interruption the deflection reverses. A distinct *time-conditioned experimental question* becomes tractable.

Modern concise physical hindsight would express induced electromotive force as electromotive force proportional to negative time variation of magnetic flux, but the 1831 historical experiment does NOT itself measure an ideal continuous flux derivative, does not include all Maxwell field theory and cannot be retrospectively called 'already an AC transformer theory'. It demonstrates a physical anomaly against the naive **steady-action** assumption, not a proof that all contemporary theories predicted zero induction. Faraday's own attempt to generalize the term 'induction' was a conceptual negotiation under preexisting electrostatic uses.

## Hard novelty / explanatory rivals

Steinle (1997/2016), Hacking, Galison, Nersessian, Rheinberger and historians of instruments have **already** articulated exploratory experimentation and scientific concept formation in considerable detail. MQR **must not** take credit for uncovering that exploratory practice creates concepts, or that Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction. A possible novel contribution would require competing interpretations to predict *different historical counterfactuals or overlooked archival evidence*, not just recoding the story as MQR 'L2 operator genesis'.

**Provisional philosophical finding:** three distinguishable objects often conflated in retrospective history are (a) materially executable intervention, (b) reproducibly interpretable report, (c) consolidated concept/law. An intervention can make a question practically researchable *before* the modern physical quantity is established. The three objects may be mutually reconstructed; they are not necessarily temporally ordered once and for all.
