## Historical comparison: composing shunts in a waveguide

Galison's Trading Zone, pp. 150–152, connects Schwinger's work to engineering practice. Two closer technical sources put pressure on how far to follow the interpretation. In CRPL-9-5 (June 30, 1948), David M. Kerns cites Saxon's February 1945 introductory memorandum and explicitly distinguishes rigorous network reduction from an assumed low-frequency analogy. In Principles of Microwave Circuits (1948), §§1.2, 3.3–3.5, 4.6–4.7 and 6.3–6.4, the direct field problem remains specifiable, while re-solving it for each combination is described as a practical obstacle. Section 3.5, eqs. (26)–(27), actually derives additive shunt admittance from repeated wave reflections. The direct wave calculation therefore supplies an alternative route to the same result. The change is the reusable operation on components, not a new Maxwell equation. The engineering text does not establish how common the workflow was before and after the representation change, or that Schwinger was its sole originator.

A concrete inverse design task follows directly: at one chosen frequency and in the model's common shunt plane, choose a second susceptance of opposite sign to cancel the first. With admittance normalized by the line admittance, b = +1 or -1 alone gives reflected power 1/5. An ideal co-located pair cancels. If the same ideal shunts are separated by a quarter guided wavelength, the cascade M = S(+1) L(pi/2) S(-1) instead gives reflected power 5/9. Here S(b) has rows (1, 0), (i b, 1); L(theta) has diagonal cos(theta) and both off-diagonals i sin(theta). For equal real unit port impedances, the reflection is (A+B-C-D)/(A+B+C+D). Python reproduced 0, 5/9, 0 at electrical spacings 0, pi/2, pi. These are lossless single-mode network results at fixed frequency; the ideal co-located shunts are a circuit operation, not proof that two physical metal irises can be overlaid, or that close irises preserve independent parameters. Higher-mode coupling, finite thickness and bandwidth can matter. Sections 5.25 and 6.3–6.4 discuss frequency sensitivity and geometry limits. No hardware was run.

## Exact Real-Lang research experiment

The [implementation](../language/real/experiments/protocol_frontier_v54.py) takes two deterministic finite state machines with known initial states, common action and output labels, and a finite protocol automaton. The latter lists which action may follow which history. It searches the reachable triple of left state, right state and protocol state, minimizing positive integer action cost. The first reachable differing output gives a minimum-cost witness. Exhausting the product establishes equality of all admissible traces in the supplied models. If the minimum is larger than a stated budget, the result retains the witness and says it is beyond budget. This argument is the product construction and Dijkstra search, not a new automata theorem. Moore (1956), §II, already studies distinguishability via experiments on sequential machines; Lee and Yannakakis (1994) study state identification, including adaptive tests. Our use here makes protocol and budget explicit in the scientific comparison. It assumes the meanings of the shared actions and outputs; it cannot learn their physical grounding.

| Permitted practice | First affordable distinction |
|---|---|
| Each primitive may be tested, but a reset is required between successive prepare/read actions | None, even if experiments continue indefinitely within this protocol |
| Preparation and read may follow each other in one run | prepare then read, cost 2, gives 0,0,1 versus 0,0,0 |
| A direct probe costing 5 is also allowed | Two ordinary actions remain the minimum-cost witness; the direct probe is the shortest in action count |
| Same ordinary actions, budget 1 | The same witness exists and is beyond budget |

There is no universal finite cutoff from agreement on one-step tests if state capacity is unbounded across candidate models. For each k >= 1, construct candidates that agree until k preparations have accumulated and then disagree on read. Every word of length at most k agrees. The first witness has length k+1. The test exercises k in {1,2,5,16,64}. For a fixed pair and protocol there is a finite bound: a minimum-cost path can be simple in the product. An independent direct word enumeration for all 1,024 ordered pairs of a 32-machine binary family verifies minimum cost for that family. The entire Real-Lang Python experimental suite passed 76 tests locally, eight new. This is a Python experiment alongside the existing Real-Lang bridge.

## Consequence for the larger claim

Even sciences with the same alphabet, the same agreed outputs for elementary experiments, and the same laws can have different experimental reach when they preserve different state between operations or can afford different compositions. Conversely, accessible compositional tests may recover the distinction. This sharpens the older question of theoretical translation: publishing a dictionary of operations can leave unanswered whether the recipients can reproduce their sequencing, state preservation and material cost. The strongest historical claim remains prospective. To challenge it, find records showing that contemporaries using a different representation routinely executed the same inverse design within comparable time, tooling and accuracy. That would favor a convenience account for that particular move. For an external test of the broader MQR claim, choose a documented joint operation, define the preceding reset and material constraints, and register a separating observation before running it.
