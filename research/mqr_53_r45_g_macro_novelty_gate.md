# R45-G — Macro theory gate and explicit hostile test

**Question:** Under what conditions can the criteria for scientific importance themselves be revised rationally, without relying on an unquestionable meta-criterion?

**Already covered:** Dewey on problem constitution; Kuhn on theory-choice values; Friedman on constitutive principles and meta-frameworks; Kitcher on significant questions; Hacking/Kusch on styles and individuation; Longino/Lari on criticism of community goals; Chang on normative pluralism and finite resources; Feest/Steinle/Brigandt and Haueis on concept genesis; Kish Bar-On on unsuccessful foundational reforms. Merely stitching these ideas together is a scholarly synthesis, not a revolution.

**Necessary discriminator for an MQR paper:**
1. Cite a **specific strong statement** from one rival that the historical case contradicts.
2. Independently substantiate the historical event in contemporary documents, not only recent narratives.
3. Offer a distinct standard that delivers a different, arguably better verdict while remaining open to criticism.
4. Run a negative case where keeping more possibilities or granting more exploratory freedom *reduces* overall scientific understanding.
5. Prove a robust category/transition distinction against the arbitrary-granularity objection.

**Stop rule:** If any strong extant theory correctly handles both cases, do not proclaim an original meta-theory. Archive a critical literature article and search for a more difficult case.

**R46:** compare Dewey ch.VI, Brigandt's chapter, Longino ch.VI and Lari's first/second-order framework against a single historically grounded sequence; prioritize actual contradictions, not more names.
