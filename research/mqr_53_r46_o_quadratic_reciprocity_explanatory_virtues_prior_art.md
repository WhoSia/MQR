## Core direct readings

- The mathematical theorem QR for distinct odd primes p,q can be formulated in terms of Legendre symbols as `(p/q)(q/p)=(-1)^(((p-1)/2)((q-1)/2))`. It does not follow that there is a unique satisfactory explanation of its truth.
- Author contrasts *transparency*: a proof makes the particular theorem's central truth-making structure easy to survey, e.g. Gauss/Eisenstein lattice-point counting; and *depth*: a proof embeds the theorem into a broader structural framework, e.g. algebraic number theory, cyclotomic fields, Frobenius and more general reciprocity results.
- §4 includes Gauss's induction proof, Gauss–Eisenstein geometric argument, and Hilbertian algebraic number theory, not three proofs of equal philosophical value. Author argues what counts as explanatory can depend on the epistemic virtue one seeks; he explicitly denies that this forces the claim that all mathematical truths have exactly one explanation.
- The cited remarks of Weyl and Neukirch are presented by D'Alessandro in service of deep structural explanation, providing a cross-link to MQR R46-G/H. Historical quotation via D'Alessandro is **not** a new independently located Weyl first-edition quote.
- Concluding limitations ask whether depth and transparency exhaust explanatory virtues and to what extent QR generalizes. Thus we must not attribute an exhaustive dual taxonomy as an established mathematical theorem.

## Philosophical stakes for MQR

A single proven mathematical proposition can attract judgments under different *explanatory goals* without there being a contradiction about the formal truth of the theorem.
