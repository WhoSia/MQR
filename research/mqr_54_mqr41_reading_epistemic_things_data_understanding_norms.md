## 1. Directly inspected held works (all previously HELD; no acquisition claims)

### 1A. Hans-Jörg Rheinberger (1997), *Toward a History of Epistemic Things: Synthesizing Proteins in the Test Tube*

Original full book (Stanford University Press), Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1DDrn0i1jOZ79tx8VxftIgQmdTrNH4tr8/view . Directly read the prologue and targeted Ch. 2 passages around printed p. 29–30, along with introductory summaries of Ch. 5 and examples of soluble RNA and subsequent techniques. Central distinction: *epistemic things* are initially not fully characterized objects under investigation; *technical objects* are stabilized components and conditions by which experimental systems work. Crucial **role reversal** is possible: an epistemic thing once characterized can become part of the technical repertoire for discovering further things. The text gives genetic-code/poly-U assay and DNA sequencing examples. Local instrumental stability is a historical achievement, not simply an input symbol assumed in advance.

**MQR collision:** 'inquiry creates scientific objects' and 'once unknown target becomes new apparatus' are explicit historical/philosophical prior art. MQR must not present this as novel.

**MQR use:** In the earlier automata/Boolean instrument toy, the operator set A was fixed by the modeler. Rheinberger shows historically why the transition from an investigandum to a reusable operational procedure deserves its own account. Do not conflate an epistemic thing with an already established real entity: at inception its referential role is partially unsettled.

### 1B. Henk W. de Regt (2017), *Understanding Scientific Understanding*

Oxford University Press full held book, Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ykJzrDLwdxW0E_g5HkIAmUH_z6qnsK7e/view . Directly inspected Introduction, chapter 2 §2.2 (understanding as an epistemic skill), chapter 4 §4.2 (intelligibility criteria), passages from historical contextualization (Newton gravity and 19th century modeling), and conclusion. An intelligibility criterion is neither only a subjective feeling nor a timeless fixed form such as 'all valid science must supply a causal gear picture'. The application of scientific models and conceptual skills varies with disciplinary/historical contexts and epistemic goals. **Objectivity ≠ context-free criterion**; de Regt provides context-sensitive criteria and insists on disciplinary/skill constraints rather than arbitrary taste.

**MQR use:** A possible science with a mathematically abstract and operationally potent explanation might satisfy criteria that another science, demanding a mechanical picture, initially rejects. Translation of equations cannot alone guarantee translation of what a satisfactory explanation *is*. This is not a thesis that all explanations are equivalent.

### 1C. Sabina Leonelli (2015), *What Counts as Scientific Data? A Relational Framework*

Author manuscript, Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Dd84tHG9Ms_OeXpfw30U5vRQZbTG6LtZ/view . Directly inspected Abstract, §§1–2, central relational-category definition and final text on data journeys. A material trace/representation functions as *data for a particular research question* through situated use, handling, movement and potential use as evidence. Transfer and formatting changes can change evidentiary utility. A file is not automatically valid scientific evidence because it contains binary numbers or because it has been transported intact.

**MQR use:** UV–Vis detector outputs d are a computational record. Whether d is relevant evidence for a claim about concentrations depends on instrument response, sample preparation, labels/standards and inferential task. **Careful qualification**: Leonelli's relational view does not imply numerical data cannot convey objective physical features or that all data uses are equally good.

### 1D. Kosmas Brousalis (2026), *Scientific Realism and Theory Comparison in the Face of Disciplinary Diversity: Why Go Local?*

Journal for General Philosophy of Science, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10838-026-09773-9 . Original publisher article held Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1jfNYzjk_T_mrkDHYTErBYlBveQZLD3ez/view . Read Abstract, Introduction, §§4–5 passages about localism, generalism, epistemic particularism, and weighing of reasons. Disciplines' diversity **alone** is not logically sufficient to replace cross-case epistemic principles with entirely local case-only justification. Brousalis argues more metaphysical/normative commitment is required for genuine strong localism; his 'pro tanto' generalism distinguishes defeasible generalizable reasons from automatic algorithmic fixed weights.

**MQR use:** MQR's previous 'local source-role typed evidence' and plural scope should not accidentally smuggle in the strong claim that *no general principle can span disciplines*. Local warrant may coexist with defeasible, transferable comparative arguments. **Fairness caveat:** Brousalis targets a specific scientific-realism debate and is not thereby a final refutation of all context-sensitive intelligibility or all methodological localism. The paper disambiguates strong particularism and softer forms of generalism; do not simplify his nuanced argument to 'globalism wins'.

### 1E. Finnur Dellsén (2021), *Understanding Scientific Progress: The Noetic Account*

Synthese, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-021-03289-z, original article held Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uq1hW_cvzOMxGO4mY5XpdlDb952O4-YG/view . Direct reading abstract, §§1–2 and passages on public availability, dependency relations, 'constituting progress' versus 'promoting progress'. Defends scientific progress as the creation of publicly accessible cognitive resources enabling increase in understanding of the dependence structure of a subject matter. Better prediction numbers, a larger set of distinctions, and conceptual proliferation are not by themselves the same as improved scientific understanding. This is a defended philosophical account, not an established universal empirical law.

**MQR use:** The earlier observational-predicate cardinalities 4→16→256 measure finite *formal distinguishability*, not actual cognitive progress. A new experimentally visible class could be an artifact, a useless representational refinement, or useful but still fail to explain dependencies.

## 2. Cross-source triangulation: five logically different achievements

The same experiment may perform one without another:

| Achievement | Question | Most relevant source | Key counterexample/failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D — Detectable differentiation** | Can the apparatus now distinguish two outcomes? | Zong 2006 (spectral stray-light calibration); finite MQR toy | Sensor artifacts or cross-talk produce a spurious distinction; or high noise hides real difference |
| **E — Evidential/data role** | Does a stabilized trace count as data/evidence for this claim, under justified provenance/translation? | Leonelli 2015 | A valid raw count may lack sample identity, calibration or fit-to-question |
| **O — Epistemic-object genesis** | Did a formerly unspecified target become tractable and eventually stabilized as an experimental resource? | Rheinberger 1997 | A new Boolean label is not an historical epistemic object |
| **U — Understanding** | Can users employ the representation to reason about, predict or explain dependencies under appropriate contextual criteria? | de Regt 2017, Dellsén 2021 | Class count rises but dependency model is not improved |
| **N — Normative authority** | How do reasons and evidentiary standards warrant acceptance; can comparisons legitimately cross fields? | Brousalis 2026 | Automatic localism, or a purported universal immutable scoring rule |

This is a **comparative analytic index, not a one-way pipeline**. O can precede or produce D/E; E can change after transport; skills affect U; N affects every other layer. Avoid false monotonicity: a new sensor can yield more traces and less justified evidence; a new model can improve understanding without increasing the number of measured classes. Do not infer O ⇒ N or D ⇒ U.

## 3. Concrete UV–Vis case, kept within source boundaries

Let y = Mx + ε denote a *simplified* instrument-response model for spectral radiance intensity, NOT Beer–Lambert absorbance. Two different (M,x) combinations may give the same y; hardware calibration experiments with known monochromatic sources can constrain M (Zong et al. 2006, held). Zong's actual correction system is richer than this simple two-channel teaching model.

(1) The detector/ADC outputs y: useful encoded trace but not by itself enough to assign all source intensities.
(2) A laser or certified reference probe informs M; separate physical intervention increases inferential warrant for x.
(3) An exported CSV containing y and M becomes *evidence for a specified claim* only with labels, reference and assumptions (Leonelli's relational issue).
(4) A fitted x can be correct while it remains unclear which chemical cause explains a spectral change; prediction/understanding are separate (de Regt, Dellsén).
(5) Cross-lab NIST traceability ensures specified comparisons, not instant truth about all interpretations or natural kinds (NIST SP 260-140).
(6) A new apparatus design that makes a previously unknown variation observable may alter what is considered a good research problem (Rheinberger); whether the resulting category warrants realist commitment depends on scoped, comparative evidence (Brousalis).

**Do not claim a physical experiment was run or that these authors endorsed MQR's five-letter notation.**

## 4. Two adversarial tests for the 'paradigm of paradigms'

### Counterexample A: more observables, no epistemic progress
An instrument introduces an unrecognized, stable, batch-specific artifact; the set of distinguishable traces expands, and the instrument even produces reproducible results within that batch. Without independent apparatus, cross-batch preparation controls and causal checks, the newly identified 'type' may belong to the apparatus rather than target. Formal D↑ does not guarantee E/O/U/N↑. **Even perfect internal replication can share confounder ancestry**.

### Counterexample B: more understanding, no new measurable classes
A new theory organizes fixed, already held observations and explains their dependence structure, improving prediction or counterfactual manipulations without changing the immediate instrument's accessible set of outcomes. U can improve without D changing. Beware that 'better explanation' and 'understanding' are themselves theory/context-sensitive and not deducible solely from metrics.

### Strong localism versus general norms
Two fields may use different criteria for reasonable understanding, and both have context-specific practices. It does NOT follow that there is no cross-field defeasible reason to require calibration transparency, provenance or explanatory accountability. These may be broadly relevant pro tanto reasons whose weight depends on the situation. No total global certification algorithm is warranted, but neither is radical normative particularism automatically entailed.
