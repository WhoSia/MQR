# MQR 5.5 — The constitution of questions and the openness of inquiry
2026-11-28 KST · MQR 47 · OPEN. Pure direction: explain how possible kinds of questions arise, how inquiry makes them tractable, and how an inquiry can itself become a target. No new phase or claim of a universal theory.

## Confronting closest philosophical predecessors
Held Hacking (1992), 'Style' for Historians and Philosophers, Studies in History and Philosophy of Science 23:1–20, Drive 19sjbvVg0Ty30xVxFIgf3unOD7iEbOAhR. Selected pp1–5, 11–15 read. Following Crombie, he treats large-scale styles of reasoning rather than specific disciplinary contents; several styles can cooperate within one field. New styles bring kinds of objects, evidence, propositions and standards into being; he distinguishes the constitution of truth-candidacy from fabrication of each fact. Self-authentication and style-specific stabilization are meant to account for durability; he does not simply say all knowledge is arbitrary or all styles eternally unchangeable. This anticipates much of MQR's founding question.

## A separation at the core of the founding question
Distinguish logical availability of an expression; semantic constitution of its target and truth conditions; historical recognition as a worthwhile question; feasible means for investigating it; warrant for a proposed answer; and sustained autonomous question generation. Logical expressibility does not imply historical recognition or tractability. A newly coined expression does not imply new evidence. Recognizing a problem does not imply a valid answer; a new discipline does not entail a new truth. These dimensions are diagnostics, not a complete ontology of all possible knowledge or an obligatory sequence.
A precise boundary: let T be a first-order theory in L, add fresh predicate R by an explicit definition forall x (R(x) iff phi(x)), phi in L with no other free variables. Every model M of T has a unique expansion interpreting R by the extension of phi in M. Conversely the reduct of any expanded model satisfies T. Thus for every old-language sentence psi, T' entails psi iff T entails psi. Substitute phi for R (renaming bound variables) recursively to eliminate R in new formulas; truth preserved under corresponding assignments by structural induction. Standard semantic conservativity, see SEP Definitions https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2024/entries/definitions/index.html . Not a theorem that conceptual change never generates knowledge, or that all new concepts are explicit definitions. A defined predicate may support reusable arguments, new recognized problems and organization, while changing no old-language logical consequences. No proof-complexity claim. In empirical inquiry a summary map can discard information: graphs C6 and two disjoint triangles have six vertices, all degree 2, but different connectivity. The new connectivity question is answerable from complete adjacency data and not from the degree sequence alone. No observation was added by naming connectivity; the question's information requirement differs. Standard quotient distinction (previous MQR), not new formal work. These examples separate conceptual novelty, empirical discrimination and epistemic feasibility.
More fundamental tension: a framework supplies the conditions under which questions are meaningful, but that fact does not confer epistemic authority on all conclusions or on the framework's application. Within mathematics, logical consistency and proof obligations are relevant; in empirical science, successful new predictions, independent constraints and interventions; in history, source provenance and competing interpretations; normative inquiry has its own argument obligations. None are a universal single scalar or proof that all styles are equivalent. Different frameworks may admit shared subquestions and non-shared ones. Criticize at the points of overlap, operational failure and coherence without presupposing a total translator. Existence of a common criterion in some comparisons not universal comparability.

## A second transformation: when the question becomes a question about the means of knowing
Held Gödel (1947), What is Cantor's Continuum Problem?, American Mathematical Monthly 54:515–525, Drive 1LzUpQQZShDqV55hWxt1GYdYG3jj-ngKv. Selected §§1–4 pp515–521 read. Gödel explicitly links asking the continuum problem to extending number to infinite sets, distinguishes the object question from decidability under specified axioms, and discusses further axioms and their mathematical fruitfulness. He distinguishes relative undecidability from the absence of a determinate answer. His philosophical view of new axioms is not itself an established theorem. His 1947 text predates Cohen; later independence is conditional on consistency of ZFC, as confirmed by SEP https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/continuum-hypothesis/ . This is a concrete second-order shift: from how many real numbers, to what the axioms can prove, to what could justify enlarging the axioms. The last question is not solved by independence alone; one may take a realist or pluralist view, and neither follows solely from the theorem. Mathematical proof and scientific empirical contact are not identical forms of warrant. Prior Cantor 1872→1879 and Rav 1999 make the lineage explicit. The genesis of the continuum question is not the genesis of all set theory.
Possible disciplines may arise from transformation of targets, inferential rules or the aims of inquiry, including questions about earlier inquiry itself. A theory of disciplinary genesis must account for why these transformations become productive, including failures, rather than list all formally possible expressions retrospectively. Candidate empirical/historical test: compare genuinely dated new questions, earlier resources, later operations and independently corroborated uptake; distinguish hypothetical expressibility in a predecessor formalism from what predecessor scholars actually recognized. For a new axiom, record new consequences separately from shortcuts to already derivable consequences. For an empirical concept, record new measurements separately from relabeling and altered targets. No general account of why particular questions arise has been proved.

Current synthesis shifts the center from institutional birth to the constitution of objects, questions and standards, not a new universal metalanguage.
