# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · Record 21 — Institutions as selectors of research questions: competing mechanisms

**2026-11-22 KST.** New BFS branch from MQR founding programme, intentionally away from infinite finite-state toy models. Source-grounded comparison and precise, falsifiable proposal. Phase 5.4 OPEN.

## 1. Why this matters
The user's request explicitly connected 'university entrance, interview, small societies' to science arising under information constraints. The comparison is about how institutions select **which explanations, methods and questions are permitted to be pursued**, not about coaching admissions, scoring real individuals or treating school interviews as scientific experiments.

A prior conceptual vulnerability in MQR R07–R20: knowing whether one *scientist* should explore or exploit says little about whether the *institution* allows diverse scientists to undertake those lines of work in the first place. Selection changes the question-generation distribution before experiments occur.

## 2. Direct reading of two held papers that qualify simplistic anti-selection rhetoric

**Dubova, Moskvichev & Zollman (2026)**, DOI 10.1177/26339137261421577, held `1XI4OoLOa4rrfA26mUPMULBb0ErwrCYrP` directly read in R07; theory-motivated experiment selection can overfit selected samples, while random exploration can give better generalization across modeled high-dimensional worlds.

These three works pull in distinct directions. One cannot say 'competition always kills novelty', 'peer review always kills novelty', 'random experiment choice is always best', or the corresponding unconditional inverses.

## 3. A precise MQR structural proposal
At least **three selection operators** jointly shape historically observed scientific knowledge:
- S_people: access to training, laboratories, entry decisions and recognition of researchers (admissions/interviews are illustrative; actual evaluative justice is separate).
- S_questions: which problems receive attention, resources, prestige or funding, and whether new question templates can be proposed at all.
- S_evidence: which sites are sampled, experiments performed and successes published or circulated.

These operators are not interchangeable. Conditioning on S_people can create Berkson/collider correlations among attributes. Competition in S_questions can either push bold, uncertain projects (Gross & Bergstrom model) or suppress risk depending on payoff structures. S_evidence can make a scientist's model appear successful on selected samples without representative success (Dubova et al.). An institution can fund unusual questions yet still use a strongly biased sampling protocol; conversely a highly standardized interview can make a narrow selection more reproducible without proving it predicts original scientific imagination.

This is a **typed analytical decomposition**, not a causal theorem. A genuine test must estimate these selections separately, with preselection populations, time stamps, experimental domain support and clear outcome definitions. Absent such data, 'selection caused scientific stagnation' is not licensed.

## 4. Strong rival and positive controls
Rival explanation: ordinary selection-bias causal diagrams, economics of science and resource-rational cognition already account for all mechanisms. If so MQR's value is integrative source-grounded comparison rather than an unprecedented general law.

Positive cases to demand:
- documented selection/competition that **actually increased risky high-payoff theory search**;
- a structured selection program that improves a legitimate evaluation criterion without claiming to measure originality;
- a small research society with dense communication that genuinely transmits new independent experimental methods;
- randomized funding/allocation that performs worse on a narrow, well-known design space.

Negative controls:
- novelty defined by publication count but no real new observable or test;
- repeated reports all sharing a single failed evidence ancestry;
- broader selection producing greater breadth merely because total budget increased;
- a model that defines the successful future question ex post, rather than records a contemporaneous newly asked question.

## 5. A researchable 'scientific flourishing under constraints' claim
**Conjecture, not result:** for resource-limited societies, maintaining at least some protected capacity for *question invention and method transmission*, separate from immediate success metrics, can sustain long-run scientific creativity under some institution structures. This proposal may be invalidated by costs, expertise demands, coordination needs or misleading anomalies. The capacity cannot be evaluated merely by number of novel titles or syntactic hypotheses; it requires a previously unavailable testable distinction, adopted method or resolved anomaly and source-dated records.

This links the possible-sciences root to ordinary social institutions without conflating science with admission selection. It also blocks a romantic claim that isolation and poverty necessarily make science flower.
