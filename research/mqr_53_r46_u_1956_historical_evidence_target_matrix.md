The reported counts in different tables are **different source experiments**, so cross-table differences cannot be interpreted as if they shared the same source sample and controls. None of the rows justifies a hypothesis test without measured variability and replication structure.

## Within-Table VI descriptive contrasts, one specific historical assay

- Light-associated difference under oxygen: 45.7 − 27.6 = **18.1** cpm/mg.
- Light-associated difference under nitrogen: 18.6 − 15.5 = **3.1** cpm/mg.
- Oxygen-associated difference in light: 45.7 − 18.6 = **27.1** cpm/mg.
- Oxygen-associated difference in dark: 27.6 − 15.5 = **12.1** cpm/mg.
- Difference-in-differences: 18.1 − 3.1 = **15.0** cpm/mg, equivalent to 27.1 − 12.1. This is **merely an arithmetic interaction descriptor**. Without replicated balanced design, measurement uncertainty, nuisance control and mechanistic exclusion, there is no licensed causal interaction magnitude.

The source's own summary interprets light/oxygen as evidence for a connection to photosynthetic energy, but its text also acknowledges uncertain actual pathways and failed supplementation tests. Our contrast is not a newly discovered biochemical mechanism.

## Philosophical contest: make the question explicit

The predicate `observed radioactivity in a centrifugally defined fraction` is a different target from `cell-free generation of specified viral protein`. Feest's tool-level account predicts inquiry can remain productive in the former despite failure of the latter; Borsboom's criterion simply says the latter attribute is **not established as a valid cause of the specified test readout**. These statements are logically compatible. Green's semantic-identification critique is conditional on a strong `concept := operation` identity not asserted by these 1956 authors, who explicitly mark their fractions as unpurified and the supposed contaminant as an alternative.

**A more severe historical question** would ask whether the different tests are truly *independent* enough to connect the same proposed biochemical target. The original's two biological preparations are related but not interventionally equivalent; it would be a category error to compare the virus signal in intact disks with a general protein label in disrupted extracts as though one were direct confirmation and one a direct refutation of the identical experiment.

## Novelty adjudication

Original data verifies that two distinct target predicates have different outcomes, but **this distinction was known to the original authors and reconstructed by Rheinberger**. It is not new explanatory content for MQR. Existing Green/Feest/Borsboom/Garner-style criteria are consistent with the source if each is assigned its appropriate claim. **R46-U: prior-art sufficient for present target-identity claim; novelty HOLD.**
