## 1. Six distinct questions behind '논리 기호'

Notation warning: the following is a **researcher's comparative vocabulary** (not a universal axiomatization of science).

1. **Physical inscription / encoding:** a written glyph, instrument display or bit pattern. Different ink marks or coding schemes can encode the same syntax.
2. **Syntax / derivability:** well-formed formulas and a proof/inference relation `⊢`. A formally legitimate derivation need not be an empirically correct prediction about a physical system.
3. **Semantics / interpretation:** `⟦φ⟧_M` relative to a model `M`; `⊨` is model-relative satisfaction, not automatically a claim that the model is the real world.
4. **Referential or sensorimotor grounding:** whether symbols reliably denote/track or enable intervention on shared physical processes. There can be successful grammar manipulation without established reference.
5. **Question and experiment grammar:** what propositions and counterfactual questions are *formulable and executable* by the practice. A changed operation or instrument may alter the observable vocabulary; a language may change which research problems it can express.
6. **Epistemic authority:** what scientists or a community take as good evidence, explanation, scope and defeaters. Effective mathematical communication alone does not confer universal authority on a scientific interpretation.

The critical inference rules that must **NOT** be smuggled in:
`same glyphs ≠ same meaning`, `same computation ≠ same measured physical quantity`, `same finitely observed data ≠ same world ontology`, `productive analogy ≠ literally true mechanical analogy`, `untranslatable word-for-word ≠ wholly incomparable sciences`, `independently discovered concept ≠ independent physical reality created ex nihilo`.

## 2. Five direct held scholarly anchors, their actual limits

**Harnad (1990), 'The Symbol Grounding Problem', Physica D 42:335–346.** Directly read abstract and discussion of iconic/categorical representations and sensorimotor affordances; held Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1AiXiFy2aZibiHVlR7tL7yhfstHWo5yDm/view . Mere syntactic tokens and symbol-to-symbol dictionaries do not by themselves supply non-circular intrinsic grounding in embodied categories; Harnad proposes icons, categorical discrimination and hybrid symbolic representations. This is **one proposed solution** for cognitive systems, not a theorem that every physical-meaningful scientific notation must be neurologically instantiated in that exact way.

**Kuhn (1983), 'Commensurability, Comparability, Communicability', PSA 1982 Vol. 2:669–688.** Directly read opening discussion and the distinction between acquiring a new language and translating it completely; held Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Ep81ueXQGpa5lqeDZfKLj9na0jSYRHxc/view . Local incommensurability need not destroy comparison or communication. Foreign lexicons can be learned without translation into every item of one's native lexicon. This warns against both 'all possible sciences are trivially translatable' and 'if primitive logic differs, no communication is possible'.

**Herbert A. Simon (1973), 'Does Scientific Discovery Have a Logic?', Philosophy of Science 40(4):471ff.** Direct abstract/introduction held Drive https://drive.google.com/file/d/1iD-HXOdyy2JEJ4bnIuCZfVAf6WOD41fj/view . ## 3. A rigorous scaffold for comparing distinct sciences, without pretending to have discovered a new formalism

Consider a scientific practice `P` specified for analysis, not for metaphysical identity, by an **open interface**

`P = (L, ⊢, Sem, A, Obs, Prep, E, N)`

where
- `L`: vocabulary of expressible hypotheses/questions, not simply fonts or Unicode operators;
- `⊢`: inferential norms (logic may vary; a different logic need not imply different observable physical predictions);
- `Sem`: domain-restricted interpretation of expressions in models;
- `A`: material operations / composed experimental protocols;
- `Obs`: observable signals, instruments and data coding;
- `Prep`: ability to identify and prepare a replicable target condition;
- `E`: evidence records, provenance, state/batch/cross-instrument ancestry;
- `N`: mutable standards for what counts as a good explanation or validated result.

This is only an index to questions and explicitly **does not** specify a unique deductive law for human sciences. It can be changed as MQR learns.

For two practices `P` and `Q`, do not assume a translator `T:L_P→L_Q` exists. If a candidate `T`, an empirical preparation bridge `b`, and experiment bridge `F:A_P^*→A_Q^*` are learned, a **conditional** observational correspondence test can ask, for selected shared preparation histories `h`:

`Obs_Q(F(a), b(h)) ≈ D(Obs_P(a,h))`

where `D` is an empirically calibrated data-conversion map and `≈` has a *stated* uncertainty and intervention scope. All `b,F,D` are *research outcomes to seek*, NOT common inputs gifted to an alien civilization. In addition, partial preservation of certain `⊢` relations under `T` can be tested on a specified fragment, but derivability across different logical consequence systems must not be silently assumed. Similarity under finitely many shared interventions cannot prove full structural isomorphism or common ontological reference.

**Two positive options**: (i) collaborative co-design of a shared perturbation, creating a partial empirical anchor, and (ii) development of a new hybrid metrological language for parts of both sciences without full one-to-one translation. **Two non-success options**: (i) apparently matching displays driven by different physical causal routes and (ii) shared finite observations while inaccessible interventions disagree. The goal is to find discriminating experiments rather than declare ultimate impossible communication.

## 4. Genesis of questions: a stronger formulation

An ordinary within-paradigm problem asks whether `φ∈L` is supported by evidence. An *origin* problem asks why `φ` enters `L` at all, and who can justify the new referents and experiment types required to state it. Not a claim that every newly formed symbol denotes a novel real kind.

Possible changes:
- **notation change:** conservative renaming/encoding, no new empirical question;
- **definitional extension:** new shorthand for an old operation/property, no necessary new empirical distinction;
- **inferential extension:** new proof methods or axioms enlarge consequences (assumptions and consistency require checking);
- **experimental/sensor extension:** a previously inaccessible quantity becomes evaluable, with independent calibration/reliability evidence;
- **semantic/conceptual shift:** an operational label acquires a new relational/function explanatory use (Rheinberger: soluble RNA to transfer RNA);
- **normative shift:** standards of what can count as a legitimate problem, explanation and validity change (de Regt / Kuhn / Brousalis).

**These changes may overlap and are NOT an obligatory one-way ladder.** A language can gain expressive power without improving truth; a new theory can improve causal understanding without new sensor values.

The early 2026-02-05 founding HWPX is most charitably reconstructed as: science **generates representations of structural possibilities and modalities** (what models permit and forbid), and these reconstructions govern what questions scientists recognize as meaningful. It does **not** imply scientists physically create nature by drawing a state space.

## 6. Two sharp countermodels for the research agenda

**A. Complete digital reproduction without semantic / empirical guarantee:** an ADC readout can be replayed bit for bit by a machine simulating the output without touching the sample. Hence output functional equivalence does not entail original sample reference or measurement legitimacy. A meaningful instrument claim must specify some independently controlled physical input-output intervention scope. (No inference that digital systems *cannot* perform physical measurements: they can when coupled to real transducers.)

**B. Stable language pair with no unique reference bridge:** two formal theories generate the same finite sequence of predictions for all currently allowed preparations. Several mappings of terms to physical referents remain consistent with data. They can still be compared operationally and partially learned. A newly constructed experiment can shrink the ambiguity but finite success does not establish a unique translation across all possible future contexts. This is standard underdetermination, not a theorem first proved by MQR41.
