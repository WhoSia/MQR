## II. Factual 1847 Regnault comparison **as transcribed by Chang**, not raw 1847 OCR

Chang ch2 Table 2.5, adapted from Regnault 1847 printed p.181:
- Same gas species, air at different initial pressure/density, across paired temperature readings roughly 95.57–339.68 °C: reported A−A' = `0.00,+0.17,-0.02,-0.04,+0.22,+0.29 °C` (6 measurements). Maximum absolute difference 0.29 °C. These are **historical point pairs**, not six independent modern instrument calibration replicates.
- Chang ch2 Table 2.6, adapted from Regnault 1847 printed p.188: air versus sulfuric-acid gas under another instrument/composition pair: `0.00,+0.46,+1.21,+1.57,+1.73,+2.09,+2.95 °C` across roughly 97.56–320.68°C, maximum 2.95°C. These are **not pointwise the same temperatures, instruments and experimental series as Table 2.5**, so don't compute formal inter-series hypothesis tests or causal effect ratios from two maxima.
- Regnault's reported interpretation per Chang (pp.80–82) was broad comparability of AIR thermometer for the tested pressures, but evidence against unrestricted interchangeability of every gas under the same simple gas-law model. Without absolute independent temperature truth one cannot conclude the gas instrument reading 'wrong' in the foundational sense merely from discrepancy; this is Tal's model-dependent individuation problem.
- **Numerical inference only:** Within reported table, gas-type change displays larger and systematically directed paired difference than tested air-pressure change. It challenges *universal* equal-sensitivity/gas-interchangeability extrapolation but does not rule out a justified thermodynamic temperature concept after refined calibration or ideal-gas extrapolation.

## III. Wedgwood high-temperature case is NOT Regnault's same-procedure comparison

Chang ch3 pp.118–126: Wedgwood pioneered a high-range clay-contraction pyrometer for kilns, where ordinary liquid-in-glass scales fail. He attempted to connect the new scale to a Fahrenheit range with linked instruments and metal expansion, with serious sensitivity to assumptions regarding initial red heat and linear relations. First 1782 article reports shrinkage usable as highest-temperature memory in relevant regimes. Later 1786 remarks document formation-dependent clay-piece variation. These are materially different observables and domains; neither series by itself measures the same fixed 'temperature unit' without empirically defended bridge models.

The actual *interesting tension* is:
(a) within-production consistency of a clay shrinkage readout,
(b) between-production lot comparability,
(c) high-temperature extension from familiar low-temperature standards,
(d) extrapolation under unknown nonlinearity.
They represent different empirical tasks. A calibration *within* one production batch does not validate an inter-batch or Fahrenheit conversion. A nearby competing pyrometer matching an old scale does not automatically show its measurement identity. This is directly analyzed already by Chang/Tal and should not be claimed as an original MQR philosophical theorem.
