## 1. Prior art collision must be stated plainly
Rheinberger explicitly describes experimental systems not merely as machines that answer questions but as vehicles for *materializing previously unasked questions*. His primary program already understands production of scientific differences, apparatus and scientific objects as mutually transforming; moreover his distinction between **epistemic things** (objects whose identity is still materially and conceptually underdetermined) and **technical objects** (relatively stabilized instrumental conditions) is a very close intellectual predecessor of MQR's R09–R33 programme.

Thus the slogan 'instruments create questions' **is not a new MQR discovery**, and neither is 'technical apparatus and epistemic object change roles over time'. This serious prior-art fact strengthens the demand for MQR to produce a discriminating historical explanation/operational criterion instead of renaming Rheinberger or Feest.

## 2. Scientific object ↔ technical object: nontrivial reversal
Rheinberger p.29–30 describes technical experimental conditions as both enabling and restricting epistemic objects. As a research object stabilizes, it may become part of the technical toolkit used to investigate other objects. Example in his text: molecular biological sequencing techniques once experimentally uncertain become standardized laboratory procedures. In the same protein synthesis setting a polynucleotide template assay first helps investigate the genetic code and then becomes a routine probe of ribosome function.

This is a bidirectional process:
  Epistemic unknown E -> research observation and stabilization -> usable technique T;
  technique T -> new experimental configurations -> later epistemic unknown E'.
It is not a guaranteed linear sequence: tests may fail to stabilize a target and technical standards may themselves become research problems.

## 3. Especially strong negative case: an allegedly improved purification removes the signal
Rheinberger's Chapter 8 account (book p.152) discusses early 'soluble RNA' research in Zamecnik's laboratory, where extracting radioactively marked RNA with a hot salt solution happened to work under acidic conditions. A subsequent supposedly 'improved' extraction method using neutral pH increased RNA yield but **eliminated the measured radioactivity**. The author's historical reconstruction therefore documents a possible **anti-monotonicity**:
  better apparent yield/technical standard -> worse ability to observe the emerging signal of scientific interest.

Precise limit: this is historical narration by Rheinberger using documented lab notes, **not MQR's physical re-experiment**. It does not imply neutral pH universally eliminates RNA radioactivity or determine the exact chemical mechanism without primary notebook/reagent audits. The original issue could include adsorption, chemical degradation, fraction contamination or assay condition changes; these are possibilities, not documented causal findings in this note.

The methodological consequence is decisive for an overly simple MQR policy: '**increase instrumentation accuracy / extraction yield**' is not a universally safe surrogate for '**increase relevant knowledge**'. One can improve a technical metric while erasing a different observation channel. The metric, object of interest and unknown confounds must remain distinct.

## 4. A third-domain falsification opportunity
Previously:
- **Faraday 1821**: material motion and apparatus spread, theory interpretation remained disputed.
- **Implicit memory 1981–1989**: test contrast preceded a 1985 label, modality/encoding experiments subsequently narrowed construct interpretation.
- **Soluble RNA 1950s**: manipulations and fraction measurements co-produced an unstable epistemic object; an apparently superior protocol might remove an informative signal.

Cross-domain candidate thesis T: **the ability to generate informative differences can be enhanced or suppressed by apparently beneficial changes in methods, and therefore cannot be inferred merely from immediate instrument yield, prediction fit or number of accepted hypotheses**.

This is a philosophically consequential *synthesis* with existing Rheinberger and Feest prior art, not original formal law. Its empirical warrant needs the method's actual inputs/outputs and the rival explanations for signal loss.

## 5. A real paper-level adverse test
Freeze (1) the investigator's announced technical optimization objective (RNA yield), (2) observed target signal (radiolabel signal), (3) exact protocol alteration (acidic/hot-salt vs neutral extraction) and date, (4) alternative mechanisms that might explain signal disappearance, (5) any subsequent recovery or conceptual reclassification. If optimizing yield predictably preserves or improves signal after controlling composition, an anti-monotonic technical tradeoff thesis is falsified for that protocol; a single anecdote cannot generalize across all science.

**One more severe limitation:** richer experimental systems can also mislead and produce false 'epistemic things' through artifacts. Novel differences must pass independent stability and rival probes before becoming evidence of natural kinds.
