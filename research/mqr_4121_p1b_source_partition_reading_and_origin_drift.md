## Close reading, section A — Griffiths & Potts pp. 1–4 (experimental and conceptual setup)
*Original problem*: distinguish two chemical species from a family of indicator spectra when pH and temperature change. Authors explicitly state that an isosbestic intersection alone does not establish a true two-species equilibrium; require *internal linearity relationships across all wavelengths*, with component spectra appropriately invariant, and *additional chemical evidence* for assigning species. They cite preexisting work rather than claiming that this distinction was uniquely their discovery.
*Physical setup*: methyl red, methyl orange, phenolphthalein; ionic strength controls (KNO3/KCl), Cary 14H, 1-cm cells. During acid titration measured temperature rises can occur due to neutralization (~2–3 degC reported); the protocol attempts control/equilibration. Spectra were digitized on even wavelength grids, some manually at ~5 nm intervals. Authors warn missing a single sequential digitized point shifts following inferred wavelengths.
*Margin*: The wavelength-index hazard is an explicit example of **representational transport failure** even before chemical inference. Temperature shift confounds a nominal pH intervention: 1.12–1.15 intervention sequence and sample-state signatures are not philosophical decoration.

## Close reading, section B — Griffiths & Potts internal linearity / results / methyl red
The paper defines a spectral *internal linearity* test and reports pH-dependent indicator results. In methyl red, the authors interpret relevant portions of the titration range with **three chemical species** while the concentration of an intermediate is small and proportional to another yellow form over a restricted range. This makes a **pseudo-two-species** spectral response possible, including apparently two-dimensional interpretation; at higher pH the proportionality changes and internal linearity weakens. Their explanation is *parameter- and domain-scoped*, not a universal chemical two-species theorem. The intermediate's similar spectrum makes experimental discrimination harder; small departures otherwise risk dismissal as measurement noise.
*Mathematical reconstruction*: writing sampled absorbance vectors as `a(t) = E c(t)`, fixed component spectra E and an affine one-parameter composition family `c(t)=c_0+u h(t)` imply `a(t)=E c_0+E u h(t)`, a straight line in spectral-vector space. **Three or more real chemical species may produce such a line** if their composition changes are constrained to one degree of freedom. Conversely, measured non-collinearity can arise from temperature-dependent E or instrument artifacts, not exclusively additional chemical species.
*Important firewall*: spectral affine rank is a statement about observation family and linearity assumptions; it cannot identify the cardinality of molecular species without an independently justified chemical model.

## Close reading, section C — Zong 2007
Zong et al. describe stray light by measured spectral line spread functions, transform their representation into a correction matrix, and apply a matrix transformation to raw spectral readings. One test uses a green bandpass filtered lamp and an array spectroradiometer (reported spectral span 200–870 nm); below 420 nm corrected stray-light signals drop from more than 4×10^-4 to about 5×10^-6 *in this example*. This is **a real published instrument demonstration**, not a test performed in MQR. The matrix captures calibration-dependent instrument response and may compensate further optical effects represented in the response.
*Margin*: A cleaner corrected curve does not by itself establish analyte speciation. A correction matrix requires its own source quality and uncertainty; this directly links MQR-4.114 provenance to 4.121 source/instrument model separation.

## Close reading, section D — Zong 2010
Zong explicitly analyzes correction uncertainty from measured spectral line spread functions, the instrument, and calibration sources; flags out-of-range stray light, blooming/smearing, detector nonlinearity, dark signals and readout quality as contributors. Stray-light correction may reduce error one to two orders of magnitude in tested settings; correction results still carry a budget. Do not equate algorithmic correction with certain recovery of a unique physical truth.
*Margin*: an output-level linearization is not chemical mechanistic discrimination. Test independent sample conditions and source family.

## Competing explanation design
Given a concave absorption-vs-concentration curve, maintain at least:
- `H_SL`: nonzero instrumental stray light (saturation from an additive transmitted-light component);
- `H_BW`: heterogeneous k(lambda) under finite spectral bandpass (log of weighted transmission);
- `H_CHEM`: evolving species distribution or temperature-dependent absorption coefficients.
Useful discriminators: vary monochromator slit/bandwidth, vary out-of-band light suppression or optical filters, separately calibrate spectral instrument response, collect full-wavelength residuals across controlled pH/temperature and independent chemical evidence. A test can perturb more than one variable, so require controls and explicit bridge assumptions before attributing causality.
**Constructed counterexample**: if `a(t)=a_0+v h(t)`, a two-species model and a three-species model restricted to one-dimensional composition motion can have identical spectra at all sampled t. A fit or isosbestic point cannot count latent species.
