# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · R28 — Construct validation is a serious rival to a new science-of-question-generation theory

**2026-11-22 KST.** Direct reading of three *already-held* full original scholarly articles, not a request for new PDFs. MQR 5.4 OPEN. This unit directly challenges MQR's intellectual priority claim: the distinction between operation, interpretation, and action is well established in measurement validation.

## 1. Full originals available and directly inspected, source levels
- **Campbell & Fiske (1959)**, 'Convergent and Discriminant Validation by the Multitrait-Multimethod Matrix', *Psychological Bulletin* 56(2):81–105. Held full original `1rpeKl0VfcGR0PYi4nqEmDNCLFdbKfX03`, directly fetched readable text (72,987 characters), opening and criteria passages inspected. Main insight: agreement among independent methods and adequate divergence of distinct traits are both needed; a *trait–method unit* has shared variance from method as well as underlying trait. Independent procedures matter, and simple repeated same-method score correlation is not enough for strong construct claims.
- **Messick (1995)**, 'Validity of Psychological Assessment: Validation of Inferences From Persons' Responses and Performances as Scientific Inquiry Into Score Meaning', held full original `1B2kz4d0AvrAtt9zcmefMqGcQ9ybjeN2Y`, directly fetched (55,820 characters), abstract and key passages inspected. The paper warns against reducing validity to a test's intrinsic property; what is assessed is interpretation/meaning and action in context. Consequential effects and alternative constructs are a critical dimension; no single metric licenses all uses.
- **Kane (2013)**, 'Validating the Interpretations and Uses of Test Scores', *Journal of Educational Measurement* 50(1):1–73, held full original `1nbQUgK4kFkjKS4X9Op_q2e5H-Vgm8epM`, directly fetched (256,417 characters), abstract and argument passages inspected. Claims grounded in observed test responses need an explicit **interpretation-and-use argument** (IUA) specifying each inference, assumption and evidence. More ambitious claims require stronger support; validating score interpretation does NOT automatically validate score use, and the use may change over time. This is not an MQR discovery.

Along with **Feest (2005, 2010, 2025 intro)** as audited R25/R27, this is extensive prior art that MQR cannot ignore.

## 2. Unifying without collapsing different purposes
MQR question-generation stages:
(a) an operational contrast becomes executable;
(b) results can be interpreted as evidence of some more general capability or mechanism;
(c) a proposed object/concept receives independent construct validity;
(d) the concept is used for an actual consequential decision, selection, policy or translation.

Kane and Messick make (b)->(d) inferential obligations **and social consequences** explicit in testing/selection settings. Campbell/Fiske make method-dependence and convergent/discriminant patterns necessary checks, not a logical uniqueness guarantee. Feest describes a provisional concept's capacity to guide research *before* validation is complete. Together they resist two mistakes:
1. **Logical shortcut:** a test or clinical/academic interview elicits an observable result; therefore the score directly equals a stable latent research/learning ability.
2. **Instrument shortcut:** a new operation partitions a mathematical state set; therefore a new natural kind with an independent existence has been discovered.

Neither analogy supports real student scoring, high-stakes test evaluation or diagnosing an individual. Epistemic methodology only.

## 3. Deliberate cross-domain counterfactual matrix

| Claim one might make | What evidence is actually needed | False shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Same task produces repeatable outcomes | Repetition across appropriate conditions, measurement reliability | 'repeatable' therefore 'unique cause' |
| Two task protocols address the same target construct | cross-method convergent and discriminant checks; process theories and known confounds | 'two correlations' therefore 'same natural kind' |
| Concept names a unique mechanism | perturbation/causal tests that separate rival mechanisms, transport/replication in relevant conditions | 'dissociation' therefore 'two modules' |
| Interview score predicts target future performance | independent target-defined criterion, representative follow-up, selection-collider adjustment | 'structured' therefore 'predicts original creativity' |
| Selection decision is acceptable | prediction validity AND decision utility, fairness, social consequences and institutional goal | 'valid score' therefore 'valid treatment' |
| Faraday and Ampère share a scientific language | operational alignment, distinct theoretic predictions, interpretive commitments and tested mappings | 'reproduced motor' therefore 'identical ontology' |

**Important caveat:** A multitrait-multimethod matrix is a useful diagnostic, not a universal proof of a construct's real referent. Values can share latent method artifacts, and measures may be independent by name but not by calibration ancestry. MQR's N-axis typed evidence ancestry fits here as a more explicit audit device, not a newly invented convergent validation principle.

## 4. Where MQR might STILL add independent value
Measurement validity theory asks what an existing test/score *licenses*. An open-horizon theory asks **how new testable contrasts, methods, concepts and task classes become available in the first place**, and how this changes the scope of previously justified interpretations. It must tie any added explanatory value to documented changes in available actions, inherited practices, institution funding and world-facing tests, not just add a generic 'question-generation' label to Kane's IUA.

Provisional claim:
> A valid argument today may become insufficient for an expanded future question/operation repertoire without any change in its original raw readings; the changed task scope, not a contradiction in the old record, triggers the need for new validation.

R18–R20's finite partition refinements formally illustrate this, but similar ideas appear in Messick/Kane (changing uses/interpretations), sequential decision theory and instrument validation. Any historical theory relying on this must show the newly feasible operation was actually accessible in the period claimed.

## 5. Adversarial gate, non-trivial failure possible
MQR explanatory claim fails as an *independently needed* theory if all case sequences can be reconstructed by established validation/update theory and existing history-of-science scholarship without novel differentiated predictions or case discoveries. In that event retain the work as rigorous integrative epistemology and explicitly drop novelty rhetoric. Conversely a source-dated sequence in which an instrument/procedure diffusion creates a new conceptual question while scores/observations and inference norms remain stable would sharpen the explanandum beyond ordinary test validation.
