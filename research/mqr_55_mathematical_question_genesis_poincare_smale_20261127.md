## Distinct targets and explicit bridge
Poincaré's printed §19 treats intersection of two asymptotic curves in the specified dynamics. Smale begins with differential equations as a motivation for studying diffeomorphisms, but says that translating back can be secondary after the simpler discrete setting yields results. On p.775 he gives a theorem for a transverse homoclinic point in the specified differentiable dynamics setting: some iterate restricted to an invariant Cantor set is topologically conjugate to a shift automorphism. The corollary locates periodic points in every neighborhood of such a point. Intersection alone must not be substituted for transversality/hyperbolic setting and the invariant-set/iterate scope must not be dropped. We do not determine whether every required hypothesis holds in Poincaré's original case. Neither the existence of many intersections nor a visually complex plot is a proof of the entire modern chaos package.
Smale also gives a concrete new mathematical task on the symbolic model: count periodic points and least periods. For the full shift on two symbols, the number fixed by the m-th iterate is 2^m. Let A_m be the number with least period m. Then 2^m=sum_{d|m} A_d. Möbius inversion gives A_m=sum_{d|m} mu(d) 2^(m/d); least-period orbit count is A_m/m. At m=1,2,3,4,6 the orbit counts are 2,1,2,3,9. This is standard elementary symbolic dynamics (Smale discusses the general lambda-symbol expression); not new math or a numerical simulation. These counts concern the full symbolic shift, not counts of all periodic trajectories of the original physical system. Under the stated conjugacy they transport to the specified invariant set and iterate only. A return-map interpretation requires a separately justified correspondence to the physical flow.
This illustrates a change in research object: from asymptotic geometry of specified equations, to a general class of maps and invariant subsets, to discrete combinatorial properties with precise transport conditions. It does not establish that such questions were unformulable before Smale or that a particular earlier source was historically indispensable. Smale acknowledges Poincaré, Birkhoff, Hadamard/Morse and reports that his own horseshoe arose while studying a variant of van der Pol's equation in Levinson's work. His account supplies counterpressure against one-line Poincaré→Smale causal genealogy; those intermediate sources and the actual chronological manuscripts are not newly read. Zuchowski's inspiration thesis is compatible with multiple ancestry, not proof of a unique causal route.
