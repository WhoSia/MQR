## Distinguishing physical causal hypotheses

Let Q denote sample heat removed, m_obs the mass drained and weighed, L melting latent heat. Correct ice at near melting equilibrium and negligible loss makes Q approximately L m_obs. If some initially sub-zero ice with mass M absorbs sensible heat, Q = L m_melt + M c_i (0-T_i)+ Q_device+Q_environment (define sign convention net heat into colder calorimeter). Separately, mass conservation at drain is m_obs = m_melt - m_refrozen - m_retained + m_spurious for a specified collection interval, with each amount mass units. Thus the mapping Q -> m_obs is not one-to-one from just reading a drain mass in the presence of unobserved initial ice temperature, freezing, drainage and capillary retention. Several distinct mechanisms produce lower drain output: sub-zero ice sensible heat term, refreezing in a passage, delayed drainage and retained meltwater. Physical model is a candidate explanation using modern physics, NOT attributed to Wedgwood.

Crucial test separation: (1) textual omission: verify Monthly Review exact review has no below-zero ice warning, by page-image collation against French original; (2) uptake: Wedgwood's notebook documents which passage he actually followed; (3) mechanism: observations of decreasing collected water and near-frozen drain substantiate the physicochemical hypothesis but do not prove missing prose was the unique cause; (4) historical consequence: determine when he abandoned this particular calibration strategy, distinguishing his 1784 published evaluation and other silver-bridge calibration attempts. ## Historical chronology pitfalls

The 1783 first separate *Mémoire sur la chaleur* cover reading date printed as 28 June conflicts with the 18 June session as reported in later Laplace collected volume; distinguish print metadata from actual meeting. The French Academy 1780 *volume* was published 1784; publication-volume year is not publication-date. The 1783 English summary existed in at least two notices, not one. The 1784 Wedgwood 26pp pamphlet reproduces his 1782 work; it is not his 1784 silver comparison.
