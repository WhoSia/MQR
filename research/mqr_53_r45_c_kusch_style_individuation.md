# R45-C — Kusch's critique of Hacking and the meta-style trap

**Source:** [Kusch (2010), *Hacking's Historical Epistemology: A Critique of Styles of Reasoning*, SHPS 41:158–173](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1dAXhPcnhe1d9gJuaEfrs1xNwP6x3W0l3/view); original article verified and targeted argument read.

Kusch reconstructs Hacking's evolving styles from Crombie and argues: (1) the inherited historiography may be outdated; (2) Hacking does not succeed in evading epistemic relativism; (3) the theory does not adequately specify **how to individuate styles of reasoning**. A style is said to open new truth candidates and standards; but overlapping or evolving styles threaten arbitrary grain choices. Kusch considers whether inference to the best explanation could justify a choice between broad, long-lived or narrowly dated style descriptions.

**Challenge to MQR:** An allegedly new 'paradigm of paradigms' must specify identity conditions for its own levels and transitions. Without a principled way to distinguish a change of local technique, research agenda, reasoning style and metanorm, counting 'meta-revolutions' is rhetorical.

**Counterexample blueprint:** Two historians partition the same sequence into one continuous style vs four short-lived styles. A framework that assigns different rational-progress verdicts purely because of the arbitrary partition is non-invariant. This is a constructed methodological demand, not an established historical refutation.

**Novelty status:** Kusch already states the central problem; a fresh criterion must actually outperform his objection.
