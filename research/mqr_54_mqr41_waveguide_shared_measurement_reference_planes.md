## A concrete historical interface

Read [Marcuvitz, ed., Waveguide Handbook (1951)](https://www.introni.it/pdf/10%20-%20Waveguide%20Handbook.pdf), ch. 3, selected passages in §§3.1, 3.3–3.5. In §3.1 the same terminal impedance parameters can be obtained by field calculation or standing-wave measurement. The represented behavior is the dominant-mode behavior at the terminal planes under the chapter's assumptions. The representation omits detailed fields in the immediate vicinity of the discontinuity. The meaning of the modal amplitudes, their normalization, and the reference planes specify what is comparable. Section 3.3 describes changing reference planes by including additional transmission-line lengths. Section 3.4, pp. 130–131, describes measuring a two-port via input impedance under three output-load conditions. On pp. 137–138 it shows that distance errors can produce an apparently asymmetric circuit for a physically symmetric structure and discusses an alternative in which some circuit parameters are extracted electrically and plane locations are inferred separately. Section 3.5, pp. 138–139, links the field methods used for the handbook's circuit results to Schwinger.

[Galison, Trading Zone (1999)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mSJUgdSg7RX1a8AFGUmf5IzhaNcxB7T-/view), Part I, presents the Schwinger case as the development of a working language across field theory and engineering practice. The handbook supports the technical mechanism and the restriction on its scope. The handbook is a 1951 codification. The chronology of how the practice formed and who adopted it remains to be tested with contemporaneous records.

## Worked distinction at a fixed external plane

For a lossless uniform line shorted a distance l from a fixed reference plane, use the convention Gamma(l) = -exp(-2 i beta l), beta = 2 pi / lambda_g. In a single fixed device, shifting the plane outward by known d along the same line multiplies the reflection coefficient by exp(-2 i beta d). Applying the inverse factor returns the original coordinate description at every propagation constant within the model.

Now fix the same external reference plane and compare two different shorts at l = lambda_g0/8 and l + lambda_g0/2. At beta0 = 2 pi / lambda_g0 the two complex reflection coefficients agree. At beta = 1.1 beta0 the difference in magnitude of the complex residual is 2 abs(sin(1.1 pi)) = 0.6180339887. Their individual magnitudes are both 1 at either beta, so the follow-up needs phase-sensitive reflection at the common plane. The change is specified in beta, rather than imposing a linear frequency relation on a dispersive guide. Python independently checked equality, the complex residual, and reversal of the reference-plane transform. This is a worked transmission-line model with no instrument run. Its effect is that a change of coordinates and a test that adds information have distinct operational requirements.

## What the optical paper already tested

Re-read [Zong et al. (2006)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1EQSG9IXVfB9MpZMRAvHxPou0hAqE2nFo/view), §§3–4 and the limitations discussion. The authors characterized one array instrument with 80 laser lines and validated the correction with a 516 nm laser omitted from characterization, as well as a filtered broadband source. A withheld wavelength is thus already part of the published validation. The paper identifies optical-configuration changes and off-array flux as limits to reuse of the correction. MQR's next empirical question is transfer across an explicitly changed input-optics configuration: compare reuse of the original correction with recharacterization, using independent dark/reference observations and an unused source or filter condition in each configuration. Where the same physical input cannot be established in both configurations, equivalence of the correction is unresolved. Check in-band response and off-band residual separately. For effect estimates report within-configuration repeats and include drift and reference uncertainty. Published figures were inspected as text and captions. No raw spectral files or new laboratory data were analyzed.

## Consequence for 5.4

The origin question now has one documented construction of a shared quantity: researchers fix accessible modes, define their amplitudes and reference surfaces, and connect those choices to a load-and-measure procedure. The evidence supports this bounded account. Extending it to a radical divergence of sciences requires understanding when communities can find or invent that joint procedure. Comparing the published codification to earlier lab records could distinguish an interface that arose from joint work from one that was standardized later. The 5.4 research status remains open.
