## Formal distinction
For a research task q, distinguish a final proof/representation P that excludes a scaffold S, an actual dated discovery trajectory H in which S is used, and a resource-bounded feasible alternative trajectory without S. P not containing S establishes only eliminability from that particular finished representation. It neither proves S was causally unnecessary for H nor proves every feasible alternative trajectory requires S. A counterexample to inferring historical necessity: a researcher uses an auxiliary diagram to derive sqrt(a), but an equally competent researcher with an already available algebraic solver can obtain the same answer without it. Conversely, if the solver itself is unavailable in the earlier practice, showing it retrospectively does not establish historical accessibility. This logical distinction is not a new theorem and the illustration is hypothetical.
Mathematical witness: for a>0, construct a semicircle with diameter length 1+a, perpendicular at the division point of segments 1 and a. Its squared height equals ((1+a)/2)^2-((a-1)/2)^2=a. The identity can be written without a diagram, but the geometric construction provides an executable ruler-and-compass procedure (ideal Euclidean assumptions). It does not prove that Descartes needed this exact route historically. The expression sqrt(a) may be available without an executable construction, and an available construction can be useful even if another method exists.

## Macro and falsifiability
5.5 should distinguish the genesis of a representational scaffold from its ultimate warrant and from its later dispensability. The question is not whether a model is true as a whole but what tasks it made accessible, which assumptions survived, which were removed, and whether that removal became possible only after the inquiry changed. Nersessian and Grosholz already provide extensive prior art; MQR's comparative framework is a research proposal, not a priority claim. A strong thesis of scaffold necessity is defeated by a contemporaneously feasible alternate route. A weaker generative-use thesis can be tested with dated notes and intermediate derivations, not final equations alone. Negative cases where scaffolds mislead, create no usable distinction or delay inquiry must be retained. Avoid claiming that all sciences need analogies or that every scaffold is beneficial. The alien-science branch must eventually distinguish shared physical tasks from shared conceptual scaffolds without supplying a privileged translator.
