## 2. Research objects: four layers and four warrants

(1) **Instrument forward kernel** P(raw detector signals | sample, light source, detector calibration, scattering, stray light, time); (2) **reported coordinates** transmittance/absorbance, potentially invertible mathematical charts; (3) **sample-dependent physical variables** chemical species, concentration, organization, path-dependent internal states; (4) **evaluation/authority** permission to ascribe an inferred concentration, a material kind, or a change-of-state claim.

An invertible coordinate chart may preserve statistics about the detector output; it need not preserve Euclidean distances or stabilize physical inference. A macro variable may coarse-grain microstates without rendering the macro explanation illegitimate. Claims about shared species/targets require independent physical/measurement bridges. Sequence effects must not be conflated with endpoint equality.

## 3. First bounded mathematical experiment: measured A nonlinear under stray light

**Research countermodel; constructed numbers, NOT measured data.** Ideal absorbance A=-log10(T), T=10^-A. With a constant nonabsorbed stray-light fraction s>=0 added in measured/reference intensity, a simple idealization is

T_obs=(T+s)/(1+s),   A_obs=-log10[(10^(-A)+s)/(1+s)].

For s=0.01, true A=2 yields observed A_obs=1.703291... . For s=0, a different sample with true A=1.703291... produces the **same single observed absorbance**. The single measurement cannot identify which instrumental/sample world produced it.

For a *linearly Beer–Lambert scaled* half-concentration comparison:
- the s=0 first model predicts observed A=1.703291/2 = 0.851645...;
- the s=.01 model predicts true A=1 then observed A=-log10(.11/1.01) = 0.96291... .
Thus controlled dilution creates a separating comparison **under the declared idealized equations**. But a deviation from one prediction is not by itself unique evidence of stray light; chemical equilibria, scattering, bandpass and other detector errors can also alter curves. This is the required J1-type discrimination / calibration ancestry check, not a theorem that dilution always identifies an instrument fault.

**First-party instrument literature:** NBS/NIST *Accuracy in Analytical Spectrophotometry*, Special Publication 260-81, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nbsspecialpublication260-81.pdf (search-extracted discussion of spectral bandpass and stray-light effects; full PDF visual inspection timed out in this session); NIST Zong et al. 2007, https://www.nist.gov/publications/correction-stray-light-spectroradiometers-and-imaging-instruments ; NIST Zong 2010, https://www.nist.gov/publications/analysis-uncertainties-stray-light-correction . Their spectroradiometer matrix approach is not automatically the same as every UV–Vis cuvette setup: instrument-class translation requires explicit justification.

## 4. Early 1.12–1.15 -> experimental sequence

**1.12 intervention composition:** compare physical action sequence v∘u with its translation in another state/coarse-graining. Different sequences need not commute; only disagreement when equivalence was explicitly claimed warrants an obstruction.

**1.13 admissibility:** an experimental intervention may be doable but alter the subject or its measurement meaning; preserving, transformative, and terminating interventions differ. Do not call transformation of an indicator species a stable fixed-target concentration measurement without chemical state specification.

**1.14 reference transport:** same vial/token can retain material continuity across chemical state or phase transition while the measured species / predicate changes. Distinguish target continuation, transformed target and successor.

**1.15 hysteresis/state sufficiency:** equality of measured A on return does not imply equality of total chemical/internal state or next perturbation-response. Search for a compact, independently anchored hidden state h, with prospective discriminating prediction; do not use entire history as an arbitrary lookup state and do not infer ontic primitive history.

Independent physical hysteresis donor: Sethna et al. (1993), *Hysteresis and hierarchies: Dynamics of disorder-driven first-order phase transformations*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 70, 3347–3350, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.3347. Its random-field Ising return-point memory is **a different physical system**, not direct evidence that the indicator solution exhibits hysteresis.
