# R45-F — Four evaluative questions a meta-philosophy must not conflate

Source synthesis: Dewey (1938) ch.VI; Brigandt (2012) in Feest–Steinle; Kusch (2010); Kish Bar-On (2022); Haueis (2023); Lari (2024); Chang (2012) ch.5; Longino (2002) ch.6.

A proposal's **question-forming value** (it renders new problems describable) is neither its **evidential success** (the claims it makes are well-supported), its **community adoption** (it becomes standard practice), nor its **resource priority** (investing further is a good decision at the available opportunity costs). These can diverge; an unsuccessful reform may enrich a later problem language, and a widely adopted method can have unresolved justification. The distinction is analytical and grounded in strong prior art, **not itself an original MQR theory**.

To make this research useful, next work needs *contrasting historical decisions*: cases where extant philosophical theories give incompatible verdicts and where available contemporaneous evidence can adjudicate. Predicted non-obviousness is NOT itself novelty.

Guardrail: avoid counting every new metric or new term as a higher-order 'paradigm'. The individuation test from Kusch applies to MQR's own categories. Longino/Lari already distinguish first-order scientific criticism from critique of community goals.
