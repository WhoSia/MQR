# MQR 5.4 — A theory can be right about everything it asks and still fail as a science

2026-11-14 KST · Working argument from the founding manuscript and the MQR 1, 31–32, 34–35, 40–41 records.

## Argument before qualifications

1. **A paradigm can be scientifically deficient even when every answer to its own questions is correct.** A paradigm includes ways of preparing, manipulating, and reading the world. Those practices determine which distinctions can be tested. A framework that excludes a consequential distinction can give correct answers indefinitely while failing to expose the distinction to inquiry. The strongest case is where evidence for that deficiency arrives only after an alternative practice has been built.
2. **A theory of scientific progress that compares answers to a fixed list of questions will systematically miss a class of revolutions.** On the old question list the rival can win nothing. On the new list a different intervention can make the world give different answers for states the old practice treats as identical. An adjudicator who demands prior success on the old list biases the competition toward the incumbent. This is a claim about a comparison rule, not a claim that every historical revolution works this way.
3. **A better science may deliberately discard detail.** Schwinger's equivalent circuits are a candidate: for selected questions at the terminals, sacrificing internal field detail yields a practical domain of new design moves. This challenges any definition of progress that requires a superset of distinctions. Improvement can be asymmetric and task-dependent. A revision that merely proliferates vocabulary or loses useful capacities without gaining any new one has no support from this argument.

## Disagreement with the literature

- **Poincaré, two different moves.** In the 1890 *Acta Mathematica* §19, pp. 225–227, the question is whether curves on a section intersect. An intersection represents a trajectory asymptotic to the same periodic trajectory in both time directions. Poincaré argues for infinitely many; this is his printed argument, not an independent reproof or the attribution of all modern chaos theory. Read with Zuchowski (2014), who studies his switches between descriptions and the limits of retrospectively applying modern definitions of chaos. In *Science and Hypothesis* chs. III, V he examines the joint empirical assessment of geometry and physical assumptions about rods and light. That argument neither identifies the languages of two possible sciences nor says that their practices will make the same phenomena accessible. The two works support different pressures on a view of progress.
- **Nersessian against Clement.** In Nersessian (2008), ch. 3, §§3.1, 3.4.5 and 3.5, S2's answer about which spring stretches more remains fixed, while a polygonal coil enables the suggestion of torsion. In Clement (1989), ch. 20, utterances 122–123 and pp. 366–367, S2 retains both bending and torsion and raises which dominates. Clement distinguishes constructing the polygonal representation from activating a physical principle. His reading treats the outcome as a major reorganization short of a completed replacement. Nersessian's §3.5 puts the emphasis on the new representation and its continuity with historical scientific reasoning. The two authors analyze the same protocol, and their analyses do not count as independent observations. Nersessian describes eleven protocols, while Clement uses ten studied subjects in a specific statement about recognizing torsion. No frequency estimate rests on harmonizing these denominators. The records support a newly posed mechanistic question more directly than a completed mechanistic transition.
- **Galison against our earlier summary.** In *Trading Zone: Coordinating Action and Belief*, pp. 150–152, Galison describes the active creation of design vocabulary and a way of working with discontinuities, rather than merely cooperation on an already fixed response. Our prior one-line summary about common terminal responses underplayed that point. His further causal account linking wartime practice to renormalization has a Schwinger retrospective statement behind it. It is not necessary to settle the causal strength of that account to dispute our fixed-task comparison. The working claim goes beyond that episode by making the admissible comparison protocol itself a possible outcome.
- **Maxwell against an origin-only reading.** Keep the distinction between Nersessian's account of the role of model construction and Chalmers' independent dielectric route to the qualitative displacement-current idea. The published electrical/optical comparison can still organize an empirical question across practices. Independently reinventing a concept and using it to change what counts as a test are different achievements. [Prior primary-text reading](mqr_54_mqr41_maxwell_nersessian_model_generated_questions.md).
- **Kuhn and Larkin–Simon as nearby challenges.** Kuhn already makes problem choice and losses visible. Larkin and Simon compare informationally equivalent representations with different computational consequences. The claim pursued here is about a comparison that changes the accessible physical protocols or what follows from them, and whether an evaluation on old tasks misses this gain. This is the boundary of the current proposal, not a priority verdict.

- **Greenberg applied to our own use of books.** In *How citation distortions create unfounded authority* (2009), Methods and the Results sections on bias, amplification and invention distinguish claims supported by primary data from repeating those claims. The supplement reports 12 primary-data papers out of 242 containing statements on the specific claim. Applying the distinction here yields a local correction: Clement and Nersessian are two readings of one spring protocol, while the present note, prior GitHub note and Notion summary likewise do not add new Maxwell witnesses. This analogy does not establish that MQR has a statistically measured citation bias. ## A worked counterexample to a fixed-question test

Consider an ideal monochromatic, mutually coherent optical setting with one scalar complex amplitude z at the detector. Preparation A can vary the overall known real gain r and measures only r²|z|². For z=+1 and z=−1, every measurement in A agrees for every allowed r. The impossibility is over the stated family, not a finite-sample artifact. Arbitrary deterministic downstream logic on identical old records cannot identify the sign relative to the later reference. With the same digitizer and intensity detector, preparation B combines the sample with a phase-stable coherent reference a before detection and reads |z+a|². The output can now distinguish z=+1 from z=−1 relative to a=+1.

| Phase of unit signal relative to +1 | Reference absent | Reference +1 | Reference −1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 degrees | 1 | 4 | 0 |
| 90 degrees | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 180 degrees | 1 | 0 | 4 |
| 270 degrees | 1 | 2 | 2 |

The rows were verified using exact integer components and squared magnitudes. Real references discriminate the displayed 0/180 contrast; 90/270 remain tied until an independent quadrature is available. Under a common phase change of both sample and reference, every B intensity remains invariant. This experiment creates access to a relative phase distinction, without making an absolute phase measurable. It is standard optics used as a counterexample to the assertion that matching all outputs of the old practice exhausts future empirical comparison. It is an ideal model, with no claim to an instrument run or that stock UV–Vis hardware has a coherent reference port. [Two-beam interference formula](https://wp.optics.arizona.edu/jcwyant/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/08/04-BASIC_INTERFEROMETRY_AND_OPTICAL_TESTING.PPTX.pdf).

The strong objection is that the old wave theory already contained phase. In this example it can: A and B can share all the mathematics. The explicit target of the counterexample is the authority of a fixed suite of practices, not the inexpressibility of phase in the old theory. Treating every future apparatus as implicitly available because the theory can describe it erases the difference between specifying an apparatus and being able to build or discover it. To advance the historical claim, seek a case where the old practice and a named rival make different predictions about the feasibility of a new comparison before anyone knows its result. Historical gains should also be paired with losses or costs, and useful cases of recoding alone serve as controls. In particular, a phase-shifting detector would be added functionality, whereas rewriting the same intensity stream in another notation would leave the pair tied.

## Working rule for further edits

Keep the original substantive claim and the reason for challenging it together. Record a restriction of a particular inference at that inference. When a strong formulation changes, show what the new wording can still explain, predict or oppose. Literature may win an argument because of its reasoning or evidence. Historical corrections, publication status and prior-art priority do not decide what question MQR can ask. A disputed conjecture stays available in its strongest defensible form and can lose to an explicit rival.

For the next depth pass, carry two rivals through the Schwinger case: did equivalent circuits simply speed up work on an already defined set of design questions, or did they make additional operations or design objectives practically available? Name one design move and what could show the first explanation suffices. Return to the originating possible-sciences question after that comparison.
