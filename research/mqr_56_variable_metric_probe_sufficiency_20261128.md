## A smooth countermodel with three common observable functions
Fix calibrated physical position x and energy E; allow positive position-dependent inertia m(x) in H=p²/(2m(x))+V(x). Introduce y in [-1,1] and e in [-0.1,0.1], with
x=f_e(y)=y sqrt((1-2ey)/(1+2ey)), s=y+ey², V(f_e(y))=y²/2, m(f_e(y))=(s'(y)/f_e'(y))².
Both derivatives positive: s'=1+2ey >=0.8; f'=sqrt((1-2ey)/(1+2ey))*(1-2ey/(1-4e²y²))>0. Hence these are smooth admissible Hamiltonians. With a common physical x, these are different potential and inertia functions, not merely a relabeling of the same fixed calibrated position.
At E=A²/2 the full time-integrated moment is J_k=2 integral_{-A}^A f_e(y)^k(1+2ey)/sqrt(A²-y²) dy. At each positive r=|y|, the two branch contributions have weights 1±2er. Their sums for k=0,1,2 are respectively 2,0,2r². Thus for every allowed E, T=2pi, <x>=0, <x²>=E independently of e. The normalized paired third moment is -4e r^4/sqrt(1-4e²r²), so the full third moment has sign opposite e for E>0 and is strictly monotone in e at fixed E. For e=0 it is zero. This is an exact continuum counterexample to sufficiency of even three moment functions when the model class is broadened; not a rejection of the earlier constant-mass theorem.

## Positive identification with a richer probe set
Assume smooth two monotone inverse branches x_+(u)>x_-(u), strictly positive smooth m, calibrated x and E, conservative motion, adequate integrability, and full noiseless moment functions J_0 through J_3 for the entire interval. For k=0..3 the Abel transform is J_k(E)=sqrt(2) integral_0^E h_k(u)/sqrt(E-u) du, h_k=a x_+^k+b x_-^k with a=sqrt(m(x_+))x_+' and b=-sqrt(m(x_-))x_-'>0. Standard integrated Abel inversion recovers cumulative integrals of h_k; differentiating gives h_k a.e. For u>0 D=h_0 h_2-h_1²=ab(x_+-x_-)²>0. The recurrence h_{k+2}=S h_{k+1}-P h_k yields S=(h_0 h_3-h_1 h_2)/D and P=(h_1 h_3-h_2²)/D. Roots of t²-St+P give the two positions in order. Then a=(h_1-h_0 x_-)/(x_+-x_-), b=h_0-a. The potential is recovered from the inverse branches and m(x_+)=(a/x_+')², m(x_-)=(b/x_-')² where derivatives exist and are nonzero. The minimum requires continuity; conditioning near it, noisy/finite data, multiwell, coordinate calibration, nonconservative dynamics and higher-dimensional cases are untested. This uses classical Abel and elementary two-atom moment algebra, not claimed as a new mathematical theorem; literature priority unreviewed.
