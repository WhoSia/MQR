## 1. Hachette's 12 December 1821 letter is a new material witness
Jean Nicolas Pierre Hachette → Michael Faraday, Paris, 12 Dec 1821, Ɛpsilon record **Faraday0160** https://epsilon.ac.uk/view/faraday/letters/Faraday0160. Source of editorial transcription: IEE MS SC 2. The French autograph transcription plus editorial English translation are provided together. Hachette reports:
- Ampère introduced/discussed Faraday's rotary-wire experiment at the Académie Royale des Sciences on **19 November 1821**;
- the experiment was repeated at the **Société Philomatique on 24 November**;
- meeting minutes noted the apparatus supplied by Faraday and explanatory letter dated **18 October 1821**;
- Hachette describes a similar apparatus devised by Ampère and the impending printed French translation of Faraday's paper.
These are the testimony of a contemporary involved actor. The society's original meeting minutes and journal publication are additional independent verification surfaces not yet read; *do not overstate independent replication of the documentary account*. There is a source-identity nuance: Faraday0158 dated 16 Nov describes sending a device to **De La Rive**, whereas Faraday0160 describes a device sent **to Hachette** with an 18 October explanatory letter. These are distinct named recipients and correspondence routes; do not conflate the parcels or assign the Paris reproduction to De La Rive's personally delivered device without evidence.

## 2. Faraday's 2 February 1822 response to Ampère
Ɛpsilon Faraday0165 https://epsilon.ac.uk/view/faraday/letters/Faraday0165, editorial record date **2 February 1822**, source SPK DD Bl 1–3. The transcribed letter's *internal date line reads Feby 2nd. 1821*. This internal date vs editorial dating mismatch is explicitly preserved; chronological placement here follows the edition and the letter's discussion of post-Nov-1821 apparatus and correspondence, but must be checked against archival metadata before being made a definitive manuscript date statement.
Faraday reports he *repeated* Ampère's rotation experiment; he interprets it in terms of a variant of the earlier wire-around-pole motion and openly contests Ampère's claim that it determines whether hypothetical currents lie round magnet axis or particles. He emphasizes his lack of mathematical preparation and withholds acceptance of unproven micro-current ontology. Thus one successful shared experiment does not force an identical inference contract or settled ontology.

## 3. Method transfer vs semantic transport: different but related historical outcomes
At least three kinds of historical transition now have dated direct witnesses:
- `REPRODUCIBLE_PHENOMENON`: a device/experiment that another scientific group can demonstrate (Hachette 12 Dec report of Paris Nov experiments).
- `METHOD_MIGRATION`: artifact, technical explanation and adapted apparatus circulate (Faraday 18 Oct, 16 Nov; Hachette 12 Dec).
- `INTERPRETIVE_DIVERGENCE`: Faraday and Ampère attach different discriminative/theory-licensing significance to motor/rotation experiments (Faraday to Ampère editorially dated Feb 1822).
The theoretical interpretation was not simply translated along with the apparatus. This historically attested split matches the conceptual structure of MQR44's O/I/S/G obligations but is **already anticipated** by Gooding's and Steinle's material-learning accounts. MQR's contribution is careful cross-model comparison and explicit scope of evidential authority, not the claim of discovering apparatus portability as an idea.

## 4. Scientific novelty and hard rival
Stronger proposition now partially supported: a materially transmitted experiment can be *reproduced across research groups while leaving theory-discriminating meaning contested*. This is stronger than R14's mere sending evidence.

Hard contrary hypothesis: all observed disagreement may be the ordinary case of rival physical interpretations of a shared effect, not evidence that fundamentally different scientific languages have become locally commensurable. The early Ampère/Faraday systems shared much physical apparatus, battery and magnetic observation vocabulary. Therefore these letters do **NOT** establish communication between fully alien ontological primitives.

Another rival: Ampère's 1821–1822 rotation apparatus made additional phenomena possible, meaning the exchange might actually expand questions in both directions; this cannot be assessed just from Faraday's reply without exact Ampère works and audience notes.
