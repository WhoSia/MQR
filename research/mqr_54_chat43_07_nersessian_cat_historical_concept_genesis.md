They establish what was *reported* about the historical research process, not independent reanalysis of individual neuronal recordings or a randomized proof that visualization caused conceptual change. Do not treat an interview as an actual laboratory raw-byte measurement.

## 2. Reconstructed process and its live alternatives

| Stage | Directly supported historical description | What changed, and what still needs checking |
|---|---|---|
| Initial aim | Lab D investigated how cultured neuronal networks could be trained to control an artificial embodiment, using feedback and plasticity ideas. | The basic target of learning was *not* created from scratch by the later CAT concept. |
| Failure/anomaly | Burst-like electrical activity was initially considered an interference with plasticity experiments; attempts to quiet it faced continuing difficulty, including unstable patterns across trials (drift). | Historical authors report a shift in priorities; the raw record/chronological independent replication has not been supplied to MQR. |
| New material-computational practice | A researcher constructed an in-silico network constrained by neuroscience, platform properties and the in-vitro apparatus; the model was compared first with outside literature, then with the group's experimental system. | Simulation is an analogy subjected to testing, not direct transparent vision of the physical dish. |
| New visual question | Repeated simulation and visualization made possible a focus on the motion and recurring spatial pattern of activity, rather than treating bursts solely as inconvenient noise. | A genuine change in what counts as *interesting signal*, not just lower noise in the existing task. |
| New investigative concepts | “Burst types,” “spatial extent,” and “center of activity trajectory” (CAT) were developed. CAT drew on the physical concept of a center of mass, but incorporated position, time and activity to describe moving network activation. | This is concept transfer, co-design and reorientation, **not** an unanticipated mathematical primitive invented ex nihilo. |
| Return to physical model | The conceptual tools were applied to the in-vitro system and related to stimulation/control protocols in the authors' account. | A stronger empirical success claim requires separate experimental primary outputs and explicit validation criteria. |

The most consequential transition is **burst-as-nuisance → burst-as-investigable-pattern → spatial/temporal analytic concepts → revised questions about network control**. It illustrates why counting operationally distinct outputs alone misses epistemic aims, experimental artifacts, analogy, interpretive decisions and material constraints.

## 3. Three rival explanations that must remain available

**Rival R1: pure post-processing/relabeling.** Perhaps CAT was always mathematically definable from a richer, already retained time-resolved signal; scientists only changed the visualization and analytic priorities. This possibility weakens a claim of *newly acquired sensor information*, but not necessarily a claim of *new conceptual role*. The historical MEAscope visualization **already displayed activity in multiple channels over time**. Therefore our synthetic [record08 histogram-only model](mqr_54_chat43_08_temporal_representation_nonfactorization.md) is a deliberately much poorer old-language countermodel, **not a faithful reconstruction of the original laboratory's instrument**.

**Rival R2: model-specific simulation artifact.** A pattern observed in a model can depend on how an imagined network and visualizer were constructed. The investigator's demand that it also track the independently studied in-vitro system is a substantive empirical constraint. The ethnographic report describes such analogical transfer and subsequent refinement, but MQR has not validated raw outcomes or quantified external reproducibility.

**Rival R3: independent experimental or social changes.** The team's changed research aims, division of labor, existing plasticity theory and subsequent experiments could contribute to the concept adoption independently of the specific visualization. The history is plausible process evidence, not a randomized intervention separating all causal explanations.

A key correction to MQR's prior frozen-grammar model: what changes here is not just a new word in a semigroup. The **purpose of measuring bursts**, what researchers classify as a significant pattern, and which interventions count as informative all co-evolve. Such a change is not automatically reducible to a fixed analyst-owned hypothesis set.

## 4. Prior art as an adversary, not an endorsement

- [Feest (2012), “Exploratory Experiments, Concept Formation, and Theory Construction in Psychology”](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WJyJHhYFi3R27QNfNOu5Jr4pmsdq5Ua9/view) directly challenges any single fixed division between theoretical and experimental criteria of individuation.
- [Mieke Boon (2012), “Scientific Concepts in the Engineering Sciences”](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/5600586/scientific.pdf) insists that concepts can be tools for creating, reproducing and intervening with technological phenomena, not just labels for measurements.
- [Hanne Andersen (2012), “Conceptual Development in Interdisciplinary Research”](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110253610.271) separates differences in disciplines' vocabularies, their categorization practices, and the actual intellectual work of interdisciplinary integration.
- [Active automata learning (AALpy)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11334-022-00449-3) already provides black-box query methods. The MQR distinction is not to claim their reinvention but to *bound what such a method proves about concepts*.

## 5. MQR founder-question return and research verdict

The original question “is scientific observation just logic and instrument readouts?” acquires a sharper answer: **the digital trace may be computationally transformed, but which transformation becomes a stable object of investigation is a historical, social, technical and experimentally constrained achievement.** The physical source of data, adopted categories, and capacity to ask new intervention-dependent questions must be distinguished.

A future test of concept formation needs at least: a documented **old question/explanatory target**, material/representational change, contemporaneous alternative interpretation, a prospective new experimental consequence, and a record of how the revision survived or failed world contact. These are *research comparisons*, not a scoring ritual or a universal necessary-and-sufficient definition of scientific novelty.
