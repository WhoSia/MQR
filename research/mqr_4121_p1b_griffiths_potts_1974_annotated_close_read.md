## Segment 1 — Printed pp. 1–2: The scientific problem and experimental operations
Question: an isosbestic wavelength is often explained as a two-species acid/base equilibrium. But can several chemical species produce an effectively one-parameter family of electronic spectra? The authors distinguish an isosbestic crossing, full-range internal linearity, and a chemical identification claim. They explicitly acknowledge predecessor work by Brynestad and others; computational analysis is the practical adaptation.
Studied methyl red, methyl orange, phenolphthalein. Solutions were prepared from dilute acid/base with potassium nitrate/chloride ionic backgrounds; titration volumes and dilution corrections are treated as meaningful causes of spectral difference. Cary 14H UV–Vis, 1 cm cells; acid added sequentially and mixed, nominal equilibration about 2 minutes; neutralization produces reported 2–3 °C warming during titration. Temperature series in stopped cells with thermostatted sample/reference, roughly 15–75 °C. **Annotation:** a nominal pH intervention is not an isolated pH intervention if solution volume and temperature also move. Experimental sequence is part of the evidence.

## Segment 2 — Printed pp. 2–4: Discretization is itself a measurement decision
Compares manually digitized chart traces at 5 nm and automated 1 nm sampling. Discusses evenly spaced wavelength grids, the risk of a missed entry shifting all subsequent wavelength assignments, and the need to sample fine enough near steep spectral gradients. Recorded digital vectors and smoothing/plotting routines are not lossless replicas of continuous original spectra. **Annotation:** a computer procedure that operates correctly on supplied coordinates does not certify correct input coordinate indexing. This is a direct 2026 MQR origin question, not a newly invented concern.

## Segment 3 — Printed pp. 3–5: Mathematical internal-linearity test
Write each measured spectrum as a vector e_j in R^m of wavelength-sampled absorbances. For a family that is affine one-dimensional in spectral space, e_3=(1-b)e_1+b e_2, using a single wavelength-independent b across the working spectral range. Formally b_lambda=(e3_lambda-e1_lambda)/(e2_lambda-e1_lambda) where denominator is sufficiently separated from zero; average reliable wavelength-specific estimates and plot residual d_lambda=e3_lambda-[(1-b)e1_lambda+b e2_lambda]. The denominator-near-zero points are especially unstable, so the original code rejects noisy/small-difference points when estimating b yet assesses residual across wavelengths. Inspection of residual shape matters in addition to RMS.
**Independent math note:** if all spectra occupy an affine line, the centered matrix [e_j-e_ref] has rank at most 1 in exact noiseless arithmetic. Conversely a single isosbestic wavelength constrains only one row of the spectrum-by-condition matrix: it does NOT force affine rank 1 globally. Rank/stable spectral geometry are conditional on the selected wavelength set, concentration normalization, noise and instrument response.

## Segment 4 — Printed pp. 5–10: pH series, digitization and failure regions
Fig. 2 compares titration responses. The authors choose methyl red as the practical focus with dilute reagents, and compare two preparations/data capture methods (KCl automated 1-nm recording and KNO3 chart-based 5-nm recording). A residual function varying systematically with wavelength can be a model warning, not just instrument noise.

## Segment 5 — Printed pp. 10–12: The central chemical interpretation
Important asymmetry: the paper attributes methyl-red behavior to acid species I, pseudo-acid species II and basic species III. II and III are both yellow with similar proposed electronic spectra. Over a substantial pH range, II and III may co-vary in a stable effective ratio, causing a pseudo-two-species spectral manifold despite the proposed three chemical species. At high pH the ratio changes and small but structured departures from internal linearity emerge. The inference to species structures is a **chemical interpretive proposal** informed by molecular chemistry and prior indicator work, not an unconditional theorem that three independent species are uniquely recovered from observed spectra.
**MQR note:** number of chemical species (latent mechanistic ontology), number of spectrally distinguishable components under selected measurement, and dimensionality of sampled equilibrium trajectory are THREE different counts. Even if epsilon_I, epsilon_II, epsilon_III are linearly independent, a constrained concentration trajectory can produce data of low affine dimension. Near equality of epsilon_II and epsilon_III adds a second, instrumental/noise-sensitive nonidentifiability mechanism.

## Segment 6 — Printed pp. 12–15: pH versus temperature and analytical interpretation
Spectra are smoothed with care (the paper warns that over-wide smoothing and bad interpolation can distort steep features). Figs. 6–8 describe temperatures from around 15 to 73 °C at an initially measured pH near 4.84. The temperature-induced changes are subtler than pH changes; component molar spectra themselves may vary with temperature, weakening the assumption of invariant endmember spectra. The authors interpret a shift toward yellow methyl-red forms at elevated temperature and retain pseudo-two-species behavior over their measured intermediate regime. They explicitly caution against treating these empirical tolerances as universal.
**MQR note:** adding a temperature axis is not automatically a clean second excitation of chemical species, because it changes both mixing proportions and response functions. A proposed discriminator must include endmember temperature-dependence or a separate spectroscopic/chemical reference.

## Mathematical reconstruction for MQR (analyst-added, not in the 1974 paper)
Let E be an m x k response matrix with one spectral column per chemical species; let C be a k x n concentration-condition matrix; in a linear, background-corrected model Y=E C. Then rank(Y) <= min(rank(E),rank(C)). If k=3 but response columns II/III are near-collinear, the numerical effective rank can be near two. If an equilibrium constraints C to move on a one-dimensional affine line, centered Y can be affine rank one even though k=3 and E has full column rank. Distinguish affine and ordinary rank: for fixed total concentration, data may lie on an affine line not through zero.
To distinguish a changing chemistry from instrumental correction: compare (i) full spectral residual maps across controlled pH and temperature; (ii) wavelength-bandpass changes at fixed equilibrated chemistry; (iii) known-intensity stray-light check; (iv) independent chemical information on distinct forms or a model with known species fingerprints. Never claim the existing scans already provide sufficient raw numbers for a new fit.

## Hypotheses and falsifiers harvested
H121-B1: Isosbestic crossing under selected pH changes is an insufficient witness for two literal species; construct spectral families with >2 chemical species and one observed effective mixing dimension.
H121-B2: Apparent species count depends on both spectral response rank and reachable concentration-trajectory rank, not on one scalar absorbance or the observed number of crossings.
H121-B3: Sample preparation/titration history can alter the map being tested (pH, temperature, volume, ionic background), so no unqualified intervention-sequence transport.
Falsifier to easy 3-species inference: independent chemical assay showing alternative two-species dynamics within the original preparation; falsifier to stable pseudo-two rule: structured off-affine spectra under a nominally in-scope parameter sweep. The paper itself does NOT certify universal chemical species identities.
