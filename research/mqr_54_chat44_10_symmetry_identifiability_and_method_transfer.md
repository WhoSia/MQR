# MQR 5.4 · MQR 44 · Record 10 — Symmetry, shared anchors, and the limits of translating possible sciences

**2026-11-22 KST.** Claim level: elementary exact finite demonstration + MQR bridge-design research conjecture. No empirical evidence for actual alien science, no theorem about all semantic translations, no stage advancement.

## 1. Three levels of 'same world' that must not be confused
(i) Equal individual empirical marginal distributions under separate native instruments.
(ii) A *paired event* or independently justified correspondence between particular output types.
(iii) A complete, unique translation preserving causal, inferential, operational and success-criterion structure.

(i) does not imply (ii); (ii) need not imply (iii). A common distribution cannot label the other's categories when it is invariant under permutation.

## 2. Finite symmetry result
Suppose scientist A reports one of three equiprobable outcomes {0,1,2}; scientist B reports one of three equiprobable native outcomes {red,green,blue}. The hypothesis is that there exists a bijective output dictionary preserving pairings, but separate unpaired output frequencies are the **only** evidence. There are exactly 3! = **6** candidate dictionaries; every one yields the same per-language uniform distribution. For a faithful representation of this toy problem, any method claiming the *unique* dictionary from those data alone must have inserted an unrecorded identifying convention or extra information.

Supply one externally validated anchor, such as 0 ↔ red from a documented shared event, and 2! = **2** dictionary candidates remain. Supply a second distinct valid anchor, 1 ↔ blue, and **1** remains. If the anchors are contradictory (0 ↔ red and 0 ↔ blue), **0** bijections remain under the declared model. The last outcome is a contradiction requiring revised assumptions or evidence, not an invitation to manufacture a bridge.

Exact enumeration code `language/real/experiments/mqr_bridge_symmetry_identifiability_v54.py` was executed locally, yielding:
`PASS: 6 symmetry-related maps; one anchor leaves 2; two anchors identify 1; conflicting anchors 0`.

This is elementary finite group/identifiability mathematics, not a new theorem. For n uniform labels, n! observationally equivalent dictionaries remain, and k distinct non-conflicting anchored labels reduce this to (n-k)! provided a bijection is appropriate. A subgroup of observational automorphisms captures the residual ambiguity. If distributions are nonuniform with sufficiently distinct stable category frequencies, the symmetry may be partially broken without paired events; if labels are ambiguous, frequencies vary or different physical observables are involved, a bijection may not exist at all.

## 3. Scientific interpretation and a nontrivial limitation
This example addresses *measurement-output alignment* only. The initial premise that native result categories refer to a common outcome space is supplied by the analyst, not earned by the agents. Correct label mapping does not establish sameness of meanings, causal intervention responses, mathematical ontology or scientific success criteria. Conversely absence of a uniquely identified mapping does not demonstrate impossibility of any translation: a set-valued bridge, context-specific bridge, shared instruments or further interventions may be workable.

The proposed MQR bridge should therefore return a **set of surviving partial mappings with identified scope**, rather than force one global dictionary. This connects to MQR 4.X identified-set and typed-transport doctrine and the four obligations in MQR44 record04, without equating the mathematical object to Kuhnian incommensurability.

## 4. New research hypothesis from the two-layer science network
Combine record08 and record09 with this proof: if two communities share a *procedure* capable of generating paired comparison events, a formerly ambiguous dictionary may become more identifiable **without needing the receiving community to abandon its own vocabulary**. Thus transfer of a method can be valuable not by transmitting yet another proposition but by *changing the evidence-generating relation*.

A question-generative network therefore has two independent capacities:
- receive independent information, and
- acquire a new operation that generates discriminating paired events, thereby altering the feasible space of bridge questions.
In the historical record a method's arrival and practical adoption must be dated before claims about subsequent novel comparisons can be made.

## 5. Counterarguments and stringent next experiment
- This output alignment is just permutation identification; it might tell us little about scientific concepts beyond commonplace measurement calibration. MQR must show empirical cases where that limited mechanism explains a real obstacle or enables a new question.
- An analyst could impose a shared event identifier from the outset and eliminate ambiguity by definition; this would hide rather than solve the bootstrap problem.
- For languages with different numbers of categories, non-bijective maps, flexible kind boundaries or intervention-specific meaning, the permutation model is inadequate. Do not force it onto such cases.
- Pairwise anchors can be locally consistent while global multi-community composition is inconsistent (record03); check path-congruence and retain distinct failure classes.

Prospective historical study: date and source (a) native output vocabularies, (b) instrument-transfer action, (c) first paired comparison, (d) novel testable queries, (e) remaining semantic divergences. Compare this against a rival explanation where fixed prior conventions, rather than innovation, already identify the bridge.
