## Adversarial fallacies to avoid

1. *Temporal condition fallacy:* Faraday saw a brief galvanometer deflection. Do not retroproject a directly measured modern `dPhi/dt` trace from 1831 — no digitized flux waveform or separately calibrated modern induction voltage was recorded.
2. *Instrument efficiency fallacy:* No sustained deflection under continued primary DC supply does not show zero electromagnetic fields or null magnetic flux. It supports the specific lack of persistent induced current under the described constant-condition preparation.
3. *Priority fallacy:* The 1821 rotation and 1831 induction are distinct discoveries. The article read in Nov 1831 was not first physically attempted on the reading date. Royal Institution says Aug 29; source chronology should remain indexed.
4. *Novelty fallacy:* Steinle and earlier MQR41 already make the claim exploratory experiments yield stable concepts; mere case recoding as four levels of MQR ontology adds no new theory.
5. *Evidence-laundering fallacy:* A current museum photograph is an independent physical custody witness for the apparatus, but it is not an unedited contemporaneous diary scan. The Royal Society manuscript catalogue demonstrates archival presence; it does not establish that each page of handwriting was directly compared with the published version.

## Competing explanations for the conceptual advance

**Account A: hypothesis-driven discovery** — a scientist already expected the reverse effect of currents and magnets, and the ring was a technical means of checking a prior question.
**Account B: systematic exploratory differentiation** — repeated configuration and timing variations disclosed the make/steady/break response, changing what counts as an answerable target.
**Account C: instrument-specific artifact explanation** — transients were actually produced by galvanometer response, contact noise or apparatus features; the proof of a general induction phenomenon required independent conductors, movements and materials, not a ring-only anecdote.

Faraday's whole First Series and following studies investigate alternative geometries (including motion across magnetic fields) beyond the ring, so claim C cannot be decided solely from one museum object; nor may we assert every alternative was fully controlled on Aug 29. Historical reconstruction must use dated source sequences and actual negative outcomes. A and B may be *compatible*, not mutually exclusive: prior expectation can coexist with exploration.
