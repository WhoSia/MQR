# MQR-5.3 R6 — Chang's Absolute Temperature and Epistemic Iteration

Date: 2026-10-31. Source: Hasok Chang (2004) *Inventing Temperature*, chapters 4 (pp.159–219) and 5 (pp.220–234), Drive ID `1RwuRfY9Ifz6iEAUZZm57z4EzHX8CP0CV`.

## Source-grounded historical reading

Chapter 4 discusses caloric and dynamic heat theories, Thomson's 1848 first absolute temperature, his reformulated second absolute temperature following Joule's heat-work interconversion, idealized Carnot models, operationalization and Callendar–Le Chatelier iterative approximation. First absolute scale is *not* modern Kelvin: Chang Table 4.1, air-temperature readings [0,25,50,75,100,150,200,231] C correspond to first-scale [0,27.545,53.291,77.367,100,141.875,180.442,203.125]. The revised thermodynamic definition using reversible heat engines T_h/T_c = Q_h/Q_c does not in itself specify an independently available physical thermometer. Image, apparatus and theoretical characterization require different justificatory steps.

Chapter 4 pp.212–217: approximate assumed closeness between gas thermometer and absolute temperature supports empirical correction via Joule–Thomson data. The iterative scheme is fallible and revisable, not an unconditional convergence theorem. PDF-extracted formula glyphs are incomplete; mathematical rederivation and page-image check remain open.

Chapter 5: Circularity and coherentism (pp.221–224); epistemic iteration (pp.224–228); enrichment and self-correction (pp.228–231); pluralism (pp.231–233). Chang assesses progress by epistemic virtues not solely by an inaccessible initial true temperature.

## MQR analytic separation (our proposal, not a theorem by Chang)

1. A sequence x_(n+1) = F(x_n,D) can converge to a biased point under model misspecification; convergence is distinct from accuracy.
2. Observable equivalence or a gauge symmetry can prevent identification of physical target despite repeatable mutual calibration.
3. Theoretical definitions, operational models, implemented apparatus and evidential reports must be separately audited.
4. Improvement in informational discrimination, conceptual enrichment, and epistemic self-correction are different claims.
