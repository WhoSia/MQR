## 1. A materially stronger timeline than '1981 before 1985'
**1981** Jacoby & Dallas, 'On the relationship between autobiographical memory and perceptual learning', *J Exp Psychol Gen* 110(3):306–340, [PMID6457080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6457080/): comparisons of recognition vs perceptual task performance already feasible, hence not all initial indirect/direct memory contrasts originated with the later term.

**1985** Graf & Schacter, 'Implicit and explicit memory for new associations in normal and amnesic subjects', *J Exp Psychol Learn Mem Cogn* 11(3):501–518, [PMID3160813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3160813/). Original abstract explicitly reports that same-versus-different study/test word-pair contextual priming advantage is conditional on elaborative study operations; both normal and amnesic subject conditions used. The meaning of the term 'implicit' does not remove dependence on task design.

**1987** Schacter, 'Implicit memory: History and current status', *J Exp Psychol Learn Mem Cogn* 13(3):501–518, [Harvard author bibliography](https://dschacter.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/implicit-memory-history-and-current-status), DOI [10.1037/0278-7393.13.3.501](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.13.3.501). A subsequently written first-person historical reflection [Schacter (1993) citation essay](https://garfield.library.upenn.edu/classics1993/A1993MJ84000001.pdf) explicitly states that he and Graf adopted the expression 'implicit memory' in 1985. **This later recollection supports attribution of the term**, but is not itself an independently verified 1985 publication excerpt or proof that no one ever used similar terms before.

**1989** Schacter & Graf, 'Modality specificity of implicit memory for new associations', [PubMed PMID2522139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2522139/). Original abstract reports that implicit memory for new associations is elaboration-dependent **and modality-specific**: study-to-test modality shifts reduced associative priming in a visual word-completion assay. This was called a theoretical puzzle because access to elaborated associative representations also appeared contingent on sensory/perceptual processing route. The narrower 1985 contrast was therefore not an unconditional cross-modality property.

**1990** Roediger, 'Implicit Memory: Retention Without Remembering', *American Psychologist* 45(9):1043–1056, [PubMed PMID2221571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2221571/), challenges a compulsory many-memory-systems interpretation of task dissociations and emphasizes general processing principles.

**2010** Feest, 'Concepts as Tools...', held complete original ID `1LePHrGcp10C4Lr5135YsMKzMc5A64a9A`, already offers an account of successive conceptual change in this research program; MQR cannot claim unprecedented historical recognition of it.

## 2. What could really count as a generated scientific question?
Possible candidate: by 1989 researchers asked whether a memory effect operationally defined through newly learned word-pair associations would transfer across **sensory modalities** after a fixed type of study processing, rather than merely whether it occurred in a single word-completion context. The 1989 study made this contrast explicit and obtained a conditionally limited effect.

**However:** perceptual modality studies were already underway before 1985; one cannot claim 'changing sensory modality had never been a thinkable question before 1985'. The historically interesting candidate is the **composite test** of associative priming × elaboration × transfer modality, not the abstract invention of modality switching. Showing that this exact composite was previously impossible or not conceived requires contemporaneous 1981–1988 experimental-repertoire audit. Without that, it is a **newly specified explanatory question**, not proof of novel operation invention.

## 3. A rival-outcome test that discriminates claims, even if theories remain underdetermined
- Strong **unconditionally associative** construct: predicts same associative effect despite study/test modality shift once learning and general memory strength are matched. A reproducible loss under modality shift constrains this strong version.
- **modality-bound transfer** account: predicts reduced associative priming when encoding/test modality differs, at least under the declared visual word-completion assay and encoding tasks.
- **generic task-overlap** account: predicts a similar shift cost through matched processing features without positing a new autonomous implicit-memory subsystem. The 1989 abstract alone cannot exclude this rival.
- **response/measurement artifact**: altered baseline perceptual sensitivity, word fragment completion ceilings or strategy shifts could contribute; controls, participant counts and item variance must be audited from original 1989 study before drawing causal conclusion.

This is a real **world-facing constraint on a concept's scope**, not merely the existence of a new journal title. It remains a published authors' reported effect, not an independent replication by MQR.

## 4. Bridge to Faraday without equating mechanisms
Faraday and Ampère both observed electromagnetic rotations while disputing their implications for unobserved magnetic currents; subsequent interventions tested the inferential scope of their explanations. In the cognitive example, people observed priming and recognition contrasts while disagreeing about independent memory systems and cross-modal mechanisms. Across both domains, **newly specified tests can preserve earlier observations while restricting the reach of their interpretations**.

The MQR generalization is bounded: the *specific physical operations* in 1820s electrodynamics are not equivalent to experimental psychology's study–test conditions, and neither case proves an arbitrary quotient map has a unique historical realization.
