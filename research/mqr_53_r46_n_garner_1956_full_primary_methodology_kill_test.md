Source text extraction covers opening, original methodological definitions, examples, conclusions and reference list; page-image figure fidelity not audited.

## Original 1956 analytic argument (not indirect Feest 2005)

**pp.149–151:** Authors explicitly reject the simplistic reduction 'perception = observed discriminative response'. They allow perception as a theoretically inferred mediating process from stimuli and responses, so the observed response cannot alone settle properties of the perceptual process. Their basis is neither methodological rejection of experiment nor a strict Bridgman-style identity definition.

**pp.151–152:** They define 'converging operations' as multiple experimental operations designed to **select among, or eliminate, rival explanations** of an observed result. The proper target is not simple replication/parallel confirmation. Original hypothetical example: different visual recognition thresholds for stimuli with socially differing content might come from perceptual discrimination **or** willingness/ability to utter the response. Cross reassignment of stimulus labels and response labels produces discriminating alternatives. They explicitly note the first and second operations must be interpreted jointly, and perfect orthogonality is generally practically infeasible.

**p.152:** They distinguish genuine convergence from repeated or merely transformed versions of the same operation; neither disambiguates perceptual versus response mechanisms. The distinction is **hypothesis-relative**: a transformation uninformative for one rival distinction can be informative for another.

**pp.153–158:** Different constraints on response categories, response availability and scaling can mimic inferred perceptual properties. They stress constraints on response system and the nontrivial transition from stimulus-response curves to claims of interval/magnitude perception.

**p.159:** Their conclusion argues properly designed operational experiments can describe properties distinct from the observable response system, but only insofar as alternative explanations are excluded. One cannot simply assign unmeasured perceptual properties, nor is perception restricted to surface response.

## Consequence for R46-K

R46-K's toy H1/H2/H3 constructs three observationally equivalent hypotheses, and proposes two interventions separating context-dependent target from shared measurement artifact. This is **not new**, since Garner et al. (1956) formally establish the need for alternative-eliminating, independent rather than parallel operations and give a concrete discriminating experimental setup. The toy contributes no new identification theorem or new methodological discriminant beyond 1956.

Feest (2005), 'Operationism in Psychology: What the Debate Is About, What the Debate Should Be About', JHBS 41(2):131–149 DOI `10.1002/jhbs.20079`, source `1JXJyWfHOAVS3Uozgf8IsL8QQ9TDlqi0V`, correctly invokes Garner et al. as a response to circular validity; she refrains from guaranteeing this solves all issues. Campbell–Fiske (1959), DOI `10.1037/h0046016`, source `1rpeKl0VfcGR0PYi4nqEmDNCLFdbKfX03`, extends measurement validity checking across multiple traits/methods. No unsupported universal assertion that these methods always identify a true latent research object.

## Negative research decision

MQR 5.3 sub-hypothesis 'conceptually novel solution through converging operations and context/artifact alternative elimination' -> **REJECTED AS NOVEL**. Continue MQR only where a new prospectively discriminating criterion improves upon the strongest actual prior-art model and its explicitly limited assumptions. This gate should be treated as a research result, not as evidence that 'scientific inquiry is solved' or as grounds to abandon all other MQR questions.
