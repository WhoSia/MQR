# R45-B — Brigandt: the epistemic goal is already part of concept dynamics

**Primary text:** [Feest & Steinle (eds., 2012), *Scientific Concepts and Investigative Practice*, Ingo Brigandt, "The Dynamics of Scientific Concepts: The Relevance of Epistemic Aims and Values," pp. 75ff](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WJyJHhYFi3R27QNfNOu5Jr4pmsdq5Ua9/view). Introduction and main argument relevant to the three-part account read.

Brigandt analyzes scientific concepts in three dimensions: **reference**, **inferential role**, and **epistemic goal**. He explicitly argues that goals drive, and may justify, alteration of inferential role and reference. Conversely, changes in scientific beliefs can transform the goals themselves. His biology cases are evolutionary novelty, homology, and gene.

**Direct confrontation:** MQR's proposed cycle 'questions → methods → concept revision → revised standards' cannot claim explanatory priority if it only restates the influence of research goals on concepts. The proposal to add an explicit 'epistemic goal' coordinate is also directly anticipated by Brigandt.

**Harder task:** Identify an actual case in which a newly possible goal cannot be satisfactorily represented within Brigandt's account because no concept/user community yet exists to carry it. That possibility is not demonstrated merely by calling the situation pre-articulated.

**Status:** negative novelty verdict, no new three-coordinate semantic theory.
