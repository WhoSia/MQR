## 1. Critical distinction: dates, materials, and phases
Steinle, *Exploratory Experiments: Ampère, Faraday, and the Origins of Electrodynamics*, University of Pittsburgh Press 2016 (English translation of 2005 German work), held Google Drive PDF `1-kviuErWDAEJMRlcbACBX7FbLR55hV1G`; directly fetched full text (~1.23m extraction characters), examined Chapter 6 pp. 228ff and Tables 6.1–6.2 in extraction, relevant book passage about pp. 251–255 and 264ff.

His chapter distinguishes (a) September 3 discovery of electromagnetic rotation, (b) September 4–10 additional experiments before finalizing article, and (c) October–December stabilization/recognition. The next-day questions and reduction of multiple coils to the 'simple case' of rotation are especially important. Steinle explicitly disputes a one-day ex nihilo creative account: Faraday was repeating and re-presenting earlier experiments during preparation of his *Historical Sketch*. His comparative analysis contrasts Ampère's already partly shaped theoretical program with Faraday's exploratory reconstruction.

Royal Institution historical piece https://www.rigb.org/explore-science/explore/blog/birth-electric-motion and APS https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2001/08/faraday-electromagnetism independently support September 3 and 1821 publication sequence. Historical bibliographic reference in Steinle (1995) https://doi.org/10.1177/007327539503300202 identifies Faraday's 1821 article *On Some New Electro-Magnetical Motions, and on the Theory of Magnetism*, *Quarterly Journal of Science*, XII, pp. 74–96, published October 1821. Publication and diary order must not be confused. Frank James/Davy Notebooks project https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/davynotebooks/2021/09/02/frank-james-on-davy-faraday-and-electro-magnetic-rotations/ documents the rivalry, publication and experimental contexts, including limits of a triumphalist single-hero narrative.

Nersessian 2008 *Creating Scientific Concepts*, held full-book PDF `168Yv277pBhy--iQrNjhMWbYcFhVx3_KR`, directly fetched and read early exemplar of field concepts. Crucial historical correction: Faraday's **1852** separate works on **geometric lines** vs **physical lines** signal different epistemic commitments (demonstrated configuration vs speculative physical reality). Do **not** project his later field ontology or Maxwell's 1861–62 theory back to 1821 rotation. Nersessian already explains hybrid analogy/models, image-based reasoning and bootstrapping.

## 2. Actual material sequence supported by Steinle's reconstruction (not an unverified verbatim diary)
- Prior to September 3: historical sketch and experimental repetitions, attempts to stabilize appropriate visual/operational descriptions.
- September 3: rotation device makes continuous motion accessible as a striking experimental effect.
- September 4–10: continued parametric tests, multiple conductor and magnet configurations, coil/bar-magnet comparisons, trials with terrestrial magnetism. Some procedures fail or are set aside. Published exposition arranges these results as reductions to a 'simple case', not simply a chronological diary.
- 1821 article: rotation and 'simple case' used to classify and compare motion, including attraction/repulsion and coils. The attempt to reconstruct known phenomena as rotations is **not** equivalent to the later modern field theory.
- 1852 and beyond: physically real force lines vs geometric line patterns, later Maxwell field modeling. A long conceptual genealogy with intermediate failures and reworks, not one unchanged 'Faraday field' present at the outset.

## 3. Three rival hypotheses and a failure-sensitive test
H-SUDDEN: September 3 discovery created the whole conceptual structure spontaneously.
H-CONTINUITY: pre-September historical survey, repetitions and visual/procedural practices already supplied essential representational resources, then a particular effect reorganized them.
H-RETROSPECT: some apparent new question classes are imposed by later historical reconstruction or printed article structure rather than inferable from the diary order.

Steinle's analysis and related original-paper cataloguing presently favor continuity + reorganization over pure suddenness. But source-specific inferences about *what Faraday believed or intended on each day* require directly verified primary diary entries and the periodical article in their editions. H-RETROSPECT remains a real threat: article order is not diary order, and retrospective field-language contaminates analysis.

A meaningful proposed MQR finding would not merely redescribe this as paradigm genesis (Steinle and Nersessian did that). It would **pre-register** the contemporaneous set of experimental options and question contrasts, then identify one exact new testable comparison made possible by a specific device/representation; compare against both sudden and continuous alternatives. Current evidence suffices for a historically informed research program, not independent causal priority.
