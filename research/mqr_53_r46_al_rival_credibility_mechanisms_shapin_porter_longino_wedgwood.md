# MQR-5.3 R46-AL — From gentlemanly trust to impersonal rules? Strong rival philosophies of epistemic authority

Date 2026-11-09 KST. Method: comparative close reading of Shapin (1994), Porter (1995/2020), Longino (2002), Secord (2004), Moxham/Fyfe (2018), and Wedgwood's publicly documented 1784 print/archives. No claim of new philosophy; candidate discriminating case.

## A. Different historical accounts solve different problems

Shapin (1994), A Social History of Truth, chs 1–5, 7 and 8, newly held Drive 1EIXL6jv0ThqAhCeY21CrDq4vB-tGyw2f, argues that fact testimony requires knowing whom to trust. In his own argument summary, knowledge of trustworthy people is **necessary but not sufficient** to stabilize claims about things. Seventeenth-century English gentleman culture furnished locally persuasive assurances of truthful speech (autonomy from material coercion, honor, civil discussion). Epistemological Decorum shows how plausibility widened to instrument- and artisan-mediated novel phenomena. Ch.7 pp.323–325 warns that modern attribution 'Boyle's law PV=k for ideal gases' shifts historical ontology and mathematical representation from Boyle's original experiments on air. Ch.8 pp.355ff illustrates Denis Papin's skilled and partly unpublished work in Boyle's 1680 experimental research and manuscript production.

Theodore M. Porter (1995; new-preface edition 2020), Trust in Numbers: The Pursuit of Objectivity in Science and Public Life, full held Drive 16AJbAioeRAovbeUBbLR6EtI2pch64rle. New preface explicitly says title does NOT mean instinctive faith in numbers. Standardizing and procedural constraint emerge in settings of contested interest; mechanical objectivity promises impersonal rule-governance where personal trust is inadequate. Porter expressly notes rule following may OR MAY NOT be a good strategy for truth-seeking. Thus Shapin's reliance on personal standing versus Porter's impersonal rules address different trust problems; can coexist in an institution.

Helen E. Longino (2002), The Fate of Knowledge, full held Drive 14AVrxujqX41GbDmiS4dlA5cKwQVdlFdu. Reading of conclusion on transformative criticism emphasizes dissemination and response to criticisms, community member responsiveness to norms and relevant diverse perspectives. A publication or elite testimony alone is not sufficient for collectively certified objectivity; actual criticism must be possible and receive uptake. Important: this is an existing philosophical account; any MQR claim 'authority requires criticism' is not novel.

James Secord (2004), Knowledge in Transit, full held Drive 1Y0N6ke4RCf6iDw7pl9S34jgwcI9VjBYt, focuses on how scientific information moves through translation, media, expertise and audiences; diffusion may change content. Moxham/Fyfe 2018 full accepted manuscript Drive 1WuCeSSXNTb1yRI41M8yK8Vv9JjApCIPp specifically documents 1752 Society corporate disavowal of guaranteeing factual certainty and the rise of expert reports in 1832. Baldwin 2018 original Drive 1I-ifooc4mT_4J6tDtg131UWaod3G3mfQ traces distinct 1970s grant-policy and autonomy rhetoric.

## B. Wedgwood-specific primary observation (not a conclusion about motives)

1784 Philosophical Transactions volume 74 has Wedgwood printed item whose title credits: 'Mr. Josiah Wedgwood, F. R. S. Potter to Her Majesty'. Historical volume metadata indexed at https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/volumes/74/ and original archival catalog https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/l-and-p_8_79/paper-an-attempt-to-comparer-and-connect-the-indications-of-j-ws-pyrometer-with-those-of-the-mercurial-thermometer-by-josiah-wedgwood . Distinct visible credentials FRS membership and court-linked artisanal practice. These titles alone cannot show which specific credentials made the paper credible, and we MUST NOT infer class status from modern stereotype 'potter=non-gentleman'. Test historians' explanations of credibility with original contemporaneous citations and reputational reaction. Royal Society record read 13 May 1784; original 28pp manuscript plus three plates, full image collation pending.

Within the SAME 1784 volume, Cavendish's Experiments on Air is followed by Kirwan's Remarks, Cavendish's Answer and Kirwan's Reply (journal volume table of contents, not all full original read). This contemporaneous serial structure is evidence the Society could circulate debate rather than only one settled validated conclusion; it does NOT show all sides were equally likely correct nor that no reviewers existed.

## C. Rival predictions to test historically

Hsocial: Credibility of Wedgwood's high-temperature readings was shaped by court association, learned-society status, technical/craft expertise, and trust networks; expect records about author standing and demonstrations.

Hprocedural: Credibility was raised by publicly comparable calibration recipes, plates, independent reference thermometers and material processing protocols; expect replicating procedures / convergence tests and documented discrepancies across batches.

Hcritical: Criticisms, corrections and opportunities for public or private refutation were constitutive of warrant; expect concrete modifications to apparatus, calibration claims or their scope between 1782, 1784 and 1786, ideally prompted by identifiable independent critique.

Heditorial: Royal Society selected novel/important work for circulation without corporate guarantee of truth; expect formal 1752 disclaimer and 1784 committee minutes or contemporaneous editorial traces.

These are **overlapping hypotheses**, not mutually exclusive. The job is evidence of relative explanatory contributions and what warranted what, rather than fitting distinct philosophers to a unique box. Without full archival correspondence and facsimile parity, claims of disproving competitors are premature.

## D. Historical caution and falsification

1. Do not conflate 17th-century Boyle, 1784 Wedgwood, and post-1900 numerical/mechanical authority as same regime. 2. Do not treat published author and physical labor as identical; Papin is a source-based caution, not proof Wedgwood used invisible technicians in a specific experimental step. 3. Do not treat Boyle's air apparatus as directly measuring ideal-gas PV=k in modern senses; Shapin's ch.7 historical ontology warns against retrospectively validated transformations. 4. A reported linear silver/ceramic thermometer conversion is not demonstration of true T without separate material, atmosphere/time and procedural control. 5. If original 1784 printed advertisement claims institution accepts claims as true, the current editorial characterization must be reconsidered; if it disclaims, such inference is explicitly blocked. 6. If independent artisanal repetitions confirm robust thermometry even under changed firing histories, previous concern about unidentifiability will be empirically weakened.
