## Main hostile result: two collapses of the proposed 'question-generative reach' metric

### Collapse 1 — Unbounded formal question space
If all syntactically definable intervention queries are counted, a sufficiently expressive language can describe arbitrarily many counterfactual experiments *before* they are technically executable, recognized or relevant. A new scientific practice may change **accessibility** dramatically while changing the set of logically expressible sentences by zero. Therefore the count of definable questions is not a useful innovation statistic.

This is already implicit in MQR42 and in Clement/Nersessian, and cannot be represented as an MQR novelty theorem.

### Collapse 2 — Fixed-model discrimination misses question creation
Let D(P,H) be the subset of admissible queries whose answers distinguish models within a *preselected* hypothesis set H. If H has one member, D(P,H)=empty for any nonempty question-making practice, even when a scientist produces a useful new measured target or model. Also, changing the members or granularity of H can change |D| with no actual scientific practice change. Consequently |D| is **model-menu-relative**, not a universal count of creative power.

Even with fixed H, a merely relabeled query may increase the raw cardinality of D without adding new operational discrimination; quotient only by an explicitly justified *action/outcome* equivalence, preserving distinct physical provenance. A quotient appropriate for one claim may erase questions important for another.

A proposed more honest object is a **witnessed, provenance-indexed transition graph**, not a number:
- node: dated research practice with accessible tools, live problems, epistemic goals, success standards, models and actual evidence;
- edges: concept-forming heuristic, proposed operation, executed experiment/proof, qualified negative result, community uptake/revision;
- receipt for each edge: source time/edition, author/community, physical/logical resource, outcome, alternative reading, prior-art challenge and whether epistemic authority transfers.

This is a proposed history/epistemology research design, *not* a discovery theorem or necessarily superior to Brigandt, Dewey, Friedman, Kitcher, Nersessian, Haueis or Kuhn.

## Relation to the just-created causal example
Chat44 record02's two binary SCMs were observationally equivalent, but differed under do(X). This demonstrates the standard Pearl-style observational/interventional gap. However:
- If both interventions were already in the practice, it is **no evidence of question invention**.
- If one intervention was newly engineered, an independent historical or empirical receipt is required to date its introduction and show new scientific use.
- If both models are known in advance, selecting a discriminating intervention is *active experiment design*, not ex nihilo new-concept generation.
Thus the example is a **negative calibration against an overstated creative claim**, not evidence of paradigm genesis.

## Corrected prospective hypotheses
H1 (practice level): A historically sourced increase in accessibility of a discriminating query may occur without any new primitive syntactic expression or new immediate observation.
H2 (concept level): A new research target may be generated before a distinguishing experiment is executable. Its later successful uptake is separate from its prospective possibility.
H3 (epistemic cross-tradition): Equal operational results and local bridge success do not force identical goals, problem structures, or ontology; equally, they do not prove total incommensurability.
H4 (methodological negative): Any scalar ranking of 'scientific creativity' that ignores changes in live H, available actions, goals and provenance will misclassify some cases.

**Known prior-art threats:** Brigandt includes epistemic goals; Kitcher analyzes significant questions; Nersessian/Clement explain model construction; Lakatos explains proof-driven concept change; Friedman and Masterman analyze framework/exemplar shifts; active learning and experimental design already maximize expected discrimination under fixed queries.
