## Finding 2: 1752 institutional disavowal of truth-certification

Moxham and Fyfe (2018), The Royal Society and the Prehistory of Peer Review 1665–1965, The Historical Journal 61(4):863–889, DOI 10.1017/S0018246X17000334, institutionally released accepted author manuscript https://kar.kent.ac.uk/65042/3/Peer%20review%20v30%20AAM%20SUBMTD.pdf, examined through web text. Their detailed source analysis says 1752 Committee of Papers decided publication by collective secret ballot on abstracts; 1752–1957 journal advertisements insist that Society collectively gives no opinion on factual certainty or the propriety of authors' reasonings. Published works indicate interest/singularity or worthwhile treatment, not a warrant that conclusions are established. From 1832 expert written review reports became more routine (and surviving records available). Thus 1784 published Wedgwood paper was not necessarily externally refereed in the modern sense; current evidence cannot identify a named reviewer of the specific paper.

## Interpretation: source publication selection is not truth likelihood

Let T mean a research claim is materially reliable under contemporaneous tests, and A editorially accepted. Bayes: Pr(T|A)=[Pr(A|T)Pr(T)]/[Pr(A|T)Pr(T)+Pr(A|not T)Pr(not T)]. No estimate of this quantity is warranted by publication alone without a prevalence/prior and acceptance conditional on correctness. In an editorial regime selecting interesting observations and reputational suitability rather than truth-testing, Pr(A|T) and Pr(A|not T) may both be high. Even if published and reliable correlate, not evidence of individual claim proof. This is basic Bayesian epistemology, NOT a novel theorem.

## Stronger mechanism-level historical comparison

Secord (2004) Knowledge in Transit, Isis 95(4):654–672, DOI 10.1086/430657, full Drive 1Y0N6ke4RCf6iDw7pl9S34jgwcI9VjBYt, conceptualizes knowledge as circulation and communication. Their claims are different: publication/public reception are channels for circulation; measurement operations and independent control support warrant. One does not substitute for the other. The hard question is whether a reported calibration could be both worthy of circulation and inadequately identified as a stable physical quantity.
