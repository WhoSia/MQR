# MQR 5.0 — Methods of Quantitative Realism

**Current phase: 5.0 — Philosophical Inquiry, Primary-Source Reading & Research Harvest**

MQR studies how scientific and mathematical knowledge is made possible, limited, translated and transformed. The present phase returns to its foundational questions: whether a common world forces convergence on a common science; whether translations between scientific languages can be discovered; what mathematical proof, existence and objectivity require; and how historical changes of concepts, standards and exemplars reshape research.

The research proceeds by sustained reading of original works, comparison of competing arguments, reconstruction of earlier MQR questions, precise separation of philosophical conjectures from mathematical results, and careful provenance-aware literature harvesting. A publication is a possible outcome, **not a precondition or progress metric**.

## Working questions

1. **World and conceptual frameworks.** Can different scientific traditions investigate the same world while retaining non-equivalent concepts, observations and standards of evidence? Distinguish the existence of a translation from its discoverability.
2. **Mathematics and its foundations.** Compare Hilbert, Brouwer, Heyting, Gödel, Cohen and contemporary foundational alternatives; distinguish logical contradiction, incompleteness, independence, interpretability and changes in mathematical practice.
3. **Normal science and mathematical research.** Read Kuhn, Lakatos, Bourbaki and critics against concrete scientific and mathematical history. Distinguish surviving effective theories from foundational frameworks and shared problem-solving exemplars.
4. **Scientific reverse engineering.** Study observation, intervention and inverse inference from nature without assuming an intentional designer; compare inverse problems, representation, causal intervention and theory change.
5. **Continuity and discovery.** Investigate what established mathematical and scientific practices preserve through conceptual change, what they exclude, and how unanticipated alternatives may become intelligible.

## Research practice

- **Read before interpreting.** Record full bibliographic data, version, page, exact claim, objections and limits. Keep original research distinct from encyclopedic or pedagogical commentary.
- **Review genealogy without substituting audits for inquiry.** Cross-reference the MQR 0.X–4.X lineage where it materially clarifies a question; do not treat repeated reviews as new discoveries.
- **Formalize when it resolves a real distinction.** Existing formal-language and proof experiments remain historical evidence; they are not independent proof of an external scientific claim.
- **Report uncertainty.** Speculation, mathematical theorem, synthetic experiment, and independent physical evidence are distinct statuses.
- **Harvest responsibly.** Use Research OS `00_INTAKE` for new material; inspect versions, authorship, provenance and duplicates before canonical filing. Unavailable or copyright-restricted literature stays on an access-pending list; use legitimate publisher, author and library routes.
- **Do not manufacture research phases or novelty claims.** Advance the philosophical question, not the document counter.
