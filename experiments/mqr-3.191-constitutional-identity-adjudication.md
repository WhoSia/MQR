# MQR-3.191 — Constitutional Identity Adjudication, Scientific-Realism Thesis vs Cross-Domain Epistemic Governance, Research-OS Reduction, Practical Added-Value Test & Whether MQR Should Stop Seeking Philosophical Irreducibility

## 0. Question
MQR-3.191 adjudicates identity after the negative sequence MQR-3.181–3.190.

The question is not merely whether MQR remains philosophically interesting.

It is whether the program now has a sufficiently different object, success criterion, and failure mode to justify a Generation-IV transition.

Three candidate identities are tested:

1. **MQR-R** — an irreducible new theory of scientific realism;
2. **MQR-G** — a general cross-domain research-governance / Research-OS architecture;
3. **MQR-A** — a claim-level epistemic-authority constitution governing what commitment a specified evidence state may license, at what scope, for what role, with what live defeat conditions.

Generation IV is not automatically tied to philosophical novelty.
But a mere relabeling or demotion does not justify a new generation either.

## 1. Binding inheritance from 3.181–3.190
The following are fixed:

- standalone realist irreducibility has not been demonstrated;
- strongest mature local methodologies reproduce all currently tested MQR licensing verdicts;
- finite automata are only an optional diagnostic lane;
- exactness is neither necessary nor sufficient for scientific authority;
- C1 Contact / C2 Defeat / C3 Present State / C4 Firewall survive as the minimal constitutional kernel;
- new primitives may not be invented merely to preserve novelty;
- future mismatch search requires a pre-existing MQR rule and strongest-local-methodology reconstruction.

Therefore 3.191 may change identity, but may not retroactively declare the negative results to be hidden novelty wins.

## 2. Candidate MQR-R — standalone novel scientific-realism theory
### Required
MQR-R would need at least one of:
- a novel truth/realism condition;
- a novel ontology;
- an irreducible confirmation principle;
- a licensing verdict unavailable to strongest mature local methodology;
- a stable global realist principle that survives disciplinary diversity.

### Evidence
MQR-3.181–3.190 repeatedly failed these tests.

Current 2026 literature raises further pressure.

Mayne (2026), *Operationalizing Scientific Realism*, argues that pragmatist realism should be operationalized locally rather than through a single global ontological principle. Different ontological questions/domains can require different principles.

Menon (2026), *How to be a scientific realist: a normative–pragmatist proposal*, explicitly reconstructs realism in normative terms involving authority and pragmatist agent-first strategies.

Brousalis (2026) treats the realism debate as a comparative methodological problem under disciplinary diversity.

Thus even MQR's recent move toward authority/pragmatism/locality cannot currently be claimed as historically distinctive.

### Verdict
**MQR-R = FAIL AS CURRENT PROGRAM IDENTITY.**

MQR may still license realist commitments.
It is not currently supported as a new standalone theory of scientific realism.

## 3. Candidate MQR-G — general research governance / Research OS
### Research OS comparison
Research OS 2.4.4 frozen kernel already contains:

- Goal fidelity;
- Frontier compression;
- Authority separation;
- Provenance through transformation;
- Failure-preserving memory;
- World-contact minimality.

Its operator families additionally cover:
- failure-locus diagnostics;
- rival-separating probes;
- evidence dependency/cable topology;
- benchmark/world-contact scope;
- reuse qualification;
- recontact necessity;
- no-new-data certificate lifecycle.

It explicitly says:
- search/simulation/model agreement/automation do not automatically confer authority;
- world contact must be minimally restored when needed;
- native Lab authority is not automatically altered by Research OS.

### Reduction test
If MQR-3.191 simply says:

> "MQR is a cross-domain governance framework that preserves scope, provenance, failure and world contact,"

then it is largely reducible to Research OS 2.4.4.

That would not justify MQR-4.0.

### Verdict
**MQR-G = FAIL AS INDEPENDENT IDENTITY / RESEARCH-OS REDUCTION STRONG.**

## 4. Candidate MQR-A — claim-level epistemic-authority constitution
The surviving distinction is narrower.

Research OS asks:
> How should a research process search, preserve, test, transport, recontact, and coordinate evidence without authority laundering?

MQR-A asks:
> Given the current evidence/contact state for a particular claim, what epistemic commitment is presently licensed, at what scope and role, and under what conditions must that commitment be withheld, qualified, reopened, or revoked?

This is a different output type.

Research OS outputs primarily:
- search/readiness state;
- operator selection;
- evidence/recontact plan;
- frontier effect;
- certificates about data needs or process state.

MQR-A outputs primarily:
- claim-role authority;
- scope-qualified commitment;
- uncertainty/risk qualification;
- HOLD/refusal;
- revocation/reopening;
- realist promotion ceiling.

- (D): licensed domain/scope;

The constitutional kernel does not replace local methodology.
It constrains the transition from local evidence to commitment.

## 5. Local-methodology sovereignty
Mayne's 2026 argument against global ontological operationalizing creates a serious constraint.

MQR-A survives only if C1–C4 are **meta-constraints on licensing**, not substantive global ontological principles.

Therefore:

- MQR does not tell clinical chemistry how to calculate measurement uncertainty.
- MQR does not tell psychology what well-being is.
- MQR does not tell NASA how to validate a thermal model.
- MQR does not tell causal inference which estimator identifies an effect.

Those are local methodological tasks.

MQR asks whether the actual commitment asserted after those tasks:
- exceeds contacted scope;
- exceeds evidence role;
- erases live defeaters;
- treats stale/changed evidence relations as current;
- confuses successful use with stronger realist truth.

This is compatible with radical methodological locality while retaining cross-domain constitutional invariants.

## 7. Research-OS boundary
To prevent merger-by-renaming:

### Research OS jurisdiction
Research-process orchestration:
- what to search;
- what to compare;
- what to perturb;
- when to recontact;
- which evidence paths are redundant;
- which operator should run next.

### MQR jurisdiction
Claim-commitment adjudication:
- what does the current evidence license us to say?
- at what scope?
- in what role?
- with what qualifications?
- what exact event would downgrade/revoke that commitment?

### Non-substitution rule
Research OS process success never substitutes for MQR authority adjudication.

MQR authority adjudication never substitutes for the local scientific method or Research OS process planning.

## 8. Practical added-value test
A new generation requires more than a semantic distinction.

MQR-A must add at least one operational capability that is not already supplied by Research OS process governance.

Four prospective capabilities are frozen:

### PAV-1 — Cross-domain claim-role normalization
Given heterogeneous local outputs, MQR should map them to a common authority vocabulary without erasing local semantics.

### PAV-2 — Commitment ceiling
MQR should detect when a claim asserted in prose/decision exceeds the authority carried by the local evidence state even if the underlying local workflow was technically correct.

### PAV-3 — Revocation semantics
MQR should specify the exact authority transition when scope, rival set, evidence dependence, or world regime changes.

### PAV-4 — Realism-output distinction
MQR should distinguish:
- useful/predictive/action-authorized;
from
- ontologically or realistically licensed;
without denying the former.

If MQR-A cannot prospectively produce a useful distinction on these tasks beyond simple Research OS restatement, the independent program should merge or archive.

## 9. Practical-value evidence from current genealogy
Existing stages show partial evidence:

- accelerated approval: MQR cleanly separates regulatory action authority from verified clinical-benefit commitment;
- model validation: MQR keeps validated use from becoming global realist transport;
- measurement: MQR separates traceability/usefulness from stronger realist promotion;
- construct validation: MQR distinguishes implementation success from warranted construct claims.

However strongest mature local methodologies can already make these distinctions.

Therefore practical added value is currently:
**INTEGRATIVE / NORMALIZING / AUDITABLE**, not **LOCAL-VERDICT-NOVEL**.

This is enough to define a prospective program.
It is not enough to claim philosophical irreducibility.

## 10. Is this merely Research OS?
Not entirely, but the distinction is narrow.

Research OS 2.4.4 explicitly remains non-authoritative toward native Lab genealogies and is designed to coordinate inquiry.

MQR-A is allowed to issue claim-level authority verdicts **only when the local Lab asks for such adjudication and supplies the relevant evidence state**.

Thus MQR can function as a constitutional service called by Research OS or a native Lab.

This is closer to:
- an epistemic type system;
- authority semantics;
- a claim-licensing kernel;

than to a full research operating system.

## 13. MQR-4.0 constitutional preamble
Generation IV begins under the following identity:

> **MQR is a local-methodology-respecting, cross-domain epistemic-authority constitution that governs the transition from evidence/contact state to scoped, qualified, defeasible claim commitment. It may issue realist commitments as one output family, but it does not claim to replace local scientific methodology or constitute a novel universal theory of scientific realism.**

### Frozen prohibitions
- No novelty preservation by adding finer coordinates.
- No global ontological principle imposed across domains.
- No Research-OS duplication.
- No authority verdict unsupported by local evidence.
- No conversion of institutional/action authority into realist authority.
- No automatic authority transfer across scope, version, population, instrument, representation or role.

## 14. What MQR-4.0 must prove
Generation IV must earn its continued independent existence by practical cross-domain value.

Required demonstrations:
1. same authority schema can represent at least three non-isomorphic scientific domains without semantic flattening;
2. at least one real claim audit catches a commitment-level error not already explicit in the local workflow output;
3. the correction changes an actual scientific statement, scope, caveat or decision-role declaration;
4. MQR adds less bureaucracy than the error cost it prevents;
5. Research OS alone cannot produce the same claim-level authority object without importing the MQR semantics.

Failure on these tests triggers:
**MERGE-INTO-RESEARCH-OS / ARCHIVE-AS-PHILOSOPHICAL-GENEALOGY.**

## 15. Literature consequence
Recent philosophy makes the transition more defensible but simultaneously kills novelty claims.

- Mayne (2026): realism should be operationalized locally; global unified ontological principles can be counterproductive.
- Menon (2026): realism can be framed normatively in terms of authority and pragmatist agent-first strategies.
- Brousalis (2026): realism adjudication is comparative and complicated by disciplinary diversity.
- Research-governance literature already treats research quality/governance as contextual and multi-site.

Therefore MQR-4.0 must be:
**local in scientific content, global only in constitutional constraints.**
