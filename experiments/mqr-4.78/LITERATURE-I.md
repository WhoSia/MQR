## 2. Negative-control design

Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen & Cohen (2010) frame negative controls as controls chosen so that the hypothesized causal mechanism is absent while relevant bias-generating mechanisms remain comparable. Later work formalizes that negative-control validity depends on substantive causal assumptions, including exclusion and comparability assumptions.

Sources:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8118596/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10515451/

Absorption:
- a negative-control label is not self-certifying;
- control usefulness depends on its relation to the same hidden bias structure;
- a null control does not generically establish absence of hidden common mode;
- control selection itself is assumption-bearing.

MQR residue:
- the authority problem lies one level earlier: what warrants the **generator/selection process** that proposes the control family as adequate for the live dependency alternatives?

## 3. Current guidance reinforces selection dependence

Recent methodological guidance on negative controls emphasizes that selection is nontrivial: investigators must specify the intended bias target, generate candidates, assess the assumptions of candidate controls, and report limitations.

Source:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13525841/

This supports the 4.78 separation:

`NEGATIVE_CONTROL_GENERATION != NEGATIVE_CONTROL_VALIDITY != GENERATOR_COMPLETENESS`.

The guidance is prior art; MQR claims no new negative-control method.
