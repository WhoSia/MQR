## Explanandum lock

Given source burden S, target burden T, evidence E, standards K, proposed relation r, and adjudicators A:

```text
(S,T,E,K,A) -> r
```

under what conditions may r earn scientific-governance authority when:

- proposer and adjudicator may be the same lineage;
- several adjudicators may share one load-bearing ancestry;
- competing standards induce different relation classes;
- a standard may be written around the incumbent mapping;
- the same frozen evidence admits multiple relation assignments;
- majority agreement may be common-mode rather than independent;
- direct and meta-level translation criteria disagree;
- every mapping witness appears to require another mapping witness;
- external standard status, recency, consensus or prestige may be mistaken for truth;
- fresh world-facing challenges can discriminate among previously admissible mappings?

The court must distinguish:

```text
DECLARED RELATION != EARNED RELATION AUTHORITY
ADJUDICATOR COUNT != INDEPENDENT ADJUDICATION
STANDARDIZED != TRUE
CONSENSUS != IDENTIFICATION
META-VALIDATED != REGRESS-CLOSED
LOCAL AGREEMENT != UNIQUE CORRESPONDENCE
CONTESTED != FALSE
UNCONTESTED != TRUE
```

## Candidate canonical objects

### 1. Translation-Authority Receipt (TAR)

Records:
- source and target burden versions;
- proposed relation;
- proposer identity/lineage;
- adjudicator identities/lineages;
- witness items;
- standard(s) used;
- alternative admissible relations;
- challenge-set basis;
- world-facing discrimination status;
- conflicts;
- meta-dependencies;
- authority result;
- open residue.

### 2. Mapping-Witness Graph (MWG)

A bipartite/provenance graph connecting:
- relation claims;
- witness items;
- standards;
- adjudicators;
- source lineages.

Purpose: expose common ancestry and self-certification.

### 3. Adjudicator Independence Envelope (AIE)

Independence is not adjudicator count.

Candidate states:

```text
SELF_CERTIFIED
COMMON_ANCESTRY
PARTIALLY_INDEPENDENT
ANCESTRY_SEPARATED
UNRESOLVED
```

No state is a truth oracle.

### 4. Standard Capture Receipt (SCR)

A standard is marked capture-risk when its decisive criterion depends on the incumbent relation, proposer-specific constructs, or a post-outcome revision that favors one mapping.

Candidate states:

```text
NO_CAPTURE_WITNESS
INCUMBENT_DEFINED
POST_OUTCOME_REVISED
COMMON_SOURCE_STANDARD
CONFLICTING_STANDARDS
UNRESOLVED
```

### 5. Correspondence Admissible Set (CAS)

Instead of forcing a single mapping relation:

```text
CAS(S,T,E,K) subseteq {EXACT,REFINE,MERGE,OVERLAP,DISJOINT,UNMAPPED}
```

If |CAS| > 1, correspondence is underdetermined at the declared surface.

### 6. Mapping-Conflict Receipt (MCR)

Localizes:
- adjudicator disagreement;
- standard disagreement;
- direct-vs-meta disagreement;
- challenge-set disagreement;
- world-facing defeat.

Conflict does not itself select a winner.

### 7. Translation Challenge Packet (TCP)

A frozen challenge family capable of discriminating among candidate mappings.

World-facing discrimination may reduce CAS, but success remains challenge-relative and open to future challenge expansion.

### 8. Meta-Translation Dependency Graph (MTDG)

Tracks:
- mapping relation;
- standard used to validate it;
- any higher-order translation needed to compare standards/adjudicators.

Cycles and unsupported infinite ascent are explicit.

### 9. Meta-Regress Boundary (MRB)

Candidate constitutional rule:

```text
NO REQUIREMENT FOR A FINAL META-TRANSLATION ORACLE
```

Instead:
- finite declared dependency graph;
- no hidden circular self-support;
- explicit unresolved meta-debt;
- direct world-facing discrimination where available;
- reopening under successor challenges.

This is a governance stop condition, not a metaphysical foundation.

## Core countermodel 1 — self-certified EXACT

One lineage:
- proposes A -> Z as EXACT;
- supplies the decisive witness;
- adjudicates the witness;
- writes the standard.

Even if internally coherent:

```text
SELF-CONSISTENCY != INDEPENDENT MAPPING AUTHORITY
```

## Core countermodel 2 — three adjudicators, one ancestry

Three named adjudicators all derive their mapping criteria from the same source standard or training corpus.

```text
ADJUDICATOR COUNT = 3
INDEPENDENT ANCESTRY = 1
```

Therefore majority count cannot manufacture independence.

## Core countermodel 3 — competing standards

Under K1:
```text
A -> Z = EXACT
```

Under K2:
```text
A -> Z = OVERLAP
```

with the same frozen source/target burdens and evidence.

Therefore:

```text
RELATION CLASS MAY BE STANDARD-RELATIVE
```

unless additional discriminating evidence earns a narrower CAS.

## Core countermodel 4 — correspondence underdetermination

Frozen evidence E is compatible with both REFINE and OVERLAP.

If no declared challenge distinguishes them:

```text
CAS = {REFINE, OVERLAP}
```

Forcing one relation is authority inflation.

## Core countermodel 5 — standard capture

An incumbent relation is EXACT.

After seeing a challenge that would downgrade it, the standard is revised so that the challenged dimension no longer counts.

```text
POST-OUTCOME STANDARD REVISION
!=
INDEPENDENT VALIDATION
```

## Core countermodel 6 — meta-translation cycle

```text
relation r validated by standard K
K comparable to K' by meta-map m
m authoritative because r is accepted
```

This is circular support, not regress closure.

## Core countermodel 7 — world-facing separator

Two relations remain admissible under semantic/internal evidence:

```text
CAS = {EXACT, OVERLAP}
```

A frozen external challenge q produces different predicted coverage.

After reveal:

```text
q defeats EXACT but not OVERLAP
=> CAS shrinks
```

This earns local discrimination, not final correspondence.

## Candidate positive principles

### A — Mapping Authority Is Earned, Not Parsed
A syntactically valid BTR edge has no automatic scientific authority.

### B — Independence Is Ancestry-Relative
Named adjudicator plurality must not be confused with independent evidence or standards.

### C — Relation Authority May Be Set-Valued
When multiple relation classes survive, retain CAS rather than force a singleton.

### D — Standards Are Objects of Audit
Standards have provenance, revision history, dependence, and capture risk.

### E — World-Facing Discrimination Can Narrow but Not Close
Fresh challenge packets may shrink CAS at declared scope without producing final correspondence.

### F — Conflict Localization Precedes Arbitration
Adjudicator/standard conflict is exposed before any higher-order constitutional decision.

### G — Meta-Regress Is Made Explicit, Not Magically Terminated
A finite governance receipt can stop execution while carrying unresolved meta-debt.

### H — Circular Meta-Support Cannot Create Authority
A cycle in MTDG does not become independent support by repetition.

### I — No Correspondence Closure
A passing TAR cannot prove unique world correspondence or future translation closure.
