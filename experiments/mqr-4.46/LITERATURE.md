## Internal MQR prior-art pressure

### MQR-3.171 — Translation-Induced Consensus Audit
Already established:
- shared vocabulary is not neutral;
- translation can erase defeat-relevant disagreement;
- mapped consensus is valid only at the granularity preserved by the alignment;
- translation-loss and residual-disagreement ledgers are required.

Therefore MQR-4.46 claims no novelty for "translation can distort" or "alignment may manufacture consensus."

### MQR-3.172 — Translation-Selection Audit
Already established:
- one valid map is not the unique correct map;
- several defensible alignments may preserve different distinctions;
- map-selection laundering is possible;
- consensus under one map is not consensus across admissible maps;
- authority should track defeat-relevant invariants across map competition.

This is the strongest internal novelty kill.

Therefore MQR-4.46 does **not** claim invention of multiple-map auditing or set-valued alignment in the abstract.

The residual problem is narrower:

```text
WHO OR WHAT AUTHORIZES MEMBERSHIP IN THE ADMISSIBLE MAP SET?
```

The new target is the provenance, independence, contestability, capture risk and meta-dependency of the mapping-witness process itself.

## Drive-native pressure

### Star & Griesemer (1989), Institutional Ecology, 'Translations' and Boundary Objects
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- scientific coordination can occur without global consensus;
- translation is many-to-many and may admit multiple coherent sets;
- standardization and boundary objects are prior art for cross-world coordination;
- obligatory passage points and translation gatekeeping make authority itself sociologically non-neutral.

MQR-4.46 therefore cannot claim novelty for "standards coordinate heterogeneous actors" or "translation is institutionally mediated."

### Tal (2017), Calibration: Modelling the Measurement Process
Drive copy present. DOI: 10.1016/j.shpsa.2017.09.001

Pressure:
- calibration is model-based rather than a direct readout of nature;
- standards are useful for maintaining coherence across measurement networks but are not automatically epistemic foundations;
- reliability is earned through model prediction, testing and coherence.

This directly defeats any rule:
```text
STANDARDIZED -> TRUE
```

### Reijula & Blanco Sequeiros (2024), Persistent Evidential Discordance
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- persistent discordance can rationally survive across lines of evidence;
- metadata and line-of-evidence analysis are needed to diagnose systematic disagreement;
- more data within the same lines may fail to resolve the dispute.

MQR-4.46 therefore treats adjudicator disagreement as a typed state rather than an automatic demand for forced consensus.

### Weatherall (2019), Theoretical Equivalence in Physics, Parts 1–2
Drive Part 2 present; MQR literature custody already records Part 1/2. DOI Part 2: 10.1111/phc3.12591; Part 1: 10.1111/phc3.12592.

Pressure:
- empirical, definitional, categorical and interpretational equivalence are distinct criteria;
- choice of what structure/equivalence to preserve is philosophically substantive;
- formal equivalence criteria do not automatically settle interpretation.

MQR-4.46 therefore cannot treat one equivalence standard as a neutral oracle.
