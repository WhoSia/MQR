# MQR-4.79 — LITERATURE COLLISION I

Status: **PRIOR-ART ABSORPTION / TARGET-CONSTITUTION RESIDUE NARROWED**

## External-validity baseline

Degtiar & Rose (2023), *A Review of Generalizability and Transportability*, already treats specification of the target population as a critical decision and reviews assumptions needed to move causal effects from study samples to targets.

Egami & Hartman (2023), *Elements of External Validity: Framework, Design, and Analysis*, decomposes external validity across populations, treatments, outcomes, and contexts and formalizes target-relative generalization.

Absorption:
- target populations/scopes must be declared;
- generalization is target-relative;
- multiple dimensions of external validity exist;
- no novelty credit for merely saying that study sample != target population.

## Construct-validity baseline

Flake, Pek & Hehman (2017), *Construct Validation in Social and Personality Research: Current Practice and Recommendations*, treats construct conceptualization and validation as a continuing scientific obligation rather than a one-time label.

Absorption:
- a target construct is not validated by measurement success alone;
- operationalization and construct scope require independent justification.

## Surviving MQR-4.79 residue

The candidate residue is not target specification itself. It is the authority structure for competing target-family constitutions:

```text
TARGET_DECLARATION != TARGET_WARRANT
TARGET_AGREEMENT != TARGET_AUTHORITY
LARGER_TARGET != BETTER_WARRANT
FINER_PARTITION != STRONGER_WARRANT
GENERATOR_SUCCESS_ON_T_K != COMPLETENESS_OF_K
```

A locally warranted target family may coexist with another admissible but incomparable target family.

Relative termination is allowed on independently warranted finite contracts or grammars.

Later off-target escape can reopen a local coverage certificate without retroactively falsifying the narrower historical statement.

## Novelty firewall

No novelty claim for:
- external validity;
- generalizability/transportability;
- target-population estimation;
- construct validation;
- reference-class declaration;
- generic scope dependence.

Candidate MQR residue:
- competing coverage constitutions;
- non-scalar comparison of target-family warrants;
- split/merge receipts for target-family revision;
- constitution-induced blindness;
- defeasible reopening after off-target escape.
