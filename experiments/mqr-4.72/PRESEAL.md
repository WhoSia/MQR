## Core question

> When a scientific or mathematical practice inherits a scope from an earlier practice, what exactly is inherited: a social permission, an instrument/proof capability, a target intention, a mathematical semantics, a world-contact relation, or some combination of these?

And:

> Can scope authority remain truth-conducive when the intended object of inquiry, the available contact operations, or the proof/measurement language changes?

## Two contact modes

### Natural-scientific contact

Authority is constrained by empirical interaction with the world through observation, measurement, intervention, replication, calibration and instrument chains.

### Mathematical contact

Authority is constrained by proof, derivation, counterexample, model construction, independence, consistency-relative results and transport across formal/semantic frameworks.

The two modes may share constitutional patterns but must not be collapsed.

## Intentionality question

Intentionality here is not private psychology.

It is operationalized as the structured directedness of inquiry:


A change in intention may alter which observations, interventions or proofs count as relevant even when raw data or formal derivations are unchanged.

## Strong falsifiers

F1 — **Institutional-fiat drift**
If 4.72 reduces scope authority to who is authorized to declare a scope, the stage fails.

F2 — **Kuhn-story absorption**
If the result is only that paradigms shape what scientists see, novelty fails.

F3 — **Theory-ladenness duplication**

F4 — **Metrology absorption**

F5 — **Mathematical-practice absorption**
If theorem/proof transport is fully standard mathematical semantics with no additional MQR authority distinction, novelty fails.

F6 — **Intentionality psychologization**
Private motives, beliefs or intentions are not primitive epistemic state variables.

F7 — **Truth-by-procedure error**
Successful execution of a method cannot by itself establish truth.

F8 — **Truth-by-consensus error**
Community acceptance cannot substitute for empirical/proof defeatability.

F9 — **Historical hindsight leakage**
Later truth cannot be smuggled into contemporaneous scope authority.

F10 — **Representation laundering**
Same mathematical or linguistic label across generations does not prove same target/contact semantics.

F11 — **Proof-system fiat**
Changing axioms and retaining a syntactic theorem label does not establish unchanged semantic content.

F12 — **World-contact loss**
If inherited scope loses all routes for the world/counterexample/proof obligation to defeat the claim, inherited authority must be downgraded.

## Candidate positive results

P1 — **Intentional target/contact separation**
The target an inquiry is about and the operation used to contact it must be represented separately.

P2 — **Defeat-route inheritance criterion**
Scope authority transports only when relevant defeat routes survive or are replaced by independently stronger routes.

P3 — **Calibration/proof-chain transport**
Traceability or derivation ancestry may justify local inheritance when semantics and scope are preserved.

P4 — **Exploratory expansion**
Scientific contact may legitimately expand the target/question space without prior theory fixing every candidate phenomenon.

P5 — **Truth-authority ceiling**
Inherited authority remains weaker than truth whenever the inherited contact system is only one fallible access route.

P6 — **Mathematical scope theorem**
Relative mathematical closure may terminate on explicit axioms/semantics, but theorem authority does not automatically transport across framework change.
