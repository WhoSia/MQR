## Kuipers lineage correction
The Kuipers literature is NOT treated as one static doctrine.

Frozen lineage:
- 2000: maximal/strong-theory framework in From Instrumentalism to Constructive Realism.
- 2005: author's own synopsis of the 2000 architecture.
- 2009/2011: probabilistic and belief-revision extensions.
- 2014: explicit correction: Kuipers states that he had wrongly assumed for 1982–2012 that the strong/maximal theory claim was needed.
- 2016: two-sided <models, postulates> theory architecture; inclusion/representation and exclusion/postulate functions are separated.
- 2020 (online 2018): refined similarity/structurelikeness and idealization-concretization over the two-sided architecture.

Therefore later Kuipers papers are treated as successor revisions, not simple summaries of the 2000 position.

MQR implication:
successor correction itself is evidence that framework transport must be tested rather than presumed.

## Constructs under external calibration

External question:

External question:

External question:

## Scoring rule for Lane A
No universal scalar.

Each axis is classified:
- HIGH
- MID
- LOW
- HOLD/NA

The classification must cite raw masked receipt fields.

Numeric values may be retained only as domain-local diagnostics.

## Native external adjudication
After Real-Language profile seal, native labels are revealed.

For each evaluable claim-axis pair, native adjudication is reduced only to ordinal direction:
- POSITIVE / SURVIVED
- MIXED / QUALIFIED
- NEGATIVE / DEFEATED
- HOLD / UNEVALUABLE

The reduction must be based on the native project's own explicit verdict/authority language.

No Real-Language axis value may be changed after reveal.

## Success criteria

### E1 — External directional concordance
Among evaluable claim-axis pairs:
>= 80% exact directional agreement.

### E2 — Cross-domain floor
For every domain with >= 2 evaluable targeted axis judgments:
agreement >= 2/3.

### E3 — Target-axis discriminance
A native failure specific to transport, identification, or world-contact must not automatically collapse all five Real-Language axes.

At least three externally negative cases must show:
target-axis contraction with at least one non-target axis remaining MID/HIGH.

### E4 — Defeat-exposure polarity
D must not be confused with claim success.

At least two cases in which a claim is defeated but the native project demonstrates a strong live failure route should receive:
claim axis LOW
and D HIGH/MID.

### E5 — N anti-counting
Lane B must reject:
number of methods = independent evidence.

If fMRI+MEG share enough ancestry that full independence is not justified, N cannot be set HIGH merely because modality count=2.

### E6 — Cross-domain ordinal invariance
Where two domains instantiate the same externally adjudicated ordering (e.g. stronger vs weaker held-out transport), Real-Language must preserve ordering direction.

No requirement is made that the numeric distance be equal.

### E7 — No scalar resurrection
No TPX or composite truth-proximity scalar may be used for adjudication.

## Promotion rules

### LEVEL-3-ORDINAL-PASS for an axis requires:
1. >= 3 evaluable external judgments;
2. judgments span >= 2 domains;
3. directional agreement >= 80%;
4. no systematic cross-domain reversal;
5. no evidence that the axis merely restates another axis.

### LEVEL-3-NUMERIC remains HOLD unless:
domain-independent interval/ratio semantics are independently justified.

### Full W/N/I/T/D LEVEL-3 promotion requires:
all five axes separately pass the ordinal rule.

Otherwise:
promote only axes that pass and keep others HOLD.

## Measurement-invariance interpretation
Cross-domain invariance here means:
the construct ordering survives across domains.

It does NOT mean:
configural/metric/scalar invariance in the strict psychometric SEM sense has been established.

Strict numeric measurement invariance remains a successor problem.

## Literature constraints
Use:
- Cronbach & Meehl: validation as an ongoing network of predicted relations.
- Campbell & Fiske: convergent evidence requires discriminant/method analysis.
- Messick: validity concerns interpretation/use and generalizability.
- Borsboom et al.: causal measurement interpretation is stronger than correlation.
- measurement-invariance literature: cross-group meaning cannot be assumed.
- OECD/JRC: aggregation/weighting sensitivity blocks arbitrary scalarization.
- Kuipers lineage: empirical progress and truth approximation require explicit target/possibility constitution and later revisions weaken/generalize the 2000 assumptions.

## Failure interpretation
A failure of external calibration may mean:
- an axis is internally coherent but domain-dependent;
- the external criterion is not commensurable;
- the adapter leaks another construct;
- the axis requires subdivision;
- the axis should remain a typed diagnostic rather than a measurement coordinate.

No axis is protected from retirement.
