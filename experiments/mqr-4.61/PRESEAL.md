## Explanandum lock

MQR-4.61 asks how a reflexive scientific constitution may revise **relations among promotion reasons over time** while preserving the distinction between:

1. evidence-sensitive constitutional history; and
2. an illicit universal score over histories.

The stage does **not** assume:
- monotone evidence accumulation;
- commutative revision;
- permanent conflict resolution;
- monotone veto power;
- fixed reason identity;
- path independence;
- inevitable convergence;
- a scalar measure of historical goodness.

## Path sensitivity admissibility

Historical path may affect current promotion authority **only** through typed state-bearing consequences, including:
- option destruction or creation;
- irreversible evidence contamination;
- provenance loss;
- dependency/debt creation;
- scope/horizon commitment;
- irreversible intervention;
- review/replication availability.

The following are not sufficient:
- elapsed time alone;
- sunk effort;
- institutional prestige;
- narrative continuity;
- number of prior promotions;
- an untyped "history score".

## Primary hypotheses

1. Same terminal pairwise relation does not imply the same constitutional authority when material history-bearing state differs.
2. Revision order need not commute when one event changes the admissibility, provenance, or availability of later evidence/actions.
3. Genuine incomparability may be resolved by new evidence and later legitimately reopen.
4. Scope/horizon revision can reverse a relation without contradiction when the indices are explicit.
5. Reason identity is not necessarily temporally rigid; typed reason mutation is admissible only with ancestry continuity.
6. Veto activation and veto retirement are both possible; veto power is not globally monotone.
7. Conflict cycles can persist without licensing arbitrary scalar tie-breaking.
8. Hysteresis is admissible only when traceable to material retained state, not to historical prestige or sunk cost.
9. Lost-option debt survives relabeling and can block naive restoration of a prior state.
10. A sufficiently enriched state may summarize relevant history, but this does not imply a universal utility over histories.
11. A reopened conflict is not evidence of constitutional failure by itself.
12. No universal historical meta-utility is earned.
