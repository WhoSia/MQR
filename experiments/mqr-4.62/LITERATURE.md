## Direct prior-art pressure

### Bayesian experimental design

**Chaloner & Verdinelli (1995), Bayesian Experimental Design: A Review.**

Direct pressure:
- once prior, model, design space and utility are constituted, experiment selection can be placed in a decision-theoretic optimization framework;
- multiple familiar optimality criteria can be justified as local instances of that framework.

MQR consequence:
- local optimization of experiment choice is strongly established prior art;
- MQR-4.62 cannot claim novelty from "choose the best experiment under a constituted model/utility".

### Information-based active data selection

**MacKay (1992), Information-Based Objective Functions for Active Data Selection.**

Direct pressure:
- expected informativeness can guide active measurement selection;
- different targets of information produce different acquisition criteria;
- the criteria assume the relevant hypothesis/model space is correct, explicitly identified by MacKay as a principal weakness.

MQR consequence:
- expected information gain and active selection are prior art;
- model-space dependence is not novel;
- the live question is what constitutional safeguards remain when the very action policy can hide omitted rivals, destroy options or alter later evidence status.

### Adaptive submodularity / greedy guarantees

**Golovin & Krause (2010/2011), Adaptive Submodularity: Theory and Applications in Active Learning and Stochastic Optimization.**

Direct pressure:
- under adaptive submodularity and related assumptions, greedy adaptive selection can receive approximation guarantees relative to an optimal policy.

MQR consequence:
- nonmyopic sequential guarantees are established in important constituted problem classes;
- MQR-4.62 must not imply greedy selection is generically defective;
- instead it asks whether the assumptions licensing those guarantees are themselves open-world scientific objects.

Direct pressure:

MQR consequence:

MQR consequence:

- MQR-4.62 must not claim novelty for exploration/exploitation or active probing.

### Theory-motivated experimentation can generate its own evidence ecology

**Dubova, Moskvichev & Zollman (2026), Against Theory-Motivated Experimentation: Can Random Experimental Choice Lead to Better Theories?**

Direct pressure:
- their multi-agent simulations jointly model experimentation, theorizing and social learning;
- across their tested environments, theory-guided strategies can collect less representative/easier-to-explain evidence and create an illusion of epistemic success;
- random selection is offered as a robust comparator within the specified experimental design space.

MQR consequence:
- generic policy-induced evidence-selection bias is now unusually direct prior art;
- MQR-4.62 must not claim that search policy shaping evidence is new;
- randomization is a powerful positive control but cannot be elevated by MQR into a universal scientific oracle because the design space itself remains constituted and incomplete.

## 2026 robustness pressure added after reveal

Two AISTATS 2026 results sharpen the novelty boundary further.

### Misinformation in a statistical experiment

**Callahan & Catanach (2026), On the Misinformation in a Statistical Experiment.**

Pressure:
- under model/inference misspecification, a design ranked as more informative can amplify bias and yield confident but incorrect inference;
- classical experiment-comparison axioms can fail under misspecification;
- robust experimental-design machinery is therefore already moving beyond naive informativeness maximization.

MQR consequence:
- `MORE INFORMATION != BETTER SCIENCE` is even more clearly prior-art territory.
- MQR-4.62's surviving distinction must involve open-frontier relation change, option destruction, ancestry and reopening rather than generic misspecification robustness.

### Representative, Informative, and De-Amplifying active learning

**Tang, Sloman & Kaski (2026), Representative, Informative, and De-Amplifying: Requirements for Robust Bayesian Active Learning under Model Misspecification.**

Pressure:
- active acquisition under misspecification can require representativeness and error-de-amplification in addition to informativeness;
- acquisition criteria can therefore be explicitly multi-factor even within modern Bayesian active-learning frameworks.

MQR consequence:
- typed multi-criteria acquisition is not enough for novelty by itself.
- The remaining MQR burden is to show value in explicitly tracking **unconceived-rival reopening, evidence ancestry/common-mode structure, branchwise option loss, and nonmyopic scientific debt**.

These papers do not alter the frozen MQR-4.62 cases or verdict criteria; they narrow the claim ceiling after the first successful scientific reveal.
