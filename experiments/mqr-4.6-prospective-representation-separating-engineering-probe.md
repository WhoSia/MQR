## 2. Rival models

### Rival A — reduced RC diffusion representation
A long resistive-capacitive line is represented using distributed:
- resistance \(R\);
- capacitance \(C\).

Inductance is absent from the causal representation.

The rival can represent:
- distance-sensitive attenuation;
- temporal spreading/retardation;
- low-rate cable behavior.

Frozen rule:
after baseline calibration, the RC rival may **not** post-hoc change \(R\) or \(C\) merely because an inductive intervention succeeded.

### Rival B — full RLCG transmission representation
The richer line representation uses:
- resistance \(R\);
- inductance \(L\);
- capacitance \(C\);
- leakage conductance \(G\).

Propagation is governed by

\[
\gamma(\omega)=\sqrt{(R+j\omega L)(G+j\omega C)}.
\]

Under the distortionless relation

\[
RC=LG,
\]

the frequency dependence of attenuation/phase distortion is strongly reduced in the idealized distributed model.

The crucial point is not global superiority.

It is that \(L\) is a manipulable coordinate that Rival A does not contain.

## 3. Baseline equivalence
In a slow, highly resistive/capacitive long-line regime where inductive effects are negligible, the richer model can reduce toward the RC description.

Therefore the rivals are deliberately chosen to be approximately baseline-equivalent over a low-rate envelope:

\[
R_{RC}\sim_{\Omega_0}R_{RLCG}.
\]

Baseline success alone cannot license realist credit for \(L\).

This directly implements MQR-4.5's intervention-equivalence requirement.

## 4. Differential intervention preseal
Intervention:

\[

\]

implemented physically by adding suitably spaced inductive loading coils while preserving the same line/cable objective.

### Frozen prediction A — RC rival
With \(R,C\) held fixed and no \(L\) coordinate in the representation:

- no mechanism-specific improvement from inductive loading is available;
- any post hoc fit that changes effective \(R,C\) after observing success is disallowed for this comparison.

### Frozen prediction B — RLCG rival
Appropriately increased/distributed inductance should:

- reduce frequency-selective distortion in the intended telephone band;
- improve articulation/transmission at distances where the unloaded line becomes poor or unusable;
- do so because the intervention changes a variable explicitly load-bearing in the richer representation.

### Fatal outcomes
RLCG differential fails if:
- properly implemented loading does not materially improve the relevant transmission outcome;
- observed improvement can be attributed to an uncontrolled change that does not depend on inductive structure;
- benefit occurs only after post-hoc retuning not licensed by the preseal.

Inconclusive if:
- loading spacing/coil parameters are grossly outside the design regime;
- unrelated line properties change materially;
- the outcome measure does not probe distortion/transmission.

## 7. External artifact world-contact replay — Pupin artificial cable
A contemporary engineering account reports Pupin's artificial cable with **250 sections**, built to represent a long cable and equipped with inductance coils at the section joints.

The same account reports the unloaded condition:

- about 50 miles equivalent: worked well;
- about 75 miles: worked fairly well;
- about 100 miles: impracticable;
- beyond about 112 miles: articulation effectively lost.

After inserting the coils, the reported outcome was materially different:

- the "drumminess" ceased;
- conversation could be carried on rapidly through the full 112-mile circuit.

This is not merely another observation of the baseline system.

It is an artifact-mediated response to a deliberate rival-differential intervention.

### Verdict
**DIFFERENTIAL OUTCOME = CONSISTENT WITH RLCG/INDUCTIVE STRUCTURE AND INCONSISTENT WITH THE FROZEN RC-ONLY CAUSAL RIVAL.**
