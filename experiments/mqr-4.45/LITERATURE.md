## Drive-native pressure

### Sankey (1993), Kuhn's Changing Concept of Incommensurability
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- Kuhnian incommensurability changed from broad paradigm disparity to localized semantic/translation failure.
- MQR-4.45 therefore cannot claim novelty for the idea that cross-theory conceptual translation may be partial or local.
- It strengthens the need to avoid a global "same burden" relation.

DOI: 10.1093/bjps/44.4.759

### Wang (2002), Taxonomy, truth-value gaps and incommensurability
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- Taxonomic structures may be unmatchable across scientific languages.
- Translation loss may affect truth-value status rather than merely vocabulary.
- MQR-4.45 must not reduce ontology change to string/label matching.

DOI: 10.1016/S0039-3681(01)00039-5

### Galison (1999), Trading Zone: Coordinating Action and Belief
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- Local coordination can survive global conceptual heterogeneity.
- This is prior art against a fatalist branch in which ontology nonidentity destroys every cross-constitution comparison.
- MQR-4.45 should seek local mapped-subspace comparability rather than global semantic unity.

### Pérez, Rubio Garcia & Zapata (2025), PROV-IDEA
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- Schema evolution and data provenance must be managed together.
- Version history and provenance of transformation are already established engineering concerns.
- MQR-4.45's revision receipts therefore cannot claim novelty merely for recording provenance of change.

DOI: 10.1145/3697008

### Goknil et al. (2014), Change impact analysis for requirements
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- Change propagation and metamodel-level impact analysis supply mature machinery for revision dependency.
- MQR novelty, if any, must lie in the scientific-governance semantics of unpaid search burden rather than generic change impact.

### Gama et al. (2014), A Survey on Concept Drift Adaptation
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- Time-varying concepts and adaptive response are mature in machine learning.
- MQR-4.45 cannot equate "concept drift exists" with a new scientific-philosophical contribution.
- Its live issue is normative/epistemic continuity of obligations under representational drift.

## External novelty-kill not currently found in Drive

### Noy & Klein (2004), Ontology Evolution: Not the Same as Schema Evolution
Key pressure:
- ontology evolution differs from schema evolution because explicit semantics and usage create multiple compatibility dimensions;
- version comparison requires more than structural schema succession.

DOI INTAKE REQUEST: **10.1007/s10115-003-0137-2**

### Flouris et al. (2008), Ontology change: classification and survey
Key pressure:
- ontology change is already a broad field containing versioning, evolution, heterogeneity and consistency problems;
- MQR must not claim invention of ontology-change taxonomy.

DOI INTAKE REQUEST: **10.1017/S0269888908001367**

### Hartung, Groß & Rahm (2013), COnto-Diff
Key pressure:
- expressive ontology-evolution mappings already include merge, split and subgraph-level changes;
- invertible and complex evolution mappings are prior art;
- MQR-4.45 must therefore distinguish its debt/provenance semantics from generic ontology diff machinery.

DOI INTAKE REQUEST: **10.1016/j.jbi.2012.04.009**

## Novelty ceiling after pressure

- scientific incommensurability;
- local translation failure;
- ontology evolution/versioning;
- split/merge ontology diff;
- provenance-aware schema evolution;
- concept drift;
- change impact analysis.
