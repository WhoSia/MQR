# MQR-4.5 — Load-Bearing Invariant Identification, Rival Representation Intervention Equivalence, Artifact-Success Attribution, Convenience-Controlled Transport & Whether Engineering Success Can Localize Realist Commitment

## 0. Question
MQR-4.4 showed that engineering usually selects representation ecologies rather than single representations, and that world-contact acts more like a filter than a unique selector.

MQR-4.5 asks:

> When an engineered artifact succeeds, which part of the representation actually earned the success?

The main danger is credit laundering:

\[
\text{artifact success}\Rightarrow\text{full representation confirmed}
\]

even when several rival representations could have generated the same successful intervention.

## 1. Rival representation intervention equivalence
Let \(R_1,R_2\) be rival representations used over engineering envelope \(\Omega\).

Let \(\Pi(R,\Omega)\) denote the materially relevant design/intervention recommendations generated by \(R\), together with success-relevant predictions.

Define local intervention equivalence:

\[
R_1\sim_\Omega R_2
\]

when the two representations induce materially indistinguishable intervention recommendations and success-relevant predictions over the tested envelope, within the engineering tolerance relevant to the artifact.

This is not global theoretical equivalence.

It asks:

> Would these rivals have built materially the same thing and expected the same success in the tested regime?

If yes, artifact success cannot discriminate their representation-specific differences.

## 2. Load-bearing invariant
Suppose surviving representations

\[
\mathcal R=\{R_1,\ldots,R_n\}
\]

all succeed under the available intervention family.

The strongest realist credit currently justified is bounded by the structure shared across all still-intervention-equivalent survivors that is actually necessary for the observed success.

Call this the **load-bearing invariant set** for the present intervention envelope.

This is a diagnostic label, not a new constitutional primitive.

Core ceiling:

\[
\boxed{
\text{artifact success licenses at most the structure required across surviving intervention-equivalent rivals}
}
\]

until a separating intervention is executed.

## 3. Pair A — graphical vs algebraic/numerical statics

### Equivalence result
Modern algebraic graphic statics writes the reciprocal relation between form and force diagrams algebraically.

Published work explicitly shows that these formulations are equivalent to equilibrium equations used in matrix analysis for planar self-stressed structural systems.

Thus a truss that succeeds under:
- reciprocal force-polygon construction;
or
- matrix-equilibrium calculation

does not, by physical success alone, distinguish graphical from algebraic representational form.

### Shared load-bearing structure
The common structure includes:
- force equilibrium at nodes;
- structural connectivity;
- compatible mapping between form geometry and axial-force balance;
- load-path relations relevant to the design.

The artifact does not need to care whether the engineer encountered this structure as:
- a polygon;
- a reciprocal diagram;
- a matrix equation;
- an algebraic graph.

### Convenience-control result
Historical dominance shifted toward analytical/numerical approaches as digital computation changed the cost of scaling calculations.

But algebraic graphic statics can computationally implement traditional graphical relations themselves.

Therefore historical dominance cannot be used as realist evidence for algebraic coordinates over graphical coordinates.

When computational convenience is controlled, much of the apparent competition collapses into a shared equilibrium quotient.

### Verdict
**STATICS REPRESENTATION-SPECIFIC REALIST CREDIT = NOT IDENTIFIED.**

Current credit localizes to the common equilibrium/load-path structure.

## 4. Pair B — Thomson cable diffusion vs Heaviside transmission-line representation

### Shared low-rate regime
Long submarine telegraph cables produced retardation and signal smearing.

Thomson represented the cable through distributed resistance and capacitance using diffusion-like mathematics.

For slow signaling in long submarine cables, inductive effects could be neglected to useful approximation.

Heaviside's richer telegrapher representation included:
- resistance \(R\);
- capacitance \(C\);
- inductance \(L\);
- leakage conductance \(G\).

In a regime where \(L/G\)-specific effects are negligible, successful slow-cable operation cannot by itself distinguish the richer representation from its reduced diffusion approximation.

### Shared load-bearing structure
- distributed-line character;
- resistance/capacitance dependence;
- distance-sensitive retardation;
- temporal signal spreading.

Success supports these shared relations more strongly than every term in the richer model.

## 5. Genuine separating intervention — loading the line
Heaviside's representation made an engineering-relevant claim unavailable to a pure \(RC\)-diffusion description.

For the distortionless relation

\[
RC=LG,
\]

a lossy line can propagate signals without the same waveform distortion as an uncorrected line.

Historically, line inductance was deliberately increased with loading coils.

Conceptually:

\[
\operatorname{do}(L\uparrow)
\]

changes a coordinate on which the rival representations differ.

This is a representation-separating intervention.

If the richer representation predicts the transmission improvement and the reduced rival cannot represent the relevant mechanism, the new world-contact carries differential evidential value.

### Attribution result
Before the separating intervention:
- realist credit localizes to shared distributed-\(RC\) transmission structure.

After successful inductive intervention:
- additional credit may extend to the load-bearing inductive/transmission structure required for the differential response.

It still does not automatically vindicate every idealization in the full transmission-line model.

## 6. Pair C — airfoil theory vs empirical correction stack

### Initial underdetermination
NACA TR-312 (1930) explicitly based airfoil prediction on both:
- aerodynamic theory;
- empirical formulae derived from variable-density wind-tunnel data.

Thus successful design predictions were not generated by theory alone.

A successful airfoil in the tested regime underdetermines causal credit among:
- theoretical pressure/lift relations;
- empirical correction functions;
- tunnel calibration/correction;
- transition/viscous behavior;
- geometry-specific interpolation.

### Theory-specific counterfactual power
NACA TR-833 (1945) developed a theory allowing:
- velocity distribution from a specified thin-airfoil shape;
- or airfoil shape from a desired velocity distribution.

This gives theory a distinctive design-direction capability.

But NACA tests directly varied scale and turbulence and found corresponding changes in lift and drag, showing that performance remained sensitive to variables not exhausted by idealized shape relations.

### Load-bearing decomposition
Possible load-bearing components include:
- geometry-pressure relation;
- circulation/lift relation;
- Reynolds/scale dependence;
- boundary-layer transition;
- surface-condition sensitivity;
- empirical correction structure.

A single successful tunnel or flight result does not identify all of these.

## 7. Separating interventions for airfoils
A representation difference becomes scientifically valuable when the system is deliberately moved into a regime where rival predictions diverge.

Examples:
- vary Reynolds number;
- vary free-stream turbulence;
- alter surface roughness;
- approach stall/high angle of attack;
- compare target pressure distributions against measured pressure fields.

These interventions identify which theoretical/empirical components are load-bearing.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{new regime contact expands realist credit only when it separates surviving representation rivals}
}
\]

## 8. Artifact-success attribution audit
### A. Enumerate surviving rivals
Do not begin only with the representation historically used.

Include alternatives that could have generated materially the same successful artifact.

### B. Compute the shared intervention quotient
Identify design recommendations and success-relevant predictions common across rivals in the tested envelope.

### C. Localize candidate load-bearing invariants
Ask which shared structures the artifact's success actually depended on.

### D. Design a separating intervention
Change a coordinate on which the rivals differ while preserving the engineering target.

### E. Expand authority only after differential survival
If one representation survives the separating intervention and the rival does not, realist authority may expand only to the structure implicated by that differential result.

## 9. Convenience-controlled transport
Historical persistence is confounded by:
- computation cost;
- notation;
- pedagogy;
- standards;
- installed software;
- professional training;
- institutional lock-in.

A representation's persistence carries stronger world-side evidence when superiority remains after these convenience variables are approximately neutralized.

Graphic statics is the canonical warning:
its historical decline coincided with computation changes, while modern algebraic implementations show that its equilibrium structure remains computationally viable.

Thus:

\[
\text{historical dominance}\not\Rightarrow\text{representation-specific world superiority}.
\]

## 10. Intervention-equivalence classes and realist authority
Let

\[
[R]_{\sim_\Omega}
\]

denote representations that remain materially intervention-equivalent in tested envelope \(\Omega\).

Current artifact evidence generally warrants commitment no stronger than structures invariant across that class.

A separating probe refines the class:

\[
[R]_{\sim_\Omega}\rightarrow[R]_{\sim_{\Omega'}}
\]

where \(\Omega'\) includes a new discriminating intervention.

This is an application of existing MQR quotient logic, not a fifth field or new realism theory.

## 11. Failure can localize more strongly than success
Artifact success may be multiply explainable.

A designed failure under a representation-separating perturbation can be more informative.

If:
- \(R_1\) predicts survival under intervention \(e\);
- \(R_2\) predicts failure or materially different response;
- the intervention is executed;

then the result directly targets differential structure.

This matches older MQR defeat-geometry doctrine.

Engineering's special advantage is that the probe can often be built into the artifact itself.

## 12. Strongest result
Engineering success can localize realist commitment only when:

1. rival representations are explicitly reconstructed;
2. shared intervention-equivalent predictions are factored out;
3. a materially separating intervention is available;
4. convenience/institutional explanations are controlled where relevant;
5. the observed response depends on the representation-specific structure under test.

Without these conditions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{artifact success underdetermines representation-specific realism}.
}
\]

With them:

\[
\boxed{
\text{realist credit can be localized to load-bearing differential invariants}.
}
\]
