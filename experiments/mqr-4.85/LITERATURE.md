## Suzumura 1976

Kotaro Suzumura, “Remarks on the Theory of Collective Choice,” *Economica* 43(172), 381–390.

Primary-source boundary: Suzumura defines consistency by excluding a finite cycle containing at least one asymmetric step and proves that a binary relation has an extended ordering if and only if it is consistent. The extension preserves both the weak relation and its asymmetric component.

Absorbed prior art:
- existence of ordering extensions under Suzumura consistency;
- generic distinction between acyclicity and the stronger consistency condition;
- generic completion of incomplete binary relations.

Not supplied by the theorem:
- scientific warrant for newly added authority comparisons;
- deletion/revocation authority;
- residual-debt discharge;
- MQR scope/provenance promotion.

## Houy 2008

Nicolas Houy, “A note on the Suzumura-consistency,” *Mathematical Social Sciences* 55, 90–95. DOI: 10.1016/j.mathsocsci.2007.08.003.

Primary-source boundary:
- Proposition 1 restates the Suzumura extension iff condition.
- Proposition 3 characterizes when an ordering extension can rationalize a specified singleton choice.
- Proposition 4 characterizes conditions under which all ordering extensions generate the same rational choice set containing a specified alternative.
- Corollary 1 gives conditions under which every ordering extension selects the same singleton.

This is stronger prior-art pressure than extension nonuniqueness. MQR therefore freezes:

```text
CHOICE_INVARIANCE_ACROSS_EXTENSIONS != EDGE_AUTHORITY_WARRANT
SAME_RATIONAL_CHOICE != SAME_AUTHORITY_EXTENSION
```

MQR claims no novelty for invariant choice across extensions.

## Duggan 2007

John Duggan, “A systematic approach to the construction of non-empty choice sets,” *Social Choice and Welfare* 28, 491–506. DOI: 10.1007/s00355-006-0176-1.

Primary-source correction: Duggan is **not** being used as a minimum-feedback-edge algorithm. The paper systematically removes particular strict-preference instances until a target regularity property such as transitivity, acyclicity or negative transitivity is reached, then unifies choices generated by different deletion orders. It connects maximal regular subrelations to familiar and new non-empty choice sets.

Absorbed prior art:
- systematic deletion of strict preferences to restore regularity;
- dependence on deletion order;
- aggregation/unification of choices across deletion orders;
- generic maximal transitive/acyclic subrelation constructions.

MQR's narrower attack is:

```text
ORDER_OF_DELETION_AGGREGATION != SCIENTIFIC_REVOCATION_WARRANT
CHOICE_UNION_OVER_REPAIRS != AUTHORITY_UNION_OVER_REPAIRS
```

A preference deletion may be mathematically useful while still lacking a scientific defeat warrant for revoking an authority edge.

## Plott 1973

Charles R. Plott, “Path Independence, Rationality, and Social Choice,” *Econometrica* 41(6), 1075–1091.

Primary-source boundary: path independence is treated as a process property concerning invariance to how choice sets are partitioned and recombined; Plott explicitly separates path independence from rationality and then studies conditions connecting them.

Absorbed prior art:
- generic path independence;
- process/partition-order invariance;
- rationalization conditions tied to path-independent choice.
