# MQR-3.188 — Observation–Authority Bridge Constitution, Quantizer Adequacy, Prospective Aliasing Attack, Calibration-Bounded Transport & Whether Exact Decision-Layer Identification Can Earn Any Scoped Scientific-Authority Claim

## 0. Genealogy audit before continuation
MQR-3.181–3.187 is reread as a single lineage rather than as seven locally successful stages.

The lineage accomplished:
- 3.181: demoted MQR from a new standalone realism theory to an epistemic-authority constitution;
- 3.182: established internal four-clause minimality but external federation reducibility;
- 3.183: showed legitimate path dependence must leave a present authority-relevant trace;
- 3.184: formalized the coarsest future-behavioral authority quotient;
- 3.185: separated finite index, state distinguishability, exact learnability, and scientific certifiability;
- 3.186: showed exact identification is legitimate only relative to a prospectively bounded fault domain;
- 3.187: failed to find a real domain where finite control/decision state also supplied an externally justified finite scientific-world state bound.

This progression was locally coherent, but it created a new risk:

> a productive formal subproblem began to select the next questions for MQR.

MQR-3.188 therefore begins with a branch-capture audit.

## 1. Trap audit

### T1 — Automata branch capture
From 3.184 onward, authority histories were increasingly represented as automata-learning objects.

This was useful for:
- state sufficiency;
- future distinguishability;
- bounded exactness;
- destructive probe accounting.

But none of these establishes that scientific authority in general is naturally finite-state or automata-like.

**Verdict:** PARTIAL OVERFIT.

Automata theory is retained as an optional formal instrument, not as MQR's constitutional ontology.

### T2 — Exactness escalation
The branch gradually moved from:
"what warrants a scoped realist commitment?"

toward:
"when can the full authority automaton be identified exactly?"

This is too strong as a general scientific requirement.

Much ordinary measurement science earns useful, decision-relevant authority under:
- continuous quantities;
- uncertainty intervals/distributions;
- model-based calibration;
- underdetermination;
- nonzero decision risk.

NIST explicitly states that metrological traceability does not by itself guarantee fitness for purpose, and conformity assessment standards explicitly incorporate measurement uncertainty into decision rules rather than requiring exact state recovery.

**Correction:** exact finite identification is neither necessary nor sufficient for scoped scientific authority.

### T3 — Quantizer simplification
MQR-3.187 wrote the bridge as:

[
q:X	oSigma.
]

This exposed aliasing, but it is too thin as a general account of measurement.

Model-based measurement literature treats calibration as modelling the measurement process, not merely applying a fixed quantizer to world states.

The bridge must represent:
- target quantity/construct;
- instrument–object interaction;
- theoretical/statistical measurement model;
- calibration and reference relations;
- uncertainty/error structure;
- scope/context of use;
- interpretation or decision rule.

**Correction:** replace the bare quantizer with a model-based bridge dossier.

### T4 — Authority ambiguity
"Authority" risks sliding among:
- institutional permission;
- regulatory reportability;
- methodological credibility;
- epistemic warrant;
- realist commitment.

A regulatory decision table may have institutional authority without licensing a strong realist claim.

**Correction:** throughout the core lineage, "authority" means **epistemic licensing strength for a specified claim** unless institution/regulation is explicitly typed separately.

Institutional authority cannot be used as a proxy for realist authority.

### T5 — HOLD self-sealing risk
If MQR requires:
- global challenge closure;
- exact finite state bounds;
- exhaustive fault domains;
- perfect equivalence certification,

then almost every real science case will terminate in HOLD by construction.

A method that can never earn promotion is not corrigible realism; it is a refusal machine.

**Correction:** scoped promotion must be possible under bounded uncertainty and live defeasibility without exhaustive future closure.

### T6 — Novelty-chasing branch drift
After 3.181 showed that many primitives were prior art, later stages repeatedly searched for irreducibility in finer formal coordinates.

This risks converting novelty pressure into ontology invention.

**Correction:** no new coordinate is licensed merely because a previous candidate was reduced.
A successor concept must solve a live scientific failure not already handled by the existing constitution.

The four-clause constitution from 3.181 survives:

They remain valid where their assumptions are satisfied.

They are not mandatory for:
- continuous measurement;

- non-digital scientific reasoning.

## 4. Model-based bridge prior-art attack
Eran Tal's calibration account explicitly treats calibration as modelling a measurement process and measurement outcomes as model-based predictors rather than raw readouts.
Model-based philosophy of measurement more generally separates the concrete instrument–object process from theoretical/statistical models of that process.

NIST's traceability guidance also blocks a shortcut:
- traceability belongs to a measurement result through a documented calibration chain;
- traceability alone does not guarantee fitness for purpose;
- measurement uncertainty must still be adequate for the intended use.

Therefore:
- model-based bridge construction is not MQR-novel;
- calibration/traceability is not enough;
- decision fitness must be separately justified.

Such a system can support a scoped scientific decision even if there is:
- no finite authority automaton;
- no finite world-state quotient;
- no exact global state identification.

JCGM/NIST conformity-assessment practice is an explicit positive analogue: uncertainty-aware guard bands can control false acceptance/rejection risk without requiring exact recovery of the true state.

## 8. Prospective aliasing attack
Observation aliasing remains central, but the correct target is not only a discrete quantizer.

A prospective bridge attack asks whether two scientifically distinct target conditions can remain observationally indistinguishable under the complete measurement dossier.

Attack classes:
1. **within-calibration aliasing** — different target states produce overlapping indications;
2. **out-of-domain aliasing** — a novel condition maps into a familiar indication;
3. **model-misspecification aliasing** — the calibration model absorbs a wrong mechanism;
4. **context-shift aliasing** — the same indication changes meaning across populations/times/environments;
5. **decision-boundary aliasing** — uncertainty straddles the threshold used for the claim.

A bridge passes only relative to the presealed attack family and validated domain.
No finite attack suite is promoted to all future aliasing.

## 9. Calibration is not a one-dimensional certificate
The latest lineage risked treating "calibrated" as a Boolean property.

That is rejected.

Calibration/validation can fail along at least:
- reference traceability;
- model form;
- uncertainty adequacy;
- range/domain;
- temporal stability;
- cross-context invariance;
- decision fitness.

NIST explicitly notes that traceability does not ensure uncertainty adequate for purpose.
Tal's model-based account likewise makes calibration an inferential modelling activity rather than a stamp.

Hence:

[
	ext{TRACEABLE}

otRightarrow
	ext{FIT FOR CLAIM}

otRightarrow
	ext{REALIST PROMOTION}.
]

## 10. Construct-validity warning
For targets that are not straightforward physical measurands, bridge validity is even less reducible to calibration.

Construct-validation literature emphasizes that validity concerns whether interpretations/actions based on a measure are supported by theory and evidence, and that validation is ongoing rather than a single statistical check.
Recent philosophical criticism also warns that statistically coherent validation can remain theory-avoidant and fail to secure a valid measure.

Therefore MQR may not universalize metrology as the sole bridge template.

Physical measurement, psychometric constructs, computational proxies, and administrative categories may require different bridge evidence while remaining under C1–C4.

## 11. Return-to-origin test on GEMS
Under the corrected framework, GEMS is no longer asked to supply a finite scientific-world state bound.

The proper question becomes:

> For the scoped RGB optimization claim, does the camera/lighting/image-processing/calibration model yield a sufficiently stable relation between physical mixture properties and the measured RGB objective over the declared domain?

The DFA is only downstream execution logic.

This is a healthier question because it permits:
- continuous physical state;
- bounded calibration uncertainty;
- finite decision/control logic where useful;
- explicit HOLD near regions where the measurement bridge is weak.

GEMS remains useful as a control-layer case, but is removed from the role of candidate universal authority automaton.

## 12. Return-to-origin test on regulated qPCR
Likewise, the finite reporting table is not the central scientific object.

The bridge dossier must include:
- specimen and extraction validity;
- amplification measurement model;
- controls;
- detection/quantification limits;
- inhibition/contamination failure modes;
- threshold uncertainty;
- domain of validated sample types and operating conditions;
- retest/invalid rules.

Exact compilation of the table is useful, but downstream.

A decision can be scientifically licensed when uncertainty/control evidence makes the relevant interpretation sufficiently stable within the validated assay scope.

Again, no finite enumeration of all physical fault states is required.

## 14. Anti-overfit doctrine
### A. Representation-plurality rule
MQR does not privilege finite automata, Bayesian models, causal graphs, assurance cases, or any other single representation class.

Use the weakest representation rich enough to expose the relevant defeat geometry.

### B. No-method-to-ontology promotion
Success of a formal method does not show that the world or scientific reasoning literally has that method's ontology.

### C. Positive promotability
A constitutional rule must leave reachable conditions under which a real claim can earn scoped promotion.

If a proposed requirement makes promotion practically impossible for ordinary well-functioning science, it must be justified independently rather than treated as caution by default.

### D. Local exactness only
Exactness claims are typed to the object actually shown exact:
- control logic;
- decision rule;
- calibration relation;
- bounded fault class;
- etc.

Exactness never propagates across bridge layers without additional evidence.

## 15. Corrected bridge transport rule
For a claim (C), world-contact episode (E), and bridge dossier (mathcal B_C):

A scoped realist promotion may be licensed when:

1. **Empirical grounding:** the observation process is demonstrably coupled to the target relevant to (C);
2. **Model/calibration adequacy:** the bridge model has survived relevant calibration/validation tests;
3. **Uncertainty fitness:** residual uncertainty is small/structured enough for the claim role or explicitly carried into the verdict;
4. **Prospective aliasing exposure:** plausible rival target states/mechanisms had material opportunities to reveal bridge failure;
5. **Scope stability:** transport is restricted to a domain where calibration/invariance evidence applies;
6. **Role firewall:** downstream prediction/control/decision success is not used as a substitute for bridge validity;
7. **Live defeat:** future bridge failure remains capable of revising/revoking the claim.

No finite-state assumption appears in the rule.
