## Core collision

MQR-4.70 is not entitled to claim that predictive state depends on interventions as a new idea.

Multiple mature literatures already make the relevant dependence explicit.

### 1. Input-output computational mechanics

Barnett & Crutchfield, *Computational Mechanics of Input-Output Processes: Structured Transformations and the epsilon-Transducer*, Journal of Statistical Physics (2015), DOI 10.1007/s10955-015-1327-5.

The epsilon-transducer extends causal-state machinery to input-output processes. Predictive equivalence is explicitly conditioned on future inputs.

**Backflow:** MQR cannot treat intervention-conditioned state as novel. The open question is constitutional: which future inputs/interventions are scientifically admitted, how does that family change, and what authority follows from family-relative state compression?

### 2. Predictive State Representations

Littman, Sutton & Singh, *Predictive Representations of State* (2001).

Existing MQR/Drive lineage already uses this as a baseline for MQR-4.69.

**Backflow:** future-test-based state representation is prior art. MQR-4.70 must work one level upstream on the test/intervention family itself.

### 3. Active causal intervention design

Hauser & Bühlmann, *Two optimal strategies for active learning of causal models from interventional data*, International Journal of Approximate Reasoning 55(4), 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.ijar.2013.11.007.

Intervention target choice changes causal identifiability, and minimal intervention sets can guarantee full identifiability under declared assumptions.

Elahi, Wei, Kocaoglu & Ghasemi, *Adaptive Online Experimental Design for Causal Discovery*, ICML 2024, PMLR 235.

Their graph separating system is already a formal family of interventions sufficient to cut graph edges, with adaptive allocation over that family.

Tigas et al., *Interventions, Where and How? Experimental Design for Causal Models at Scale*, NeurIPS 2022.

Both intervention target and intervention value are chosen for causal discovery.

**Backflow:** separating intervention families and adaptive selection are strong prior art. MQR novelty cannot be a new optimizer or another minimal experiment-set result.

### 4. Minimal experimental design / identifiability

Gevertz & Kareva, *Minimally sufficient experimental design using identifiability analysis*, npj Systems Biology and Applications 10, 2 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41540-023-00325-1.

The framework searches for a minimal data-collection design sufficient for parameter identifiability.

**Backflow:** “minimal sufficient experiment set” is not an MQR primitive.

## Candidate publishable residue

The live candidate is a combined formal-governance object:

1. a **lattice of intervention families** under inclusion;
2. the induced **lattice/poset of predictive partitions**;
3. a **separating hypergraph** over history pairs and interventions;
4. an **admissibility envelope** restricting which interventions can enter a family;
5. an **exterior-separator reserve** representing known or discoverable probes not currently admitted;
6. a **transport map** between intervention families across domains;
7. an explicit distinction between:
   - minimal relative to current family;
   - minimal relative to a frozen finite universe;
   - open-world minimality, which remains unearned.

The mathematics in items 1–3 is largely classical.

The potentially useful MQR contribution is the governance theorem/countermodel layer: a scientific authority state cannot inherit universal minimality from a family-relative quotient when the intervention constitution itself is open, endogenously selected, or nontransportable.

Whether that claim is novel enough for publication is **OPEN** until executable and naturalistic calibration.
