## Finite Court object

- H: frozen history/state candidates.
- I: frozen intervention universe.
- R: frozen response map H × I -> O.
- U subseteq I: admitted intervention family.
- P(U): partition of H induced by equality of response signatures over U.

Primary object: the map U -> P(U), not one privileged partition.

## Zero-credit structural theorem

Under fixed response semantics:

    U subseteq V
    => P(V) refines P(U).

Adding interventions may split a class or leave it unchanged, but cannot merge histories already distinguished by U.

Restricting a family may collapse classes.

This is elementary and earns zero novelty credit.

## Separating-set representation

For each pair of histories:

    SEP(h,h') = {u in I : R(h,u) != R(h',u)}.

U distinguishes the pair iff U intersects SEP(h,h').

For a declared target distinction set, minimal family construction therefore has a classical separating-system / hitting-set baseline.
