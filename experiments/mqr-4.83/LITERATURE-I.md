## External literature collision

### Statistical experiment comparison

Blackwell/Torgersen already establish a partial order of informativeness/sufficiency for statistical experiments. Recent work extends or completes this order in restricted settings and under ambiguity.

Absorption:
- generic dominance/refinement comparison is prior art;
- partial comparability is prior art;
- a finer/more informative object can dominate another under an independently defined order.

MQR residue cannot be “partial order exists” or “more information may dominate.”

### Adequacy-for-purpose

Parker’s adequacy-for-purpose view makes model adequacy relative to target, user, methodology, circumstances, and purpose.

Absorption:
- model quality/adequacy is purpose-relative;
- more detail or accuracy is not automatically globally better.

### Domain-relative model transfer

Bakeev (2026) argues that model transfer may fail because target domains use different tractability standards, and methodological embedding may be needed.

Absorption:
- source adequacy need not transport to target-domain adequacy;
- common formal model structure does not imply common evaluative authority.

### Postnikov/truncation mathematics

Postnikov towers and truncation functors already provide mathematical comparison maps across truncation levels. Gepner & Heine (2026) study Postnikov completeness and limits across truncation functors for higher categories.

Absorption:
- cross-level truncation maps are prior art;
- deeper truncation data and convergence/completeness questions are prior art;
- mathematical refinement maps alone are not an MQR novelty.
