## 2. Candidate A — defeat-route noncommonness laundering
### External reduction
Classic N-version software reliability work already rejects the inference from apparent multiplicity/independent development to independent failure. Knight & Leveson and related work show correlated/common-mode failures among independently developed versions; later reliability models explicitly parameterize dependent failures.

NASA and reliability engineering likewise treat common-cause failure and genuine failure independence as load-bearing.

### Internal reduction
MQR itself already contains:
- proto-MQR common-mode dependency graphs;
- MQR-2.29/2.30 evidential noncommonness;
- MQR-3.80–3.82 common-mode mediation and graph repair;
- MQR-3.135/3.159 hidden common-cause/open-world dependence;
- Research OS P11 Evidence-Family Graph & cutset accounting.

### Verdict
**A = REJECT AS SUCCESSOR-HARD DISTINCTNESS CANDIDATE.**
The claim “two apparent challenge routes sharing a decisive failure ancestor count as less than two independent routes” is not novel and is already internally saturated.

No such pair has yet been found.

## 4. Candidate E — challenge-generator exteriority

### External attack
NASA IV&V is a strong rival. Technical/managerial independence is not merely personnel separation:
- the IV&V team develops an independent understanding of the problem;
- independently selects system segments to analyze/test;
- independently chooses analysis methods;
- independently selects technical issues/problems to pursue.

Thus generic “independent challenger must choose its own questions/tests” is mature prior art.

### Narrow residue
The remaining candidate is:
**ONTOLOGY-LEVEL GENERATOR EXTERIORITY** —
two verification regimes can satisfy organizational/technical independence yet both generate challenges from the same requirements ontology or representation vocabulary, while a third route can formulate a failure only after changing that vocabulary.

This is narrower than ordinary IV&V independence.

### Fatal condition
