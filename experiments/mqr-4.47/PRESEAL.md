## Explanandum lock

Let:
- M be the currently admissible mapping-relation set;
- Q be a declared family of candidate challenge probes;
- S subseteq Q be the selected challenge packet;
- D be the declared defeat-route universe available at constitution time;
- A(q) be the declared ancestry of probe q;
- R(q) subseteq D be the defeat routes exercised by q;
- O(q) be outcome state after reveal;
- Sel be the selection rule.

The court asks when:

~~~text
(M, Q, S, D, A, R, Sel) -> authority to narrow M
~~~

is legitimate when:
- S can be cherry-picked from Q;
- many probes can share one measurement/data/representation ancestry;
- probe count can be inflated by splitting one route;
- some defeat routes can be silently omitted;
- the relevance rule can be defined by the incumbent mapping;
- probe construction may encode the preferred discriminator;
- post-outcome challenge selection can masquerade as prospective discrimination;
- an expanded challenge family can reopen or reverse a prior narrowing;
- the set of all relevant future separators is itself open-ended.

## Candidate canonical objects

### 1. Translation-Challenge Constitution Receipt (TCCR)

Records:
- candidate relation set;
- declared candidate challenge family;
- selected packet;
- selection rule;
- selection timing;
- selector ancestry;
- probe ancestry;
- route coverage;
- omitted declared probes/routes;
- discriminator-capture indicators;
- challenge-expansion history;
- narrowing result;
- reopening conditions.

### 2. Separator Family Registry (SFR)

A pre-outcome registry of candidate probes available for the declared court.

The registry is not assumed complete.

### 3. Defeat-Route Coverage Matrix (DRCM)

Rows = probes.
Columns = declared defeat routes.
Cells = exercised / not exercised / unresolved.

This prevents probe cardinality from laundering route coverage.

### 4. Probe-Ancestry Graph (PAG)

Tracks shared:
- instrument;
- data;
- transformation;
- representation;
- calibration;
- generator;
- selector.

Named probes sharing one load-bearing lineage remain common-mode.

### 5. Separator-Selection Receipt (SSR)

Records why S was selected from Q before outcome reveal.

Candidate selection states:

~~~text
PRESEALED_RULE
POST_OUTCOME_SELECTED
INCUMBENT_DEFINED
ANCESTRY_DEPENDENT
UNRESOLVED
~~~

No state is a truth oracle.

### 6. Discriminator Capture Receipt (DCR)

Flags when the test's construction, scoring or relevance rule depends on the candidate mapping it is supposed to discriminate.

Candidate risks:
- incumbent-defined relevance;
- candidate-derived labels;
- candidate-derived scoring;
- post-outcome criterion revision;
- selective exclusion of counterprobes.

### 7. Challenge Expansion Receipt (CER)

Compares a prior family F with an admissible extension F'.

Possible states:

~~~text
STABLE_NARROWING
WEAKENED_NARROWING
REOPENED
REVERSED
UNRESOLVED
~~~

Current stability under one finite expansion never implies future closure.

### 8. Challenge Residue Ledger (CRL)

Carries:
- uncovered declared defeat routes;
- omitted candidate probes;
- unresolved ancestry;
- unrepresented challenge classes;
- known expansion candidates.

### 9. Separator Admissibility Envelope (SAE)

A membership predicate over challenge packets.

SAE is not a universal ranking or optimizer.

## Core countermodel 1 — separator cherry-pick

Frozen candidate set:

~~~text
CAS = {EXACT, OVERLAP}
~~~

Available pre-outcome challenges:

~~~text
q1 supports EXACT
q2 supports OVERLAP
~~~

Selecting only q1 yields an apparent EXACT narrowing.
Selecting only q2 yields an apparent OVERLAP narrowing.

Therefore:

~~~text
WORLD-FACING RESULT
WITHOUT SELECTION PROVENANCE
!=
AUTHORIZED NARROWING
~~~

## Core countermodel 2 — challenge-family gerrymandering

One underlying defeat route is split into five near-duplicate probes while another independent route appears once.

~~~text
PROBE COUNT = 6
DEFEAT-ROUTE COUNT = 2
LOAD-BEARING ANCESTRY COUNT MAY = 2 OR LESS
~~~

Majority-by-probe can therefore be manufactured by partitioning.

## Core countermodel 3 — nominal plurality, common ancestry

Three probes use:
- the same raw dataset;
- the same preprocessing;
- the same calibration;
- different surface prompts or wrappers.

~~~text
PROBE COUNT = 3
INDEPENDENT ANCESTRY = 1
~~~

Therefore nominal probe plurality cannot manufacture independent world contact.

## Core countermodel 4 — route-coverage hole

A challenge family can contain many successful probes while never exercising one declared defeat route capable of separating the candidate mappings.

~~~text
MANY PASSING PROBES
+ ONE UNCOVERED LOAD-BEARING ROUTE
!=
ADEQUATE SEPARATOR FAMILY
~~~

## Core countermodel 5 — incumbent-defined relevance

The candidate mapping defines which observables count as relevant separators.
A counterprobe is excluded because it is "irrelevant" under the mapping being tested.

This is circular challenge constitution.

## Core countermodel 6 — discriminator capture

The scoring rule contains a feature derived from the incumbent mapping or standard, so the nominally world-facing outcome mechanically favors that relation.

~~~text
WORLD CONTACT + ENCODED TARGET
!=
INDEPENDENT DISCRIMINATION
~~~

## Core countermodel 7 — post-outcome selection

Q is declared before reveal, but S is selected only after outcomes are known.

A prospective registry alone is insufficient if the packet-selection rule is retrospective.

## Core countermodel 8 — expansion instability

At F1:

~~~text
CAS = {EXACT, OVERLAP}
q1 -> EXACT
CAS_F1 = {EXACT}
~~~

At a pre-authorized expansion F2:

~~~text
q2 -> OVERLAP
CAS_F2 = EMPTY or REOPENED
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
CURRENT NARROWING
!=
EXPANSION-STABLE NARROWING
~~~

## Core countermodel 9 — stable finite expansion, open future

Several finite expansions all preserve EXACT.

Still:

~~~text
REPEATED STABILITY
!=
COMPLETE CHALLENGE SPACE
~~~

The result may earn family-relative robustness, not a stopping rule.

## Core countermodel 10 — symmetric positive control

A presealed selection rule chooses probes from ancestry-separated routes with declared coverage, no post-outcome edits, and a held-out world-facing challenge independently narrows CAS.

This should earn scoped provisional narrowing.

## Candidate positive principles

### A — World Contact Does Not Self-Authorize Test Selection
A probe can contact the world while its selection remains biased or underjustified.

### B — Selection Provenance Is Load-Bearing
The candidate family, selected packet and selection rule must be distinguished.

### C — Coverage Is Route-Level, Not Probe-Count-Level
Probe multiplicity cannot substitute for defeat-route coverage.

### D — Probe Independence Is Ancestry-Relative
Surface-distinct probes sharing one load-bearing lineage remain common-mode.

### E — Relevance Rules Are Objects of Audit
A relevance predicate may itself encode the incumbent mapping.

### F — Discriminator Capture Defeats Independent Narrowing
A test whose design or score imports the answer cannot supply independent discrimination.

### G — Challenge Expansion Can Reopen Mapping Authority
Later admissible probes may weaken, reopen or reverse prior narrowing.

### H — Finite Expansion Stability Is Local Robustness, Not Closure
Repeated survival earns family-relative stability only.

### I — No Challenge-Space Completeness
No finite TCCR proves all future relevant separators represented.
