## 1. Noncompensatory decision rules are baseline

MQR-4.68 does not invent:
- hard criteria;
- veto thresholds;
- partial ordering;
- incomparability;
- feasibility-first selection;
- outranking;
- lexicographic priority.

ELECTRE-family methods already allow a sufficiently bad criterion to block an outranking relation even when other criteria are favorable.

A 2026 Scientific Reports paper on PEOM additionally combines hard/soft threshold bands, noncompensatory elimination, and compensatory ranking. Therefore hysteretic thresholding plus hard gates is also not enough for MQR novelty.

## 2. Adaptive robust / satisficing decision is baseline

Robust satisficing and deep-uncertainty decision methods already:
- use acceptable target levels rather than a single expected-value optimum;
- evaluate robustness across uncertainty sets/scenarios;
- preserve adaptation over time;
- use moving-horizon or staged updating.

Shavazipour & Stewart (Omega 2026) explicitly combine multi-stage, multi-scenario, multiobjective robust choice with moving-horizon adaptation.

Therefore:
**CONTEXT CEILINGS + ADAPTATION != MQR NOVELTY**.

## 3. Preference / tolerance revision is baseline

Wang (Synthese 2026), *Preference revision and Bayesian updating*, directly treats dynamic preference revision rather than only static coherence.

Research OS Harvest already contains a broader preference-revision genealogy:
- Darwiche–Pearl / iterated revision;
- CP-net and preference-change work;
- minimal-change revision;
- path-dependent update witnesses.

Therefore:
**ENDOGENOUS TOLERANCE REVISION != MQR NOVELTY**.

The surviving burden is whether scientific tolerance changes carry a distinct authority/provenance contract not reducible to ordinary preference revision.

## 4. Cross-lab donor: RITHM revision-burden geometry

RITHM M05.119 already uses a vector burden object and explicitly treats semantic/definition load as noncompensatory.

This is a strong internal novelty kill:
- vector burden is old inside Research OS;
- heterogeneous dimensions should not be silently summed;
- semantic load may have gate-like authority.

MQR-4.68 must not rebrand this as new.

Potential donor:
the **reason a dimension is noncompensatory** must be tracked separately from its numeric magnitude.

## 5. Composite-indicator warning

OECD/JRC's *Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators* is already in Drive.

It is relevant because aggregation requires normalization, weighting, and compensation choices. MQR cannot treat a composite score as neutral merely because it is mathematically well-defined.

4.68 must distinguish:
- a score representation;
- a decision rule;
- the authority warrant for weights/normalization/compensation.

## 6. Deep-uncertainty donor

Stanton & Roelich (2021), already in Drive, surveys practical decision making under deep uncertainty.

This pressures any MQR claim that uncertainty automatically licenses one universal optimizer. But it also pressures MQR in the opposite direction: robust and satisficing families already provide non-Bayesian/non-single-distribution decision rules.

## 7. Robust property-preservation donor

Abate et al. (secure compilation; Drive) treat robust preservation of properties across adversarial contexts.

MQR-specific wording such as "preserve authority-relevant distinctions under future contexts" is therefore not novel merely as a preservation-under-context schema.

Possible residue, still OPEN:
- whether scientific reopening authority requires a warrant genealogy unavailable in ordinary property-preservation statements.

## 8. Scalarization attack standard

The Court must not claim victory merely because:
- one weighted sum fails;
- finite weights cannot imitate a veto without tuning;
- multiple weight vectors reverse pairwise choices.

Baseline attack set:
1. linear positive weights;
2. nonlinear monotone scalarization;
3. context-conditioned weights;
4. lexicographic representation;
5. sufficiently large penalties;
6. constrained optimization;
7. robust satisficing;
8. explicit lookup / finite utility representation.

The key distinction is:

```text
REPRESENTATIONAL REDUCIBILITY
!=
AUTHORITY REDUCIBILITY
```

A finite lookup table can represent any finite verdict surface. That fact does not establish why the table or utility should govern science.

## 9. 4.68 candidate residue

The only plausible residual object is not a new MCDM algorithm.

It is a **warrant-bearing scientific loss contract** whose thresholds, vetoes, context changes and revision rights carry explicit provenance and defeat conditions.

Candidate object:

`C = (I, B, V, R, W, H, X)`

The potentially non-baseline part is `W/H/X`:
- why a ceiling has authority;
- where it applies;
- when it expires;
- what new evidence can revise it;
- what reopening event defeats it.

Whether this is genuinely more than provenance-enriched constrained decision making remains **OPEN**.
