## Question

Does a real scientific-governance regime exhibit the 4.68 distinction between:

1. a rule being representable as a decision threshold;
2. the rule possessing scientific authority because its scope, provenance and revision conditions were independently governed?

The target is **not** to find an institution that uses a veto.
That would be trivial prior art.

The target is to find a regime in which a later rule change has a different epistemic status depending on whether the change was prospectively authorized or retrospectively introduced after outcome exposure.

## External comparator A — ICH E9 statistical principles

ICH E9 requires interim-analysis scheduling / governing considerations and stopping guidelines to be described in the protocol or amendment. It warns that deviations from planned procedures may invalidate results and says consequent changes to statistical procedures should be specified in protocol amendments, with effects on analysis and inference addressed and Type I error controlled.

Transport to 4.68:

- **OBSERVED IN DOCUMENTED GOVERNANCE:** timing and stopping rules have authority conditions beyond their numerical values;
- **OBSERVED IN DOCUMENTED GOVERNANCE:** revisions are not treated as freely exchangeable with the original rule;
- **OBSERVED IN DOCUMENTED GOVERNANCE:** revision provenance and inferential consequences matter.

This supports the *existence* of warrant/revision governance.

It does **not** establish:
- MQR's typed loss coordinates;
- a universal noncompensatory authority geometry;
- reopening dominance as a primitive;
- cross-domain transport of severity labels.

Source:
ICH E9, *Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials*.

## External comparator B — FDA adaptive-design guidance

FDA's adaptive-design guidance defines an adaptive design as allowing **prospectively planned** modifications based on accumulating data without undermining integrity and validity.

The 2019 drug/biologic guidance further treats design, conduct, analysis and reporting as a coupled package whose adaptive behavior must be evaluated.

Transport to 4.68:

```text
same numerical adaptation
+
different prospective authorization state
→
different scientific-governance status
```

This is compatible with 4.68's candidate claim that a threshold value alone underdetermines its authority.

But ordinary adaptive-design methodology already explains much of this through prespecification, operating characteristics, bias control and error-rate control.

Therefore the naturalistic witness is **not** a novelty witness.

## External comparator C — post-hoc analysis switching

FDA guidance on Bayesian methods for medical-device trials recommends against switching between frequentist and Bayesian analysis after a trial has begun, characterizing such post-hoc switching as weakening scientific validity.

This is a particularly sharp analogue of F10:

**POST-RESULT RESCUE IS NOT AUTHORITY-NEUTRAL.**

However, the baseline explanation remains strong:
- selection after data exposure changes error properties / interpretability;
- the problem can be modeled with standard statistical decision and design theory.

No MQR-specific primitive is required on this evidence alone.

## External comparator D — 2025/2026 current guidance pressure

The 2025 FDA/ICH E20 adaptive-design draft and 2026 FDA Bayesian-methodology draft continue to treat adaptive timing, adaptation rules and primary inference as explicitly governed objects.

Because these are draft/nonbinding documents, they are used only as **current comparator pressure**, not as settled implementation authority.

## Naturalistic adjudication

### Survives

**N1 — PROSPECTIVE-AUTHORIZATION MATTERS**

A numerical/statistical rule can have a materially different scientific status depending on whether its adaptation/revision path was prospectively specified and governed.

**N2 — REVISION RECEIPTS MATTER**

A change to an analysis/stopping rule is not automatically equivalent to having used the revised rule from the beginning.

**N3 — CONTEXT/SCOPE MATTERS**

Exploratory, interim-monitoring and confirmatory uses can legitimately impose different constraints even when they operate on related evidence.

### Does not survive as MQR-specific

**N4 — VETO / NONCOMPENSATION**

Already baseline decision theory.

**N5 — DYNAMIC REVISION**

Already baseline adaptive-design / preference-revision machinery.

Not established by these sources.
