## Explanandum lock

MQR-4.63 does **not** ask whether MQR can beat weak baselines on hand-built examples.

It asks whether the typed distinctions promoted in 4.62 remain empirically or simulation-operationally separable across heterogeneous acquisition worlds when compared against strong, domain-appropriate acquisition policies.

The target distinctions are:
1. exterior-rival recovery;
2. future-separator retention;
3. reopening capacity / latency;
4. evidence-ancestry independence;
5. branchwise option destruction;
6. contamination / intervention-semantic drift;
7. nonmyopic exploration debt;
8. policy-induced blind spots.

## World eligibility criteria

A world may enter the calibration pack only if, before observing MQR-vs-baseline outcomes, it satisfies all applicable criteria:

1. **Actionability** — an agent can choose among at least three distinct evidence-generating actions or action families.
2. **Sequentiality** — current acquisition can alter the value, availability, or interpretation of later acquisition.
3. **Scientific distinction** — there is at least one explicit rival, causal structure, parameter/model class, or hypothesis distinction whose discrimination can be measured.
4. **Strong baseline availability** — at least one recognized acquisition rule can be implemented without inventing a strawman.
5. **MQR-relevant exposure** — at least one of option preservation, reopening, ancestry, blind-spot risk, contamination, or exploration debt is operationally representable.
6. **Outcome independence** — world inclusion must not depend on whether MQR wins.
7. **Reconstructability** — assumptions, action space, state transition, and scoring can be made auditable from public/Harvested sources or a clearly declared synthetic abstraction.
8. **Heterogeneity** — the final pack must span at least three materially different acquisition regimes.

## Required world families

The final pack must include at least three of the following families:

- **Causal intervention / structure discovery**
- **Active sensing / sequential measurement**
- **Theory-guided scientific experiment choice**
- **Measurement contamination / destructive sampling**
- **Adaptive monitoring under distribution shift**
- **Sequential R&D / option-value scientific search**

No family may be selected because preliminary MQR results look favorable.

## Baseline-matching constitution

Each world must use the strongest practicable baseline family justified by the world contract.

Candidate baseline families:
- expected information gain / Bayesian experimental design;
- uncertainty / disagreement / entropy acquisition;
- causal identifiability / intervention-set design;
- adaptive greedy under explicit adaptive-submodular assumptions;
- robust / representative / de-amplifying acquisition;
- random exterior acquisition;
- cost-normalized acquisition;
- dual-control / exploration-exploitation rule where action changes the system;
- oracle upper bound only when its extra information is clearly labeled and excluded from fair policy comparison.

No universal baseline is forced across all worlds.

## Frozen primary metrics

### Conventional performance
- terminal prediction/model error;
- rival discrimination accuracy;
- information gain or entropy reduction where defined;
- identification success;
- cost / action count;
- regret or control loss where defined.

### MQR excess metrics
- **ERR — Exterior Rival Recovery:** recovery rate of rivals outside the current acquisition generator/model.
- **FSR — Future Separator Retention:** proportion/count of still-reachable actions that can separate live rivals after each acquisition step.
- **RCR — Reopening Capacity Ratio:** reachable reopening routes after local closure divided by routes available before closure.
- **RL — Reopening Latency:** actions required to regain discriminating access after defeat or hidden-rival arrival.
- **EAI — Evidence Ancestry Independence:** effective independent acquisition ancestry relative to nominal evidence count.
- **BOD — Branchwise Option Destruction:** future discriminating routes destroyed per outcome branch.
- **CSD — Contamination/Semantic Drift:** degradation in comparability or intervention semantics induced by acquisition.
- **NED — Nonmyopic Exploration Debt:** weighted unresolved live distinctions whose unique separators were skipped or destroyed by short-horizon policy.
- **BSI — Blind-Spot Index:** exterior region/rival mass systematically unsampled by the policy while internal confidence/fit improves.

These are vector outputs. No aggregate MQR score is permitted by default.

## Frozen ablations

At minimum:
- full typed policy;
- **-OPR** option-preservation ablation;
- **-ARR** reopening-reserve ablation;
- **-EAI** ancestry-aware ablation;
- **-NEDL** exploration-debt ablation;
- **-Exterior** exterior/random challenge ablation;
- baseline-only policies.

Ablations must remove one declared distinction without silently changing other policy machinery.

## Frozen hypotheses

H1. A strong local acquisition baseline may match or exceed MQR on conventional metrics while differing materially on at least one typed MQR metric.
H2. Option preservation can matter only in worlds with genuine action irreversibility or separator destruction; it should collapse to no effect in reversible worlds.
H3. Reopening reserve should show value only when hidden-rival arrival or post-closure defeat is possible.
H4. Ancestry auditing should matter only when nominal evidence can share a common generator/instrument/selection path.
H5. Nonmyopic exploration debt should remain zero in worlds where all live distinctions retain alternative separators.
H6. Random exterior acquisition may improve ERR/BSI without being universally best on cost or local information.
H7. Robust informative acquisition may absorb some MQR advantage under misspecification; such collapse is a valid result.
H8. If all MQR excess metrics are predictable from existing robust-design quantities across the pack, the distinct MQR acquisition layer is **not independently earned**.
H9. If at least one typed metric supplies cross-domain predictive/diagnostic separation after matched-baseline and ablation control, that distinction survives as a candidate scientific contribution.
H10. Cross-domain survival does not imply a universal acquisition policy.

## Claim ceiling

MQR-4.63 may earn:
- calibrated typed acquisition diagnostics;
- domain-conditional evidence that certain MQR distinctions survive strong baselines;
- evidence that some distinctions collapse into existing robust-design criteria;
- reusable reconstructed-world benchmark machinery;
- a sharper publication target.

It may not earn:
- universal superiority of MQR;
- one best acquisition policy;
- world completeness;
- universal scientific utility;
- causal claims from purely synthetic worlds without an explicit bridge;
- claims that historical scientists should have followed MQR unless the historical information state is reconstructed.

No result-dependent mutation is permitted after executable reveal.
