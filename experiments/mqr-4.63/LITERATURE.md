# MQR-4.63 — Literature and Baseline Boundary

Status: **HARVEST + CURRENT-LITERATURE NOVELTY PRESSURE / PRE-REVEAL**

## Core baseline pressure

### Causal experiment selection
Hyttinen, Eberhardt & Hoyer (2013) provides constructive experiment-selection machinery for causal discovery under explicit structural assumptions. MQR cannot claim novelty for selecting interventions to maximize identifiability or separating-system coverage.

### Active sensing / POMDP information gathering
Veiga & Renoux (2023) surveys decision-theoretic active sensing, including POMDP belief-state planning and information-gathering reward structures. MQR cannot claim novelty for sequential information-gathering policies.

### CMS data scouting / parking
CMS Collaboration (2025) documents a real scientific acquisition architecture that explicitly trades event richness, event rate, storage and deferred computation. The MQR use is as a reconstruction donor for retention/option metrics, not as evidence that MQR caused or normatively explains CMS design.

Drive custody:
- `12qY34eu7IR89Tj5laG8Lf1gWqKHSZVTY`

### Theory-motivated experiment choice
Dubova, Moskvichev & Zollman (2026) directly shows in a computational model that experiment-selection strategy can shape the evidence ecology and that random experiment choice can outperform theory-motivated strategies in their tested settings.

Drive custody:
- `1XI4OoLOa4rrfA26mUPMULBb0ErwrCYrP`

MQR cannot claim novelty for the generic proposition that experiment-selection policy can self-select easier evidence.

### Robust Bayesian active acquisition under misspecification
Tang, Sloman & Kaski (AISTATS 2026) develop R-IDeA, combining representativeness, informativeness and error de-amplification under model misspecification.

This is a particularly strong baseline pressure:
- representativeness is not an MQR invention;
- informativeness + robustness can already be multi-factor;
- model misspecification is already explicitly treated.

### Misinformation in experiment design
Callahan & Catanach (AISTATS 2026) show that under model/inference misspecification, designs ranked more informative can amplify bias and classical information-ordering intuitions can fail.

Therefore MQR-4.63 must not promote:
`MORE INFORMATION != BETTER SCIENCE`
as its empirical novelty.
