# MQR-4.31 — Transportability / Calibration Literature Audit

## Pearl & Bareinboim (2011)
Transportability is relation-specific and domain-difference-specific.

A result does not transport merely because source and target look similar.
The relevant question is whether the target relation is identifiable from:
- source experiments,
- target observations,
- causal/domain-difference assumptions.

MQR consequence:
transport TARGET and EVALUABILITY must be explicit before SURVIVAL is interpreted.

## Vandenberg & Lance (2000)
Cross-group/time measurement equivalence is staged and cannot be presumed.
Partial/noninvariance matters.

MQR consequence:
componentwise transport may differ across claim elements.
One global T score can hide partial transport and construct drift.

## Tal (2017)
Calibration distinguishes instrument indications from measurement outcomes and treats calibration as model-mediated, scope-bound inference.

MQR consequence:
the existence of a source-regime successful indication/receipt is not itself a target-regime transport result.

## MQR synthesis

Transport is best represented as a directed, typed relation:

T(phi_i, r_source -> r_target)

with:
- explicit component phi_i;
- explicit regime pair;
- evaluability state;
- survival state conditional on evaluation;
- receipt/provenance.

Transport is therefore graph-like rather than scalar.

## Non-transitivity rule

Default:

A -> B PASS
and
B -> C PASS

DOES NOT imply
A -> C PASS.

Composition requires independent evidence that:
1. the transported claim component is semantically the same or explicitly bridged;
2. the B-state used as target in A->B is the same admissible object used as source in B->C;
3. bridge assumptions remain valid over the composed path;
4. no successor-only distinction is silently erased.

Thus:
TRANSPORT EDGE COMPOSITION = HOLD BY DEFAULT.

This inherits MQR's older rule:
formal composability != epistemic composability.

## Research vs engineering

Transport semantics are not restricted to scientific Labs.

Research example:
a representation law trained/fit in earlier EPISTEME contexts can be prospectively defeated in fresh worlds.

Engineering example:
native Hancom semantics can survive or remain untested across implementation-generated open/save round-trips.

The common structure is:
claim component + source regime + target regime + evaluability + survival.

The interpretation of the claim differs by lineage kind.

Therefore lineage kind is explicit in Real-Language v0.3.
