## 2. Candidate A — FDA accelerated approval on surrogate endpoints

### Apparent mismatch
FDA may approve a drug before direct clinical benefit is fully verified, using a surrogate endpoint reasonably likely to predict benefit.

A naive MQR comparison could say:
- MQR C4: surrogate success is not clinical-benefit truth;
- FDA: approval granted;
therefore mismatch.

### Strongest local-practice reconstruction
FDA explicitly distinguishes:
- the surrogate endpoint from clinical benefit;
- validated surrogates from reasonably likely surrogates;
- accelerated approval from traditional verification;
- initial approval from later confirmatory evidence.

Current FDA materials state that:
- a surrogate endpoint is not itself a clinical-benefit measure;
- accelerated approval may rely on a reasonably likely surrogate;
- confirmatory studies must verify predicted benefit;
- approval may be withdrawn or labeling changed if benefit is not verified;
- endpoints that fail confirmatory support may cease to be accepted for the same use.

### Frozen-MQR verdict
C4 does not prohibit action under uncertainty.
It prohibits laundering a narrower evidential role into a stronger realist claim.

Therefore MQR may type the result as:
**CONDITIONAL REGULATORY ACTION AUTHORIZED / CLINICAL-BENEFIT REALIST CLAIM NOT YET FULLY VERIFIED.**

This is compatible with the mature FDA structure.

### Result
**APPARENT MISMATCH DISSOLVES BY AUTHORITY-ROLE TYPING.**

The Pepaxto withdrawal episode is a positive witness of C3/live defeat already implemented institutionally: accelerated approval was later withdrawn after confirmatory evidence failed to verify benefit.

No MQR-specific licensing differential survives.

## 3. Candidate B — clinical prediction models with imperfect calibration

### Apparent mismatch
Modern clinical-prediction guidance allows a model to remain clinically useful even when calibration is imperfect in regions irrelevant to clinical decisions.

A naive MQR bridge rule could overreact:
"miscalibrated => HOLD."

### Strongest local-practice reconstruction
Current BMJ guidance separates:
- discrimination;
- calibration;
- overall fit;
- external validation;
- clinical utility/net benefit.

It explicitly notes that:
- external validation should use different relevant data;
- a single successful external validation does not prove transportability everywhere;
- calibration drift may require updating;
- a model can be miscalibrated in some regions yet still clinically useful if those regions do not affect decision thresholds;
- decision thresholds should be chosen prospectively and clinical utility assessed with net benefit/decision curves.

### Frozen-MQR verdict
MQR-3.188 already allows risk-qualified, role-specific transport.

Thus MQR evaluates:
- whether the miscalibration crosses the decision-relevant threshold region;
- whether the claim is "probabilities are calibrated" or "the model is useful for this decision."

If high-risk-region miscalibration does not alter the action rule in the scoped use, MQR may license **SCOPED DECISION UTILITY** while refusing **GLOBAL CALIBRATION ACCURACY**.

### Result
**APPARENT MISMATCH DISSOLVES BY CLAIM-ROLE TYPING + RISK-QUALIFIED TRANSPORT.**

No new MQR rule was needed.

## 4. Candidate C — well-being construct validation

### Apparent mismatch
Alexandrova & Haybron show that in well-being research, construct-validation practice can be theory-avoidant, relying heavily on psychometric/statistical checks while neglecting philosophical/normative constraints on what well-being is.

Frozen MQR C1/C4 would refuse to promote:
"this questionnaire validly measures well-being"
if the bridge from items/scores to the normative construct is not adequately grounded.

This looks like a real mismatch with practice-in-use.

### Strongest local-practice reconstruction
However, the strongest mature testing methodology is not merely factor structure/correlation.

The AERA/APA/NCME *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* organizes validity around:
- intended interpretations and uses;
- multiple sources of validity evidence;
- integration of validity evidence;
- samples/settings used in validation;
- safeguards against inappropriate score interpretation;
- reliability/precision and decision consistency.

Alexandrova & Haybron themselves argue that construct validation is coherentist **in principle** and criticize its implementation in well-being research for failing to respect all relevant sources of knowledge, especially the normative character of well-being.

Thus the strongest local methodological position already has room to reject the same theory-avoidant validation practice.

### Result
[
R_{mathrm{MQR}}
eq R_{mathrm{practice-in-use}}
]

may occur, but:

[
R_{mathrm{MQR}}=R_{mathrm{strong local methodology}}
]

remains available.

Therefore this is an **IMPLEMENTATION-GAP WITNESS**, not a methodology-novelty witness.

## 8. The decisive distinction: methodology vs enacted practice

This is useful, but it is not philosophical irreducibility.
