## Finite residue bound

Define the internal probe residue:

```text
R_int(P,T) = { p in Closure(P) : Relevant_D(p) and p notin T }
```

where T is the executed probe set.

A finite zero-residue certificate is allowed only when:

1. Closure(P) is finite by construction or has a proved finite normal form;
2. the enumerator is complete for that declared closure;
3. every D-relevant probe in Closure(P) is executed or mechanically discharged;
4. the world-to-probe admission map is explicit;
5. missing/failed execution cannot be silently converted to PASS.

Then MQR may conclude only:



## The Probe-Closure Paradox

A finite proof that all probes in P have been discharged does not prove that P contains every future world-separating probe.

If completeness of P is itself justified by the same representation under test, closure becomes circular.

Therefore probe-class completeness and world adequacy are distinct obligations.
