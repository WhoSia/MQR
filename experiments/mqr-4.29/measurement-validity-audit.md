# MQR-4.29 — Measurement-Validity Audit

## Purpose
Interpret the successful synthetic calibration without promoting it beyond what it establishes.

## Validation traditions actively used

### Cronbach & Meehl (1955)
Construct validity is not established by one coefficient or one benchmark. A construct gains meaning through a broader network of theoretically expected relations.

MQR implication:
passing the synthetic benchmark is internal construct calibration, not external validation.

DOI:
10.1037/h0040957

### Campbell & Fiske (1959)
Convergent evidence is insufficient without discriminant evidence. A putative trait should relate more strongly to measurements of itself than to different traits/method effects.

MQR implication:
S4 self-axis recovery and S5 cross-axis leakage are distinct requirements.

DOI:
10.1037/h0046016

### Borsboom, Mellenbergh & van Heerden (2004)
A stronger realist conception of measurement validity requires that variation in the measured attribute causally produce variation in measurement outcomes.

MQR implication:
the synthetic benchmark satisfies this relation by construction, which is useful for internal calibration but cannot establish the same causal measurement relation in historical/real scientific packets.

DOI:
10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.1061

### Messick (1995)
Validity attaches to the interpretation and use of measurements, not merely to numerical consistency.

MQR implication:
W/N/I/T/D values are licensed only for their frozen operational interpretation. They are not automatically interval-scale epistemic quantities or truth distances.

DOI:
10.1037/0003-066X.50.9.741

## MQR-4.29 interpretation
The synthetic calibration establishes:

LEVEL 2 — INTERNALLY CALIBRATED OPERATIONAL INDICES.

It does not establish:

LEVEL 3 — EXTERNALLY CALIBRATED SCIENTIFIC SCALE.

Reasons:
1. The synthetic generator and index definitions share the same construct constitution.
2. The benchmark deliberately orthogonalizes the latent axes.
3. Real scientific cases may couple W/N/I/T/D through common causal structure.
4. Numeric differences such as .25 -> .50 and .50 -> 1.00 are normalized index differences, not proven equal epistemic intervals.
5. Cross-domain measurement invariance has not been tested prospectively.

## Valid use
Allowed:
- report W/N/I/T/D as typed normalized operational indices;
- compare exact same-axis values under the same scoring constitution;
- use Pareto dominance across profiles;
- audit decoy invariance and axis leakage;
- preserve raw receipts behind every coordinate.

Not yet allowed:
- universal scalar aggregation;
- interval-scale arithmetic across axes;
- claims that a .20 difference has the same meaning across domains;
- conversion to probability of truth;
- final-truth distance.

## Successor requirement
External calibration must use real or externally adjudicated cases not constructed from the scoring definitions themselves, with prospective axis-separating predictions and cross-domain invariance tests.
