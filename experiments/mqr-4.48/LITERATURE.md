## Drive-native external pressure

### Kuhn (1999), *Fault Classes and Error Detection Capability of Specification-Based Testing*
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- fault classes can be explicitly hypothesized;
- different fault classes induce different detection conditions;
- those conditions admit a hierarchy;
- some implementation faults may fail to fit the chosen specification-derived fault classes.

Novelty kill:
- MQR-4.48 cannot claim invention of fault-class structure, detection-condition hierarchy, or the fact that modeled fault classes need not exhaust real faults.

Residual use:
- a strong donor for separating **named route class** from **defeat content/detection condition**;
- motivates coverage-preserving refinement/subsumption rather than cardinality-based counting.

### Weyuker (1983), *Assessing Test Data Adequacy through Program Inference*
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- adequacy criteria can be satisfied by test sets that still miss errors;
- mutation adequacy implicitly selects a class of likely/simple errors;
- the assumed error distribution/model matters;
- test-data adequacy should not be equated with correctness or raw traversal criteria.

Novelty kill:
- MQR-4.48 cannot claim novelty for “coverage criterion is model-relative.”

Residual use:
- supports the hard firewall:
  `COMPLETE COVERAGE UNDER ONE ROUTE MODEL != COMPLETE ERROR COVERAGE`.

### Bartocci et al. (2023), *Property-Based Mutation Testing*
Drive copy present.

Pressure:
- mutation testing is explicitly relative to a fault model;
- mutant relevance depends on the property being tested;
- redundant/duplicate/subsumed mutants show that nominal mutant multiplicity can overstate distinct test requirements;
- mutation coverage can be strong while still remaining relative to modeled mutations/properties.

Novelty kill:
- MQR-4.48 cannot claim novelty for subsumption/redundancy among fault representatives.

Residual use:
- direct machinery donor for:
  - route split count inflation;
  - route alias/subsumption;
  - coverage-equivalence tests that should depend on distinguishable defeat content, not route cardinality.

## Hilbert's twenty-fourth problem — analogy pressure, not novelty authority

External verification after preseal:
- Rüdiger Thiele's 2003 paper presents Hilbert's unpublished twenty-fourth problem as asking for criteria of proof simplicity and a general theory of proof methods.
- A 2018 historical/philosophical treatment states that Thiele found the note in Hilbert's Göttingen Nachlass in 2000.
- Related proof-identity literature asks when two proofs should count as the same proof and why computer science should care.

The analogy to MQR is real but limited.

Shared structural move:

~~~text
OBJECT-LEVEL SUCCESS
-> META-LEVEL CRITERIA FOR METHODS
-> CRITERIA THEMSELVES BECOME FORMAL OBJECTS
~~~

Hilbert-24:
- what makes one proof simpler than another?
- what is the right theory of proof methods?
- when are proof transformations methodologically meaningful?

MQR-4.48:
- what makes one defeat route distinct from another?
- when does a route split/merge preserve the underlying defeat content?
- when are two coverage descriptions equivalent across route ontologies?

Important difference:
- Hilbert-24 concerns proof simplicity, proof identity and method in mathematics;
- MQR-4.48 concerns empirical/scientific defeat ontology, separator adequacy, ancestry and reopenable world contact.

Therefore Hilbert-24 is best treated as a **deep methodological analogue**, not a direct predecessor of MQR's formal objects.
