## Explanandum lock

Let:
- O be a declared defeat-route ontology;
- R(O) be its named route set;
- A(r) be route ancestry/dependence;
- C(q,r) be probe-to-route coverage;
- W(r) be the declared defeat content carried by route r;
- O' be a successor route ontology;
- T(O,O') be a typed route-transport relation;
- H be newly discovered defeat content not represented in O.

The court asks when a coverage claim:

~~~text
COVERED(R(O))
~~~

may legitimately support separator adequacy when:
- route identity is nonunique;
- one route can be split into several names;
- several defeat mechanisms can be merged into one route;
- route labels can drift while underlying defeat content remains stable;
- distinct labels can alias the same defeat content;
- route ancestry can be entangled;
- route partitions can produce identical counts but different coverage meaning;
- two ontologies can each be internally complete yet disagree on whether a packet is adequate;
- hidden defeat content can create a new route after a PASS;
- route revision can change the meaning of old coverage receipts.

## Candidate canonical objects

### 1. Defeat-Route Ontology Constitution Receipt (DROCR)

Records:
- route ontology/version;
- route declarations;
- route identity witnesses;
- defeat-content atoms/classes used by the declaration;
- route ancestry;
- split/merge relations;
- probe coverage;
- hidden-route discoveries;
- revision mappings;
- coverage authority state;
- unresolved residue.

### 2. Route-Identity Witness (RIW)

A route is not identified by its label alone.

RIW records declared defeat content and ancestry sufficient to distinguish:
- SAME;
- REFINE;
- MERGE;
- OVERLAP;
- DISJOINT;
- UNMAPPED.

This is an audit relation, not metaphysical route identity.

### 3. Defeat-Content Ledger (DCL)

Tracks declaration-relative defeat content carried by each route.

Purpose:

~~~text
ONE ROUTE NAME
MAY CARRY MULTIPLE DEFEAT CONTENTS

MANY ROUTE NAMES
MAY CARRY ONE SHARED CONTENT
~~~

### 4. Route-Ancestry Graph (RAG)

Tracks load-bearing common ancestry among routes:
- same instrument;
- same data;
- same failure mechanism;
- same representation;
- same intervention;
- same derivation/generator.

Named route plurality does not imply independent defeat geometry.

### 5. Route Split/Merge Receipt (RSMR)

Typed ontology revision:

~~~text
EXACT / REFINE / MERGE / OVERLAP / DISJOINT / UNMAPPED
~~~

A split does not manufacture independent defeat content.
A merge does not erase inherited route debt.

### 6. Coverage-Transport Ledger (CTL)

Transports probe-to-route coverage across route-ontology revision.

Key question:

~~~text
DOES COVERAGE OF O
STILL COVER THE RELEVANT DEFEAT CONTENT
UNDER O'?
~~~

### 7. Coverage-Equivalence Envelope (CEE)

Two route ontologies are coverage-equivalent only at a declared defeat-content surface if their induced probe coverage preserves the same declared defeat distinctions.

Equal route counts or equal percentages are insufficient.

### 8. Hidden-Route Discovery Receipt (HDR)

A new defeat route discovered after a prior PASS is typed as:
- NOVEL_ROUTE;
- REFINEMENT_OF_EXISTING;
- MERGE_CORRECTION;
- PREVIOUSLY_UNMAPPED_CONTENT;
- ANCESTRY_REVELATION;
- UNRESOLVED.

HDR reopens only the affected adequacy scope; it is not automatic global falsification.

### 9. Route Residue Ledger (RRL)

Carries:
- uncovered defeat content;
- unmapped predecessor routes;
- unresolved ancestry;
- split/merge ambiguity;
- known route-discovery candidates;
- coverage lost under revision.

### 10. Route-Constitution Admissibility Envelope (RCAE)

A membership predicate over declared route ontologies.

RCAE is not a universal optimizer or final ontology selector.

## Core countermodel 1 — route split gerrymander

Ontology O1:

~~~text
rA carries defeat content {x}
~~~

Ontology O2:

~~~text
rA1 carries {x}
rA2 carries {x}
rA3 carries {x}
rA4 carries {x}
rA5 carries {x}
~~~

If route count is treated as defeat diversity:

~~~text
1 -> 5
~~~

without any new defeat content.

Therefore:

~~~text
ROUTE SPLIT != NEW DEFEAT CAPACITY
~~~

## Core countermodel 2 — route merge laundering

Ontology O1:

~~~text
rA -> {x}
rB -> {y}
~~~

Ontology O2:

~~~text
rAB -> {x,y}
~~~

A single probe covering x but not y cannot inherit FULL coverage merely because both routes were renamed rAB.

Therefore:

~~~text
MERGED LABEL COVERAGE
!=
FULL PREDECESSOR DEFEAT COVERAGE
~~~

## Core countermodel 3 — same label, changed route

Version O1:

~~~text
route robustness -> {x}
~~~

Version O2:

~~~text
route robustness -> {x,y}
~~~

Same label does not preserve route identity or prior coverage authority.

## Core countermodel 4 — different-label exact alias

~~~text
O1: r_old -> {x}
O2: r_new -> {x}
~~~

with preserved ancestry and defeat content.

Different labels need not create a new route.

## Core countermodel 5 — equal-count coverage inequivalence

Two ontologies each contain two routes and a packet covers one of two.

~~~text
O1 routes = {x},{y}; packet covers {x}
O2 routes = {x,y},{z}; packet covers {z}
~~~

Both report:

~~~text
route_count = 2
covered_count = 1
coverage = 50%
~~~

but the retained defeat content differs.

Therefore equal coverage statistics do not imply coverage equivalence.

## Core countermodel 6 — internally complete, cross-ontology disagreement

O1 partitions defeat content as:

~~~text
{x,y} / {z}
~~~

O2 partitions as:

~~~text
{x} / {y,z}
~~~

A packet may cover every named route in O1 yet fail a load-bearing route in O2.

Therefore:

~~~text
COMPLETE IN O1
!=
COMPLETE IN O2
~~~

unless a typed content-preserving transport witness exists.

## Core countermodel 7 — ancestry-entangled routes

Two named routes use different labels but arise from one common failure lineage.

~~~text
ROUTE COUNT = 2
LOAD-BEARING ANCESTRY = 1
~~~

Route plurality does not manufacture defeat independence.

## Core countermodel 8 — hidden-route discovery

At t0:
- all declared O0 routes are covered;
- separator packet earns provisional authority.

At t1:
- new world-contact reveals defeat content h absent from O0.

The correct response is:

~~~text
REOPEN AFFECTED COVERAGE AUTHORITY
+
RECONSTITUTE ROUTE ONTOLOGY
~~~

not:
- pretend O0 was complete;
- declare every historical local result meaningless.

## Core countermodel 9 — refinement preserves local coverage

O1:
~~~text
r -> {x,y}
~~~

O2:
~~~text
r1 -> {x}
r2 -> {y}
~~~

If the predecessor probe genuinely exercised both x and y and CTL preserves that witness, the refinement can preserve local coverage.

This is the positive control against ROUTE-REVISION-FATAL.

## Core countermodel 10 — split without content witness

Same syntactic refinement as above, but predecessor evidence only exercised x.

Coverage cannot be duplicated onto both successors.

## Core countermodel 11 — noncommuting route revision

Direct:
~~~text
O1 -> O3
~~~

Composed:
~~~text
O1 -> O2 -> O3
~~~

produce different coverage-transport judgments.

This is a route-ontology reopening event, not proof that one path is metaphysically correct.

## Core countermodel 12 — hidden ancestry discovery

Two routes were treated independent.
Later evidence reveals shared calibration or generator ancestry.

The route set may remain extensionally unchanged while its independence geometry changes.

Coverage authority must be revisable without pretending the route labels changed.

## Candidate positive principles

### A — Route Identity Is Witness-Relative
A route label or count does not fix cross-version route identity.

### B — Split/Merge Must Preserve Defeat Content
Route cardinality may change without creating or erasing defeat content.

### C — Coverage Is Content-Sensitive
Coverage equivalence depends on preserved defeat distinctions, not equal percentages.

### D — Route Independence Is Ancestry-Relative
Named route plurality may collapse under shared failure ancestry.

### E — Coverage Must Transport Across Revision
A prior coverage receipt remains authoritative only where defeat content is demonstrably transported.

### F — Hidden Routes Reopen Locally
New defeat content reopens the affected coverage claim without automatic global invalidation.

### G — Route Revision Is Reopenable
Noncommuting or loss-bearing revision paths remain explicit and can reopen adequacy.

### H — No Final Defeat-Route Ontology
Finite route stability never certifies the complete space of ways a claim may fail.

### I — Route Contestability Is Not Adequacy-Fatal
Scoped separator adequacy can survive explicit versioned route constitution.
