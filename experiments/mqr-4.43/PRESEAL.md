## Explanandum lock

Given:
- an open rival frontier;
- finite scientific attention/budget;
- fallible generator portfolios;
- no complete probability distribution over future rivals;
- no canonical scalar value function over unknown discoveries;
- no right to infer frontier completeness from search history;

```text
UNKNOWN-RIVAL PRIOR
+

=

```

The court must distinguish:
1. impossible universal optimization over the unknown;
2. explicit normative or mission constraints;
3. structural procedural obligations that can be audited without pretending to value all possible unknown discoveries on one scale.

## Candidate objects

### 1. Research-Attention Budget (RAB)

A declared finite resource budget:

```text
B = total search/research attention available over a declared horizon.
```

Units may be compute, experiments, person-hours, instrument time, samples or another explicit resource.

Budget units are not automatically commensurable across domains. A court must declare its cost model rather than infer one.

### 2. Search Lane

A search lane is a generator/query/representation route eligible to receive attention.

Each lane carries:
- generator identity;
- ancestry identity;
- search class;
- claim scope;
- activation cost;
- admissible horizon/window;
- query or representation dependencies;
- current status;
- observed search history.

A nominally distinct lane may still share ancestry with another lane.

### 3. Activation Cost

For lane g:

```text
a_g = minimum resource needed for a qualifying exercise of that lane.
```

An allocation smaller than `a_g` may be accounting expenditure without reopening competence.

This creates the distinction:

```text
NOMINAL RESERVE != ACTIVATION-CAPABLE RESERVE
```

### 4. Activation-Capable Reopening Reserve (ACRR)

A repair to the 4.42 FRR surface.

An ACRR must be:
- spendable;
- sufficient to activate at least one declared reopening lane according to its frozen activation contract;
- not entirely trapped in common-mode ancestry;
- periodically exercised or explicitly carried as debt;
- auditable after use.

ACRR does not establish frontier completeness.

### 5. Exploration Obligation

An exploration obligation is not a claim that a lane is scientifically valuable.

It is a declared governance commitment such as:
- exercise at least one noncommon-ancestry generator within horizon H;
- challenge the current query language;
- exercise a representation-mutation lane;
- replay an external-case transfer;
- investigate a persistent residual/anomaly class;
- perform a root/instrument challenge.

Its authority comes from the declared research contract, not from a hidden prior over future discoveries.

### 6. Exploration Debt Ledger (EDL)

Exploration debt is procedural, not metaphysical.

A debt item records:

```text
{
  obligation_id,
  lane_or_class,
  activation_threshold,
  last_qualifying_exercise,
  due_horizon,
  overdue_age,
  ancestry_gap,
  unresolved_escape_history,
  status
}
```

Default rule:

```text
EXPLORATION DEBT IS A VECTOR / LEDGER, NOT A SCALAR.
```

Two portfolios can have the same total spend or same count of overdue lanes while differing materially in which obligations are unpaid.

Scalarization requires externally declared weights and must expose them.

### 7. Rival-Search Allocation Receipt (RSAR)

The RSAR records:

```text
claim scope C
budget B and units
horizon H
eligible lanes G
generator ancestry A
activation costs a_g
mandatory exploration obligations O
local FCR exploitation obligations X
cost model K
allocation schedule S
observed search history Y
debt ledger D
reopening-reserve state R
policy family P
declared normative inputs N
forbidden inferences Z
```

RSAR is a governance receipt, not an optimality theorem.

### 8. Allocation Admissibility Envelope (AAE)

Instead of a unique optimal policy, MQR-4.43 tests a set-valued object.

A schedule is admissible relative to a frozen RSAR when it:
- stays within budget;
- satisfies declared hard floors/ceilings;
- preserves an activation-capable reopening route;
- does not permanently starve a mandatory obligation;
- exposes ancestry/common-mode concentration;
- carries unpaid exploration obligations as debt rather than silently retiring them;
- reopens after an admitted escape.

The AAE may contain multiple incomparable schedules.

```text
ADMISSIBLE != OPTIMAL.
```

### 9. Structural Dominance

Policy A may dominate policy B only relative to an explicit declared contract when A:
- uses no more declared cost;
- violates no obligation satisfied by B;
- carries no additional debt on declared coordinates;
- preserves at least as much activation-capable reopening competence;
- improves at least one declared coordinate.

This is contract-relative Pareto dominance, not universal scientific value.

### 10. Search Opportunity-Cost Vector

Opportunity cost is represented without forced scalarization.

Candidate coordinates:
- local FCR exploitation deferred;
- exploration activations deferred;
- ancestry-diversification obligation deferred;
- query/representation challenge deferred;
- unresolved exploration debt;
- instrument/root challenge deferred.

A scalar tradeoff is unauthorized unless the weights are declared outside the checker.

## Core impossibility target

### Allocation Non-Identifiability under Observational Equivalence

Freeze two admissible worlds:

```text
W_A: next-step frontier escape is reachable only through lane A.
W_B: next-step frontier escape is reachable only through lane B.
```

Before the next allocation:
- claim scope is identical;
- current frontier is identical;
- generator metadata is identical;
- observed search history is identical;
- budget is one activation;
- the policy sees the same RSAR surface.

Any deterministic policy based only on that common surface must choose the same lane in both worlds.

Therefore it cannot capture the one-step accessible escape in both.

Target principle:

```text
OBSERVATIONALLY IDENTICAL OPEN-FRONTIER HISTORIES
DO NOT IDENTIFY A UNIVERSALLY CORRECT NEXT SEARCH ACTION.
```

This is stronger than saying prediction is hard. It blocks universal allocation optimality without additional assumptions.

Randomization can prevent deterministic starvation or optimize a separately declared minimax criterion, but it does not make the unknown scientifically commensurable by itself.

## Frozen attacks

### Attack 1 — Twin-World Allocation Indistinguishability

Instantiate W_A and W_B with the same visible RSAR and opposite one-step escape lane.

Target:

```text
UNIVERSAL NEXT-ACTION OPTIMALITY = REJECT
```

unless an additional prior/loss/mission criterion is supplied.

### Attack 2 — Hidden Utility Smuggling

Construct two policies with identical procedural validity but different rankings only after assigning values to unseen discoveries.

If the evaluator ranks them without an explicit value declaration:

```text
HIDDEN UTILITY SMUGGLING = DETECTED
```

### Attack 3 — Hidden Prior Smuggling

If historical yield is converted into expected future discovery probability without a declared statistical model or prior, reject the resulting expected-value claim.

```text
PAST YIELD != SELF-JUSTIFYING PRIOR OVER UNCONCEIVED RIVALS
```

### Attack 4 — Greedy Discovery-Yield Starvation

Lane A produces many cheap admitted but low-structural-impact rivals.
Lane B produces no rival for many rounds and then exposes a representation escape.

A policy maximizing recent rival count indefinitely starves B.

Target:

```text
DISCOVERY COUNT != SAFE ALLOCATION OBJECTIVE
```

### Attack 5 — Greedy FCR Exploitation Trap

A local root/query investment repeatedly improves current-frontier discrimination while an unfunded search lane would expose a rival outside the current query language.

Target:

```text
LOCAL FCR GAIN != PERMISSION TO STARVE FRONTIER SEARCH
```

### Attack 6 — Nominal Reserve Goodharting

Two schedules both report the same reserve fraction.

Schedule R1 spreads reserve below every lane's activation threshold.
Schedule R2 fully activates a declared reopening lane.

Then:

```text
NOMINAL RESERVE(R1) = NOMINAL RESERVE(R2)
but
REOPENING COMPETENCE(R1) != REOPENING COMPETENCE(R2)
```

The FRR metric can therefore be Goodharted unless activation thresholds are represented.

### Attack 7 — Reserve Hoarding

A reserve can remain forever unspent and still look "available".

Require either:
- qualifying exercise within the declared renewal horizon; or
- explicit debt/deferral receipt.

```text
UNEXERCISED FOREVER != LIVE REOPENING COMPETENCE
```

### Attack 8 — Common-Mode Portfolio Inflation

Allocate across many nominal generators sharing one ancestry while one independent ancestry lane receives nothing.

Target:

```text
GENERATOR COUNT DIVERSIFICATION != ANCESTRY DIVERSIFICATION
```

### Attack 9 — Equal Scalar Debt, Different Debt Geometry

Construct two debt states with equal:
- total overdue count;
- total missed budget;
- scalar debt score;

but different:
- ancestry gaps;
- query challenge gaps;
- representation challenge gaps;
- escape-relevant unresolved debt.

Target:

```text
SCALAR EXPLORATION DEBT = DEFAULT REJECT
```

### Attack 10 — Cheap-Rival Spam Goodharting

A generator can maximize admitted-rival count per resource by emitting cheap variations from one local grammar.

If allocation follows rival-count efficiency, meaningful nonlocal search can disappear.

Target:

```text
RIVALS PER COST != DISCOVERY VALUE
```

### Attack 11 — Adaptive Policy Path Dependence

Two policies begin from the same RSAR but early stochastic/noisy outcomes route later attention differently.

The final frontier depends on the allocation path.

Require path provenance and forbid treating the realized frontier as policy-independent evidence of what existed to be found.

### Attack 12 — Retirement by Recent Silence

A lane with repeated NO_NEW is retired, but a later admissible world places the only frontier escape behind that lane.

Target:

```text
RECENT SILENCE != RETIREMENT AUTHORITY
```

unless retirement criteria and successor coverage are explicit.

### Attack 13 — Randomization Overclaim

A randomized anti-starvation schedule gives each declared lane nonzero or bounded-gap access.

It does not prove:
- equal scientific importance;
- calibrated discovery probability;
- optimal expected value;
- coverage of generator classes not represented in the declared portfolio.

Target:

```text
RANDOMIZATION CAN SUPPORT PROCEDURAL COVERAGE
WITHOUT BECOMING AN EPISTEMIC ORACLE.
```

### Attack 14 — Unknown-Class Escape

Even a perfectly fair schedule over declared lanes can miss a rival generator class not represented in the eligible portfolio.

Target:

```text
FAIRNESS OVER G != COMPLETENESS OF G
```

### Attack 15 — Budget Expansion Non-Closure

More search budget may reduce declared debt and expand exercised coverage.

It still does not establish frontier completeness.

```text
MORE ATTENTION != OPEN-WORLD CLOSURE
```

### Attack 16 — Opportunity-Cost Scalarization Failure

Two schedules can trade:
- current discrimination;
- ancestry diversification;
- representation challenge;
- anomaly search;

in opposite directions.

Without declared weights, the evaluator must return INCOMPARABLE rather than fabricate a total order.

### Attack 17 — Mandatory Escape Reallocation

When a frontier escape is admitted, the old allocation plan must be invalidated or re-audited if:
- new obligations appear;
- query language changes;
- FCR changes;
- generator ancestry changes.

Target:

```text
ESCAPE DETECTED -> ALLOCATION RECEIPT REOPENED
```

### Attack 18 — Exploration-Debt Laundering

A missed mandatory exploration obligation cannot disappear because the lane is relabeled, merged into a common-mode generator, or removed from the eligible set.

Debt retirement requires:
- qualifying exercise;
- explicit obligation withdrawal with provenance;
- or successor-coverage evidence.

## Positive candidate principles

### Principle A — No Universal Open-Frontier Optimum

Without an explicit prior/loss/mission structure, the court should not emit a unique optimal allocation across admissible open worlds.

### Principle B — Set-Valued Guidance Can Survive

Finite attention can still be governed by an AAE containing all schedules satisfying explicit hard constraints.

```text
GUIDANCE WITHOUT TOTAL ORDER = POSSIBLE
```

### Principle C — Anti-Starvation Is Procedural, Not Probabilistic

For a finite declared portfolio with finite activation thresholds, a bounded-gap or round-robin exercise rule can guarantee qualifying access to each mandatory lane within a declared horizon, subject to sufficient budget.

This is a scheduling theorem, not a discovery-probability theorem.

### Principle D — Reopening Reserve Must Be Activation-Capable

The 4.42 FRR is strengthened:

```text
FRR -> ACRR
```

for allocation governance.

A reserve that cannot activate a lane is accounting, not competence.

### Principle E — Exploration Debt Must Remain Typed

Debt is indexed to obligations and ancestry/query/representation classes.

```text
DEBT LEDGER > DEFAULT SCALAR DEBT SCORE
```

### Principle F — Adaptive Search Requires Explicit Update Semantics

Observed history may change future schedules, but the update rule must declare what it is using:
- hard escape trigger;
- budget exhaustion;
- common-mode detection;
- explicit statistical model;
- explicit mission priority;
- contractual anti-starvation rule.

Undeclared conversion of yield into expected scientific value is forbidden.

### Principle G — Reopening Capacity Must Be Exercised

A live reserve can expire procedurally.

A qualifying court may require periodic exercise, successor coverage, or explicit debt carry.
