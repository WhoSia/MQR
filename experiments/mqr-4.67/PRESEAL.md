## Anti-self-confirmation constitution

MQR-4.67 explicitly treats self-confirming simulation as a primary failure mode.

Every substantive claim is exactly one of:

- **OBSERVED** — directly read from external source, repository artifact, execution log, connector result, or naturalistic record.
- **PROVED** — mechanically checked theorem under explicit formal premises.
- **ENUMERATED** — exhaustive result over a frozen finite grammar.
- **SIMULATED** — generated by a declared model or stochastic/synthetic environment; evidence about that model only.
- **INFERRED** — reasoned consequence from identified premises; neither observation nor theorem.
- **OPEN** — unresolved, missing, underdetermined, or untested.

Hard rules:

1. **SIMULATED ≠ OBSERVED.**
2. A simulation cannot certify that its own state variables, transition rules, loss coordinates, or event grammar are scientifically complete.
3. A simulator agreeing with the theory used to build it is not independent confirmation.
4. Parameter robustness within one simulator family is not cross-world validity.
5. Multiple implementations of one frozen semantics are implementation-diversity evidence only.
6. An attractive compression ratio is not epistemic evidence.
7. A scalar objective that reproduces the typed verdict surface does not thereby become scientifically privileged.
8. Failure of one scalarization is not proof that all scalar representations are impossible.
9. A synthetic harmful-merge witness is a counterexample inside the frozen machine, not proof of naturalistic frequency.
10. Naturalistic reconstruction may test transport, but hindsight-selected cases cannot be called prospective.
11. Any favorable MQR-specific result receives a higher novelty and alternative-explanation burden.
12. If established bisimulation/state-abstraction machinery subsumes a result, record **BASELINE_ABSORBED** rather than rename it.
13. No result-dependent mutation of loss coordinates, budgets, event grammar, or verdict thresholds after executable reveal.
14. The canonical uncertainty phrase for unsupported extension is **OPEN**, not model-completed prose.

## Typed approximation object

A candidate approximate quotient `q : S → Q` may merge states that the exact 4.66 Q4 relation separates.

For each merged pair `(s,t)`, define a typed loss profile:

`L(s,t) = (SEP, REOPEN, PROV, EXT, RELEASE)`

where:

- **SEP** — future rival/separator capability loss;
- **REOPEN** — reopening-trigger mismatch;
- **PROV** — provenance/common-mode alias;
- **EXT** — exterior-route / novelty-route alias;
- **RELEASE** — current or future release/commit authority mismatch.

These coordinates are not assumed commensurable.

## Loss states

Each loss coordinate uses:

- `NONE`
- `BOUNDED`
- `MATERIAL`
- `FATAL`

Interpretation is coordinate-specific and must be frozen before reveal.

No default numeric encoding has authority.

## Componentwise budget constitution

A budget is a vector of per-coordinate ceilings, not one number:

`B = (B_SEP, B_REOPEN, B_PROV, B_EXT, B_RELEASE)`.

A merge is componentwise admissible iff every coordinate is no worse than its own declared ceiling and no hard veto fires.

This relation is a partial preorder at most.

## Hard veto candidates

Before reveal, the following candidate vetoes are eligible to be tested:

- any `FATAL` coordinate;

- exterior-route alias that can activate a previously absent rival under one admitted NOVELTY continuation;
- accumulated loss crossing a coordinate-specific re-expansion threshold.
