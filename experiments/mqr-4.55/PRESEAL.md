## Governing question

MQR-4.54 asked:

> when may two representations be treated as scientifically the same?

MQR-4.55 asks:

> when may the family of consequences used to test that sameness be treated as scientifically adequate, when relevant consequences can be unconceived, tacitly generated, representation-dependent, or revealed only by new interventions?

The problem is not merely finite sample size.

It is **endogeneity of the consequence family itself**.

## Core constitutional object

Let the currently admitted consequence family be:

~~~text
F = P ⊔ A ⊔ M ⊔ D ⊔ S
~~~

where:
- `P` — observational probes / readouts / comparison contexts;
- `A` — interventions / actions / manipulations;
- `M` — admissible morphisms / translation / transport checks;
- `D` — defeat routes / anomaly / failure distinctions;
- `S` — scope / embedding / boundary contexts.

A consequence-family constitution is:

~~~text
Ψ = (T, F, Γ_F, Π, E, H, R)
~~~

where:
- `T` — target claim / decision / use contract;
- `F` — current typed consequence family;
- `Γ_F` — family-generation grammar: how new consequences may be proposed;
- `Π` — provenance / ancestry / common-mode graph of family members and generators;
- `E` — adversarial expansion routes, including off-grammar routes;
- `H` — heuristic / intuitive / diagrammatic / tacit proposal ecology;
- `R` — reopening law after new consequence discovery.

`H` is deliberately inside the constitution but outside direct claim authority.

## Frozen admissibility obligations

A consequence-family receipt can earn only scoped authority.

### A1 — Target relevance
Every load-bearing family member must state which target distinction or authority condition it can affect.

### A2 — Typed-channel declaration
Probe, intervention, morphism, defeat and scope consequences must not be silently collapsed into one undifferentiated test count.

### A3 — Ancestry / common-mode audit
Nominal multiplicity does not establish epistemic diversity. Shared generator, instrument, model, training lineage or representation may create common-mode blindness.

### A4 — Generator-grammar disclosure
The current rules by which new consequences are proposed must be inspectable.

### A5 — Off-grammar escape route
At least one live route must be capable of proposing a consequence not generated by the incumbent grammar.

### A6 — Adversarial expansion
The family must be prospectively exposed to additions that could overturn the current adequacy verdict.

### A7 — Serialization invariance
Splitting one consequence into many labels or merging equivalent consequences must not manufacture family adequacy.

### A8 — Tacit-competence exposure
When a test's discriminating function depends on skill not captured by text/code, that dependence must remain an explicit executable/reconstructible competence obligation rather than invisible pedigree.

### A9 — Heuristic role integrity
Intuition, aesthetic response, expert recognition, diagrams, analogy and model manipulation may propose tests or representations; they may not directly mark their own products PASS.

### A10 — Holdout expansion
At least one consequence family or generator must be withheld from the construction process when the scientific setting makes such separation feasible.

### A11 — Reopening reserve
A newly material consequence must be able to reopen the current WCICR/WCCFR authority without being dismissed merely because it was absent from the original family.

## Frozen countermodel bank

### CM1 — Many probes / one ancestry
Ten nominal probes derive from one representation and one measurement pipeline. All pass. A single independently generated probe breaks the target equivalence.

Pressure:
`N_PROBES` does not measure consequence-family independence.

### CM2 — Probe saturation / intervention escape
Every observational probe in `P` agrees, while a newly admitted intervention in `A` produces different reachable consequences.

Pressure:
observational saturation does not establish intervention adequacy.

### CM3 — Intervention saturation / morphism escape
All current interventions preserve the target relation, but a cross-representation transport in `M` exposes a distinction erased by the incumbent family.

Pressure:
action-space success does not establish translation/transport adequacy.

### CM4 — Defeat family generated from the target
Every registered defeat route is compiled from assumptions shared with the target constitution.

An externally generated anomaly lies outside the defeat grammar.

Pressure:
a theory cannot earn robustness merely by surviving failures it taught the evaluator to imagine.

### CM5 — Serialization inflation
One load-bearing consequence is split into many labels and nominal coverage rises while the consequence quotient is unchanged.

Pressure:
raw family size and coverage fractions are representation-sensitive.

### CM6 — Generator-grammar closure / world escape
The incumbent generator grammar is finite and internally closed. A consequence proposed by an off-grammar route separates the target.

Pressure:
grammar closure does not imply world-facing consequence closure.

### CM7 — Expert plurality / training common-mode
Several experts independently nominate the same consequence set but share the same training lineage, canonical examples and representational toolkit.

A differently trained route discovers an escape.

Pressure:
headcount independence does not establish generator independence.

### CM8 — Productive intuition
An expert's pre-deliberative structural suspicion proposes a consequence absent from the formal generator. Prospective world contact validates the separator.

Pressure:
useful consequence discovery need not originate in a fully explicit formal rule.

This does **not** make the intuition itself evidence for the target claim.

### CM9 — Seductive intuition
A consequence receives high intuitive salience, elegance or felt-rightness, but prospective world contact shows it is irrelevant or misleading.

Pressure:
intuition is not a truth oracle.

### CM10 — Formal completeness / primitive omission
A formal consequence language is complete relative to its current syntax/semantics, while the scientific distinction later turns on a predicate or operation not expressible in that language.

Pressure:
formal completeness within a constituted language does not establish scientific consequence completeness.

### CM11 — Protocol-complete / competence-incomplete
A textual protocol fully specifies the nominal procedure, but a transferred team cannot reproduce its discriminating function until missing tacit/embodied competence is reconstructed.

Pressure:
procedure custody is not discriminating-capability custody.

### CM12 — Same current family / different expansion futures
Two inquiry programmes have identical current `F` but different `Γ_F / Π / E / H / R`; one can discover and reopen on an escape that the other systematically cannot generate.

Pressure:
current family contents alone do not determine consequence-family adequacy.

## Frozen primary hypotheses

### H1 — No self-certifying consequence family
Passing every currently admitted consequence does not establish that all material consequences have been admitted.

### H2 — Consequence-family adequacy is generation-sensitive
Two identical current families can have different epistemic quality when their generator ancestry, expansion routes and reopening capacities differ.

### H3 — Rigor is necessary in role, not exhaustive in reach
Formal rigor can certify or refute inferences inside a constituted consequence language and can expose hidden gaps, but does not by itself generate every scientifically relevant primitive, representation, intervention or anomaly.

### H4 — Intuition is proposal-bearing, not authority-bearing
Skilled recognition, diagrams, analogy, aesthetic signals, tacit know-how and model manipulation can rationally affect search and consequence generation, but require prospective adjudication before altering claim authority.

### H5 — Some intuition can be operationalized
A phenomenon initially called “intuition” may later decompose into explicit cues, heuristics, models or learned pattern recognition. MQR-4.55 rejects treating ineffability as a protected primitive.

### H6 — Common-mode capture is more important than nominal family size
Ten consequences with one ancestry can provide less escape capacity than one genuinely exterior consequence route.

### H7 — Local adequacy without completeness is possible
An open consequence family can earn scoped authority if its construction, ancestry, expansion and reopening are themselves world-contacted and challengeable.

## Consequence-family relations

MQR-4.55 freezes four distinct relations.

### Content inclusion
One family contains more currently admitted consequences.

### Quotient-equivalent refinement
One family has more labels but no new material consequence class.

### Generator expansion
One constitution can generate candidate consequences unavailable to another.

### Authority improvement
A consequence ecology earns more scoped authority because a new route adds independent world-contacted discrimination or reopening capacity.

The Court forbids:

~~~text
MORE TESTS
=
MORE INDEPENDENCE
=
MORE ADEQUACY
=
MORE AUTHORITY.
~~~

## Claim ceiling
