## Research-OS ordering

The independent 4.44 structure was frozen before this literature pass.

```text
THINK -> ARGUE -> ATTACK -> SEARCH -> TEST -> REVISE
```

The literature does not decide the court. It is used to kill novelty, donate distinctions and expose stronger failure modes.

## 1. Agenda setting and value-laden research priorities are established prior art

Heather Douglas explicitly treats the choice of which knowledge to pursue as a value-laden decision point in science. Kevin Elliott likewise argues that scientific aims must be made explicit and critically assessed.

- Douglas, H. (2023), “The importance of values for science.” DOI: 10.1080/03080188.2023.2191559.
- Elliott, K. C. (2017), *A Tapestry of Values*, especially the chapter “What Are We Trying to Accomplish?” DOI: 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190260804.003.0004.

Pressure on MQR:

```text
"research agendas depend on values" = NOT NOVEL
"explicit goals matter" = NOT NOVEL
```

4.44 must therefore not present EOCR as a discovery that agendas are normative.

## 2. Longino/Kitcher already move objectivity upstream into social constitution

Longino's critical contextual empiricism makes standards, assumptions and cognitive goals criticizable within social epistemic practice. Kitcher's well-ordered science treats agenda selection as a problem of publicly informed deliberation rather than a conclusion from epistemic success alone.

The project's own MQR-1.53-B and MQR-1.98-B already classified this area as prior-art-heavy:

```text
SCIENTIFIC-PLURALISM-ESTABLISHED
DEMOCRATIC-AGENDA-SETTING-ESTABLISHED
VALUE-LADEN-RESEARCH-AGENDAS-ESTABLISHED
NOVELTY-HOLD
```

4.44 therefore cannot claim novelty for participation, pluralism, stakeholder inclusion or democratic priority setting.

## 3. Meta-level criticism of the goals themselves is directly prior

Lari (2024) separates first-level scientific criticism from meta-level criticism of a community's cognitive goals and shows that standards appropriate under a fixed goal can become circular when the goal itself is contested.

- Lari, T. (2024), “What counts as relevant criticism? Longino's critical contextual empiricism and the feminist criticism of mainstream economics.” DOI: 10.1016/j.shpsa.2024.02.005.

This is a direct novelty kill for the bare 4.44 move:

```text
FIXED-GOAL CRITICISM != GOAL-CONSTITUTION CRITICISM
```

The surviving MQR task must be more specific: executable provenance and burden-preservation rules for an already explicit obligation constitution.

## 4. Who sets the standards is itself a known regress/circularity problem

Leuschner argues that pluralist objectivity can become circular if the framework does not answer who sets the standards determining qualified participation; criticism of well-ordered science raises analogous appointment/action-guidance problems.

- Leuschner, A. (2012), “Pluralism and objectivity: Exposing and breaking a circle.” DOI: 10.1016/j.shpsa.2011.12.030.

Pressure on MQR:

```text
"the constitution of the critics/agenda matters" = NOT NOVEL
```

4.44 must not stop at “audit the agenda setter.” It needs an executable invariant that survives changes in labels and partitions.

## 5. Classification itself has consequences

Bowker and Star's *Sorting Things Out* shows that classification systems are infrastructural and consequential: categories can render work visible/invisible, support organizational forgetting, create residual categories and change what can be represented.

- Bowker, G. C. & Star, S. L. (1999), *Sorting Things Out: Classification and Its Consequences*.

The project Drive copy was inspected after preseal.

This materially strengthens Attack 1/2/5/6:

```text
OBLIGATION TAXONOMY IS NOT A NEUTRAL LABEL LAYER.
```

However, Bowker/Star do not provide the specific finite burden-union invariance checker proposed here.

## 6. Unconceived alternatives block obligation-ontology closure

Stanford's problem of unconceived alternatives is established prior art against assuming that the currently considered rival space is exhaustive. Sample explicitly reframes that challenge in terms of scientists' epistemic obligations.

- Stanford, P. K. (2006), *Exceeding Our Grasp*. DOI: 10.1093/0195174089.001.0001.
- Sample, M. S. (2015), “Stanford's Unconceived Alternatives from the Perspective of Epistemic Obligations.” DOI: 10.1086/683324.

Pressure on MQR:

```text
OPEN OBLIGATION ONTOLOGY = PRIOR-ART-COMPATIBLE
WORLD-COMPLETE EOCR = UNAUTHORIZED
```

4.44's omitted-burden twin world is therefore an executable local countermodel, not a claim to invent the unconceived-alternatives problem.

## 7. Research-priority setting already treats legitimacy as more than optimization

WHO's 2025 ethics guidance for health research priority setting treats scarce research resources, affected populations, stakeholder inclusion and value judgments as constitutive of legitimate priority setting. Reviews of priority-setting practice likewise emphasize stakeholder selection, context, process and follow-up rather than a purely technical ranking.

- WHO (2025), *WHO guidance on the ethics of health research priority setting*, ISBN 9789240110953.
- Viergever-style and later review literature on research-priority-setting methods supplies process machinery, not an open-world adequacy theorem.

Pressure on MQR:

```text
PRIORITY-SETTING PROCEDURE / PARTICIPATION = PRIOR ART
```

## 8. Surviving candidate contribution

```text
NOT:

  a new theory of values in science

BUT:

  (2) split/merge-equivalent constitutions are compared by declared burden union;
  (3) agenda-source count is separated from ancestry count;

  (6) endogenous agenda formation remains admissible but dependence-marked;
  (7) escape reopens the obligation constitution;
  (8) no finite passing constitution earns world-complete obligation ontology.
```
