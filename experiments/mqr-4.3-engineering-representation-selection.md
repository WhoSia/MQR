# MQR-4.3 — Representation Selection under Engineering World-Contact, Mathematization, Invariant Transport, Counterfactual Control & Whether Mathematical Representations Survive because of World Structure, Task Selection, Institutional Selection or Coevolution

## 0. Return-to-Origin
MQR-4.3 returns to an early MQR question:

> When multiple representations describe, predict, or control the same phenomenon, why do some representations persist across repeated world-contact while others remain local?

The engineering case is especially severe because representations are repeatedly exposed to:
- fabrication;
- scaling;
- loading;
- failure;
- measurement;
- standardization;
- optimization;
- interoperability;
- economic constraint.

The stage does **not** assume that mathematical survival proves that the world is intrinsically mathematical.

## 1. Four rival explanations
Presealed rivals:

### H_W — World-Structure Fit
Mathematical representations persist because they track stable world-side relations better than alternatives.

### H_T — Task-Selection Fit
Engineering selects or reformulates problems so that mathematically tractable relations become disproportionately important.

### H_I — Institutional Selection
Schools, professional institutions, standards, bureaucracies, firms and states preferentially stabilize mathematical/scientific representations because they are teachable, inspectable, standardized and portable.

### H_C — Coevolution
World constraints, mathematical formalisms, instruments, standards, engineering objectives and institutions mutually reshape one another. No single one-way "science -> engineering application" relation is sufficient.

## 2. Historical witness A — École Polytechnique and institutional mathematization
École Polytechnique's official history states:
- founded in 1794 as École Centrale des Travaux Publics in response to a shortage of engineers and high-level officials;
- renamed École Polytechnique in 1795;
- mission: broad scientific education with strong emphasis on mathematics, physics and chemistry;
- graduates were prepared for artillery/engineering, mining, bridges and roads.

This is not evidence that engineering began in 1794.
It is evidence that a state institution deliberately made mathematics and physical science into a **portable prerequisite layer** for multiple engineering corps.

### Rival effect
- H_I: strongly supported.
- H_T: supported; the training pipeline selected problems and competencies expressible through shared scientific foundations.
- H_W: compatible but not sufficient; institutional design itself did causal work.
- H_C: supported because scientific education and state engineering practice were coupled rather than sequential.

## 3. Historical witness B — Glasgow engineering science and Rankine
Marsden's history of Glasgow distinguishes academic engineering from apprenticeship and professional practice and identifies the rise of academic engineering as changing the structure and status of practical engineering.

The first British university chair of engineering was established at Glasgow in 1840.

Marsden's reconstruction emphasizes **economy, efficiency and measurement** as prime movers in the differentiation of engineering science.

Rankine worked on:
- heat engines;
- steam-engine economy;
- ship resistance;
- applied mechanics;
- thermodynamics;
while embedded in engineering education and professional institutions.

Hutchison's history of Rankine shows that the rise of thermodynamics involved the reconciliation of Carnot's engine theory with heat-work interconvertibility, with Rankine developing theoretical machinery intertwined with engineering questions.

### Key inference
The engineering problem did not merely wait for a finished physics theory.

Instead:

### Rival effect
- H_T: strongly supported.
- H_I: supported through academic engineering/professional institutionalization.
- H_W: supported insofar as thermodynamic relations successfully constrained real engines.
- H_C: strongest fit; theory and engineering objectives co-developed.

## 4. Historical witness C — nineteenth-century electricity and telegraph practice
Bruce Hunt's history of nineteenth-century electrical theory and practice treats theory and electrical technology as historically intertwined rather than sequential.

A particularly sharp example comes from Maxwell's use of null methods.
Historical work on Maxwell and Cavendish shows that Maxwell connected a precision experimental tradition to **telegraph engineers' null practices**, using it in his methodological program for electrical measurement.

The telegraph also created pressure for:
- reliable resistance measurements;
- comparable units;
- transmission calculations;
- instrument standardization.

Thus engineering practice did not simply consume electrical theory.
It helped select what counted as a useful measurable quantity, a reliable method and a transportable formalism.

### Rival effect
- H_W: supported by successful electrodynamic regularities.
- H_T: strongly supported; cable and telegraph performance generated specific calculational problems.
- H_I: strongly supported through unit/measurement standardization and professional coordination.
- H_C: strongly supported; theory, instrumentation, units and infrastructure coevolved.

## 5. Historical witness D — thermodynamic and mechanical representation transport
Carnot, Clausius, Thomson and Rankine did not merely produce more accurate verbal descriptions of engines.

They produced representation structures that could:
- abstract from particular machines;
- compare efficiencies;
- express counterfactual ideal cycles;
- transport calculations across engine designs;
- support design reasoning beyond previously observed instances.

The relevant advantage was therefore not only descriptive fit.

It was **productive transport**.

A representation that preserved relations under changes of apparatus could support reasoning about machines that had not yet been built.

## 6. Mathematical representation is not one thing
MQR-4.3 rejects an equation-centric picture.

Engineering mathematics includes:
- equations;
- geometrical constructions;
- graphs;
- reciprocal diagrams;
- dimensional analysis;
- optimization surfaces;
- statistical tolerances;
- simulations.

Classical graphic statics is an instructive example:
Rankine/Maxwell reciprocal constructions encode equilibrium relations visually/geometrically, and modern work still generalizes this tradition using duality and higher-dimensional mathematical structures.

Thus the historical selection target is better called:

**formal transformable representations**

rather than "equations" alone.

## 8. But mathematics is not sufficient
Walter Vincenti's aeronautical-history program is a major warning against reducing engineering knowledge to applied science.

Engineering knowledge can include:
- descriptive knowledge;
- prescriptive knowledge;
- tacit knowledge;
- empirical design rules;
- human-performance requirements;
- production knowledge.

Historically useful artifacts can precede a satisfying theory of why they work.

## 9. Rival adjudication
### H_W — world-structure fit
**SURVIVES / NECESSARY BUT INSUFFICIENT.**

If mathematical relations did not repeatedly constrain real artifacts, engineering world-contact would kill them.
World resistance matters.

But H_W alone cannot explain:
- why specific quantities become salient;
- why some formalisms become standards;
- why training institutions converge on particular representations;
- why useful engineering knowledge can precede or exceed formal scientific theory.

### H_T — task-selection fit
**SURVIVES / STRONG.**

Engineering objectives strongly select representations that support:
- calculation before construction;
- efficiency comparison;
- scaling;
- tolerance allocation;
- optimization;
- prediction under intervention.

This partly explains why mathematically expressible variables become central.

But it cannot by itself explain persistent success when artifacts meet an independent resistant world.

### H_I — institutional selection
**SURVIVES / STRONG.**

École Polytechnique, university engineering, professional societies, measurement standards and later industrial education all stabilized representations that were:
- teachable;
- examinable;
- reproducible;
- interoperable.

Institutional lock-in can also preserve non-optimal representations.
Therefore institutional survival is not evidence of truth by itself.

### H_C — coevolution
**BEST CURRENT EXPLANATORY SYNTHESIS.**

The historical cases are most naturally reconstructed as:

[
	ext{world constraint}
leftrightarrow
	ext{engineering task}
leftrightarrow
	ext{instrument/measurement}
leftrightarrow
	ext{formal representation}
leftrightarrow
	ext{institution/standard}.
]

This is not a claim that all factors have equal causal weight.

It is the claim that the mathematization of engineering cannot be reconstructed accurately as a one-way transfer from finished science into passive practice.

## 10. Selection vs truth
A central MQR correction:

[
	ext{representation survives engineering selection}

otRightarrow
	ext{representation is ontologically true}.
]

Selection can reward:
- tractability;
- coordination;
- conservative safety margins;
- compatibility with existing standards;
- local approximations;
- economic convenience.

However survival under **heterogeneous noncommon world-contact** is still evidentially relevant.

A stronger representation has more realist significance when its persistence depends on:
- successful transport to new regimes;
- independent failure opportunities;
- interventions it was not fitted to;
- nontrivial counterfactual success;
- robustness across alternative measurement surfaces.

Thus engineering selection can contribute to realist warrant only when institutional/task selection cannot explain the success alone.

Engineering is epistemically interesting for MQR because it repeatedly converts representations into material commitments.

A bridge equation, circuit model, heat-engine relation or aerodynamic representation is not merely compared with past observations.

## 12. The important asymmetry
Science often asks:

> Which representation best explains/describes the observed world?

Engineering adds:

> Which representation remains useful when we deliberately create a world-state that did not previously exist?

This is a genuine extra selection pressure.

Call it **constructive counterfactual exposure**:

a representation is exposed not only to observations of existing systems, but to the behavior of systems designed from its implications.

This label is descriptive, not a new constitutional primitive.

## 13. Potential connection to MQR-4.x authority constitution
MQR-4.0–4.2 adjudicates what claim commitment current evidence licenses.

MQR-4.3 adds a possible source of especially strong evidence-state structure:

**artifact-mediated world-contact**.

But MQR must not infer:

[
	ext{artifact works}

	ext{all theoretical vocabulary is realist}.

A device may work despite:
- idealization;
- compensating errors;
- parameter fitting;
- empirical correction;
- redundant mechanisms.

Therefore successful engineering artifacts can strengthen only the claim dimensions actually load-bearing for their design success.

This reconnects MQR-4.3 directly to the claim-role-scope constitution.

## 14. Overfit attack
MQR-4.3 explicitly attacks its own attractive thesis.

### Attack A — survivorship bias
We preferentially remember mathematical representations used in successful engineering traditions.

### Attack B — mathematizable-task bias
Engineering education and institutions may select tasks amenable to mathematical formalization.

### Attack C — invisible scaffolding
Formal equations often rely on tacit fabrication knowledge, standards and empirical coefficients hidden from the final representation.

### Attack D — lock-in
A notation/model can persist because surrounding infrastructure is expensive to replace.

### Attack E — success without realism
A model can be predictively/design-useful within a narrow regime while its ontology is false or irrelevant.

All five block any inference from engineering mathematization directly to mathematical realism.

## 16. Novelty status
The broad ingredients are not MQR-novel:
- engineering as knowledge-generating rather than mere applied science;
- historical co-development of science and technology;
- variation-selection accounts of engineering knowledge;
- institutional histories of engineering science;
- model/representation pluralism.

MQR-specific value, if any, lies in importing this history into its existing distinction between:
- representation success;
- scope;
- role;
- world-contact;
- realist authority.

No new philosophical primitive is promoted.

## 17. Next empirical discriminator
A useful successor should compare **matched representations of the same engineering target** that differ in:
- mathematical abstraction;
- empirical/tacit supplementation;
- transport range;
- counterfactual performance.

Candidate historical families:
- analytical vs graphical statics;
- empirical airfoil tables vs aerodynamic theory;
- telegraph cable rules vs field/transmission theory;
- steam-engine indicator/empirical practice vs thermodynamic cycle representation.

The test should ask whether representation survival is predicted better by:
- world-structure fit,
- task fit,
- institutional embedding,
- or their interaction.
