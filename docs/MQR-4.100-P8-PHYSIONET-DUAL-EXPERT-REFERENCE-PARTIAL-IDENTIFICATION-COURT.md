## 1. Data origin and what expert independence actually means

The original PhysioNet `ANNOTATORS` metadata distinguishes `.q1c` = **second-pass manual waveform boundaries by human expert 1**, `.q2c` = **second-pass manual boundaries by human expert 2**, `.qt1/.qt2` = first passes, `.man` = selected reference beat fiducial labels, and `.hea`/`.dat` = original binary ECG waveform metadata and signal. Second-expert files exist on exactly **11 of 105** records. This dataset features two separately attributed readers, not two independent clinical reference truths; evidence does not prove that reviewers were blinded, that errors are independent or that selection was random. The original study explains that QRS fiducial locations were typically generated by **an automated QRS detector**, hence shared anchor provenance is particularly relevant.

## 2. Deterministic second-pass pairing semantics — source *not* truth

Executable audit: `experiments/mqr-4.100/p8_dual_expert_qtdb.py`, WFDB reference library 4.1.2, Python 3.11, numpy 1.26.4, pandas 2.2.3. Original ECG headers establish **250Hz** sampling, hence one sample = **4ms**.

Join units: the 487 selected `.man` reference `N` beat anchors across the 11 records; match each expert's `.q1c` or `.q2c` `N` to the nearest unassigned reference fiducial within ±10 samples. This is **source annotation matching**, not waveform-visual clinical adjudication.

Read each waveform's QT as `T end − QRS onset` from the expert's own `.q1c/.q2c` boundary markers. Parser contract: most recent `(` within 80 samples before each N, the next lower-case `t` peak within 200 samples after N but before next N, and first `)` after the T peak, before the next N. Missing any part produces a **missing interval**, not zero. `.qt1/.qt2` first-pass binary files are archived but not used as a second-pass gold standard; their untyped boundary markers `|` do not independently identify all waveform types without the other annotations.

Bounded human-source pairing outcome:

| Actual source object | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| Selected `.man` beat opportunities in 11 records | **487** |
| Expert 1 `.q1c` beat anchors | **487** |
| Expert 2 `.q2c` beat anchors | **404** |
| Both readers matched to same reference N within ±10 samples | **404** |

**Important selection counterexample:** `sel102` contains 85 selected reference beats, but expert 2 `.q2c` contains only 3 beat anchors and **2** complete QT intervals. `sel213` has 71 reference beats but 70 reader-2 anchors and 69 complete pairs. It is wrong to treat existence of two source files as meaning full comparable annotation coverage.

The reference beat association is insensitive in substance to a ±5, ±10, ±15, ±20 or ±30 sample match tolerance: ±5 yields 401 comparable QT pairs, and all thresholds ≥10 yield 402. At the illustrative **40ms** disagreement threshold the paired-difference count stays **88** across these tolerances. This is an exploratory robustness check, not preregistered timing optimisation.

## 3. What the paired human-source differences measure

Among the **402 selected, matched and completed** second-pass expert pairs, with `d=QT_2−QT_1`:

- mean signed `d = +2.04975ms`; median `0ms`;
- mean absolute `|d| = 27.24378ms`, maximum observed `372ms`;
- `88/402 ≈ 21.89%` have `|d| ≥ 40ms`.

These are *human annotation difference summaries conditional on matching and a parser*, **not estimates of either expert's true QT-error rate**. They should not be used for diagnosis, patient decisions or clinical normative thresholds: 40ms was chosen as an illustrative *difference tolerance* for a finite-source methodological demonstration.

**Extreme nonuniformity of the disagreement:** `sel213` contributes **34** of its 69 complete pairs to the 40ms-or-more category; `sel223` contributes **30** out of 31. Together these just **two** records contribute **64/88** events exceeding the illustrative threshold. Excluding both leaves **24/302** comparable pairs over the other nine records, which is not comparable to the pooled 88/402 without specifying the source-record weights. The 11 records are not a representative draw from a predeclared target population.

## 4. Sharp finite-sample missing-pair partial-identification result

Define an *illustrative operational functional* over the fixed 487 reference-beat opportunities:
\(\,g_i=\mathbf{1}\{|QT_{2i}-QT_{1i}|\ge 40\,\mathrm{ms}\}\,\), when both readers' labels hypothetically exist. On **402 actually complete pairs**, 88 satisfy `g=1`. There are **85 missing/partial** comparisons. Under absolutely no missingness mechanism restriction or exchangeability, letting each missing `g` take either 0 or 1 produces the **sharp finite-reference-set bound**
\[
88/487 \le (1/487)\sum_{i=1}^{487}g_i \le (88+85)/487.
\]
Numerically **[0.18069815, 0.35523614] = [18.07%, 35.52%]**. The endpoints are attainable completions of unmeasured reader-2 labels, *if one posits their counterfactual existence*, **not** identified factual labels, clinical ground truth or source-population confidence intervals. This is a classical worst-case missing-data bound (not an original identification theorem).

Source-conditional observed fraction `88/402=21.89%` falls within the interval, but should never be treated as representative of all 487 absent an assumption about why `q2c` is sparse on `sel102`.

## 5. More fundamental truth and risk nonidentifiability

Assume a latent numerical reference boundary (T_i) exists. The observed ((QT_{1i},QT_{2i})) do not point-identify individual reader accuracy. Two observationally equivalent assignments of latent QT truth, (T_i=QT_{1i}) and (T_i=QT_{2i}), exactly preserve the same 402 observed pairs but reverse the readers' absolute-error rankings. Even a third measurement is not automatically an independent external truth.

A correct *nontrivial lower bound on the combined error burden* is available **only conditional on the measured 402 pairs**:
\[
\frac{1}{2\cdot402}\sum_{i=1}^{402}\big(|QT_{1i}-T_i|+|QT_{2i}-T_i|\big)
\ge\frac{1}{2\cdot402}\sum_{i=1}^{402}|QT_{1i}-QT_{2i}|
\approx13.62189\ \mathrm{ms}.
\]
By triangle inequality this bound is sharp if each possible (T_i) lies between the two annotator estimates; it does **not** allocate the burden to a named reviewer, identify truth or provide an upper bound without restrictions. This classical property is explanatory applied mathematics, not an original MQR theorem. Even whether a single unique physically correct QT boundary exists may depend on annotation convention.

This is the proper 4.100 observation-quotient distinction: **two reviewer reports refine the empirical observed disagreement `σ`-field, yet do not refine latent truth equivalence classes enough to determine expert risks**. Reviewer identity ≠ statistical independence, and a shared waveform/QRS fiducial produces dependence routes.
